Case No. 1693 11112021

Date: November 10, 2021.

Approximate Time: 17:32 local time.

Weather: N/A.

Season: Spring.

Witnesses: A. R. P., 43 years old.

Description: The witness reported seeing and photographing at 17:32 (HL) on
November 11, 2021, what he considered an aerial anomaly while he was in the
commune of Maipú, in the Metropolitan Region of Santiago.

The user described what he saw as amorphous and white, always to the northeast of his position.

The user's location was georeferenced at coordinates 33° 28’ 12’’ South Latitude and 70° 44’ 21’’ West Longitude (Figure 2).

As audiovisual support for their report, the user provided a total of 11 photographic files, which were analyzed through ELA (Error Level Analysis).
(Analysis) and CMF (Copy and Motion Forgery) found that they were not digitally altered.

Additionally, the inspection of the metadata information confirmed that they were taken within a 16-second span, at 17:32 (HL) on the reported date.

Weather conditions were analyzed to understand the wind regime at the time of the captures. Using observations made from the Comodoro Arturo Merino Benítez International Airport (SCEL).
In Santiago, it was confirmed that between 16:00 and 18:00 (HL), the situation featured winds of intensity declining from 17 to 13 knots (31.5 to 24.1 km/h) originating from the south-southwest. These winds could push objects towards the north-northeast.

On the other hand, according to data collected by the radiosonde flight and gathered from the Santo Domingo Airport (SCSN), up to 5,500 meters of altitude, winds were predominantly from the northwest. Up to 1,500 meters, winds were reported as variable.
The winds were weak, with intensities varying from 1 to 3 knots (1.9 to 5.6 km/h). Above that and up to 3,100 meters, they came from the north-northwest at 14 knots (26 km/h). The SkewLopP diagram presents this information in Figure 3.

Thanks to the cooperation of the SCTB Airport Manager and the DGAC-SDO Operations Subdepartment Head, the pilot of the photographed aircraft was contacted by email, who stated, "I do not remember having seen anything that caught my attention, neither on this nor on other flights."

He added that, as he flew over Maipú, he was heading north or northeast and at an altitude of 2,500 to 3,500 feet.
The above resulted consistent with the object being able to move under 2,500 feet in a direction similar to that of the plane.

Considering all of the above, it is believed that there is a very high probability that what was reported was some type of partially inflated bag being dragged by the wind, whose volume changed geometrically according to the deformations caused in it by the breeze that pushed and pulled it away from the witness. This explains not only that its appearance changed, but
that it would also be difficult to detect if its shape appeared linear to the observer as the surface presented to view decreased. In addition, under these circumstances, it would stop shining. (Figure 5)

Independent advice was sought from an engineer who is an external advisor to the SEFAA. Additionally, to identify and contact the pilot of the photographed aircraft, valuable cooperation was obtained from the Head of the Section.
Air Traffic Investigation; from the Head of the Aerodrome Eulogio Sánchez and
the Responsible of the Sub-Department of Operations.

Images: The case was studied from 11 original photographic files of
5.184 x 3.456 pixels, in RAW format and captured with a
Canon EOS 7D model camera and a Canon EF 75-300mm f/4.0-5.6 lens. When
photographing, the depth of field was set to infinity and the shutter speed for
all was 1/400 second.
Conclusion: Based on the analysis of the photographer's testimony and the subsequent geographical, forensic, imaging, and meteorological studies, it was concluded that what was reported was a small object dragged by the wind that appeared in some photographs taken of an aircraft in flight. It was not seen by the aircraft's pilot and did not pose a threat to its navigation.

Given the distortions in its volume, it is estimated that it was a T-shirt-type bag, dragged by the wind. The changes in shape, volume, and
The visibility would be directly related to the breezes that dragged and carried them away from the witness.

Therefore, the case was closed and classified as "Probably Explained."
