Case No. 1866 27022023

Date: February 24, 2023.

Approximate Time: 18:52 local time.

Location: Cerro Arqueado de Barrera, Lo Barnechea.

Witnesses: F.R.P., 29 years old.

Images: The user provided three photographs.

Description: The user described the photographed object as a saucer-shaped object, silver, with no information on its size, attaching to the report the files "image0.jpeg", "image1.jpeg" and "image2.jpeg". The object under study is located in the first image and is shown alongside an enlargement in Figure 1.
Figure 1. Study object as visible in Image 1866-1.

What was reported was not seen by the user at the location, but rather, upon downloading the photographs he had taken of the local landscape, he noticed an unusual object in one of those images.

In his report, the user noted that (sic): “I took several sequential photos and upon reviewing them, I noticed the object. It does not correspond to a common aircraft or a local animal. The silence in the area where I was located is absolute,
"I'm always on the lookout for birds and helicopters (because I love them), but this time I don't have words to describe the object."

The location of the place where the photographs were taken was georeferenced with the coordinates 33° 15’ 23’’ S, 70° 29’ 41’’ W and 2085 m.s.n.m. (Figure 2)

Image 1866-1 (Figure 1) was captured with a wide-angle lens: the focal distance equivalent to 14 mm, the optical center of the lens was very close to the sensor (at 1.5 mm), which resulted in a wide field of view and a large depth of field. On one hand, more content from the scene was captured—evident in seeing the ground in front of the photographer's feet—and what was in focus was sharp. However, an additional degree of
distortion in the vertical axis since the object under study was located away from the focal center (Figure 3).

The silhouette is not consistent with that of a flying saucer. Considering that the
Metadata reported that the photograph is a composite of two images and that the content shows the existence of two oblique axes, suggesting a slight lateral displacement.

Additionally, since it was only perceived after reviewing the photos, all of the above allows for the consideration that what was studied was small and flew silently very close to the camera.

Conclusion: According to the analysis of the testimony and the results of the geographical, forensic, and photographic studies, and as indicated by the advisors.
The specialized consultants concluded that the report originated from the photography of an insect in flight. The characteristics of its silhouette suggest it was a dipteron.

Therefore, the case was closed and classified as "Explained."
