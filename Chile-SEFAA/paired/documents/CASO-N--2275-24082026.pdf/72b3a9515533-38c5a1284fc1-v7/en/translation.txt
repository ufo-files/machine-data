The forensic examination revealed that the photographs captured two versions of each scene to then generate a composite image.

The technical information of the iPhone 15 cell phones demonstrated that the devices automatically photocompose two shots to optimize them for different uses.
Aspects such as lighting, noise reduction, color accuracy, or focus level are considered. Then, they are combined into a single layer.

The iPhone 15 series adds an additional processing step, combining this optimized result with a larger image to add details, resulting in a default image with a resolution of 5712 x 4284 pixels when using the main camera or 8000 x 6000 pixels when digital zoom is applied.

Additionally, digital zoom was used in each of the photos, so the
The camera did not physically move its lenses to zoom in on the image, as it would with optical zoom. Instead, it took the center of the image sensor and digitally cropped and enlarged it, delivering final images of 2269 x 4032 pixels.

The geometric examination of the photograph prior to digital cropping (Figure 10) revealed that the object studied in image 2275-1 was a parasitic reflection or flare (Figure 11).

These optical aberrations occurred within the camera lenses.
To impact them with light coming from the luminaries and the reflections caused by the transparent closure the cellular phone faced when taking the pictures. For that reason, they only existed inside the equipment, which was consistent with the testimony regarding "I noticed after reviewing the images."

According to the analysis of the testimony and the results of the geographical studies,
The forensic and photographic analysis concluded that what was reported were not flying objects, but rather parasitic reflections or flares. By their nature, they had no relation to aerial operations.
