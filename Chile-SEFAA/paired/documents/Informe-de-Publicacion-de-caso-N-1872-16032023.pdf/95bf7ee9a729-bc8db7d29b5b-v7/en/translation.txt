Case No. 1872 16032023

Date: March 11, 2023.

Approximate Time: 11:30 local time.

Location: Tomé, Biobío Region.

Witnesses: G.C.M., 52 years old.

Images: User provided 1 photograph.
Description: The reported sighting was described as a silver circle whose size was not indicated, but it was visible for 1 to 5 seconds by a witness. The location from which it was first seen or the direction in which it disappeared was not specified.
The user finalized writing that (sic): “I didn't realize there was something in”
"In the sky, only after taking the photo did I realize there was something in the image. The context of the photo was to send a record of how the weather was in my city."

The user's location was georeferenced at coordinates 36° 37' 20" S
72° 56' 57" W, a location south of the city of Tomé and 800 meters from the coast. (Figure 1)

Figure 1
The two images that supported the report were analyzed through pericial examination.
of ELA (Error Level Analysis) and CMF (Copy and Movement Forgery)
noting that they were not digitally altered.

The examination of the information contained in their metadata confirmed that they
were captured at 10:53 and 10:54 (HL) on the reported date by the main camera of a
Motorola Moto G9 Plus smartphone. The device was automatically configured to
act as a wide-angle lens (focal length of 5.5 mm) and its maximum aperture is F1.8.

Figure 2. Study object, image 1872-2.
Figure 2 shows what was reported as seen directly, and Figure 3 shows the variation in contrast and brightness levels. In the latter, the presence of a halo around the main body of the object is enhanced.

Figure 3
The sky review using edge-detection algorithms detected an additional presence of a bird in flight. Its characteristics are consistent with the silhouette of a seagull. (Figure 4)

Figure 4
Along with the fact that it was only perceived once after reviewing the photos, all of this allows for the consideration that what was studied was small and flew silently very close to the user and moving diagonally away from their camera. This raised the hypothesis of encountering a biological origin, possibly a wasp, and led to the consultation of entomologists to improve its identification.

Conclusion:

According to the examination of the testimony and the results of the studies
Geographical, forensic, and photographic analysis, along with independent information from the specialized advisors consulted, concluded that what was reported was a flying insect. It was identified as a bee, very likely native.

Therefore, the case was closed and classified as "Explained."
