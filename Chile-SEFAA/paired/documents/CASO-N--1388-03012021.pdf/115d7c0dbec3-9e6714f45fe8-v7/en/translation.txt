To verify the working hypothesis that the reported object had a biological origin, independent expert opinions were requested from entomologists at the National Museum of Natural History.

An analysis was also requested from specialists at the Wildlife Ecology Laboratory (LEVS) at the Faculty of Sciences.
Forest and Nature Conservation Sciences at the University of Chile.

Entomologists consulted indicated that the photograph shows a blurred object whose silhouette appears to correspond to a specimen of Bombus dahlbomii, a native species with striking coloration that varies from yellowish to intense orange, flying from right to left in the scene.

Figure 5 shows a specimen of this species.

Figure 5. Bombus dahlbomii.

According to the National Inventory of Species of Chile
The Ministry of the Environment, the Bombus dahlbomii is a robust bee of a size that varies between 16 and 23 mm in small workers, 17 to 22 mm in males, and 26 to 33 mm in queens, and has black antennae, legs, and body. It exhibits abundant orange-colored pilosity, but can appear in various tones, and both in Chile and Argentina there are almost white individuals. In Chile, historically it was found from the Coquimbo Region to the Magallanes Region, including Chiloé Island.
and Tierra del Fuego. Currently, the populations have dramatically decreased.

Other names include: Bee-wasp, red bee-wasp, muscardin, duillin and diwmeñ (Mapudungun), Giant Patagonia bee-wasp, Don Basilio. Synonyms are Bombus chilensis and Bombus grandis.

Conclusion: According to the analysis of the testimony, the studies
Geographical, forensic, and photographic analysis, along with the results of the experts consulted, concluded that what was reported was the accidental inclusion of the flight of a native bee or wasp (Bombus dalhbomii). This insect crossed the scene from right to left, behind the person who was photographed, and was also not noticed by the photographer.
