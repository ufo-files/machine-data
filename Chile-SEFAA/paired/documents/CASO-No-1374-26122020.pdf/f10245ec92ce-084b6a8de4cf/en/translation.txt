Description: The informant reported seeing and photographing an
anomaly while making an astronomical observation, from the
commune of La Cisterna, Santiago Metropolitan Region, around
00:13 on December 25, 2020. In his report, he only indicated that
the reported object was located northwest of his position, and that
it showed movement "westward."

With this information, the photographic analysis of the images and the
confirmation received by the user of the reported (Figure 2), raised the
hypothesis of being in front of a possible astronomical origin.
Figure 3. Reported Phenomenon

Based on the above, independent expert opinions from two astronomers and an air traffic controller, all external advisors to the CEFAA, were requested.

One of the astronomers indicated that what was reported is a lunar sub-crater, part of the so-called "Sea of Rains," Mare Imbrium. When the Moon is in phase and the Sun illuminates the crater diagonally, in the interface zone between shadow and light, it thus displays the characteristics of a crater.
The air traffic controller reported that the photographs show
the Sinus Iridum bay, located near the Jura Mountains on the edge
of Mare Imbrium. He added that this bay is one of the lunar
formations that stands out in a 10-day-old Moon and is notable for
being on the edge of the terminator (the boundary between light and
shadow). At that time, a part of the bay is illuminated, and due to
the lunar curvature, sunlight also touches the mountains from a
certain height, making them appear to be floating.
Images: Three photographs in JPG format, captured on December 25, 2020, were received and are displayed below.

Conclusion: Based on forensic, photographic, and astronomical studies, as well as independent results from consulted advisors, it was concluded that the photographed object was a fragment of the wall of a lunar sub-crater that delimits the Sinus Iridum in the Mare Imbrium (Rain Sea).
