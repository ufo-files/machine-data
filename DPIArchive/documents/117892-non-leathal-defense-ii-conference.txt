John B. Alexander, Ph.D., the Conference Chairman retired from the Army in 1988. He
entered in 1956 and rose from private to sergeant first class, went to OCS in 1963, and
retired with the rank of colonel. His military background includes combat experience
commanding Special Forces teams in SE Asia from 1966-1969. An infantry officer. he
also had several assignments in intelligence and research and development. His last
position was Director of Advanced Systems Concepts for the US Army Laboratory
Command. Along the way he also spent five years as a deputy sheriff in Dade County,
Florida. From 1988 until he again retired in May of 1995, he was the Program Manager
for Nonlethal Defense at Los Alamos National Laboratory. Dr. Alexander's education
includes a B.G.S. from Nebraska-Omaha, M.A. from Pepperdine, and Ph.D. from Walden
University: Later his education included work at UCLA, MIT, and most recently the
Senior Managers Progtam in National and International Security at Harvard. He has
written many papers about nonlethal weapons and chaired the first major conference on
the topic held at Johns-Hopkins in November 1993. For that work, in 1994 he was
selected as an Aerospace Laureate by Aviation Week and space Technology In 1995 he
eceived a Department of Energy Award of Excellence for support to the Nuclear
Veapons Program. Since 1994 he has served as a US delegate to NATO/AGARD fo
studies of nonlethal weapons, a position he still holds. Currently he is Director for
Scientific Liaison for a private research institute in Las Vegas, Nevada.
