2.0 Description
The proposed power generation system is based upon the fact that the Lorentz Force in
certain conditions can be non-conservative, thus indicating the important relationship to
open systems and hence external forces. In this particular application, there is a frame of
reference shift between bound ions and conduction electrons in a conducting medium.
To create an optimal frame of reference shift, it is important to set up an asymmetry of
the electric field and establish a leakage current through a conductor and a dielectric,
which also serves to store charge. This will result in a force that can be used in a power
generation system.
This can be accomplished with asymmetric capacitors, utilizing corona generated in air as
a leaky dielectric and a non-leaky dielectric for storing charge. This combination of
dielectrics is straight forward, but ungainly, and results in structural mass that is not part
of the resulting force. Utilizing corona as a leaky dielectric is expedient; however, it is
very inefficient and consumes a tremendous amount of power. A bifilar pendulum can be
used to measure generated force with respect to voltage and current, and a rotor can be
used to measure the instantaneous power generated in rotation when balanced against a
calibrated drag.
In the proposed effort a pendulum and rotor will be utilized. The performance of the
asymmetrical capacitors is directly related to the nature of the materials and geometries
that will be employed. For example, purity of materials, surface area with respect to
volume, and cross-sectional areas will be investigated. Analytical models will be

3.2 Technical Objectives
There are four main technical objectives discussed within this proposal. They are:
• Investigate the efficiency of generating the force with respect to conducting
material purities and geometries.
• Investigate the efficiency of generating the force with respect to leaky dielectrics
and geometries.
Investigate the ability to scale the force.
• Utilize this technology for energy/power generation applications.
4.0 Work Plan
In order to achieve the objectives of this proposal the project is divided into five major
tasks in accordance with a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS).
4.1 Investigation of conducting materials (WBS 1.0)
The conduction electrons in a conductor can be treated as a "gas," using Fermi-Dirac
The electrons can have a Maxwellian velocity distribution and
randomly distribute energy throughout a conductor. When an electrical field is placed
across a conductor, the conduction electrons drift towards the positive pole and will do so
with a spectrum of energies due to thermalization. In principle, the purer the material, the
smaller the effect will be. Therefore the electrons can become ballistic in nature, and the
resulting drift velocity increases many orders of magnitude. Parametric analysis and tests
will be conducted to ascertain how purity can impact this phenomenon. As an example,
for a given voltage and geometry, the drift velocity of the conduction electrons will be
measured for a variety of conductor purities. Similarly, for a given cross section of
conductor, the current will be varied in order to evaluate change in drift velocity. These
types of tests will be conducted on a bifilar pendulum and a rotor in order to evaluate the
magnitude of the force. Resulting test data will be put into a performance matrix. The
resulting analysis will develop a performance model that will include variables such as
voltage, current, purity, drift velocity, and geometry.
4.1.1 (WB 1.1) A parametric analysis will be conducted to investigate the
interdependency of voltage, geometry, drift velocity, and purity with respect to
the force. This will be accomplished by the development of performance models
in order to evaluate trends for design and test.
4.1.2 (WBS 1.2) Capacitors will be designed and constructed based on
performance models. Test requirements will be generated.
4.1.3 (WBS 1.3) Tests will be conducted utilizing a bifilar pendulum and a rotor
in order to observe performance of the capacitors. The results will be compared
to the performance models. A performance matrix will be generated.

4.2 Investigation of leaky dielectries (WBS 2.0)
Generation of the force is related to stored charge and a leakage current. It is ideal to
have one material that has a high dielectric constant as well as high electron mobility.
Currently the asymmetric capacitors utilize corona to provide a leaky dielectric and basic
non-conducting materials for a non-leaky dielectric in order to store charge. This adds
mass that does not participate in the generation of the force. A single material that can
both store charge and be leaky would be optimal. Parametric analysis and tests will be
conducted to investigate material compositions and the resulting impact on dielectric
constant, electron mobility, and mass. These types of tests will be conducted on a bifilar
pendulum and rotor in order to evaluate the magnitude of the force. Resulting test data
will be put into a performance matrix. The resulting analysis will develop an analytical
performance model to include variables such as voltage, current, composition, dielectric
constant, electron mobility, and geometry.
4.2.1 (WBS 2.1) A parametric analysis will be conducted to investigate the
interdependency of composition, dielectric constant, electron mobility, and mass
with respect to the force. This will be accomplished by the development of
performance models in order to evaluate trends for design and test.
4.2.2 (WBS 2.2) Capacitors will be designed and constructed based on
performance models. Test requirements will be generated.
4.2.3 ( WBS 2.3) Tests will be conducted utilizing a bifilar pendulum and a rotor
in order to observe performance of the capacitors. The results will be compared
to the performance models. A performance matrix will be generated.
4.3 Investigation of scalability (WBS 3.0)
The magnitude of the force is related to current density and hence cross sectional area.
Ultrapure materials and leaky dielectrics allow structures to be thin, thus minimizing
nonessential mass.
Parametric analysis and tests will be conducted to investigate the
relationship between force, current density, and cross section. These types of tests will
be conducted on a bifilar pendulum and rotor in order to evaluate the magnitude of the
force. Resulting test data will be put into a performance matrix. The resulting analysis
will develop a model to include variables such as cross section, length, voltage, and
current.
4.3.1 (WBS 3.1) A parametric analysis will be conducted to investigate the
interdependency of current density and cross sectional area with respect to the
force. This will be accomplished by the development of performance models in
order to evaluate trends for design and test.
4.3.2 (WBS 3.2) Capacitors will be designed and constructed based on
performance models. Test requirements will be generated.

4.4 Power Generation (WBS 4.0)
In order to produce useful work, the generated force must be applied to technologies that
are readily available. This would allow the current infrastructure to be interfaced for
power distribution. To accomplish this, the generated force will have to be cyclic in
nature either by internal generation or by continually driving a rotating system
(flywheel). Parametric analysis and baseline tests will be conducted to investigate the
relationships between the generated force and power generation techniques.
4.4.1 (WBS 4.1) A parametric analysis will be conducted to investigate the input
requirements for the generated force with respect to the available techniques for
power generation, their efficiencies, and the ability to put power back into the
input supply. This must be accomplished with a net power gain. Performance
models will be developed in order to evaluate trends for design and test.
4.4.2 (WBS 4.2) A power generation system consisting of driving supply, power
takeoff, and feedback will be designed and constructed, based on performance
models. Test requirements will be generated.
4.4.3 (WBS 4.3) Tests will be conducted with existing capacitors coupled with a
driving power supply, power takeoff system, and feedback system. The results
will be compared to the performance models, and a performance matrix will be
generated.
4.5 Reporting/Cost Tracking/Administration/Scheduling (WBS 5.0)
Monthly reports and a final report will be generated to include all analysis and
conclusions. The reports and test hardware will be deliverables. The schedule will be
used to track and manage the effort.

5.0 Future Research
5.1 Materials
It is anticipated that the parametric analysis and tests will identify a grouping of
conductors that, when in ultrapure and thin-film form, will show a dramatic increase in
drift velocity of the conducting electrons.
An example of such a material would be
bismuth layered with magnesium and possibly gold. It is anticipated that the magnitude
of the force will be directly related to current density through the materials.
5.2 Leaky Dielectries
It is anticipated that the parametric analysis and tests will identify a group of
semiconductor materials that will have a high dielectric constant and electron mobility.
An example of such material is the group III-IV semiconductors that could exhibit this
effect in ultrapure form and appropriate alloy ratios. It is anticipated that the stored
charge and leakage current combination will greatly enhance the force when coupled to
ultrapure conductor materials. Similarly it is anticipated that the magnitude of the force
will be very sensitive to current density and cross sectional area of such a combination of
leaky dielectrics and conductors.
5.3 Scalability
It is anticipated that the parametric analysis and tests will identify geometric constraints
on the thicknesses of the conductors and leaky dielectrics with respect to the magnitude
of the force. For example, for an optimized thickness of leaky dielectric and conductor
combination, the cross sectional area will be increased in order to maximize the
magnitude of the force. It is anticipated that the optimal geometry will be in the form of
a rectangular panel and that the magnitude of the force will scale linearly with cross
sectional area of the leaky dielectric and conductor combination.
5.4 Power Generation
It is anticipated that the results of a power generation system will allow the direct
development and technology implementation for a 1,000 watt or better system. The
"stepping stone" demonstrator will also allow the needed test verification of independent
sources. As the capacitor technology improves, the system should become smaller,
lightweight, and less expensive.
5.5 Further Research
When fully developed, this system would provide propellantless transportation that is
silent and leaves no emission trail. The system has few moving parts, so cost,
maintenance, and risk would be greatly reduced. Vehicles would be able to travel great
istances with only a small generator required. However, since this system is functiona
n the atmosphere, under water, and in space, the possibilities are endless. Th
transportation industry - airplanes, trains, and automobiles - all could be affected by this
type of system.
