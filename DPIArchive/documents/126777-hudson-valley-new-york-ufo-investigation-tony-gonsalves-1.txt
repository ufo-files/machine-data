LOS ANGELES
I) April 8, 1982, the Northrop Corp. made an

For $65 million, Northrop took over an abandoned
Ford assembly plant in the Los Angeles suburb of
Pico Rivera. Workmen hauled away the hulks of
half-built Thunderbirds, then attacked the building
with acetylene torches-and fire extinguishers to

paint bays and assembly lines were gone, a new
three-story engineering center had risen next to the
plant, and Pico Rivera-or limply Peek"-had been

On Monday, Jan. 3, 1983, Peek opened for

A-Y° near the Los Angeles airport-reported to their
new work stations. With the new year and the new
plant came a renewed sense of excitement: many
workers felt that they had been directly challenged
by President Reagan to help protect the nation by
building a revolutionary weapon, the B-2 stealth
bomber, code-named Project Senior C.J.
In the 15 months since winning the bomber

create a new Advanced Systems Division. Major
subcontractors, including several of the largest

LTV of Texas to handle the titanium frame and inner

Boeing to construct the immense outer wings.
With each passing month, Northrop engineers felt
more certain that they were close to unlocking the
secrets of stealth. In deepest secrecy, the company
experimented with a one-quarter scale mini-bomber

warning (EW) systems, might detect the bomber at
great distances. But Air Force and Northrop experts

At Pico Rivera the company had built the largest

All of this, of course, was undertaken in the "black
