UFOlogy-Summer, 1976
Scrapbook
One of the most witnessed close encounters with entities reported
occurred on June 26 and 27, 1959, in Boianai, Papua, New Guinea. It'is a
favorite case of many ufologists but is only one instance of some 80
sightings which took place during the Papuan Flap of June, July, and
August, 1959.
Papua at the time was an Australian territory in southeast New
Guinea, although it is now an independent country which includes the
adjacent islands. The total population is over 500,000.
On June 26, Rev. William B. Gill of the Anglican mission at Boianai
and 30 witnesses saw several bright, maneuvering lights near the
mission. One of the objects was much larger than the others and had a
blue beam of light coming from its upper deck.
At about 6:00 p.m. on the following evening, Father Gill was called
out of the mission by Anna Borewa, a medical assistant at the mission,
who excitedly reported strange lights moving toward the settlement.
Once outside, Father Gill saw two small UFOs cavorting over the hills to
the west of the mission and one large disc-shaped object hovering above
the mission with four landings pods or legs visible underneath. Sud-
denly four human figures appeared on the top deck of the object, two of
whom began setting up some sort of machine in the center of the deck.
By now there were about a dozen people from the mission looking up
at the object. At about this time, Father Gill noticed one of the oc-
cupants looking down at them, and Father Gill stretched his arm above
his head and waved. Surprisingly the figure on the deck waved back.
Father Gill then started flashing a torch at the craft and after a couple of
minutes, the UFO started making swaying movements in apparent
response.
The Gill Sighting
Papua New Guinea
June, 1959
Two of the figures went below deck but returned about 6:30 p.m. and
turned on a blue spotlight which they directed skyward. The spotlight
was turned on twice for a few seconds each time. At around dusk, the
object started moving away and by 8:00 p.m. was no longer visible. That
same evening near 11:00 p.m. a terrific ground level explosion of un-
determined origin occurred near the Boianai Bishop's house.
Later Father Gill estimated the size of the top deck to have been 20
feet across and the bottom 35 feet across. The object's altitude varied
between 450 and 2,500 feet.
A colleague of Gill, Rev. Norman Crutwell wrote a report summarizing
the more important sightings in the flap. The Australian Department of
Air and the British Air Ministry labeled the Gill sighting as inconclusive,
although they suggested that the "objects" could have been reflections
on the cloud cover from a light source of unknown origin.
Dr. Donald H. Menzel has put forward a somewhat controversial
explanation of the Gill sighting. Menzel believes Gill. was actually ob-
serving Venus and being afflicted with myopia and astigmatism, Rev:
Gill only misinterpreted the shimmering planet's movements.
On the other hand, Dr. J. Allen Hynek points out that Rev. Gill was
wearing fully corrected glasses at the time, and Venus was separately
identified by Gill. In addition many witnesses confirm Gill's story and
state that the UFO was below the cloud cover.
Furthermore, Rev. Gill, a graduate of Brisbane University and an
Anglican priest, simply had no reason to concoct such a story and
conspire with others to lie about the sighting. The Gill sighting remains
one of the most credible and documented humanoid cases on record.
