F-117 CRASH IGNITES RUMORS,
SPECULATION
By Steve Douglass
The explosion and crash of a F-117A stealth attack jet did
more than light up the night sky around the small New Mexico
town of Las Luz, it also fueled rumors and speculation about the
mission and payload of these "black" aircraft based at nearby
Holloman Air Force Base.
The crash happened about 9:20 PM (Mountain Time) on
Tuesday August 4th. The aircraft was on a night training mission
when (according to witnesses) it caught fire, exploded, and hit the
ground. The pilot, Capt. John B. Mills, ejected safely and only
suffered minor injuries from the mishap. He parachuted into the
parking lot of a nearby shopping center, walked to a residence
near the crash sight, and called Holloman Air Force Base to alert
officials to the crash and to report that he was safe. Air Force
officials said that Mills was treated for minor injuries at the 49th
Fighter Wing Hospital at Holloman.
Witnesses says that there were three separate explosions,
one big and two smaller ones. Otero County Deputy Sheriff Leon
Ledbetter witnessed the crash and said the aircraft had a orange
glow at the back and a bright white glow at the front" Ledbetter
at first thought he was seeing a missile from the nearby White
Sands Missile Range,
"But it didn't look right," he said. Deputy
Ledbetter noted that just before the aircraft crashed he saw a small
red streak come out of the top and later realized that it was
probably the pilot ejecting. Ledbetter then raced to the scene and
was the first law enforcement officer to arrive.
"When I pulled up
I could see the airplane sitting just northeast of Don Scotts (Radio
Communications Company) communications tower.
There's a
trailer house that sits just to the west of the tower and it was on
fire," Ledbetter said.
Volunteer fire units from all over Otero County arrived on the
scene shortly after the crash and began fighting the trailer fire.
The fire fighters were told to move back, when Holloman crash
teams arrived, and until they could determined whether there
were any munitions on board the aircraft that might explode. The
mobile home was allowed to burn and it was completely de-
stroyed. Several fire fighters were taken to the base hospital and
treated for smoke inhalation.
No sooner had the fire been put out then security teams from
Holloman began securing and roping off the crash sight. The
crash area was declared a "National Security Zone" and the
residents near the sight were evacuated to motels. Because of the
right secürity surrounding the crash, rumors and speculations
began to circulate throughout the community. Intercepts began
receiving calls from sources at the base, suggesting that there was
something "not right" about the crash. Unconfirmed reports and
base scuttlebutt ranged from theories that the crash was not a
F-117A, to that the aircraft contained nuclear materials and the
sight was evacuated due to radiation in the area. 'The tight security
and the lack of public information only fueled these rumors.
Adding to the "radiation" scare rumors were the facts that several
fire fighters were sent to the base hospital, any civilians in the area
of the crash were also asked to report to the base hospital for
examination, and men in special toxic chemical suits were seen
at the crash site.
The rumors were soon squelched when the real reasons for
concern were made public.
It seems that the RAM (radar
absorbing material) gives off toxic smoke when burned and base
officials were concerned that anyone who breathed in the smoke
may have been exposed to these toxic gases. Although never

gram these frequencies into your receiver,
point your antenna due West and slowly turn

due East, On the frequencies are broadcast
steady "white noise" which will sound like a
open carrier. These transmissions. are. used
by Navy ships in much the same way you'll
be using them, to help them find the birds
from any place
in the world.

geosycronous orbit over the equator, so you
should point your antenna to the south.,
however they can be moved out of their
orbits to provide coverage in certain areas,
especially during a conflict.

transmissions to
FLTSATCOM satellites are very directional,
the downlink is not, so you don't have to be
pointed directly at a satellite to hearit Ihave
