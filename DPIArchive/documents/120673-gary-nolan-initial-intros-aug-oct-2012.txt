7- completely removing an intron. The author correctly suggests a potential "pseudogene"
". Essentially a
pseudogene is a (presumably) non-active gene that at some point in evolution was transcribed as messenger
RNA, reverse transcribed by a retroviral reverse transcriptase and then re-inserted back into the genome as
"waste" or even potential future fodder for evolution. There are many cases where over evolutionary time
pseudogenes re-enter evolution as useful sequences.

terrestrial primate. As the author notes, there are no known pseudogenes for FoxP2 anywhere in any known
vertebrate species. Now, one of the problems with pseudogenes is that being non-functional, there is no

see. But-- that's almost inconsequential to the fact that it's 100% a gene that humans SHOULD NOT HAVE.
