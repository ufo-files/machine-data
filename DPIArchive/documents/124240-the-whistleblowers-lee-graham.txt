One Researcher's Opinion
Were The Feds Out To Catch A Spy?
As UFO was completing the
research for this article, a promi-
nent West Coast UFO researcher,
who requests anonymity, stated that
he has information that may point
to why this all transpired. Quite
familiar with Graham and his story,
the researcher suggests that the en-
tire scenario may have been set up
to catch a Soviet spy.
The source refuses to say where
or from whom he received the in-
formation that he thinks backs up
this suggestion. He feels that during
the sequence of events referred to
above, the AFOSI and the FBI had
reason to believe that one of the
other AeroJet employees, who like
Graham holds a security clearance
and has an interest in UFOs, may
have been suspected of working for
Soviet intelligence.
Aware that Graham speaks to co-
workers about his interests,
counterintelligence agents may have
developed a plan to give informa-
tion specifically to Graham, bank-
ing on that information eventually
reaching the suspect and whetting
his or her appetite.
Graham, who has undergone two
investigations, was not under any
type of suspicion. But according to
the researcher's belief, Graham
would have been used as a conduit
in a counterintelligence operation to
expose the suspected Soviet agent.
There is no way to determine if
this view has any validity, because
neither AeroJet nor the Air Force
are talking.
The UFO connection
Of course we keep coming back
to the UFO connection. Graham's
problems started with his inquiries
on MJ-12, Aquarius and other
UFO-related material. So far, he
has been able to keep his security
clearance after several attempts to
revoke it.
Perhaps an indirect message to
Graham is coming through. He
received a letter from the Air Force
rebuking him for being a
"nuisance" and tying up taxpayer
dollars with his FOIA requests.
It should also be noted that the
Freedom of Information Act simply
does not make information as free
to citizens as might be assumed.
Since the Reagan Administration,
the federal government has
drastically curtailed access via the
FOIA.
An example is NASA's recent at-
tempt to persuade Congress to hold
the space agency exempt from
FOIA, claiming the Japanese were
legally "stealing" shuttle secrets by
using the FOIA process. It turned
out the Japanese spy excuse was a
fabrication, that NASA was merely
attempting to conceal embarrassing
information about the Challenger
tragedy. -D.E.
