Ecklin Generator Experiment
For this experiment, we used the stator from a 25 horsepower motor
with alternate poles removed. Thus there were 12 active poles.
The rotor from a l horsepower motor had its coils removed and the
core machined so it resulted in 6 poles.
The the rotor laminations
were not insulated perfectly. There could have been a short
circuit under stress.
For a driver, we used a 600 Watt series motor. This was regulated
with a triac
(light dimmer) so we could try the experiment at
speeds up to 10,000 R.P.M.
With the output short circuited, the load on the motor
decreased, which resulted in a speed increase.
With the output unloaded, the generator was difficult to control
and the motor required full power. With a pure ohnic load
resistance of the
same resistance as the generator, the output was
a reasonably low load to the motor (about 200 W).
We then used about 96 W for magnetization and got 80 W out. The
ohmic load was composed of a combination of heating elements
and
lamps.
By increasing the magnetic field to around 12,000 ampere-turns
for the whole generator, this effect decreased.
The input poles were then connected in different ways. This
resulted in some connection configurations receiving power from a
12 V lead-acid battery.
At no time did we get more energy out of the generator than we put
into it.
