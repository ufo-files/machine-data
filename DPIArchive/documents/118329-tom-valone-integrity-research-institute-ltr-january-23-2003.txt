Dear Steven,
Here is a revised Proposal for the Hartman Project that we discussed. I have been
looking for a good reason to build this full-scale version for about a year now. Two
other (Ivan Kruglak and Terry Rudd) benefactors have already committed to split
the funding three-ways with a $1000 donation each.
Note that the plan calls for all four purposes or goals to be fulfilled with the
proposed budget. Each ramp will be individually adjusted for the maximum
propulsion force and magnetic gradient. Four different magnet styles and strengths
will be tested, all with the NdFeB increased level of power, as compared with
Hartman's AlNiCo magnetic resources. Multiple ramps will be linked to maximize
the kinetic energy gained by designing the optimum descending track as well. The
connection of all six ramps will be in a circle, with small lights blinking as the ball
passes by individual pickup coils. The steel ball bearing(s) that will be used
accumulate a residual magnetic field as well as an induced magnetic field. Both
fields contribute to the induction coil response that trigger the lights.
The closed circular track is proposed to be about 3 feet in diameter overall. It will be
constructed to be securely attached to a flat platform for easy transportation and
exhibition. It is understood that the funding group reviewing this project will gain
ownership of this demonstration Hartman Project device assembly.
An exciting and interesting follow-up possibility is that with any magnet-powered
machine, once a particular model is proven to work, everything can be scaled up for
a more robust version. This is certainly the case with the Hartman Project. It is
anticipated that the next evolutionary step may involve replacing the ball with a
small, magnetic four-wheel cart, as with a particular Johnson patent. From that stage,
which I believe is fully possible, commercial applications can then be visualized.
