Thank you for contacting me about Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs). I appreciate
this opportunity to correspond.
Although little attention has been paid to the subject of UFOs in recent years, the
controversy still excites nearly as much emotional response now as it did during the late
1940s. After 21 years of investigating UFOs, the Air Force concluded that whatever was
being seen was not hostile, nor a threat to national security, and therefore needed no further
inquiry. People have accepted this view, and media coverage is still rare in anything but
tabloid and UFO organization publications.

You may know that during the 1960s, national interest in the subject of UFOs was so
acute that two House Committees held hearings to learn more about the matter and quell
concerns. In 1966, the Armed Services Committee held its hearing, and in 1968 the Science
and Astronautics Committee held its hearing. These hearings served as forums into the issue,
and did not attempt to solve the issue of UFOs.
The Armed Services Committee hearing dealt primarily with the United States Air
Force reports on UFOs: After hearing testimony, Members of the committee expiessed
disbelief in extraterrestrial vehicles and confidence in the Air Force. The House Science and
Astronautics Committee hearing excluded all Air Force material and heard testimony from
various "experts" on the subject of UFOs. The witnesses called were primarily college
professors and were unable to explain the UFOs due to lack of evidence.
I am unaware of any current congressional hearings dealing with UFOs, but I have
taken the liberty of forwarding your comments to the House Science Committee for their
review.
