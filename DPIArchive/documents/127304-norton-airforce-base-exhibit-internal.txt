'Checked out' B-1B
arrives for air show
By JOHN WHITEHAIR
Sun Staff Writer
SAN BERNARDINO - Yes,
that was a B-1B nuclear bomber
circling Norton Air Force Base
on Thursday morning.
Although the Air Force's fleet
of 98 of the long-range bombers
was grounded Wednesday after
an accident in Texas, the B-1B
that came to Norton for display at
Saturday's open house already
had been certified for safety, an
Air Force spokesman said.
"The B-1 you have seen has
already undergone inspectioniỊạẠạ
said Maj. Dennis Pierson, a
spokesman at the Strategic Air
Command headquarters in Oma-
ha, Neb.
The four-engine jet made sev-
eral passes over the San Bernar-
dino base and landed. Later, a B-
52G bomber, the nation's oldest
bomber, also landed at the base
for the open house.
Wednesday, the Strategic Air
Command, responsible for the na-
tion's nuclear bombers, grounded
the B-1Bs until each has under-
gone a safety inspection.
The grounding order came on
the heels of the crash Tuesday of
a B-1B near Dyess Air Force
Base in Abilene, Texas. All four
on board ejected safely before
the jet crashed and exploded in a
field outside Abilene.
Witnesses have said they saw
smoke and fire coming from one
ofthe four engines.
Pierson said the cause of the
crash is under investigation.
He said mechanics carefully
go over the B-1B bombers during
a three-hour inspection and they
check the electrical, hydraulic
and fuel lines.
"They will be looking for un-
usual wear and leaks in the
lines," he said.
As of Thursday afternoon, 32
of the jets had undergone inspec-
tions, Pierson said.
Today, the base again will be
the center of unusual flying when
the Air Force flight demonstra-
tion team, the Thunderbirds, ar-
rives at 11 a.m. for Saturday's
show, scheduled from 8 a.m. to
4:30 p.m.
The arrival will be
closed to the public.
Norton spokesman Capt. Gary
Jacobs said the clouds that hung
over the San Bernardino Valley
on Thursday may clear in time for
the airshow.
