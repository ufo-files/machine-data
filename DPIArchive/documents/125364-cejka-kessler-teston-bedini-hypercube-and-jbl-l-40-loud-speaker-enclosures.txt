OBJECTIVE MEASUREMENTS OF
RELATIVE LOW-FREQUENCY PERFORMANCE
Measurement Method
The relative low-frequency response characteristics of the HYPERCUBE
and the JBL 1-40 enclosure was determined by measuring the response
of each under identical electrical and environmental conditions using
an audio signal generator, a power amplifier, and a sound pressure
meter. The measurements were made on the grounds of WUFT-TV which is
characterized by a large open area to ensure the absence of acoustical
reflections and a sufficiently low ambient voice level so that the
radiation from each speaker would not be contaminated by spurious sounds.
The sound pressure meter and integral microphone assembly was placed
on the surface of the ground approximately six feet away from the en-
closure undergoing test to eliminate all reflections below 500 Hz.
The first series of measurements were made with the JBL L-40 speaker
system. The audio signal generator output was held constant over the.
frequency range from 10 Hz to 1,000 Hz, and the sound pressure as
indicated by the sound pressure meter, were recorded. After the measure-
ments with the JBL L-40 were completed, the identical measurement technique
and conditions were used to measure the sound pressure response of the
HYPERCUBE enclosure.
It is important to note that the HYPERCUBE enclosure was
equipped with woofer, and tweeter driver systems
identical to the driver system employed by the JBL L-40
loudspeaker system so that any difference in performance
would be due solely to the characteristics of the enclosures.
All objective measurements of frequency response were limited to frequencies
below 1,000 Hz since the frequency response performance characteristics

TEST RESULTS
The results of the measured comparison of the HYPERCUBE and L-40 low-
frequency response characteristics is shown in the figure in which
the measured low-frequency sound level radiations from each speaker
are plotted. This figure is typical of at least eight sets of measure-
ments. The only differences between the eight observations (which were
not significantly large) occurred above 500 Hz and were due to con-
structive and destructive interference phenomena between the direct
radiation from the speaker and pressure radiations reflected from the
ground at the location of the sound pressure meter. This phenomena was
minimized by placing the sound level meter on the surface of the ground.
With this placement of the sound pressure meter, the undesirable effects
of ground reflections below 500 Hz are virtually eliminated.
An examination of the figure discloses that the low-frequency performance
of the HYPERCUBE speaker system, which appears to be due solely to the
characteristics of the HYPERCUBE enclosure, is superior to the performance
of the L-40 enclosure. This is demonstrated by the fact that the minus
3 db response frequency for the HYPERCUBE is 42 Hz while the minus 3 db
response for the L-40 is only 49 Hz. Furthermore, since the measurements
were conducted down to 10 Hz, the pressure response for the L-40 disappeared
at 40 Hz, whereas the response of the HYPERCUBE disappeared at a frequency
10 Hz lower at a low-frequency of 30 Hz. This difference appears to be
due solely to the low-frequency radiation characteristics of the enclosures
since both enclosures employ identical drivers.
The results of the subjective listening tests appeared to indicate that
the HYPERCUBE enclosure produced less "coloring" of the low-frequency
transient characteristics of the reproduced music than the L-40 enclosure.
The subjective listening tests of the directional and stereo performance
of the two HYPERCUBE enclosures revealed what this observer considers to
be a remarkable non-directional characteristic. The apparent low-frequency

CONCLUDING COMMENTS
This observer frankly concedes that he can offer no explanation for the
performance of the HYPERCUBE. However, it appears that two factors may
be involved:
(a) The irregular shape of the enclosure may inhibit the
roduction ot resonances of the back wave within th
closure which would produce undesirable "coloration
•
(b) the small rigid triangular surfaces of different dim-
ensions with which the HYPERCUBE enclosure is fabricated
may be serving as secondary radiators which reproduce
non-resonant radiations essentially in phase with the
radiation of the woofer cone.
Although the objective measurements of the comparitive response of the
HYPERCUBE and L-40 loudspeaker systems constitute a reliable measure
of the relative low-frequency performance of the two enclosures, the
subjective listening tests in a typical living room environment using
recorded program material represent the subjective opinion of this
observer only. The nature of subjective listening tests are such that
each individual should listen for himself using familiar program material
in a familiar playback environment and form a personal judgment.
