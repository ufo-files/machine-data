new products are being produced almost as rapidly as the proverbial hare with his
prolific capabilities. The state of the art is proceeding like the tides. Each day

At GOLDEN ENTERPRISES we are pleased to announce our latest breakthrough in
creative design which we call the ION-TESTER. This clever device consists of a
unique circuit design that has the ability to sense minute ion charges and display
these tiny charges on a panel meter. For years, scientists have known that these
tiny charges have a definite influence on our bodily functions and well being. The
positively charged particles are not conducive to well being, while on the other hand,
the negatively charged particles have a way of making us feel good. While all the
information is not yet in, scientists are hard at work discovering more each day of
the whys and wherefor of these mysterious ions.
The ION-TESTER is able to detect the presence of negative ions.* This small, but
extremely sensitive meter, can detect a charge on a pocket comb at a distance of
six feet. Its Input impedance is exceptionally high, allowing the instrument to
detect minute currents.
The ION-TESTER is a complement device to our negative ion generators. (Golden
Enterprises Plan #103 (ION-AIR) and our Model #115 (MINI-AIR). With the
ION-TESTER you can check to verify that your negative ion generator is indeed
producing negative ions. This gives you a ready indicator that can provide a 24-
hour surveillance on your fon generator, keeping you informed as to the product-
ivity of negative ions.
There are several other uses for this unique tester. The following is only a partial
list of possible applications:
1.
Checking your laundry for static electricity
2.
Static buildup on carpets, drapes, etc.
3.
Observing storm fronts as they move in
This list is only limited by ones own imagination.
The ION-TESTER consists of four (4) readily available solid state devices. The input
stage (Q1) is a FET. Ihis is the heart of our mighty little tester! Its input imped-
ance is nearly 1, 000 million ohms (1,000, 000, 000 ohms), or 10°
ohms. At these input
impedance levels, current becomes more important than voltage. We are now sensing
tiny currents on charged particles. Q2 is a common base stage driving a common
emfter stage (Q4). A constant current source is used (Q3) to stabilize the drift
currents. On a 24-hour test, the drift was 2% of the total meter scale. This is
negligible and can be easily corrected by resetting the meter zero with R6. The
cransistors used are the garden variety type and inexpensive... and basically, -
THAT'S IT!

CONSTRUCTION PLANS
The ION-TESTER can be constructed for about about 00, using readily avallable com-
ponents. To assist you in bullding the project, you can order a printed circuit board,
ready to assemble, from Golden Enterprises for $5. 00. The ION-TESTER is also
available in complete kit form for $40.00.
The ION-TESTER may be constructed in any small, convenient housing.. Our unit was
put into a 4"x 6"x 3" glamour cabinet. This worked out very well and the following
instructions are based upon using this type of housing.
The housing comes in two pieces. The meter mounting hole is cut in the front panel with
a one-inch chassis punch. For the next step, a pair of aircraft tin snips is used to
cut the hole to proper size to accommodate the panel meter. See Figure 1. Drill the
holes to mount R6 and R8 and put them on the back panel, as they are only used
occasionally.
The circuit board is mounted on 3/4" to 1" standoffs or spacers to elevate it as high
as possible. See Figure 1. This board is mounted on the same part of the housing as
the meter. A piece of double-sided masking tape serves as the battery holders. Once
the wiring is completed, put the top part of the chassis over the main frame (only
temporarily). The input to the FET (QI) is noted. Directly above this, drill a 3/8"
hole in the top part of the chassis. It is very important for this to line up with the
input to the FEI, The input to Ql should be a teflon jack as shown in Figure 3 below.
The sense wire will plug into this jack. A paper clip unfolded so that it is straight
is plugged into this jack. This should be positioned so that it falls in the center of
the 3/8" hole in the upper part of the housing as shown in Figure 2.

INSTRUCTIONS FOR USE
When using your ION-TESTER, it is important to remember the following points:
1. When taking reference readings, set the tester approximately 16" from the
measuring source on a steady table.
2.
For spot checking, hold the ION-TESTER in the palm of your hand extended at
arms length so that your own charge does not influence the reading.
3.
The meter reads DOWNSCALE and is normally (in the absence of negative
lons) at 50 or slightly above.
4. There is no ON-OFF switch because the battery life will be approximately
equal to the shelf life. This is due to the fact that a very tiny current is
consumed by the tester.
5.
The tester does not indicate absolute quantities, but indicates the presence
of negative lons. When the ION-TESTER is exposed to a flow of negative
fons (such as from the ION-AIR negative ion generator) the meter needle will
read downscale.
6. When measuring the ions from your ION-AIR negative ion generator, you may
expect a slight fluctuation in the meter reading. This is normal, as the flow
of ions is not at a perfectly steady rate.
7.
The small wire extending upward is the sensing wire and care should be
taken so that it does not touch the housing.

TESTING INSTRUCTIONS
Connect the two nine-volt batteries and with a good volt ohmmeter, measure the
voltage on the Q2, Q3 collector at the base of Q4. All these points are common to
each other.
It should measure near -3.65 volts. Check the schematic for the
other voltages. If the voltages check out, you can now check the activity and sensi-
tivity. Collect the following Items from around the house: Pocket comb, plastic
produce bag, and a silk cloth.
Adjust RB for the maximum down scale reading (about 80 on the meter). Next, adjust
R6 to its mid-position (It should move about 4 divisions). Now, re-adjust R8 to
100 on the meter scale. Your meter is now ready to use.
To test, perform the following procedures:
Run your pocket comb through your hair, then point it at the meter whtle standing about
3 feet away. The meter should read all the way down scale temporarily. It will soon
come back up as the charge bleeds off the comb and into the meter. Move forward
slightly and observe that the meter moves down scale again. Now, try the plastic bag.
Rub it vigorously over your clothing and then point it at your ION-TESTER. Once again,
the meter moves down scale, indicating the presence of negative ions. Do the same
with the silk cloth,
As you become more familiar with your ION-TESTER, you will think of many other
interesting applications.

Clean the teflon jack with alcohol.
