In order to extend the early warning coverage for the defence of the North American continent
a plan has been developed for the reporting of vital intelligence sightings.
Reports originating from
airborne or land-based observers witl be known as CIR VIS (pronounced SUR-VEES) messages.
Who Reports -
Pilots of all Canadian aircraft, air traffic controlers, aeradio operators, and other ground
observers should originate CIRVIS reports as and when applicable.
When to Report -
(1)
CIRVIS reporte should be made immediately upon vital intelligence sighting, except when
originated by the pilot of an aircraft which is over a foreign country other than the U.S..
Greenland or Iceland.
(2)
When a situation previously reported changes sufficiently to warrant a supplementary
or amplifying or cancellation report.
(3)
In the event a CIRVIS report from an aircraft cannot be made by radio, it sould be
filed immediately upon landing. Such reports should be made by the quickest available
means:
To Whom to Report •
(1)
CIR VIS messages passed from an aircraft by radio should be transmitted to the nearest
or most conventent Canadian or U.S.
Government air traffic control facility or aeradio
station.
There is no need to address such messages as these facilities have detailed
Instructions for the delivery of CIRVIS messages.
(2)
When reports cannot be passed to the facilities listed above, they should be passed to
the nearest Canadian or U.S.A. military or consular authority.
(3)
CIR VIS reports originated at an air traffic control or aeradio facility shall be passed
In accordance with detalled Instructions issued by appropriate authority.
