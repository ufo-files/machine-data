1. On 20 July 1952 at 05558, CAPTAIN CASEY PIERMAN OF CAPITAL AIRLINES
was in the cockpit of his DC-4 aircraft performing a check list prior to take-
off from WASHINGTON NATIONAL AIRPORT, WASHINGTON D.C. The aircraft was on the
parking ramp heading 020°.
CAPTAIN PIERMAN looked up and observed a clear
bluish white light travel from 190° to 010° at a 30° angle above the horizon
in horizontal fli ht until it disappeared in the distance. CAPTAIN PIERMAN
stated that he had to turn his head slowly through a 45°
quadrant in order to
observe the object while in its flight and estimates that he observed it for
five (5) seconds or less.
CAPTAIN PIERMAN states that he did not attach any
significance to this light until later events demanded attention to it.
2. Immediately after performing his check list, CAPTAIN PIERMAN took off
from WASHINGTON NATIONAL AIRPORT on a Heading of 180° and climbed to 1200*
before making a right turn on course 330°. Upon gaining 12001 and course 330°,
CAPTAIN PIERIAN stated that he switched over from Tower Control to AIRWAY
TRAPPIC CONTROL CENTER (ATCC) at WASHINGTON NATIONAL AIRPORT. At this time
ATCC informed him that their radar scope indicated two or three objects on the
screen traveling at high speeds.
ATCC instructed CAPIAIN PIERMAN to steer
290º so as to intercept the objects which were approximately nine (9) miles
ahead of him. At this time CAPTAIN PIERMAN'S rate of climb was approximately
6001 per minute and his altitude was between 3500% and 4000'.
3. Immediately after ATCC instructed CAPTAIN PIERMAN to steer course 290°
he stated that the following events occured within 5-8 minutes in the order in

3-5 minutes after take-off - ATCC informed pilot that objects

information he reported to ATCC.

The copilot told
