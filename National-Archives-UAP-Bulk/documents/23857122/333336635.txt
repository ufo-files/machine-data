Objects were sighted in the sky Saturday night July 25, 1953
commencing approximately 9:30 P.M.
They were first observed by me
the second floor of the Denison Herald Building in down-town
Denison and had reached a point of approximately 30 degrees upon the
horizon.
When first seen they were a little east of south and were
out in almost a straight line with the point directing straight
The objects consisted of several large red lights however when
through field glasses, each unit consisted of several smaller
lights at least eight or ten
in number and these seemed to move slowly
more or less in unison within the larger unit.
These objects gave the impression of flying in a sort of tandem
formation,
From the straight line first seen, the last three objects
went into triangular formation and the others
changed position position
to form a resemblance to the Great Dipper or an inverted question mark.
From that formation they stretched into a different formation consisting
of triangles at either end.
These would vary around in mere or less a
drifting pattern and finally when they reached a point directly overhead,
they again were stretched out in almost a straight line.
At this time, fifteen or twenty minutes later than time objects
were first sighted by me, the ones at the rear of the formation started
growing dim.
The last ones blinked out first and were followed a few
seconds later with others disappearing in turn until they were all gone;
however, some of them would blink out to appear very dim a few seconds
later for a brief period, perhaps three or four seconds.

I was sitting in my backyard and spotted these seven (7) red lights at an
estimated altitude of four (4) or five (5) thousand feet at 2140 Saturday night,
25 July 1953.
These stayed in sight sixteen (16) minutes.
For approximately ten
(10) minutes, six of these lights stayed in an identical position without raising
or lowering.
Occasionally some of these lights blinked but one light, approximately
two (2) or three (3) thousand feet above the others seemed to blink constantly.
At the end of approximately ten (10) minutes, the top light that blinked all the
time began an ascent straight up in the air without any swaying from either side.
It went straight up. After it reached approximately eight (8) or ten (10) thousand
Ieet, the other six lights began to move in the same relative position to each other,
straight up in the same manner in which the first light began its ascent.
ascended up to a considerable altitude at which time the lights began to go out.
They seemed to pop out like they were turned out with a light switch with no blinking.
Three (3) of the lower six (6) lights remained in sight for another couple of minutes.
These three kept ascending in the same vertical position until gradually their
lights began to fade and go out of sight as if they were going behind a cloud.
When I first sighted these I made an estimate of the angle from my house to where
I was looking at them and I estimated my eyelevel was on the 35° angle in relation
to the earth.
These objects were about a 40° heading from my house.
difficult to determine the exact altitude. I don't believe they were balloons.
They remained in a steady position and remained like that for a good while.
lights looked just like any ordinary red lights, like a light on top of radio station

Statement by A/IC larry W. Skinner
At 2139 Base Operations called us in the tower, via intercom, and asked us if
we could see any lights east of the tower. At the
time we were all watehing the
landing strip. Looking to the east we did see what appeared to be seven red lights
suspended just over Denison. They looked just like any part lights of an aircraft.
The only difference was that these lights seemed to be stationary until we saw that
they were rising very slowly. We took pictures of them with a stereo-camera we
keep in the tower for such purposes. We took about 10 pictures of different lens
openings and shutter speeds.
The lights kept rising slowly until about 2155 when they
faded out.
Throughout their ascent, they appeared to keep the same formation except
which was to the upper right.
That one light drifted farther apart from
Just before fading out, each light flickered on and off. But that
could have been the atmospheric condition.
Any indication of altitude was practically impossible, since we did not know
the size of the lights nor the distance they were from the tower.
