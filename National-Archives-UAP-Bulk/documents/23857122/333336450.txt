1. On 6 February 1953 at 2000 A.S.T. the Naknek Detachment of the
64th Fighter-Interceptor Squadron informed 795th AC&W Squadron radar
operations that personnel at King Salmon Tower, King Salmon, Alaska had
sighted a light in the sky to the west of the field.
Nothing unusual
could be detected by radar.
The 705th AC&W Squadron controller called
the tower asking for more information and was informed that the object
had dissappeared.
King Salmon, Alaska is an isolated C.A.A. airfield located at
156° 39W, 58° 41 'N.
There was no reported air traffic in the area.
Weather observers at King Salmon Airport reported they had not sent up
weather ballons that night.
The sky was very clear and the temperature
was very low approximately -22°F.
The Chief Controller later interviewed James N. Burris, Chief
Airways Operations Specialist (Airport), Mrs. Marcelle L. Adair, wife of
Joe L• Adair, Chief Airways Operations Specialist (Communications), anc
Victor Mahler, Aircraft Conmunicator.
These are all Civil Aeronautic
Administration personnel and all saw the light.
Their signed statements
are inclosed.
The magnetic headings given in the statements were deter-
mined at the time of the interview from land marks observed at the time
of the sighting.

I, James N. Burrus, Chief Airways Operations Specialist (Airport),
residing at King Salmon, Alaska make the following statement.
On 7 February 1953 at approx 0800Z I observed a light west of the
CAA Control Tower located at King Salmon Airport, at which I was on duty.
The light appeared when first noticed by me to be approx 20° elevation
above the horizon.
Having served 14 mos. at this airport I have never
noticed a light at that position or elevation. Having no A/C in the
vicinity at the time I made an attempt to notify radar operations at
Naknek AFB, in order to do this I had to relay the message through the
Fighter Det, stationed at King Salmon Airport.
I obtained a pair of 6
power Field Glasses so that I might observe the light more closely.
light appeared to be oval shaped and changing in color from a reddish
orange to white.
The frequency of change was not regular, one time the
change would be
very rapid ant the next very slow.
estimate the distance of the light.
The position of the light remained
stationary with a slight side movement. The oval wold become longer
vertically and then return to a normal oval. There was no noticable
change in width. When the light changed it was like a piece of metal
being made white hot, the white was very distinctive from the reddish
The size of
the light looked approx. one foot in diameter.
After a period of approx. ten minutes I turned away to answer a phone
call and upon returning to observe the light I was unable to see any-
thing where the light had been.
The above is a voluntary statement and is true and correct to
the best of my knowledge.
