Report of sighting an unidentified object submitted by Mrs. James Duvall
Of 2210 Charleston Place, Lewisdale, Maryland.
2.
Mrs. Duvall reported seeing a round white light, appearing slightly larger
than a star, something like an airplane's landing light, although no beam was seen.
3.
She first saw the object while sitting on her porch.
The object was moving
from northeast to the southwest,
and then veered slightly to the left and disappeared
over the city of Washington. The light would alternately go fast then slow and at
times appear to hover.
The elapsed time from when the light first appeared in the
northeast until it disappeared to the south, was from 20 to 30 minutes.
4. The manner of
observation was ground to air, and was visual. A few times
binoculars were used, and no difference was observed.
5. The times and dates of sighting were approximately 2200 hours on August 30,
September 1, 2, 3, 4, 7. Light conditions were those of an average night.
6.
The location of observers was the same on all nights, that being 38° 58'N and
76° 57'W.
7. Identifying information of all observers is as follows:
it has
been learned that Mrs. Duvall has been calling Operations (Bolling) for at least a
year, and without further confirmation from people in the vicinity, it may be con-
cluded that Mrs. Duvall may be overestimating a very routine thing.
Mrs. Elizabeth R. Cornish, mother of Mrs. Duvall, age 75, same address,
estimate of reliability is unknown.
Mrs. Madline C. Nickles, sister of Mrs. Duvall, same address, estimate of
reliability is unknown.
Weather conditions at all sightings were very good. Ceiling was high,
visibility was clear, no cloudcover, and thunder storms in the area.
9. Aircraft is prevalent in that area. Military, commercial, and private
aircraft do fly in that vicinity regularly.
Commercial aircraft have been in the
area, according to the observers.
Mrs. Duvall observed two aircraft, which seemed
to fly over the unknown object, and did not seem to be in the usual line of flight of
the commercial aircraft.
10.
As stated in paragraph 7, it is felt by the preparing officer that the
reliability of Mrs. Duvall can be somewhat doubted, as to what she actually sees.
The
preparing officer does not doubt that Mrs. Duvall sees something, but after quite
extensive investigation has concluded that the light seen is nothing other than a
landing light on a commercial aircraft. It has been learned that commercial aircraft
do fly nearby, coming over nearby Riverdale on their way to Washington International
Airport, and do turn on their landing lights in this vicinity. When there is traffic
at the
airport, they go into a Riverdale pattern, which might account for the unknown
object to go fast and slow, and even appear to hover, and then west into the south-
west, in the vicinity of Washington International Airport -
