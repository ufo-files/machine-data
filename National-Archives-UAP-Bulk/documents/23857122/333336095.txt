1. At EATON RAPIDS, MICHIGAN on 20 July 1952 at 06303 while standing
on his front lawn looking east, MR. ALLAN H. MILLER and his WIFE visually
observed one (1) object of light bluish radiance with five (5) pointed
prongs which appeared to be about fifty (50) miles distant, although he
reasoned that it must have been in outer space.
2. At first appearance the object observed was stationary at approxi-
mately two (2) thousand feet altitude.
Its first maneuver was to glow brighter
and move in a circular motion as if spinning.
then travel northward approximately
two (2) or three (3) thousand feet in horizontal flight.
The number of times
this maneuver was executed is not known by the observer but he states that some-
time during these maneuvers the object would suddenly ascend to an estimated
forty (40) thousand foet in vertical flight, then return to its original position
at two (2) thousand feet.
The rate of speed attained by the object in vertical
flight appeared to be at the speed of light since this change in altitude was
accomplished instantly.
At times the object would move directly away from the
observer toward the east diminishing in brilliance as it did so, changing from
a bluish glow of light to a gölden color.
Following this maneuver the object
would fade completely for approximately thirty (30) seconds and then reappear
in its full brilliance.
3. MR. MILLER states that after he and his WIFE had observed the object
for approximately thirty (30) minutes he telephoned the CIVIL AERONAUTICS
ADMINISTRATION office in LANSING, MICHIGAN, reporting what he had seen and
asked the observers at the airport to watch the sky to the east for this object.
After his telephone call was completed MR. MILLER returned to his lawn and
observed the objeot for another thirty (30) minutes.
During the remainder of
his observations MR. MILLER states that the object followed the same pattern
of movements as already described.
4. MR. MILLER states that he could hear no sound nor could he distinguish
any trail or exhaust from the object. He further states that he observed no
airoraft in the vicinity at any time during the sighting.
TENTH AIR FORCE DIRECTOR OF INTELLIGENCE COMMENT:
1. The investigating officer, CAPTAIN DAVIS, states that MR. MILLER is a
non-alcoholic and appeared to be conscientious and reliable.
The investigating officer further stated that he queried the CIVIL
AERONAUTIOS ADNINISTRATION at LANSING, MICHIGAN in reference to MR. MILIBR'S
telephone call to that organization.
The person on duty at the time of the
reported sighting made no report of the telephone call.
The 661st AC&W Squadron
at SELFRIDGE AFB, MICHIGAN identified two
(2) friendly aircraft in
the vicinity during the time of the sighting.
NOTAMS indicated that the UNIVERSITY OF MINNESOTA at MINNEAPOLIS,
MINNESOTA was scheduled to release a cosmic ray balloon on 19 July 1952.
ities and the prevailing winds could possibly have placed the balloon in the
general vicinity during
the time of the sighting, however, the mere presence
of the balloon would not account for the sudden changes in altitude at such
tremendous speeds as reported.
