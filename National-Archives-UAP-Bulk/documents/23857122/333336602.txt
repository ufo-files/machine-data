9 July 1953
The following infornation was obtained by long distance telephone
from Ist It HARRIS, Robert NMI, A0 590921, 47th Communication Squadron (C),
Ent Air Force Base, Colorado Springs, Colorado. At time of sighting It
Harris was at Cordell Hall, Oklahona A & M Campus, Stillwater, Oklahoma,
where he will remain until 23 July 1953.
1.
As close as I could say it was elliptical; a dim white light
compared with a ceiling light or the light a search light would throw on
the bottom of a cloud. It was moving. It is possible that it could have
been a
search light; however, it moved back and forth in a limited arc.
As far as I could tell, there was no overcast.
As far as I know, the only
thing that would throw up a light like that is the airport at Iulsa, Okla.,
approximately 70 miles away. The most objects I saw were two (2), one of
which was intermittent. One was directly above the other. There is a
possibility of a trail. The uppermost light seemed to disappear occasion-
ally.
There was a very limited range of movement, I would say a 10° arc.
I heard no noise. If these objects were at the distance I think they
were, the movement would have been quite rapid, I would say somethere be-
tween 5 and 750 mph.
This is based on what I have observed from aircraft.
I left the objects to make the call to report them; when I went back they
were not there. Fran time to time I saw stars above the object. I have
never seen anything like this before.
2. I saw this object at approximately 2300 (CST) on 6 July 1953-
I observed this for approximately two and one-half (2z) minutes.
3. I observed it from the ground with the naked eye.
4• I was outside on the ground at Cordell Hall, Oklahona A & M
Campus, Stillwater, Oklahoma.
The object was about 15° or 20°
from the
horizon. It was about 120° North. The A & M Campus is located in the
North Central part of Stillwater, Oklahoma.
I have two (2) years experience as Control Tower Operator with
the Air Force. I am a private pilot and have a CAA Certificate as a con-
trol tower operator. I have approximately one hundred (100) hours in the
air.
Further around towards North from 120° from East on up to about

to 90° there was some lightening. It was off down
to the horizon.
7. Meteorological condition is unknown.
8. I did not take any pictures and did not obtain any fragments.

10. There was no aircraft in the air to the best of my knowledge.

9 July 1953
The following information was obtained by long distance telephone from
Mr. Earl Lo Iba, Box 131, Goodwell, Oklahoma, telephone no. 3076.
Mr. Iba
is temporarily residing at Cordell Hall, Oklahoma A & M Campus, Stillwater,
Oklahoma.
1o What I saw looked like two (2) search light flashes that varied
back and forth, one hitting right on top of the other.
They would flash back
and forth and then center on one.
It looked like the beam came from the
ground.
You could see part of the beam at times and other times it would
fade completely out. It was larger than a search light beam. I saw this
two (2) or three (3) different nights.
I just thought it was a search light.
There are no search lights in this part of the country that I know of; how-
ever, I haven't spent much time here for the last five or six years.
There
was no sound. The color was the same as a search light beam. It looked
like a search light beam reflecting off a cloud. I couldn't tell how many
objects there were. When I saw these before, they were in the same locality•
They looked like a search light crossing one
another then all at once they
would stop one on top of the other. I couldn't see any trail or exhaust.
There was no propulsion that I know of.
They moved rapidly.
They moved
lot faster than an ordinary search light beam would have moved.
They would
place and stop on top of one another. I didn't see them disappear.
2. I saw this at approximately 2300 (CST) on 6 July 1953.
3. I observed these with the naked eye.
4. I was watching through the second (2d) story of the dormitory.
There was a screen on the window and the window was open.
The object was in
the direction of Southeast of the dormitory, almost to a right angle - 85°
to 90°.
5. I have had no experience observing aircraft. I was in the ground
forces of the service for thirty-three (33) months.
6. On this particular night it was cloudy; the overcast
was more or
less in the background of the object I was watching. The cloudiness was
Southeast of me.
7. I don't know anything about meteorology.
8. I did not take any pictures.
I don't remember hearing or 'seeing any aircraft in the air on that
particular night.
