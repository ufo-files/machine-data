Dear
Sirs,
The evening of July 31, 1967, at approx. 9:55 P.M. was
a very exciting evening for my wife, neighbor
and myself. I
had just finished setting up my small 234 power refractor
telescope when my wife.
wu noticed a bright star-like
object in the western
sky•
She had first thought it Venus but
then we realized that Venus had already set. I continued
setting up my scope using my low power H12.5 mm. eyepiece.
At first it was hard to focus as the object began to slowly
move in a South Westerly direction. The object was approx.
approx. 3 times the speed the moon travels across the eyepiece.
After about three minuets time I was in focus. At first I
thought I wasn't in focus because in the scope I saw multiple
lights.
I had to move my scope ahead of the lights & let
them pass the eyepiece. I did this several times,
trying to look for some small detail I hadn't previously
seen.
At this instant approx. 10:00 P.M. (to the unaided eye),
a spark like light appeared to fall from the ofject and then
a second light seemed to tumble out like sparks from a
Roman candle flare.
Each light being visible for enly
three or four seconds.
I saw these happenings both by my
telescope and by my unaided eyes. My wife
saw these lights at all
times with her eyes.
She is not familiar with the telescope
enough to keep an object in view of the eyepiece. I didn't
want to chance missing some important feature in the time it
would have probably taken her to sight the lights in the
scope. This was not selfishness I felt, strickly vital so I
remember any and every detail that could be seen.
What I saw in the scope can be clearly seen in my
enclosed color illustration. This is painted as close
to actual size (as seen in my eyepiece) and with all the
detail which I was able to see. The most unusual thing
occured each time the spark-like lights exited from the
lighted areas. Every light except the one falling out would
flutter or dim.
As the orange lights left they tumbled as
a football would bouncing on the ground, appearing only for
a few seconds then they appeared to fade away or to burn out.
This left only the white light and one big orange one.
The other two lights had left.
