                 The Colombian uncertainty on what can happen at the border has
             been accentuated by recent incidents with the neighboring country.
                 Border relations between Venezuela and Colombia have never
             been devoid of problems.    Despite the undeniable progress that has
             been achieved by the negotiating commission of the conflict, which
             was created by the two countries, the incidents continue to recur.
             That is why Colombian authorities were greatly displeased by the
             statement made by Venezuelan Foreign Minister Jose Vicente Rangel
             last week, in response to a journalist's question concerning the
             alleged overflight by a Venezuelan aircraft over Norte de
             Santander in Colombia.   The Colombians are sighting UFO's, Rangel
             had said.   Apparently there are persons there who have a more
             vivid imagination than Gabriel Garcia Marquez, he noted.
                 The truth is that, according to reports available to the
             Colombian authorities, over the past weeks there have been
             Venezuelan overflights in the jurisdiction of the municipality of
             Tibu.   The first occurred July 22 at 11:30 in the morning.
             According to the official report, three F-16 aircraft, belonging
             to the Venezuelan Armed Forces [FAV], that had been camouflaged,
             suddenly flew over Colombian territory, causing great alarm among
             the authorities.
                 According to official reports, the aircraft overflew at an
             altitude below 3,000 feet.    According to reports supplied by
             military authorities of the zone, -- that were made available to
             the Colombian Foreign Ministry in the wake of the incident -- the
             aircraft entered 35 km inside the jurisdiction of Tibu.     The
             entrance and consequential violation of Colombian airspace
             occurred in the sector known as Vetas de Oriente.    The aircraft
             later passed over Tibu and Campo Dos and again overflew Venezuelan
             territory in the area of Tres Bocas (see map).    This same
             operation had been carried out by Venezuelan aircraft on three
             opportunities, the most recent on July 27.
                 Arms at Discretion [subhead]
                 Another source of tension between the two countries has been
             Venezuelan gunrunning operations to the FARC [Revolutionary Armed
             Forces of Colombia].   Although Venezuelan authorities have been
             reluctant to admit publicly that there are ties between Venezuelan
             gun runners and Colombian guerrillas, the truth is that Colombian
             security organizations have evidence which demonstrates that the
             existence of such ties are close and cause of concern.    Among the
             weapons seized from Colombian subversive organizations are FAL and
             AK-47 rifles, M-60 machine-guns, grenades. Browning pistols,
             Miniuzi submachine-guns, and approximately 35,000 rounds of
             ammunition from the neighboring country.    According to authorities
             of the FARC 16th Front, which operates in Vichada, and whose
             commander is Tomas Molina Caracas, a.    K.  A.  "El Negro Acacio,"
             conducts permanent operations on the Venezuelan border.
                 According to authorities, another of the leaders of the group,
             is Josue Eliseo Prieto alias "Commander Esteban," considered one
             of the heads of finances of the FARC.    The other leader is known
             as "Chiguere," who was imprisoned for a long time in Venezuela,
             but who managed to escape with the complicity of the prison guards
             in Puerto Ayacucho, capital of the state of Amazonas.
                 "Esteban" was killed by men of the 4th Army Division last May
             26 in an operation staged between the municipalities of Garcitas
             and Totumo (Vichada).   Four Venezuelan citizens were captured in
             the operation.    Nonetheless, some authorities of that country
