May 21, 1948
MEMORANDUM TO DEAN SAVILLE
Yesterday, at your insistence and in spite of my objections,
I informed you that a certain project which we have in the Research
Division is connected with the recent tests in the Pacific. Although
I did not divulge the nature of the connection in any way, the
coupling of the project to the tests is, in itself, a matter which
has a fairly high security classification, and as I informed you at
the time, I could see no reason for your having this information.
I therefore am writing this note to make it a matter of record in
my files, so as to protect those of us who have been properly
cleared for this particular work.
The movement of the men on our
staff to the Pacific was accomplished without anyone in the
University, other than those actually participating, being aware
of where they were going.
The very fact that we were able to
accomplish this shows that we are competent to respect the security
requirements.
Security regulations provide that even if e man is cleared
to receive classified information, he may only receive it if there
1s a definite reason for him to have it. Those of us who have been
connected with highly classified work in the past and who still have
to be connected with it always take the attitude that we wish to know
as little as possible and only acquaint ourselves with items of a
classified nature which are absolutely essential to our work.
If, as you indicated yesterday, it is your desire to be
informed of all the details of work which we undertake, which I-pinge
on highly classified contracts, then I would say that the only proper
policy for the University to adopt is to not take any work which
either comes under security regulations or 1s even remotely connected
to other projects which are classified. As things are going today,
this would mean, in essence, that we would eliminate the majority
of our Government contracts.
Athelstan F. Spilhaus
