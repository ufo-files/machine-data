Chapter Twelve
The Minnesota Years
1949-1967
"The time you are living in is always exciting. It is the only time you have."
Athelstan Spilhaus
The Minnesota years were by far the most productive and satisfying for Dr. Spilhaus
from a professional and personal standpoint. This was a time when his tri-continental
upbringing and lifestyle became most useful in his life and work. He was brought up in
Africa, educated in Africa, Europe, and the United States, and had an entrepreneurial
background from the Spilhaus side, and an intellectual and educational background from
his mother's family, the Muir's. He became more and more Americanized as he became
well known in industry, science, academia, government, international diplomacy, politics,
and the world of arts. He became a leader in America's entry into space. His involve-
ment in world events at this time became his life.
"After the War I kept up my military connections in science and enjoyed it. I was in on
such things as the first ice island in the Arctic that was sighted by Joe Fletcher, who had
been an officer with me during WWII. He was doing these patrols from Alaska, and he
sighted Fletcher's Island which is still floating out there somewhere. Such ice islands
move circularly in the Arctic Ocean, and Fletcher's Island was in an ideal place for
research. The Air Force slapped a security lid on it, and since I was Chairman of the
Scientific Advisory Board at the time I declared this ridiculous because of the size of the
island. The secrecy was broken, and we were able to use it for research. We have since
identified more of these islands. They were so interesting for study, that I often took time
from my academic duties, got into uniform, and went up to Alaska.
"Until then, I'd been used to this life of traveling to Europe with the Air Force and doing
many varied activities, which people do much more today. In those days it wasn't so
common. I took over the Institute of Technology on January 1, 1949, and no sooner had I
settled in my seat than I found I had a commitment to go to the Pacific and New Zealand
"I decided, if they appointed me, they'd better get used to my way of life, so I went,
having first stirred the pot nicely at the Institute. The only thing I did in the first month
was simply to say to the faculty, "I have to go on this trip, and I'm not going to change
anything, or stir up anything, prior to my leaving. I'm simply going to say that I do not
know what the rules of the faculty are in regard to many things, but you do have rules. I
have noticed that in flunking students for low achievement, the rules have not been
applied precisely in every case." I paused and then said, "They will now be applied."
