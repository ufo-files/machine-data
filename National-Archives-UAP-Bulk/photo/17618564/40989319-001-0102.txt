The design of instruments for detection of sound at long
ranges must be based upon two considerations, (a) the frequency
spectrum of the source, as determined by Fourier analysis and (b)
the transmission characteristics of the atmosphere. The second
factor has been considered in detail in Appendix A, in which the
conclusion was draw that, for sound refracted at the second at-
mospheric inversion (approximately 100-110 IN high), the atmosphere
transmits sound best in the band below 1 ops. For sound refracted
at the first inversion (50 IN) as well as for sound refracted at
low-level inversions ("direct" waves) atmospheric transmission 1s
good to frequencies considerably above 1 cps. As the sound de-
tected at long ranges (1000 IM and upwards) may arrive at the de-
tection point after several refractions at the first inversion, or
may oven travel as a "direct" wave, the equipment used would have
to respond to frequencies considerably above 1 cps unless it can bo
shown that comparatively little energy in this region is omitted
