of Sounding Balloons
Audouin Dollfus
Section d'Astrophysique, Observatoire de Paris
Meudon, Seine et Oise, Fr›nce
1. THE TECHNIQUE OF CHAINS OF DILATABLE BALLOONS
Dilatable sounding balloons with membranes of elastic latex or neoprene are
currently used for lifting light payloads up to 100,000 feet. We developed a
technique for linking many such balloons to carry heavy payloads.
Each balloon is manufactured with two necks, one at the bottom and the other
diametrically opposite at the top. A vertical cable is passed through the two
necks; the inferior neck is linked to the cable and carries the lift; the zenithal
neck is gas-tight around the cable, but can slip along this cable to accommodate
the balloon's increase in size with altitude (Figure 1).
By ropes and winches we were able to build up more than 30 such balloons one
above the other, like a string of beads. The first balloon at the top has a single
neck and is less inflated than the others in order to avoid a premature burst which
might cause the cable and fragments to fall on top of the second balloon, and so on.
Preliminary experiments were conducted with French Delacoste 2,000-gram
balloons, and with British Beritex 4,500-gram balloons carrying 16 pounds each at
85,000 feet. We finally used Dewey and Almy large 7,000-gram neoprene Darex
balloons, each lifting 70 pounds at 100,000 feet.
