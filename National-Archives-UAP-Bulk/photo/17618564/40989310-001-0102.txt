In late May 1947, Mogulạwent on the road for the first time. Moore,
Peoples, Trakowski, others from NYU and Watson Laboratories, and their
equipment flew west from New Jersey aboard several aircraft dedicated to the
project (B-178, C-54s) to Alamogordo Army Air Field, New Mexico, where they
set up shop in a hangar in the field's North Area. Their neighbors were a
group of Signal Corps radar men under the command of Captain L. H. (Larry)
Dyvad and assigned to AMC's Watson Laboratories. They were there to track
V-2 rocket launches out of the adjacent White Sands Proving Ground, and
taking advantage of Mogul's Triple A standing and the mutual Watson
Laboratories association, Moore enlisted their aid in tracking his
balloons. (104]
These launches included Mogul and simple meteorological arrays, the
latter to chart local "wind fields and check current conditions before
releasing the former.
The meteorological flights were clusters of 3 to 7
neoprene weather balloons lifting a "tail" of 3 to 5 ML-307B radar-reflecting
targets linked together one above the other. Each corner-reflector target
measured about three feet on a side, and Moore used multiple targets because
he found the signal return from one was too faint to be useful[105] Similar
target arrays were used on many of the Mogul launches. I will discuss these
radar targets in some detail below.
Two numbered Mogul launches, Flights 5 and 6, were made during this
first field operation at Alamogordo, on June 5 and June 7 respectively. Both
were huge and complex, about 700 feet from top to bottom, each including
almost 30 meteorological balloons (the polyethylene balloons were not yet
available) and several payload, radiosonde (meteorological data collection and
radio transmission/tracking devices), control, parachute, and dropable-ballast
packages. All of this was strung together with a complex rigging of heavy-duty
monofilament nylon line and hand braided nylon "lobster twine," plus various
fittings, many of which were cobbled together on site. 106] I will return to a
consideration of this rigging material below.
Flight 5 was airborne for almost six hours, successfully maintained a
period of constant-level flight at 51,000 feet, and grounded east of Roswell,
where It was recovered by the Mogul team. Flight 6, less successful, flew for
about three hours, and about half of the array was recovered about 18 miles
east-southeast of Alamogordo.
