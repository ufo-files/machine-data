HEADQUARTERS STRATEGIC AIR COMMAND
ANDREWS AIR FORCE BASE
WASHINGTON 20, D. C.
10 September 1948
Major General Roger M. Ramey
Commanding, Eighth Air Force
Carswell Air Force Base
Fort Worth, Texas
Dear Rog:
Yesterday Lindbergh was in my office and we were discussing
the 509th Bomb Wing. As you know he went to Labrador with one of the
squadrons.
Lindbergh states that the commanding officer of the squadron
with whom he went was an excellent flyer and had an excellent crew on
his airplane. On the other hand, he thought that maintenance in the
squadron and throughout the entire wing was very poor. He stated that
aircraft were dirty and had material and equipment piled all over them
and that mechanically he thought they were in poor condition. He also
states that he thinks the technique of flying of the major crews of the
509th is very poor. One of the suggestions which came up in our con-
ference was that we require each of the aircraft commanders to fly
each month in a T-6, or other small type airplane, in order that they
might perfect their technique in making landings. It appeared to him
that the case at Goose Bay where the pilot overshot the runway was
just poor technique and it is his opinion that this could be materially
improved if they had the opportunity to make landings in a small air-
plane. He made this suggestion as a result of his own experience in
flying. I think it to be a good idea myself and suggest that we assign
two T-6's to the group and require both the pilots and co-pilots of our
crews to make at least ten landings each month in a T-ó.
