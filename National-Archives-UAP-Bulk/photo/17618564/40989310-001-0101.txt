The Mogu team returned to New Mexico in late June 1947, again enlisting
the aid of Captain Dyvad's radar men in tracking Mogul's aerial behemoths and
their lesser cousins. As before, the team conducted numerous unlogged
flights-some Mogul arrays, many meteorological clusters, and substantial
numbers of test-support (explosives and instrument) launches, all using the
same sort of rigging, instrumentation, and tracking gear as before. The first
numbered Mogul launch in this set was Flight 7, lifting off at 5:21 am. on
Wednesday, July 2. This was the last of the Moguls employing neoprene
meteorological balloons, 20 of them, and the array measured about 450 feet
from top to bottom. After nearly seven hours in the air, this flight grounded in
the Sacramento Mountains about 31 miles east of Alamogordo AAF. All that
was recovered by the Mogul team was "one balloon neck."
At just after 3 a.m. the next day, Thursday, July 3, the first polyethylene
balloons ever to be flown were launched by the Mogul team. This was Mogul
Flight 8, an array over 200 feet tall employing 10 conical General Mills
balloons. Flight 8 was aloft about three hours before touching down about 20
miles west-northwest of the base, and it was last seen dragging north across
the desert Nothing from this flight was recovered.
