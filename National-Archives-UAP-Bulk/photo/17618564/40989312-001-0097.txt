Chapter Twenty Three
The Franklin Institute—1967
"My first impression was that the Franklin Institute would be like the World's Fair, and
I'd be able to play with all the toys. It was very enjoyable, to be sure, but very different
from the World's Fair. I didn't have ten million dollars to put on a show for six months,
and I found I was expected to go out and raise money, which I didn't enjoy at all. I was
good at ideas, but I thought other people should do the fund raising. Wynn Lepage,
Chairman of the Board of Managers, was not used to the idea that in addition to my being
President of the Franklin Institute, I kept up all my other activities, writing the comic
strip and everything else-what I called my ongoing work." About two years later,
when we had a discussion about my leaving. I said, "Gentlemen, the best way I can put it
is, I really am forced to relinquish this wonderful job because I find the job interfering
with my work.i Money raising should be a separate profession. In any event, it's not
something a scholar or an idea man ought to do-although some do it very well. Maybe
it's not so much that they shouldn't to do it, but whether they can do it well.
