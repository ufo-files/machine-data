"After I went back to New York University in 1946, I found that Federal funding of
research in universities had been invented. Although there had been some grants to
universities before World War II, they were really nothing to speak about. We soon
became experts in contract research, whether for good or bad, I don't really know.
"At that time, though, it seemed mostly good, and because of my military connections, I
was made the first Director of Research at NYU. That may not sound like much now, but
after two years, I had built up a two-million-dollar research factory at The Heights. In
fact, once again I'd practically taken over The Heights. Then I did what I later came to
think was a dreadful thing. I paid the people who did my research on contract more than
they received from the academic side. In effect, I raised their salaries. In that way, I
could get the best people on the research. Later on, when I was a Dean, I swore I would
never have a Director of Research, because he had ways to skim off the cream of the
crop. This tactic happened in lots of universities men going into contract research and
as a result lost to teaching. Nevertheless, I was on that side, and I made a great success of
contract research. I was still able to do some teaching in meteorology, though not very
acuvely, and I still worked with the Air Force.
"The success of teaming up university people with government in wartime was apparent
to Vannevar Bush and the rest of us. We recognized the importance of continuing this
cooperation in peacetime. Bush set up advisory boards using the professors in what

government began funding universities to do military research, veterans administration,
and all other kinds of research. We created some interesting programs and projects. My

Rusk of NYU's famous rehabilitation unit in charge of the program.
"Artificial limbs have come a long way. Before then, there hadn't been so many ampu-

was the state of the art. We had people working on instrumented platforms so we got
accurate pressures and everything, which led in time to these marvelous limbs companies
