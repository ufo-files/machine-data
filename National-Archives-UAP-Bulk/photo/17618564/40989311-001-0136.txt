Who is Athelstan Spilhaus?
Athelstan Spilhaus' influence has reached far beyond academia and science. He has
touched lives in many countries at every level. Some of Athel's inventions have been
called "quietly monumental". His invention of the Bathythermograph, revolutionized the
study of oceans and was adapted for military use in World War II. The Sea Grant
college is another of his ideas which became a reality. His co-invention, the artificial
horizon, is standard equipment on every airplane in the world. For many years he was
the author of a syndicated comic strip, Our New Age", which explained scientific
principles in lay terms. Many of his peers were jealous of the ten million readers and
"pooh-poohed" the whole thing. Yet, President Kennedy once said to Athel, "The only
science I ever learned was from your comic strip in The Boston Globe."
He designed the sculpture in front of the McGraw-Hill Building in New York City. His
work in meteorology in World War II, while he lived in caves in China with Mao Tse-
Tung, won him the Legion of Merit. He has been an advisor and representative to many
governments all over the world, and was America's first ambassador to UNESCO. He
was Dean of the Minnesota School of Engineering for almost twenty years—a time of
high visibility and productivity for Athelstan. He invented a space clock, and produced
maps of Earth that emphasize the prominence of the oceans on our planet. He has put
together what is considered the finest collection of antique mechanical toys in this
country. He has served on govermental, industrial, and academic boards of directors too
numerous to mention. He has been a friend and mentor to some of the best known
figures of this century. He holds over fifty patents on every type of invention imaginable.
He loves to invent scientific games which are fun as well as educational. He was chosen
by South Africa to improve and rework their meteorological services, and has been a
scientific director of weapon's effects for two atomic tests, as well as being a consultant
for the U.S. Armed Forces Special Weapons Project.
He was appointed to the United States National Committee for the International Geo-
physical Year in 1955. Athel had already predicted the use of satellites, when President
Eisenhower announced that as part of a follow-up to IGY, the U.S. would start construct-
ing them. President Kennedy appointed him as U.S. Commissioner to the World's Fair in
Seattle from 1961 to 1963 and Athelstan was largely responsible for its success. Johnson
appointed him to the Board of the National Science Foundation.
Athel has been President of the American Association for the Advancement of Science-
the world's largest scientific organization. He is also a Fellow of the American Institute
of Aeronautics and Astronautics, as well as many other technological and scientific
organizations.
