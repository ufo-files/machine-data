Although the internal difficulties of this story and its inconsistency with
other, credible Roswell accounts were obvious, the possibility that a
prominent public figure may have been caught up in the case made it worth
pursuing. Since Montoya, New Mexico's second-ranking state elected official,
was alleged to be in Roswell for a public ceremony on a "special day," it was
easy, if time consuming, to check out. It did not.
Walter Haut and I independently combed through the Roswell
newspapers for the two-week period July 1 through July 14, 1947, and I
checked both Albuquerque papers for the same dates. Other than community
Fourth of July festivities, no special ceremony of any kind took place in
Roswell or at the base, and Montoya was not mentioned in connection with
the Independence Day activities. There was nothing in any of the papers about
Montoya being present in Roswell during that period for any reason.
I discussed Anaya's story with two very close family-political friends of
the Montoyas. Through one of them, I approached Joseph Montoya's widow,
Della. She told him she did not remember her husband being in Roswell for
anything at that time, and she most certainly did not remember a hysterical
telephone call from him about a flying saucer and little men from outer space.
Similarly, neither of her friends, one of whom has known the Montoyas for
almost 50 years, had heard anything about Joseph Montoya being involved in
the Roswell Incident before I mentioned the possibility.(14]
As with Frankie Rowe's story, there is an outside chance Anaya's is
based on something real, but once again, it seems unlikely to have been
anything to do with the Roswell Incident.
Unfortunately, although the bandwagon has square wheels, it keeps
bumping along.
