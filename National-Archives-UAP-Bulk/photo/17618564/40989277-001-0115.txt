Foreword
In early 1994, the General Accounting Office (GAO) tasked the Air Force,
along with other governmental agencies, to locate records regarding alleged events
that have become known as the "Roswell Incident." My guidance to Air Force
researchers who assisted in this inquiry was simple: find the information and let them
review it. If it is classified, declassify it. Hold nothing back, put this issue to rest
once and for all. From the outset there was no predisposition to refute or overlook
any information. The result was a report completed in July 1994. This document was
made available to the public and published as The Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction
in the New Mexico Desert.
Subsequent to the release of that report, we discovered a pattern of actual
events, alleged events, and anecdotes indicating that more analysis was required.
This analysis encompassed an extended time period and revealed probable
incorporation of actual U.S. Air Force research activities, aircraft mishaps, and other
operations that were molded into a collection of time compressed scenarios. This
report is the result of that additional analysis. (Contrary to allegations of illicit
conduct, cover-ups, and other unusual activities, this further examination did not
provide information to even remotely suggest that this was the case.) What was
discovered, and is included in this publication, are a collection of carefully
documented Air Force activities that are the most probable explanations for the
series of the events in question.
As you examine this document, you will notice that the actual events are
vastly divergent from anecdotes provided to substantiate the scenarios traditionally
associated with this topic. Instead, you will find a fascinating glimpse of history that
illustrates outstanding accomplishments and self-sacrifices of the men and women that
are the U.S. Air Force.
In 1997, as we celebrate our 50th anniversary, I encourage the exploration of
our rich and unsurpassed history which stands as testament to the many achievements
that have made the U.S. Air Force the greatest aerospace power in history.
SHELIA E. WIDNALL
Secretary of the Air Force
