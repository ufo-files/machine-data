A Family Biography
Born in Portugal, Athelstan's father, Karl Spilhaus, was orphaned young, and with his
two sisters, was taken to his grandfather's house in Lubeck, Germany, where he lived
until he joined the family firm in Africa in about 1890.
In the truest sense of the word, Karl Spilhaus was a merchant. He began his career
buying scrawny sheep in the Kalihari Desert in about 1890. He was thought to be the
first white man to cross that desert. Around 1920, he began the first cold storage com-
pany in Capetown. Not trusting the international currency market, he bartered goods for
goods; and, consequently, through two world wars, when inflation soared, he never lost a
shilling on currency fluctuations.
Athel's mother arrived in South Africa in an interesting way. Around 1892, John Cecil
Rhodes was wending his way up through the spine of Africa, establishing the empire. He
was looking for someone to establish a school system and be superintendent. Rhodes
interviewed Sir Thomas Muir, and promptly hired him. At the same time Muir was
offered a professorship at Stanford University in California, where his uncle John Muir,
the famous naturalist, was living. Sir Thomas, however, accepted Rhodes' offer and
arrived in Capetown in May, 1892.
