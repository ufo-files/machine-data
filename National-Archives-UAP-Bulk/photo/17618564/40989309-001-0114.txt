FOR RELEASE: 21 April 1967
AIR FORCE MISSILE DEVELOPMENT CENTER, Holloman Air Force Base, New Mexico--
The unidentified flying object reported to Holloman Air Force Base, N.M., on March
2, most likely was caused by an accidental radar chaff drop from an aircraft. The base
had aircraft returning from a training chaff drop mission in the vicinity of the sighting
at the same fime.
Officers assigned to investigate the report said their conclusions were based on
reported movement patterns of the unidentified flying object and supporting data from
surveillance radars.
The chaff consists of long, norrow metallic strips dropped from aircraft to confuse
ground radars attempting to pinpoint an aircraft as a target.
The chaff had a falling and wind movement pattern and moved similar to the direction
and velocity reported for the unidentified flying object.
In addition, the chaff was observed on surveillance radars at White Sands Missile Range
in the same area at the same time as the unidentified flying object sighting.
