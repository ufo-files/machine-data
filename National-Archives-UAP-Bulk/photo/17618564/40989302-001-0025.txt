DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE
WASHINGTON
OFFICE OF THE SECRETARY
11 July 1957
MEMORANDUM FOR THE UNDER SECRETARY OF THE AIR FORCE
SUBJECT: Application for Correction of Military Record of
Colonel William Mitchell
The Air Force Board for the Correction of Military Records,
in recommending that the subject application be granted, appears
to have based its recommendation in part upon the conclusion
that the court-martial conviction was not supported by the evi-
dence since "the record is devoid of proof of what would seem to
be an essential element of the crime, the intent to discredit and
the intent to be contemptuous or disrespectful." (Par. 28 of
the Board's findings. See also pars. 21, 29, 39, and 41.) In
my opinion, there is no legal basis for such a conclusion.
Colonel Mitchell was charged with violating the old 96th Article
of War which read as follows:
"Though not mentioned in these articles, all disorders
and neglects to the prejudice of good order and military
discipline, all conduct of a nature to bring discredit
upon the military service, and all crimes or offenses
not capital, of which persons subject to military law
may be guilty, shall be taken cognizance of by a general
or special or summary court-martial, according to the
nature and degree of the offense, and punished at the
discretion of such court."
The 1921 Edition of the U.S. Army Court-Martial Manual con-
tains the following statement concerning intent (par. 280):

distinguished as follows: Those in which a distinct
and specific intent, independent of the mere act, is
essential to constitute the offense, as murder, lar-
ceny, burglary, desertion, and mutiny; and those in
