ABSTRACT
This report deals with acoustic detection tests on Project "Fitz-
willian" conducted by the Geophysical Research Division of Watson
Laboratories in April and May of 1948. The efforts of this group,
using balloon-borne microphones and a radio telemetering system, were
directed toward detection of acoustic disturbances near the tropopause
in the atmosphere. Two field parties monitored each of the three ex-
plosions. One party remained at Alamogordo, New Mexico for all tests,
and the other was stationed at Kwajalein for Test X, Guam for Test I, and
labu, H. I. for Test I.
Positive results were obtained at Iwajalein, and there 1s a pos-
sible return on the Guam record. The remaining records generally indi-
cate disturbances, but it is not possible to positively identify any of
them as due to the explosion. A comparison of these results with the
results on ground equipment made by other agencies nay lead to more de-
finite conclusions. The evidence of these tests indicates that the
disturbances produced by the explosions are not unusual enough at ranges
over 1,000 miles to permit identification without an array of three or
more microphones.
