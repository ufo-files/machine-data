The Crate Flight
According to Frank Kaufmann, on the night of July 8, the cadavers were
sealed in body bags, placed in a closed wooden crate, and removed to a
deserted hanger, where the crate was illuminated by a spotlight and kept
under armed guard. The late Sergeant Melvin E. Brown, a cook stationed at
Roswel AAF in July 1947, claimed to have been posted as a guard that day al
both the recovery site and the hanger, according to his daughter, Beverly
Bean. Bean says Brown told her and other family members he saw the strange
bodies under a tarpaulin on a truck at the ranch, but that when he and his
squadron commander looked into the hanger that night, all they saw was the
sealed crate.[38]
Late the next afternoon, July 9, the crate was moved to a bomb loading
pit, still under guard. There it and its guards were loaded into the forward
bomb bay of a B-29 (tail number 7301), which then departed for Fort Worth
AAF. Robert Slusher, a member of the bomber's crew, says the flight was
unusual on several counts. It was ordered hastily, the outbound leg was flown
very low and with the crew-space pressurization off, and they returned
immediately after delivering their cargo and passengers. Arriving in Fort
Worth, Slusher recalls, the flight was met by six men, including three MPs and
an undertaker, who had been a classmate of the B-29's bombardier, First
Lieutenant Felix Martucci. Slusher also remembers they had Major Marcel as a
passenger on the return leg, and that, when they landed at Roswel, Martucci
