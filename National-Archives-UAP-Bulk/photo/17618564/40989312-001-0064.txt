Chapter Fifteen
The International Geophysical Year
"After leaving UNESCO, I could have gone on and become a deputy to Henry Cabot
Lodge at the United Nations, but I was awfully tired of that kind of nonsense. Even to
this day, I admire the patience of people who argue interminably about the law of the sea,
or argue about the arms reductions agreement. These things may be terribly important
issues, but the politicking is just not to my taste-I simply didn't have the patience. The
same arguments would be repeated over and over again, but the rhetoric never addressed
itself to the central problems and that confuses a logical mind; so, I bowed out of my post
at UNESCO, with the reason that I was on the National Committee for the International
Geophysical Year (IGY), which I thought was a tremendously important project. It was
also an international activity, but one that was far more manageable and which had a far
more central purpose than UNESCO. I knew I couldn't be on IGY and travel all over the
world for it, continue working on the UNESCO board, and still play fair with my
University by stopping in once in a while.
"This was a full-fledged, world-wide IGY. The work went on for years and years-it set
the patter for much of the subsequent co-operation in geophysics, where the participa-
tion of people was needed to cover areas of global geography, not just politically defined
areas.
"Another reason for my leaving UNESCO was that all through my peripheral jobs, I
wanted to retain and do my job at the University, which I thought these peripheral jobs
assisted. I couldn't take on too many. When I was put on the National Committee for
IGY, I decided this was a case where, having toured the world for UNESCO, I'd have to
go to places like Antarctica for the IGY, and I couldn't do both. So these two things tied
in-IGY started in the fall of 1957, and I got out of UNESCO in 1958.
ạIt was at about this time, 1957, that I was invited to attend a meeting of the Pacific
Science Council in Bangkok, Thailand. While I was in Bangkok, I decided to go down
and see Antarctica and the IGY activities at the South Pole.
