At 16592, 17 January 1956, Site 17 (3445N 13110E) of the 618th AG&W
Squadron reported an unidentified track in the Lima Gulf area.
The track
was at 30,000 ft., heading East, range 72 nautical miles at a speed of 1500
mots.
The track was flashed at 17082, and later killed at 1730Z, unidentified.
Two F-86D interceptors were scran bled at 17162.
The first interceptor
was airborne at 17242 and was under GCI control a 17347. However, the track
faded before the fighters could make contact.
The track appeared to be a normal return about the size of a B-29 type
target. There was continuous presentation consisting of a single paint on
the scope for each rotation of the antenna from 1659Z to 17182.
The return
was bright and clear with no evidence of fuzzyness.

surface winds, north at 5 mph, ceiling 9,000

The track was first observed by Jerold M. Tuttle, A/2C, AF16314108, the
scope operator, bearing 172 degrees, range 72 miles, heading 150 degrees at
approximately 1000 knots.
(See page 5 for graphic display of the track)
The track proceded on a heading of 150 degrees with the speed increasing from
1500 to 1800 knots.
During the first five minutes that the track was under observation,
maintenance personnel attempted to determine whether the track was caused by
interference.
"Parrot" was turned off to preclude the possibility of inter-
ference. It was noted that the track crossed over angle markers at several
points, thus precluding several other types of interference. The determination
at this time by maintenance personnel was that the track was not caused by
interference.
The track continued on a heading of 150 degrees to a range of 160 miles,
at which time the track slowed its speed to approximately 500 knots, and for
3 or 4 sweeps of the antenna seemed to stand still. It then moved off on a
heading of 080 degrees.
The track continued this heading with speed increasing to approximately
2000 knots.
When the track reached a bearing of 100 degrees at 40 miles range
it turned to a heading of 280 degrees and moved to a bearing of 105 degrees at
19 miles.
