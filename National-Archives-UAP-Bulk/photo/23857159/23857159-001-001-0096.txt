Mrs. Bernard Fudem, 1121 West Poplar Street, York, Pennsylvania, reported seeing
an unidentified flying object at approximately, 0055 hours EST, on 10 January 1954 in
West York, Pennsylvania. (Reference TWX MAI-1-6-E, 14 January 1954.) A brief tele-
phonic interview with the observer revealed the following account of the sighting:
Mrs. Fudem stated that her attention was attracted by a bright moving object
forming an arc in the sky.
The formation of this arc appeared to be fairly high
above the horizon.
The object was cone-shaped with a round base. This base bore a
vivid blue color and stood out clearly against the sky.
Attached to the base or tail
of this object was a bright yellow flare.
Mrs. Fudem alleges that she has seen all
kinds airplanes, including jets, but never before had she seen anything like the
phenomenon referred to above. She stated further that in her estimation this un-
known object moved much faster than a jet plane.
According to Mrs. Fudem's statement
the night was clear and cool with little wind and the sky was studded with stars.
Mr. Fudem also observed this object but could not add anything to his wife's des-
cription.
(See attached "Observers Data Sheet" for additional details.)
Mrs. E. A. Street of 401 Hill crest Road, York, Pennsylvania, reported seeing an
unidentified flying object at approximately 0045 hours EST on 10 January 1954 in York,
Pennsylvania.
Telephonic conversation with Mrs. Street disclosed the following in-
formation:
Mrs. Street and her husband had just turned into the driveway of their home when
her attention was attracted by a moving bluish light in the southern part of the sky•
At first she thought it was a meteor.
She looked again and saw, what she described
as, a shapeless object predominately blue in color streaking through the air with in-
credible speed and leaving yellow flames in its wake.
This object appeared to be in
the process of exploding.
In the words of Mrs. Street, "It looked like it was a
plane exploding in the air. To the best of her recollection the weather at the time
of observation was clear with very little wind.
Mrs. Street's husband did not ob-
serve this unidentified object.
(See attached "Observers Data Sheet" for additional
details.)
Two other individuals, allegedly reporters for the York Gazette and Daily
observed this unidentified object on 10 January 1954.
They were not interviewed by
this reporter.
An account of this incident appeared in the morning edition of the York Gazette
and Daily and the evening edition of the York Dispatch.

report given by Mrs. Bernard Fudem to the Bureau of Police, City of York, Pennsylvania
