At approximately 07/0630Z, Mr. Cal Reeves, station manager for Reeves
Aleutian Airlines at Cold Bay, saw a small flash reflected from a lamp.
He turned and saw a big flash lighting the whole sky. The flash appeared
very close. The direction was S-SW of Cold Bay, color was similar to an
are weld.
Duration of the flash estimated as 25 to 30 seconds.
He did
not hear or feel the shock wave. Upon returning home, his wife said it
had shaken the house.
Mr. Wallace G. McNiese, postmaster at Cold Bay, saw a blue flash approxi-
mately SW of Cold Bay, described the color as light blue, estimated dura-
tion 35 seconds. The walls in the house shook. Mr. McNiese is a long-time
resident and has experienced volcanic activity and emphasized that this
was not volcanic activity or an earthquake.
Further stated it did not feel
like a ground explosion.
Personnel at the 714th ACW Squadron approximately 7 miles NW of Cold Bay
saw the flash.
Some reported feeling the shock wave and hearing an explo-
sion. Others felt and heard nothing.
The shock wave was described by one
observer as being sharp and similar to a sonic boom.
U. S. Weather Bureau at Cold Bay estimated distance as 7 to 10 miles using
sound-flash method.
Residents at Sand Point (55°20'N 160930'W) on Popof Island saw the flash
but did not feel or hear the shock wave.
Personnel at Cape Sarichef (54°36'N 164955'W) did not note either the
flash or the shock wave.
No aircraft were known to be flying in the vicinity at the time. No vol-
canic action was discernible the next morning.
