Elevation is 0 degrees for an object on the horizon, and 90
degrees for the point directly over the observer (zenith). Thus,
an object half-way up from the horizon to the zenith has an
elevation of 45 degrees.
Bearing (or 'azimuth") is the angle along the horizon, starting
from north and moving clockwise eastward. Thus, an object
directly toward the east, no matter what its elevation is above
the horizon, has a bearing of 90 degrees, an object in the south
has a bearing of 180 degrees; toward the west, 270 degrees and
so on. North is, of course, zero.
EXAMPLE:
An object is seen in the northeast and one-third way
up from horizon to overhead. Thus, the object has a bearing of
45 degrees, and elevation of 30 degrees. Similarly, an object
naving a bearing of 180 degrees and an elevation of 60 degrees
would be seen directly south and two-thirds of the way up from
the horizon.
