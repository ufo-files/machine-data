MEMORANDUM
To:
E. U. Condon
From:
R. Craig
Date: 21 February 1968
Subject: Comment on Air Base Cooperation with
the CU-UFO Project
On 8 Movember 1967, a military pilot, while airborne, reported,
to the Chicago Center, visual observation of an unknown object and radar
tracking of six other unknowns. The single object, which was observed for
two to four minutes, was a fast-moving large ball, changing color from
red to yellow to white. The report indicated that six more objects were
tracked by radar within a range of one and ahalf to five miles.
The Chicago
Center stated that the visual UFO also was sighted from two inbound TWA
aircraft, but no details of this observation were available.
No report of this sighting was sent to the CU Project, Project
" personnel learned of it in mid-December during telecon with the Offutt Air
Base UFO officer, who mistakenly assumed that questions pertaining to a
different UFO report pertained to the 8 November event, and Indicated he
had no information beyond an initial brief report from the Chicago Center,
sent to Offutt because the pilot was based there. The officer, Capt. Roy
Cunningham, did agree to contact ther pilot and send CU whatever infor-
mation about the event he could get from an interview with Pilot
Vanevenoven.
Since the report seemed a particularly interesting one, project
personnel sought additional Information from the Chicago Center. Telecon
with Chicago Center confirmed that the report had been made by both the
military pilo! an a TWA pilot and crew. Tapes of conversations with the
pilots, made during the observations, had, by December 10, been destroyed.
All Information regarding the sighting had been sent to Chanute AFB
Command Post.
Telecon 10 December with Major Nichols, Chanute AFB, revealed
that, since the sighting was 70 miles from Omaha, all information was
shipped to Base Operations at Offult AFB.
Since no information had yet been received from Capt. Cunningham
of Offutt APB, he was again contacted by telephone on 4 January 1068. He
indicated he had not had a chance to interview pilot Vanevenoven because
Vanevenoven and his crew had taken Christmas vacation as leave. Although
Vanevenoven had been unavailable, Capt. Cunningham indicated he could
now reach him, and would do so very soon. He said he would get a report
of the UTO incident to us shortly.
