Measurement of Residual Radioactivity
It has been postulated in previous reports of
UFO sightings that a form of nuclear propulsion might be
used in such a vehicle.
In seeking evidence of this as a possibility, first
the nature of the soil was considered.
This type of
Florida
sea sand is not pure silica, S102
Instead, it generally
contains calcium,
magnesium and iron impurities.
This
offers the possibility that a detectable gamma ray-emitting
isotope might be present.
It is recognized that the half-
life of gamma-emitting isotopes of silicon are in the range
of 2 seconds to 2.62 hours; therefore, I would not expect
to find evidence of residual radiation four days later.
The gamma-emission isotopes of oxygen have maximum half-
lives of 71 seconds, so the situation would be similar
for this element.
The two isotopes, Ca 47 (half-life=4.5 days) and
Fe 59 (half-life=45 days), however, are possible emission
sources for detection if a very sensitive scintillation
counter is used.
As mentioned before, a scintillation survey was
made over the entire area of the sighting and at no time
possible to detect a signal level higher than the
normal background count, plus instrument "dark current."
The instrument utilized was a Precision lllb Scintillator,
in the 'fast count" made with all readings on the O to
0.025 MR/hr scale.
The background (plus dark current)
readings ranged from 0.004 to 0.010 MR/hr.
If a nuclear propulsion jet stream had impinged on
the calcium and impurity contained sand, I believe that a
residual radioactivity level would have still existed and
the sensitivity of the Precision lllb Scintillator would
have been sufficient to detect it.
The observations and
readings did not confirm this as a fact.
SUMMARY
The observation of undisturbed loose litter in the area,
irregular spacing of the holes and lack of any measureable
radioactivity make it difficult for me to substantiate the
reported sighting on the basis of factual or measureable
evidence on the date of inspection.
