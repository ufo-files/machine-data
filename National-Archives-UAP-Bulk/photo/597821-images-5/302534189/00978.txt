7 July 1964
Capt. R. H. Henry
Public Information Officer
MacDil Air Force Base
Tampa, Florida
Dear Capt. Henry:
in response to the newspaper article in the Clearwater Sun dated
6 July 1964, and our telephone conversation today, the following is my
description of the lights which we saw Saturday evening, 4 July 1964,
I was in a group of people watching the Clearwater fireworks
display. It was at approximately 8:30 p.m. that we noticed a row of
five red lights in the sky to the north of the fireworks. From our loca-
tion at the foot of Jones Street, the lights appeared to be hovering over
Clearwater Bay, at approximately 30° elevation from the horizon.
The five lights were stationary, while a sixth red light seemed to
travel around them. As the moving light turned and moved north, a blue
(or possibly greenish-white) light seemed to replace the red.
This indi-
cated that it might be an airplane with a blue light on one wing-tip and a
red light on the other.
Most of the people in our group seemed satisfied that the lights were
either on helicopters or some other type of aircraft, or that they were part
of the Clearwater or Dunedin fireworks display. With my sister,
felt that this was improbable.
We walked to the waterfron
to get
out of the street-light, where we thought we might see better. The lights
were clearly visible from the extension of Jones Street which runs to the
water-front beside the Belvedere Apartments building.
As we watched, one of the lights (possibly the moving light) traveled
west toward the horizon and disappeared in the vicinity of a cloud bank.
By 8:40 p.m., when the fireworks ended, the lights had completely
disappeared -- not by moving away; they were just no longer there, as
if someone had flipped a light-switch and turned them off.
As we started to leave the water-front area, we noticed a small plane,
apparently a private plane, overhead, with a red light on one wing and a
blue (or white) on the other. We felt that this might be the moving light
that we had seen in the area of the five stationary lights.
We later learned, in discussing the lights with our cousin, that she
had also seen five stationary lights in the sky to the north, as they watched
fireworks from a dock on Treasure Island. We were unable to establish the
exact time, although she was reasonably sure it was between 8:30 and 9:30 p.m.
