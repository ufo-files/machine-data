Thousands See Fireball

By GARY STRANGES
Dana DeGeorge didn't get much sleep last night, what with
an interview from the Utica newspapers and a telephone call
from the New York Times.
And, it looks as though the picture he took of a fireball wing-
ing its way across the night sky will be keeping him awake
for nights to come.
Dana, 14, thinks the fireball just might be a UFO, but some
authorities say it was a meteor. (UFO is, of course, an un-
identified flying object).
The Proctor High School freshman and a friend snapped
four pictures of the fireball last night on Dana's $8 used 35
mm. camera.
THE FIREBALL, identified by a number of authorities as
a meleor, excited thousands from the Eastern Scaboard to
Ohio as it flashed across the sky.
In Utica and Central New York, the spectacle prompted a
flurry of fearful calls to police agencies and rave reviews
from a number of other onlookers.
The photo that came out, revealing a spectacular ball of
fire and tail, was beamed nationally by The Associated Press
Wirephoto network.
Dana said he is sending an enlargement of the photo that
appears in today's O-D to the National Investigation Commit-
tee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP), a private body that thinks
the Air Force isn't telling all it knows about UFOs.
"I don't really think it was a meteor," Dana said today, a
little groggy from a near-sleepless night.
"But I'in not qualified," Dana admitted, "I'm more inclined
to think it was a UFO than a meteor."

and Mrs. Ralph DeGeorge of 1200 Greenview Drive, and his
buddy, Vincent Femia, 14, of 1609 Crouse St., were working
on their scrapbook of UFO newspaper clippings.
Both boys want to be astronomers and have geared their
studies toward science.
Dana and Vincent went outside to hang around when "Vin-
