Approximately 4 rolls of #120 Tri-X Film and one 35mm X 36 exposure Tri-X Film was
available to the NICAP Investigators for examination. They had been developed by
the 'Worcester Telegram & Gezette" photo-lab and were considered to be technically

#120 film strips for supporting analysis by NICAP Investigators but said attempt
was fruitless. The one roll of film in question was the only roll of film containing
any images of interest to the naked eye, the others being apparently clear and blank.
It was the opinion of Investigator O'Neill that the images visible on the one roll of
were excellent examples of images of brighter stars resulting from a hand-held camera
exposure of from 1/2 to 1 second duration.
based his opinion on the following
points which were consistent with a star-image interpretation.
1)
All frames contain multiple traces, none of which were as much as
1/4 inch total length.
2) The multiple images of all frames were of varying density and irregularly
spaced.
3)
On all frames, the varying density traces were so configured that any and
all variations from a straight line evidenced by one trace was duplicated
in all other traces to the extent that any one trace could be superimposed
over any other trace at some point.
4) In addition, any pair of traces could have their high and low points
connected by straight lines that would be parallel.
The above conditions could only be normally met as a result of camera and film movement
during exposure of light sources in a fixed telationship to each other.
