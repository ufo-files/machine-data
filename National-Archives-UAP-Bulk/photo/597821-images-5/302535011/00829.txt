Chardon, Ohio . . . August 17, 1964, 4:00-6:00 A.M.
Mr. George Inaman and Clyde Young also observed what probably
was the same object from outside the police station on the square.
Both said that through binoculars (7 x 35) it was red on the bottom
and white on top.
Mr. Young defined it as a bright light with two
smaller lights circling around it. He watched from 4:00-4:40 A.M.
he said, but "couldn't figure out what it was."
Mr. Inaman said that without the binoculars (Mr. Young's)
the object was very bright. With the binoculars, there was a circu-
lar outline, but the surface of the object was covered by bluish-
green "light-bulb-like" surfaces.
At about 5:00 A.M. he saw two
just barely visible white objects circle the central object in a counter-
clockwise fashion.
After its second circling, the objects moved
southward a short distance and disappeared. At about 5:00 A.M.,
the object disappeared completely for about 10-15 minutes. Yet, no
clouds were noted. Mr. Inaman, a gas station attendant, of 42
years, left for home at about 6:30 A.M. The object was still visible,
and had risen to a higher angle of elevation.
Mr. Inaman wears
glasses.
Summation: Only one object of large size, the direction was
directly E, and the climb of the object was at a slight angle to the
S. Its highest elevation was 40° -45° . Duration, direction, elevation
coincide with Venus, but what about the faint, circling objects?
