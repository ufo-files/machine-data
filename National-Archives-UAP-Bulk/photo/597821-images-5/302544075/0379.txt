Dear Mr Durland,
Your letter of 31 August 1965 was forwarded by the Air National
Guard Commander at Fargo, North Dakota.
Consultation with the meteorologist leads me to believe that
the following is a plausible explanation:
"Dust devils" are described by the meteorologist as short
duration miniature dry storms which may swirl in clockwise
or counter clockwise direction. North Dakota sumers
frequently have these strong small whirlwinds.
this pheno-
menon could plausibly account for the 27 six to seven foot
diameter swirls made in the wheat fields of Veblem Township
this past August.
A clear, calm, hot dry summer afternoon
over a fat field is conducive to producing intense whirl-
winds of 5-10 foot diameter.
The whirlwind is a result of
rapidly rising parcels of air that have become lighter by
being heated at the field level. Although they have been
most frequently observed picking up dust, they probably occur
as frequently without dust.
The temporary deformation of the wheat along the rapid wind
path might contribute to later compaction of the soil.
The
wheat in the center of the whirlwind would be affected as
would that in the peripherial area.
Thank you for your observations.
Sincerely,
7Q
HECTOR QUINTANILLA, Jr, Major, USAF
Chief, Project Blue Book
