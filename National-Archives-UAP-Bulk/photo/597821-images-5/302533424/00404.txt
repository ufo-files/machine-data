Editor's Comment:
Congratulations to Mr. Tom Douglas for digging up the basic
information relating to this sighting. But now we'll help Mr. Doug
las by clarifying some facts unknown to him.
First of all, I refer him to the report submitted by an excellent
source, Ben Wolfe.
Mr. Wolfe is manager of Shady Grove Airport,
an active parachutist himself, and is in touch with parachuting
operations around the area. He told L. Moyers and myself that no
jumping took place at his Port that night. The following facts are
contained in an affidavit signed by Mr. Wolfe. It reads:
The following facts are submitted by the undersigned to refute
statements found in the June 9, 1964, issue of the newspaper called
the Wooster Daily Record.
1. No jumps were made on June 8th, 1964.
Tapes at FAA at
Akron-Canton A.P. can verify this.
In the inte
of safety, we
notify the tower at Akron-Canton Airport one to three minutes be-
fore release of parachutists.
2. As members of PCA, we follow their regulations, one of
which is that no jumps are to be made when winds are above
18 MPH.
3. Spotting your exit point is extremely tricky at night. At
25 knots it's almost impossible to pick an exit point.
Because
jumping is so extremely hazardous under those conditions, no re.
sponsible jumper would attempt it.
4. The light seen by myself and two fellow observers was of
such magnitude that it would be impossible for such a source or
beacon to be carried by a jumper.
5. We have a permanent NOTAM filed with PAA.
This notice
to Airmen is required.
This lets them know that our jumps take
place from 9:00 A.M. to 9:00 P.M. This has not been violated by
us at any time.
Larry Moyers and A. E. Candusso witnessed the signing of the
affidavit.
I have talked to persons in North Lawrence who saw the
light in the sky that night. The time correlated with the sighting in
question.

No blimp is based in Akron.
