REPORT FROM THE U.S. WEATHER BUREAU AND THE POCATELLO MUNICIPAL
AIRPORT at Pocatello Airport, Pocatello, Idaho.
Mr. Harold Jacobsen, U.S. Weather Bureau, gave the following
weather information concerning the evening of the phenomenom (15 June 1968):
The night was clear with 2/10th coverage from 2300 hrs., IST, 14 June 1968
to 0100 hrs., MST, 15 June 1958. Wind velocity in knots at surface was
200 degrees and at 6,000 fast, 10 knots. At 10,000 feet, 275 degrees at
13 knots. No further elevations were recorded. Visibility was generally
in excess of 15 miles at all times. Temperature gradient was not available
and there were no thunderstorms in the ame at that time. At 2215 hr3., MST,
(The approximate time when Simonson first noticed the phenomenom), a balloon
was launched on 1h June 1968 to determine weather conditions in the upper
atmosphere, Mr. Jacobsen further stated that there was nothing unusual
in the weather that night. The moon conditions were halfway between full
and the last quarter. The moon rose at 2349 hrs., MST, on 14 June'1968
and set at 1012 hrs., MST, 15 June 1958. Mr. Simonson stated that at the
time he observed the phenomenon he did not observe any moon in the skies.
Mr. Donald Washington, FAA and chief of control tower operations and
Mr. Jack Stoltz, Air Traffic Control Specialist stated that nothine unusual
occurred on the evening of the phenomenom.
