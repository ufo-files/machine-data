Recommendations:
1.
Instructions be issued to investigating Air Force personnel to
remain completely objective in the reporting and recording of observed
data.
The distortion of the true orientation of the two larger
footprints would have been very misleading had the photographs not
been available to show them to be located in other than symmetrical
manner•
It is suggested that an appointed member of the civil engineering
personnel of each Air Force Base be given a very brief course in
obtaining soil samples and taking soil strength data readings. Capt.
Skillen, AFIT/CEC, has suggested that this course could be given to
such personnel during their regularly scheduled periods of instruc-
tion at the Air Force Institute of Technology at WPAFB. In this
manner, a more accurate estimated weight acting on the depressions
can be made in the investigation of future reported sightings.
For instance, it is highly desirable that cone penetrometer (or other
similar tests) be performed at a number of stations in the immediate
vicinity of the depressions as well as within the depression itself.
In this manner, the strength of the undisturbed soil would be estab-
lished as well as the degree of compression of the soil under the
footprint area.
3. It is mandatory that a more accurate layout of the footprint
locations and orientation be made. Pieces of string tied between
pegs can serve as valuable reference lines. At the minimum, a
base line should be drawn by scribing a straight line in a conven-
ient location on the soil. All points would then be referenced
from this line so that an accurate diagram can be reconstructed of
the site by participating investigators. Photographs of the site
should include this base line (or Lines) as a reference.
In this
particular case, no record whatever was made of the angular rela-

handicap in the analysis of the evidence.
