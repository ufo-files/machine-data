DISCUSSION OF BACKGROUND INFORMATION
Weather conditions were such that there was haze and scattered
clouds in the area of the sighting. There was also a temperature
inversion at the 2 - 5,000 It altitude level. Stars could be
seen and this was indicated in all the AF Forms 117 submitted to
Wright-Patterson Air Force Base.
A.
1.
Sirius was the brightest astronomical object in the
skies during the period of the sightings. It has a stellar
magnitude of -1.58, has characteristic colors of blue-red-white-green,
and would have been visible in the southeastern sky during most of
the sightings. (See Atch #1)
2.
The scintillation of Sirius would have been increased
by the temperature inversion.
3. Common celestial objects when seen through haze are
often misinterpreted. (See Atch #2)
4. Clouds passing in front of the star would have caused
it to disappear and then reappear.
5. Stationary lights when seen against a black background
can appear to move. This process is called autokinesis. (See Atch #3)
B.
1. Initial report indicated a simultaneous ground visual
and air visual at about 0335 local. This was not substantiated by
the pilot's form 117. The pilot indicated he sighted an unidentified
light at about 0435 local.
