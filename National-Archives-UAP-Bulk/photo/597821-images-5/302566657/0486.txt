At 2123_05 May 67. I the undersigned did witness the following:
Upon returning to the team house, located at To Chou, from inspecting the guards
I went to the door on the west side of the house. I noticed an orange light rising
up into the sky on an approximate azimuth of 285 deg (checked by compass on this day).
I sat in the chair watching 1t, after avhile I called it's attention to Sg:
Burdish, who had just gone to bed. Sgt Burdish watched for awhile from his bed, then
coma to the door where I was.
This light traveled to an altitude of from 10-15 thousand feet, when it reached
it's peek, it started to travel to the left. It went to the left quite a ways.
distance undertermined, where it started to blink, by getting real dim then back to
it's normal brightness. After blinking a few times, it made a circle and headed
to the sane point of ascent, where it headed straight down, almost in it's same path
of ascent,
away to the right.
Wheu the first light was about quarter to half way down it seeed to explode,
however rather then shattering, it seemed to shoot three more lights downward, then
it started blowing in this direction.
SFC Carlson had come into the team house when the first light started to descend
so he and I watched the first one while Sgt Burdish kept watching the path of the
second light.
As the first light came closer it appeared to be a parachute flare, however it
passed about 10 feet above our bunker, and we saw a clear (either nylon or plastic)
cylinder about 3 feet long by 1 feet in diameter.
The original light was still
glowing in the lead end of the cylinder.
We watched it until it traveled out of sight, behind one of the houses.
appeared to have traveled outside of our perimeter, but not over the ridge behind
our camp, on the east side.
After we lost sight of the first light we went back to the door to locate the
second light.
The second light did exactly the same as the first, except it went to the right
and emitted five lights, plus a sixth which was real weak, and extinguished after
a short time.
This second light blew over the mountain on the north side of our
camp, traveling in a NNE direction.
The duration of flight (from time of observation to termination of observation)
for both lights was 30 min,
The eight lights emitted traveled straight down in the path of ascent.
It was a clear bright night with a strong westerly breez.
