November 8. 1967
Robert J. Low
University of Colorado
Department of Physics and Astrophysics
202 Woodbury Hall
Boulder, Colorado 80302
Dear Bob:
This is a very belated reply to your letter of June 14,
with an interesting report from Peterson Field at Colorado Springs.
With all my travelling I simply have not had tire until this morning
(Sunday) to go into the sighting and give it any serious consideration.
I am not able to make a definitive analysis, but at least I have some
ideas. I ran into a good many similar cases in World War II, some
much more spectacular. One of them is discussed in some depth in
my first book and you will find some of the basic theory in my test-
book, Elements of Radio Propagation, which undoubtedly you can get
from ISSA, if you do not have it in your own library.
It has all of the elements of multiple-hop or multiple-
propagation. At first I tried to see whether some combination of re-
flections involving the two aircraft could produce a ghost image in
the observed position. But in this I was unsuccessful.
If we assume that the ceiling was exactly 3,000 feet, the
Braniff plane could have produced a double echo as follows: The first
would have been a direct reflection back to traffic control. The second
would have been a reflection vertical to the ground, back to the plane,
and thence to Traffic Control. This second reflection would produce a
ghost image some 3, 000 feet behind the plane. This figure does not
correspond to the estimated two miles for the ghost target.
