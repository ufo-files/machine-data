June 20, 1967
Mr
Mirs.
Maple Shade, New Jersey 03052
Dear Mr. Mrs.
Thank you for your letter of June 15. I have no idea
what it was you saw. You have given so few facts. In the first
place, I do not in any sense believe that you saw a dirigible. I
do not believe it was any form of a ship. It had nothing whatever
to do with Cape Kennedy I am sure. And again 1 am quite sure
that the ship does not come from outer space. My only point in
writing you is to try to find out whether indeed you really saw
something. And second, what kind of stimulus it was that provoked
your reaction.
I am well aware of the mythical air ship of 1395-97.
And indeed your description so fits the sightings of that period that
I feel there is definitely some sort of relationship. I have discussed
these sightings in both of my books, "Fiying Sucers, " published by
the Harvard University Press in 1953, and in "The World of Flying
Saucers, " by Donald H. Menzel and Lyle G. Boyd, published by
Doubleday. The first you may be able to get in your library, The
second one is still in print and available at your booksellers. It is
well recognized that the 1890-97 sightings were a combination of
hoaxes and natural phenomena, viewed with the imagination of the
various people.
Your descriptions are too detailed, too pat. If some-
thing of this sort had been in the area we should have had hundreds
of reports, not just a few.
We do not know whether the sky was clear or cloudy
or many other things. One explanation that comes to my mind is
that a bright searchlight on clouds might have produced something
similar to what you reported.
Sincerely yours,
