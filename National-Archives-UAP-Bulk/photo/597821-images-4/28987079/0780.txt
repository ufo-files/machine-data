Subject: Planet Jupiter
Reference:
Encyclopedia Americanna Vol 16, 1956 Edition and Encyclopedia
Britannica Vol 13, 1956 Edition.
Description: With the exception of the Sun, Jupiter is the largest member
of the Solar System. Since its orbit lies outside that of the earth, it is
possible to be observed above the horizon of the earth all night long.
Jupiter's shape is elliptical with a polar flattening of about 1/15 its diameter.
The albedo of Jupiter is 44% which means a high reflectivity of incident
light making it often the brightest object in the skies. The predominate color
is yellow with several nearly parallel bands of varying widths, which are
at times red-brown, neutral grey or even bluish in color. A slight darkening
of the limb farthest from the sun can be observed.
