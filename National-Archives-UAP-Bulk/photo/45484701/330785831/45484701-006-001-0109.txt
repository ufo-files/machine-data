SUBJECT:
12 June 1952
Aerial Phenomenon Reportedly Sighted, South Omaha, Nebr.,
2304 hours, 9 June 1952
DETAILS:
1. On 11 June 1952, T/Sgt WILLIAM CATLETT,
AF-17155500, Hq. & Ha
S9, Strategic Air Command, furnished this agent
the following information
concerning subject incident:
At 2304 hours, 9 June 1952, CATLETT, accompanied by his
wife, Mrs. PHYLLIS JOAN CATLETT, observed a glowing red light hovering
in the sky to the south of their residence, 1709 Maaison Ave., south
Omaha, Nebr. Mrs. CATLETT first observed the light then called her
husband.
CATLETT estimated the length of time the object was in
view was from four (4) to five (5) minutes. Initially, CATLETT thought
the object was a clearance light on a plane but, after observing it,
noted that instant light was too bright for a clearance light.
According to CAILET!, the light seemed suspended in the
air for approximately three (3) minutes, after which it fell slowly
downward out of sight.
d.
CATLETT compared the size of instant light to that of
"an over-sized star" and described it as being of "disc shape".
He
noticed no evidence of propulsion or exhaust from the object, which
appeared to him to fall with a slow motion.
The object was observed in a south/southwest direction
from the CATLETT residence.
CATLETT advised that there were a number
of T-ll type aircraft in flight in the area, and he believed the object
could easily have been observed from these planes.
As CATLETT recalled, the weather on the night of 9 June
1952 had been very clear, bright, and warm, with stars prevalent.
20
CATLETT expressed the opinion that the phenomenon he and his
wife observed was not the result of natural causes such as the falling
of a meteor or star.
