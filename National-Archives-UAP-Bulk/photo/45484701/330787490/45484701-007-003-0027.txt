-Vallejo Times-Herald, Wed., June 21, 1950
AIR FORCE SPOTS
BAY FLYING DISC
Saucer Seen
Five Times
At Hamliton
A disc-shaped object roaring
at between 1000 and 1500 miles
an hour made at least five passes
over Hamilton Air Force Base,
Marin County, early
today.
Three trained Air Force men
reported the incident.
It was first seen by Corp. Roger
G. Pryor, a control tower operator,
who said he saw a blue flame
shooting
out
as
the
"saucer"
flashed
by from the southwest
and headed toward the northeast
at about 2000 feet elevation.
'STAR' KEEPS GOING
"I thought it was a falling star,
but it didn't fall-it just kept on
going," Pryor said.
The first pass
occurred
at
1:35 a.m., Pryor said. The object
was going so fast that he couldn't
see it on the subsequent passes.
His observation
was verified,
however, by Staff Sgt.
Ellis R.
Lorimer, another control tower
operator,
and Staff Sgt.
Virgil
Cappuro, of the Airways Commu-
nications staff, as it returned from
the, northwest for
another pass
north of the field.
OBJECT DESCRIBED
All three said the object ap-
peared circular, thick in the center
and tapering to the edges.
Its speed was so great that its
size could not be estimated
The three airmen used binocu-
lars as the disc made three more
passes in the same area. It was
accompanied
by
a roar
like
thunder.
The blue flame looked like an
acetylene torch, they said
On at least three passes, it ap-
peared to be directly over the
Hamilton beacon, which is just
north of the field.
The sky was clear, though fog
prevailed elsewhere in the Bay
area.
