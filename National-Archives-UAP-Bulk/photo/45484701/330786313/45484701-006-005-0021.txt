2.
On 21 July 1952, Nr. KENNWEH V. DAVIS, 1504 Candelaria Road,
Placitas, New Mexico, was interviewed in the Hilton Hotel, Albuquerque,
New Mexico, and stated substantially as followe:
About 2155 hours, last Friday night, 18 July 1952, my daughter
and I were lying in the front yard of my home at Placitas, New Mexico,
serving the Constellation Corona Borealis. About in the center of thi
Constellation, there appeared a yellowish-orange glowing object which was
moving at a tremendous rate of speed. From our place of observation
Corona Borealis was almost directly overheed and this object moved
directly to the Hast for about 15 or 20 seconds, made a 90° turn South
for about 5 seconds, turned 90° West and remained on that course for 2 or
3 seconds, when it again turned 90° South and stayed on that course till
out of sight. The object did not appear to flame out, nor did it make any sound
that was audible to us. I have no idea as to its size or shape; however, it
appeared more brilliant than a star and to be flying on the same level as the
stars. Its altitude appeared to remain constant. My daughter and I had been
out observing the stars for about one half (s) hour prior to sighting this
object, and our eyes were accustomed to the dark. The entire period of our
observation of this object was about one (1) mimite. It was not a star or
meteor, as we see falling stars and meteors almost every night there. I have
not seen this object since, although I have been watching for a similar
occurrence.
Mr. DAVIS advised that during the war he was a Navigation,
Meteorology and Trigonometry Instructor for the Army Air Corps at Long Beach,
California, and was familiar vith the Constellations and their positions in
the sky, and also the action of meteors, constellations and falling stars.
3. An Aeronautical Chart was furnished Nr. DAVIS, which he used to
