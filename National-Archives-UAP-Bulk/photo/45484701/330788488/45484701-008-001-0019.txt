Investigation initiated by District Commander, DO #1, Elmendorf
Alaska, upon receipt of report from HENRY FLECHSING, Indian, Alaska,
that several employees of the J.J.Badraun Construction Company claimed to
have seen an unidentified object between Indian, Alaska, and Hope, Alaska,
located in Turnagain Arm, at 1700 hours, 29 July 1950,
AT INDIAN, ALASKA:
At 0730 hours, 30 July 1950, HANRY FLECHSING was interviewed at
the site where the object was reportedly seen.
He stated that DONALD MAY,
Mr. PENNER, Mr. TALBOT, LOCKE JACOBS, CECIL DORNING and OLE OLANDER, all
employees of Badraun Construction Company, reported having seen the object,
described to be from one hundred (100) to two hundred (200) feet long, of
mound shape, projected from twenty (20) to thirty (30) feet into the air
and located two (2) or three (3) miles from point of view. FLECHSING
further stated that he immediately proceeded to the position where this
object had been seen, arriving at approximately 1730 hours (one-half hour
after the object was first seen), however, the object had disappeared from
sight.
3. On 30 July 1950, the writer interviewed the individuals named in
preceding paragraph and they gave substantially the same information as
had FLECHSING.
They further stated that the object re-appeared on the
morning of 30
July 1950 and all agreed that the object they saw at 1700
hours 29 July 1950 was the object presently in view to these individuals
and to the writer during this interview.
At approximately 0830 hours 30 July 1950, the writer viewed the
object in question while standing on the Indian side of Turnagain Arm.
At this time, dense clouds were settled on the water between the object.
and Hope, Alaska, which is located across the Arm from Indian, These
clouds offered a suitable background to make the object visable, however,
not identifiable. As the clouds lifted, the object progressively commenced
to disappear. When the clouds completely lifted, thus making buildings
docated at Hope, Alaska, visable, the object completely disappeared from
view, a result of having blended into the mountainous coastline' on the
Hope side.
