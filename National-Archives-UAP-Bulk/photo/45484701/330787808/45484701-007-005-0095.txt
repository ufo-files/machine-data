AT PORTLAND, OREGON
On 28 June 1949, Mr. HUGH M. MARQUIS, 2240 S. E. 59th Avenue, Port-
land, Oregon, was interviewed in this office and advised that on 23 June 1949
at about 2100 hours (PDT), as he was driving his car west on Division Street and
near S. E. 102d Avenue, accompanied by his wife and children, he noticed a flash
of light in the sky due west of him over Portland. Upon closer observation, he
noticed an undeterminable number of objects in a formation similar to a flight
of geese. He advised that the objects were traveling horizontally in a southerly
direction at an estimated 15° angle above the horizon at the same altitude as
. the lower clouds, which he estimated at 2000 feet. He stopped his car and
observed these objects for a period of three or four minutes. During his
observation, he noticed that the objects were of no apparent color, appeared to
change shape as they moved, and the entire group was about the size of an object
2½ inches in diameter held at arm's length.
They maintained a straight course
with no apparent maneuverability nor exhaust trail. They passed behind or through
a small cloud and reappeared on the other side. The only light observed was
the initial flash, which he believed to be the reflection of the sun upon the
objects. There were no visible means of support or motivation. Mr. MARQUIS
estimated their speed at approximately 75 or 80 miles per hour.
He could hear
no sound relative to the objects; however, the motor of his car was running. The
objects disappeared from view behind a hill. They were first sighted through
the windshield of the car, but after stopping, he observed them through the
open window of the left door. The observers' position at the time of sighting
was 45° 30' 16" North Latitude and 122° 33' 26" West Longitude.
