When it was near the zenith, the area of the light became apparent.

they used to be, but I thought that I could detect that its leading edge was
olearly defined, whereas its trailing edge was a little vaguer and sonewhat

pipe of some sort.
The inexplicable thing about it to me was the question of its speed.
It seemed to hold a constant speed, which was comparable to that of a transport
plane at medium altitude - say 300 mph at 6,000 feet. But if it was actually at
25,000 feet, at which the engine might have been inaudible, an angular velocity
equivalent to 300 mph at 6,000 feet would be on the order of 1,500 mph at 25,000
foot.
Moreover, I haven't seen any kind of airplane that trailed a luminous
flame big enough to be seen from the ground at such an altitude.
I forgot to mention that the color of the flame was distinctly yellowish.
It made quite a contrast with the bluish star Vega, which was not far from its path.
