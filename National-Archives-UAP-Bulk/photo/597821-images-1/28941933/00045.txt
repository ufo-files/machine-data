Memorandum for Record
SUBJECT: Trip to George AFB on 20-23 May 1952
On 17 May 1952, It E. J. Ruppelt called Major Vincent Wolrath, Wing Intelligence
Officer of the 146Highter Bomber Wing at George ArB, Calif. Major Wolrath stated
that due to the recent flurry of sightings he deemed it very advisable to send some-
one from ATIC to George AFB.
It Ruppelt had orders cut and departed by comercial
air on 20 May 1952 arriving at George AFB late on the evening of the 20th.
On 2l May 1952, It Ruppelt contacted Major Wolrath who reviewed sightings
on (1-9-11-13-1% and. 20 May.
Several of these sightings from a T-o, seemed to be
repetative and plans were made to attempt to observe the object on the afternoon
of 2l May.
It Melzer, who had piloted the I-6, during previous observations was con-
tacted. He offered to fly that P.M. and take It Ruppelt along as observer and to
assist in setting up an observation team.. Both the Ground Control Intercept
(Radar) and Fighter Interceptor squadron of ADC were contacted and briefed on the
sightings and plans to observe from a I-6. They agreed to stand by all afternoon
on "D' channel of VHF in case the object was sighted, If it was, F-86's would be
scrambled and radar would attempt a pick-up. A ground station and a ground observer
team headed by Major Wolrath was equipped with a ground VHF set and would have a
telescope to make ground observations from the George AFB line. Weather was
alerted in case a theodolite was needed. All Vi sets were set up for "D" channel
on a special frequency to keep the interception of messages to a minimun.
At 1330 PIST, the I-6 was airborne. It Ruppelt had a Lieca with a 135 mm
elephoto lens in case photos could be taken. At 1430 PiST, the ground obser-
ration team called the T-6 and stated they were observing the object through a
telescope and it appeared to be a balloon. Also it was identical to several of the pre-
vious reports as previous observers were on the ground watching. The aerial search
was contimed with no success and at 1440 PDST the object disappeared. The aerial
search was broken off and the I-6 landed.
It Ruppelt contacted Edwards AFB and was informed that at 1438 PDST a Rawin-
sonde balloon from their station burst at 75,0001-80,000' SSE of their station
(approximately over George AFB).
It was concluded that the object was this balloon,
On 22 May 1952, the entire day was spent interviewing sources or other
sightings. It will be noted that although some of the reports were attributed
to balloons, others could not be and are still being investigated.
It Ruppelt returned to W-P AFB on 23 May.
The cooperation received at George AFB was excellent.
