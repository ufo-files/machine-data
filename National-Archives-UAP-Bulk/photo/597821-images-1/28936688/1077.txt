HONOLULU AIRPORT, BALLOON WITH RADIOSONDE ATTACHED WILL BE RELEASED FROM
HONOLULU AIRPORT AT 1635R.
EXPECTED TO REACH 10000 FEET AT 1645HM
Mr. ROBERT L. TOLFF, Meteorological Aide, US Weather Bureau, Honolulu
Airport Station, reported, 30 September 1949, that a radiosonde balloon had
been released by that facility at 1636 hours, Hawaiian time, 30 September 1949
(02362 1 October 49) and was in flight for forty-eight (48) minutes; flight
terminating by burst of balloon.. WOLFF reported that the balloon involved was a
helium filled, white color, pure gum rubber sphere which was inflated to a dia-
meter of 7 feet. Suspended 50 feet from the balloon was a radiosonde transmitter
in a box approximately 4" x 10" x 10".VAttached to the radiosonde transmitter
was an aerial three (3) feet in length
Midway between the transmitter and -
balloon was a paper parachute, thres (3) feet in dianeter, so contrived that
when the balloon burst, the parachute permitted the transmitter to glide, rather
than plummet, to the earthe
The balloon used on this date was white in color,
so selected because of the clear day and the color reflecting the sunlight, er-
abling its flight to be followed with ease through an optical instrument know
as the theodelite. Three (3) colors of balloons are employed by the local Weather
Bureau according to WOLFF, white for use on the bright clear day; yellow for use
in haze; and black for use on a cloudy day. Rate of ascent of balloon is dependent on
winds; however, the average rate is roughly 1000 feet per minute. As the balloon rises,
it expands in atmospheric pressure, reaching diameter of from 12 to 18 feet, accord.
ing to WOLFF. WOLFF further advised that if atmospheric conditions are favorable,
it is possible to pick out and follow a balloon at a haight of 45,000 feet. After
consulting a Winds Aloft Computation Sheet prepared from the flight of this balloon,
Mr WOLFF expressed the opinion that the unidantified flying object observed could
well have been the balloon released by the weather Bureau, as this balloon was in
flight during the time and followed the path described, Mr. WOLFF further advised
that the balloon was "tracked" by Ir. W. A. THRUGOOD, Meteorological Aide, US
Weather Bureau, Honolulu Airport Station.
