At 1215 hours CST, 2h December 1952, while conducting a flight test of an
Automatic Cunlaying Radar set similar to one half of an AN/APG-Ll equipped with
automatic target acquisition (short title is either ATA or ALO) installed in the nose
of the armament flight test 8-35 No. 44-92054, an unseen phenomenon was detected and
tracked intermittently by the ATA of the AGL Radar set for a period of approximately
30 minutes. The phenomenon was also detected at the same time and approximate
position in space by the AN/APS-23 navigation radar installed on the B-36.
The B-35
aircraft had completed a portion of a flight test plan which called for simulated
attacks by an P-8h fighter. On 24 December two fighters (T-8h's) participated in the
simulated attack, and they had left the area a few minutes earlier to return to their
base. The fighters were being used to evaluate the ATA portion of the AGI, Radar set.
The AGI radar operator had complained of what he thought was noise.
It later became
apparent that this "noise" was the same phenomenon as was detected and tracked a few
minutes'later by both radar sets. The flight plan and report are attached as
inclosures 1 and 2.
2.

a. The phenomenon was detected by both radar sets.
b.

of the B-36 during descent from 40,000 to 15,000 feet was unexplainable, and
seemed to indicate that something intelligent was guiding it.
