STAT SHENT
The following statement is submitted in accordance with Paragraph
79, AF Letter 200-5 dated 29 April 1952.
I, Capt. Robert B. Bailey, the pilot of a C-47, on a course of
h3 degrees, 7000' altitude and at an indicated airspeed of 170 mots,
noticed an unusual object in the vicinity of the Vichy Radio Range
Station, which approximated a circular light which, from way position,
appeared to be approximately one foot in diameter.
The light of varying
intensity appeared to be on a collision-interception course from
approximately 233 degrees. I turned on my landing lights to warn
the object of my presence and notified the co-pilot and Crew Chief
of my sighting.
They corroborated my observation and watched as the
object appeared to position itself approximately one mile off the
leit wing or at 328 degrees. The light varied in intensity and color
from red through amber to green. It remained visible for 5-10 minutes
during which time it dropped back, relative to the aircraft, then
increased its speed and made three dives and zooms on a course
parallel to that of the aircraft before disappearing. The Crew
Chief and a seaman passenger reported the light disappeared upward
at a right angle to the previous course. I executed a CIRVIS
in-flight report to the Vichy Radio Range Station.
ROBERT B. BAILEY
Captain, USAF
