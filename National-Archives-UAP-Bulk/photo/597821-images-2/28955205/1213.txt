AMERICAN AIRLINES
CASIE ADDRESS AMAIR
LA GUARDIA FIELD • NEW YORK AIRPORT STATION, N. Y. • HAVEMEYER 6-8900
June 29, 1953
Chief Air Tactical Intelligence
Attention:
ATIAA - 2C
Wright - Patterson AFB
Dayton, Ohio.
Dear Sir:
In accordance with a request submitted by Flight Service
through the New York Air Route Traffic Control Center, Amarican Airlinea
Captain D. W. Kelly has submitted the following statement concerning an
observation which he made while operating American Airlines flight 359
between Boston and La Guardia, on June 24, 1953:
"Tlight 359 was just north of Noonsocket at 4,000 feet on
a compass course of 247 degrees.
At 2121 Eastern Standard Time a large
flash of light was seen in the sky to the south.
Bearing to the light
was approximately 190 degrees.
The flash appeared to have occurred at
an estimated altitude of 14,000 feet. The light then broke into two
separate lights, and the light farthest west broke up into two separate
Lights, one much larger than the other.
All lights fell seaward and
were extinguished immediately upon reaching the surface.
I estimate it
took between one and one and one-half minutes for the objects to fall.
"I can only guess, but it is my observation the objects
fell into the Sound in an area bounded by westerly, Rhode Island; Block
Island and Montauk Point."
If we may be of further assistance concerning this incident,
please advise.
Youra very truly,
AMBRICAN AIRLINES, ING.
Bowman
A. B. Borman,
Supervisor of Flight Dispatch
ABB:et
Director Intelligence Headquarters
USAF
Washington 25, D. C.
