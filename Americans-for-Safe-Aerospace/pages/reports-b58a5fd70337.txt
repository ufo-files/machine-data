May 1, 2024
Published account
COM-2024-128
Departing Johannesburg, South Africa in Boeing 737-800, abeam Vaal Dam climbing through FL290.
While climbing through FL290 departing Johannesburg, South Africa, a commercial airline captain and first officer observed a delta-shaped craft moving directly toward their Boeing 737-800 in clear conditions. The object, described as a perfectly equilateral triangle approximately half the size of their aircraft, passed overhead within an estimated 200 feet at high velocity on an opposite heading. The craft displayed intense white/gold luminosity underneath that faded to metallic coloring, with what appeared to be a field around it giving the impression of atmospheric reentry effects. No traffic alerts were displayed on cockpit systems, no TCAS warnings activated, and ATC reported no radar contact.

Off Coast of South Korea
A Department of Defense contractor with active TS/SCI clearance recorded multiple UAP events using US UAS electro-optical and infrared sensors off the coast of South Korea. Across five videos, the contractor captured several distinct objects: a smooth white seamless TicTac-shaped UAP, a rust-colored peanut-shaped UAP, and another two-meter TicTac observed in both EO and IR modes. Context matters
Understanding the record.
Clear distinctions help every reader assess an account responsibly.
What does a published report establish?
It records what a witness reported and what ASA chose to make public. Descriptions, estimated distances, and reported behavior should be read as witness accounts unless separately supported.
How should I interpret the supporting material?
Witness videos, recordings, illustrations, and analyst notes serve different purposes. Read each attachment with its caption and context. An illustration depicts a recollection; it is not a photograph of the event.
How is witness privacy handled?
ASA publishes de-identified accounts. For questions about sharing your own observation, use ASA’s
confidential reporting channel
.
