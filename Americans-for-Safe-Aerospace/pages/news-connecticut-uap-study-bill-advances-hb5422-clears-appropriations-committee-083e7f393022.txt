Connecticut's bipartisan UAP research bill took a significant step forward today as the Appropriations Committee voted HB5422 out of committee, sending the legislation to the House floor for a full vote. Americans for Safe Aerospace submitted testimony in support when the bill was first heard in March.
The bill, authored by Rep. Joe Hoxha (R-Bristol) and cosponsored by Rep. Aundre Bumgardner (D-Groton), would direct a study examining the benefits of establishing a dedicated state center for UAP research, and the resources required to operate one. The legislation reflects growing momentum at the state level to apply rigorous scientific methodology to a topic that has gained increasing attention from federal authorities.
Key Amendments
As the bill moved through committee, sponsors worked with stakeholders to strengthen the legislation:
New Study Partner: The study will now be conducted by the Connecticut Academy of Science and Engineering (CASE) rather than the University of Connecticut as originally proposed. CASE, established by the Connecticut General Assembly in 1976 and modeled after the National Academy of Sciences, provides expert guidance on science and technology issues to state government. The organization's independence and established track record conducting policy-relevant research for the state makes it well-suited to lead this effort.
Data Sharing Provisions: The bill's language has been amended to address concerns raised by the Department of Emergency Services and Public Protection (DESPP) regarding data sharing protocols. The amendments reflect careful consideration of how to balance research objectives with legitimate public safety and security considerations. The revised language has not yet been posted publicly, we will update this article with specifics once the amended text is available, or you can check the Connecticut General Assembly website for the latest version of HB5422.
What's Next
With the committee vote behind it, HB5422 now advances to the House floor. The bill's path forward will depend on support from House leadership and rank-and-file members.
Americans for Safe Aerospace will continue to monitor HB5422's progress and provide updates as the bill moves through the legislative process.
Not in Connecticut? Start the Conversation in Your State
Connecticut's progress on HB5422 follows New Jersey's landmark UAP research law, which went into effect in January. As more states take action, momentum is building for a coordinated, science-driven approach to understanding UAP.
If you're interested in bringing similar legislation to your state, Americans for Safe Aerospace can help. ASA provides education and guidance for constituents and lawmakers looking to establish UAP research initiatives at the state level. Whether you're a concerned citizen, a pilot who has witnessed unexplained phenomena, or a legislator exploring policy options, we're here to support your efforts.
