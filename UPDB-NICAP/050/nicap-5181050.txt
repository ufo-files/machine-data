Animal Reaction Feature:

During the time that 5 flat bottomed, spinning domed objects
were hovering 12 to 18 inches above the grass, cattle in a nearby field were
reported to have been bellowing and to have broken through a fence in four
places.1 [Observations of the objects
and of the cattle were made independently by different people. The direction in
which the cattle were moving relative to the objects was not stated.1jw]

1

The sighting:

At 10 a.m. on an overcast Sunday morning, while working a
swather in a field, 36-year old Edwin Fuhr noticed a metal dome 50 feet away in
a grassy area.1 He got off the swather
and walked to within 15 feet of a flat-bottomed dome that was 12-15 feet off
the ground and spinning very fast.1 The
grass was moving at the base of the object.1
Fuhr backed up to his swather, which was still running, and climbed into
the seat where he saw four more identical objects, all with the grass moving at
the base as they spun.1 He could see
what appeared to be grooves of a slightly darker color on the spinning dome but
could see no other surface details.1
Later measurements of marks in the grass indicated the diameters of the
domes were roughly 10 feet.

The objects ascended in a step formation to about 200 feet
where they stopped, a 6 foot long puff of dark gray 1vapor1 came from an
exhaust like extension on the base of each.1
Following the appearance of the vapor was a downward gust of wind.1 The objects remained in a perfect line for
1-2 minutes, then suddenly ascended into low clouds.

Because of the noise of the swather engine, Fuhr did not
know if there was sound associated with the domes.

EM effects are questionable.1 When Fuhr returned and tried to put the swather into gear and to
move the steering wheel, but he could not, but he admits it could have been his
fear rather than a case of the swather not operating properly.

No physiological effects were reported.1 Physical traces were left in the grass.

Langenburg

On
September 18, 1974, J. Allen Hynek received a newspaper clipping about the
Langenburg incident from a correspondent in Winnipeg, Manitoba.1 He conferred with Ted Phillips, then began
making inquiries in Saskatchewan about the case.1 Because it seemed like a good case, arrangements were made for
Phillips to travel to Langenburg on September 21.1 Phillips interviewed the witness, Edwin Fuhr, as well as Fuhr1s
mother and the investigating RCMP officer, Ron Morier.

1111 The summary of the event is as follows,
from Phillips1 report:

Shortly
after 10 on Sunday morning, September 1, 1974, Edwin Fuhr, a 36-year-old
farmer, began harvesting his rape crop in a field located some 1,500 ft. south
of his home.1 After Fuhr had been
swathing for nearly an hour he was closing on a slough located at the south end
of the field.1 As he approached the
slough he slowed the swather to a crawl, looked up to check his position
relative to the grassy area and saw a metal dome about 50 ft. away, sitting in
the grassy area between the slough and the crop area.1 At first he thought it was a metal goose blind.1 He stopped the swather and walked to within
15 ft. of the object.

1111 As he approached the metal dome he
noticed that the grass around the base was moving and the object itself was
spinning at a high rate of speed.1 Fuhr
became quite frightened and backed away toward the swather which was still
running at full throttle.

1111 When he reached the swather he moved
behind it and climbed up to the seat.1
At this point he could see the spinning dome from a slightly higher
elevation.1 As he glanced to the left he
saw four more metal domes, all the same size and all were spinning.1 They seemed to be hovering 12 to 18 inches
above ground level.1 The grass was
moving at the base of each object.1 Two
of the objects (3 & 4) were very near each other.1 The objects were arranged in a rough semi-circle around the
slough.1 He could hear no sound as the
swather was running.

1111 As he watched he could see what appeared
to be grooves of a slightly darker color on the spinning dome; no other surface
details were seen.1 Fuhr tried to push
the swather into gear but couldn1t move the throttle or the steering wheel.1 He admits that he can1t be sure if he was
just weak from extreme fear or if the swather was not reaction properly to the
touch.

1111 After watching for what seemed to be
several minutes, the objects suddenly burst into the air.1 The near object (1) left the ground first,
followed by the remaining four (5 ascending last).1 The objects ascended in a step formation into the overcast
sky.1 Suddenly, at about 200 ft. they
stopped, a puff of dark gray 1vapor1 was seen coming from exhaust-like
extensions located at the base of each of the objects.1 The vapor was about 6 ft. in length and was
followed by a downward gust of wind which almost knocked Fuhr1s hat off and

suddenly ascended into the low cloud cover and disappeared.1 Fuhr remained on the swather for at least 2

1111 Fuhr learned later that cattle in a

1111 After the ascent, Fuhr went to the
