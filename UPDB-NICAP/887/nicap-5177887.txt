Brad Sparks:
July 29 [28?], 1952.  20 miles W of Port Huron,
Mich (at 43.01 N, 82.81 W).
9:40-10 p.m. (EST).  One of 3 USAF F-94B's on an
ECM exercise at 9,000-9,500 ft from 61st FIS at
Selfridge AFB climbed to 20,000 ft on a 2701 heading
when it was vectored to a UFO headed S [or SE?] at 625
mph from Saginaw Bay by GCI air defense radar
[apparently FPS-3, Port Austin AFS, Mich.] (callsign
1Avenger1) [tracked about 7 mins evidently].
Ground radar told pilot Capt. Edward J. Slowinski
(Sloan) to look at his 3 o1clock low position for a
target (to the N), but found nothing, then told to look
at 3 o1clock high (radar man recalled 1low1 then 1high,1
pilot said he was told 1high1 then 1low1).  F-94
turned right to pursue [~9:47 p.m.].  Object
suddenly reversed course with a tight 1801 turn back N
on ground radar scope [evidently at 300 mph to match
F-941s speed, in a visible loop on the radar scope on a
right turn paralleling the F-941s right turn but
tighter].  As the F-94 continued right turn, radar
observer Lt. Victor Helfenbein picked up target at 4
miles range on APG-33 airborne radar, level with jet
altitude, at 601 relative or 2 o1clock (about 3301 to
3601 azimuth depending on how far into the turn) (pilot
said Helfenbein reported 2:30 o1clock).  Airborne
radar contact made [for possibly 20 secs during the
turn] then at dead ahead 12 o1clock position radar got
lockon for 30 secs until target 1jumped lock1 when it
apparently almost doubled its 4-mile [or 4-5 mi]
distance in one sweep of the ground radar accelerating
to 1,400 mph average speed [4-mile jump in 10-sec sweep
of radar, thus reaching peak 2,600 mph at about 20 g1s].
Jet briefly put on afterburner to try to close
distance with object on 3601 heading at 21,000 ft
increasing speed with afterburner to about 350 knots IAS
(about 490 knots TAS or 560 mph) [for about 3 mins?],
but object would put on a burst of speed and pull away
from the jet.  F-94 pilot first saw multiple lights
ahead as if from a jet aircraft, but no exhaust or
trail, and followed the GCI vectoring to target ahead
between 12 o1clock and 1 o1clock positions.  Object
appeared 1many times larger than a star1 then 1took on a
reddish tinge, and slowly began to get smaller, as if it
were moving away,1 and changed color from reddish then
bluish-green then white then red again in sequence (both
crew members in agreement) low on the horizon to the N
(possibly the star Capella and unrelated to radar
target, though Helfenbein was an expert celestial
navigator since 1943 with 1,400 flying hours and had
never seen anything 1like this before1; also another
F-94 followed same N route about 10:30 p.m. with
Capella still visible but did not see it as unusual or
anything else). F-94 continued N heading
[for about 5 mins] at about 300 mph as object maintained
lead at 6-10 miles range, with GCI telling F-94 crew
they were not gaining on the target on scope.
Chase ended with F-94 about 5 miles N of peninsula
(map shows 10 mi ENE of Burnt Cabin Point at 441071 N,
821451W) return due to low fuel, object then slowed to
200-300 mph before disappearing after another 1-2 mins.
