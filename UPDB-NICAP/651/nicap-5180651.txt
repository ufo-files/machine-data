Flock of turkeys react to orange-red hemisphere or domed discForm
97-AR
Date: Tuesday, 26 Nov 2005

From: Joan Woodward, Animal Reaction Specialist
Subject: Stover
MO, August 31, 1969

Cat:
4
To: NICAP

Animal Reaction
Feature:

At about 1:30 AM on a calm clear night, a man and his wife
heard their flock of turkeys reacting in an alarmed manner as if a predator were
in the area [no information about whether the turkeys were in a coop or in an
outside pen.1jw]1 Looking out, they saw
an orange-red hemisphere or domed disc at an elevation of about 60 degrees from
the horizon [no other distances estimated, although the Skylook editor
implies this was a light high in the sky1jw].1
The object flew to the NW with a swinging motion from side-to-side.
Duration of the sighting was estimated as 3-4 minutes.

No sound was reported.1
Witnesses suggested the clamor of the turkeys prevented them from
hearing any sound.1 No EM effects and no
physiological effects reported.1
[Duration of the turkeys1 response was not reported.1jw]

(copied below):

UFO Scares Turkeys

1111 Mr. and Mrs.
Smith (real name in our files) were awakened about 1:30 in the morning of
August 31st by an alarm given by their flock of turkeys, and they
rushed to the bedroom window to see if some predator was attacking the
birds.1 To their surprise, they saw a
round, orange-red object moving across the sky at two o1clock position,
considering 12 o1clock to the directly overhead.

1111 Mrs. Smith, who
was interviewed by your editor, said the object was about the size of a
volleyball, as compared to the size of the full moon, and it was almost flat
across the bottom and rounded over the top.1
They could observe no other lights or distinguishing features and there
was no smoke or vapor trail.1 The object
remained in view for three or four minutes and it seemed to swing from side to
side 1like if you hit a ball hanging on a string1 as it moved in a
north-westerly course.1 There was no
sound1however, the Smiths did not go outside and the clamor set up by the
turkeys would have covered any lesser sound.1
The night was calm with stars shining.

1111 The Smiths live
about seven miles from Stover , Mo., off Highway 135.1 They are not certain the glowing object was the cause of the
turkeys1 fright, but there seemed to be nothing else in the vicinity to account
for it.

1111 It is known that
dogs can hear high pitched sounds that human ears cannot, and we wonder if this
is also true of fowls.1 It doesn1t seem
that a high, moving light would alarm the birds, but a sound could.1 Can any of our readers offer information on
this score?
