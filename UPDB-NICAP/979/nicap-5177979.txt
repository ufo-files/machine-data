Midway Airport 40 targets flying in miscellaneous directionsBrad
Sparks:
Sept. 2, 1952. Midway Airport, Chicago, lllinois
(BBU 2025)
12:01-7 a.m. (CST). CAA radar controllers Robert
L. Terneuzen (GCA), Ralph L. Frick, Dale E.
Warner, Warren J. Weber, and Radar Mainenance Technician
Gordon R. Coplenad, at Midway Airport tracked as many as
30 targets simultaneously, flying in various directions,
1average1 speed of 175 mph at about 2,000 ft ASL.
ADC 755th AC&W radar station, Williams Bay /
Elkhorn, Wisc., claimed Midway Airport Tower Supervisor
called at 2:50 a.m. saying there were 40 targets plotted
by Midway Airport radar from 3,000 to 6,000 ft, speed
120-150 mph, azimuths 1801 to 3001 (Midway Tower claimed
the 755th was not alerted by Midway until 5:14 a.m., and
that it was indirect, through Chicago ARTC).
Targets were the size of blips from 1light planes1 or
larger (best target quality in the 6-10 mi range) and
moved in no particular pattern except, sometimes
1erratic1 and sometimes in straight lines up to 15 miles
long and in at least one instance of 1flying formation1
with an aircraft. At about 4 a.m. [CST], 2 targets
flew in formation with an inbound DC-4 airliner, one on
each wing of the DC-4 about 1 mi away (the precise
minimum distance for the PAR-1 elevation / height-finder
radar beam1s 3.61 azimuth resolution, 0.50 mi at 8 mi
distance, to prevent the 3 blips merging into one
blip). Wing formation started from near Lake Shore
[about 8 miles away] until 1 mile out on final approach
when each target flew off from the DC-4 at the same
time, in opposite directions, as if controlled [possible
hoax prank by ADC 4706th fighters]. Two F-86
fighters, piloted by Capt. (later Col.) William W.
Maitland and Lt. Beverly J. Dunjill (latter returned
from 100 combat missions in Korea as a combat flight
instructor, test pilot, and 62nd FIS Ops Ofcr), 62nd
FIS, 4706th Defense Wing, O1Hare AFB / Airport, Chicago,
scrambled by ADC from O1Hare AFB at 5:30 a.m. [CST],
took off at 5:50 a.m., were under Midway Airport control
searching the area from 5:55 to 6:19 a.m. and were
repeatedly vectored 1through1 targets at 800 to 4,000 ft
altitude attempting to 1ram1 or 1hit1 them without
seeing anything visually [apparently the F-861s carried
no airborne radar]. In fact this is misleading
since the 37-foot wingspan F-86 jets were much smaller
than the roughly 1 mile radar targets on the screen and
could not possibly be 1directed1 more precisely than
that 1 mile region (the F-86 radar blips themselves were
about 1 mile in size thus not precise in locating the
F-861s either). Overcast cloud layer at 4,700 ft,
sunrise at ground level about 5:18 a.m. CST. ADC
755th AC&W air defense radar in Elkhorn / Williams
Bay, Wisc. (421371011N, 881321191W) did not detect
targets on its CPS-6B, but from ~70 miles away the
earth1s curvature, beam elevation and ground clutter
would block radar detection of targets below about 5,000
ft near Midway Airport. Note: Teletype
report by 4706th Defense Wing on Sept 10-11 apparently
reports all event times erroneously as 1CDST1 that
should be 1CST.1 (Sparks; BB files;
Martin Shough; Berliner)
