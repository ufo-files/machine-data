T-6 Crew Observes Unidentified White LightBrad
Sparks:
Nov. 4, 1952; Caribou, Maine (BBU)
5:28 p.m. (EST). USAF pilot of T-6G at 2,500
ft heading 3601 Mag, 1st Lt. Charles E. Young, rear
cockpit pilot Capt Leon H. Pagan, both of 74th FIS,
Presque Isle AFB, Maine, saw a slow moving light
changing colors behind them. Young sighted light in the
W above low bank of clouds at about 101 elevation,
changing colors from red to blue to white, notified
Pagan
in the back who also saw it, and described it as a white
light like aircraft navigation light or star or planet,
about
251 elevation. T-6 was turned into direction of light
for
about 40 miles and pilots noted 2501 Mag azimuth from
Limestone AFB and 2451 Mag azimuth from Presque Isle
AFB, before they returned course. Senior Control Tower
Operator, A/2c Earl S. Goldsen, 1974th AACS Sq
Detachment 1, notified at 5:36 p.m. and sighted light in
the
W that stopped and moved to the NW. Northwest Airlines
Flight 12 landing at Presque Isle AFB also reported
seeing
the 1star.1 (Sparks; BB files; Project 1947)

Dan Wilson:
November 4, 1952; Caribou, Maine (BBU)
5:30 p.m. EST. 1st Lt. Charles E. Young, pilot of
a T-6 aircraft,
and Capt. Leon H. Pagan in the rear cockpit observed a
white light that
appeared to be stationary or moving very slowly at an
elevation of
approximately 25 degrees. The light descended toward the
horizon and
disappeared after having been under constant observation
for a period
of approximately 35 minutes.The color of the light
changed from red to
orange to blue-green to white. The light was also seen
by A/2C Earl S.
Goldsen the Senior Control Tower Operator at Presque
Isle AFB, Maine.
