Splinter of unvarying light moving at high velocity below the nearly full Lunar disk

An elongated, thin Splinter of unvarying light was observed moving at high velocity below the nearly full Lunar disk. This thin splinter of bright light was observed at about 5:50 PM to be moving very rapidly. Initially, it was seen South and East of the Lunar disk moving toward the Northern horizon. It was at a higher altitude than the Scattered Cloud layer at approximately 6,500-7,000 ft. The object was observed from the cockpit of a Cessna 182T by two general aviation licensed pilots, each with nearly 1,000 cockpit hours. One is an Air Force Reserve Aviation Crew Chief and the other a Retired Naval Aviation Reservist with an additional 2,000 hours of aircrew time in tactical and multiengine aircraft. Neither had ever seen an airborne object moving as fast as the light splinter. First impression was of a guided missile in flight but lacking an exhaust plume or contrail. The object was moving much faster than an orbiting satellite. Photo were taken of the Moon and clouds at the approximate time of the observation. An attempt to photograph the object were foiled when it moved behind the clouds. Photo is attached showing a possible location of the object indicated. At the time, the Cessna 182 was in the vicinity of KMCZ enroute to KECG from KFAY at 5,500 ft on a heading of approximately 060 degrees at approximately160+ mph. Angular size was about a length of a thumbnail width at arm's length or about 3 minutes of arc. Length to width ration seemed about 10:1. Photos do not constitute evidence but rather indicate sky and cloud conditions at best.
