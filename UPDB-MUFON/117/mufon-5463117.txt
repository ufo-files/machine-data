It was mid November of 1977, several hours after sunset on a clear night. I was driving north to Spokane in eastern Washington on rural Palouse highway with my boyfriend. While we weren1t close to any towns, I imagine the nearest one would have been Garfield. 

As we reached the crest of a hill, we were shocked to see a huge wheat field below to our right ~ about 100 feet away ~ completely blanketed in a clear bluish-purple light. The light was 16-20 inches deep and appeared to be the exact same depth and quality across the entire field, all the way along 20 or so acres up the long hill. The light was contained and box-like and perfectly square at the edges as though cut with a knife. It did not extend or shine beyond its even edges. The light had similar qualities to a black light, making everything in the field look crisp and detailed.

We paused a moment, looking in every direction for an explanation. There was no moon that night, nor were there clouds, street lights, houses, or other traffic ~ just vast plowed wheat fields on a starry night. While it was clear that the light was self-contained, my mind was searching for something to justify it. 

Once the enormity of the situation sunk in, my boyfriend hit the gas. As we passed the field the light went out, as though someone had flipped a switch. After we1d gone a mile or so and calmed down some, we turned around and went back to look at the field. Everything about the area looked ordinary. From the time we first saw the field to the moment the light went out may have been a minute or so, though in hindsight I wonder if we may have lost some time.
