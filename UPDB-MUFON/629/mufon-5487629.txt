star-like cluster of 3 lights. White (blinking) on top, gren and red flashing on bottom. 3-5 objects all alike.

These star-like light clusters look like a blinking triple star cluster. In bird binoculars you see a blinking white light on top and below rotating red and green lights. 
You can easily pick them out with the naked eye. 3-5 or 6 appear over this bay between (Dutch West Indies) St. Maarten and St. Barths (St. Bartholemew, French West Indies).
They differ from stars as they blink the wrong colors (red to green), are very bright where no star event is occurring like a Venus-close-by etc. event. Some stay in place all night but one is notable for drifting higher and to the south over the course of 3 hours or so.
Best viewing is after 9 p.m. - over Pointe Blanche Bay looking to St. Barth's , south from here. St. Barth's is 14 miles away so a good distance marker. Some large lights from the east side hills (top of hills) on St. Barth's often coincide with UFOs. So UFOs may be man-made/military origin. 

They were noticed Oct. 11, 2015 after dark. 
The best time, due to some clouds, to watch has been 9 p.m. to midnight but they are there all night, every night since 10-11. And they may have been here a few days earlier but that was the date I got binoculars out and took notes. 

There are some night time flights and helicopters but they make noise and or have lights that look like aviation lights. And for Pete's sake - no one has the budget to hover all night in one place!

One of those prominent ones, the 'drifter' that goes late at light up and to the south just appears. If=t could be it is hovering in daylight and only shows up because of darkness. But it gets 'joined', by another one that drifts in from the north after 8 p.m. or so. It takes up a spot to the left of the main one (to the north) and sits there.

These are not so high on the horizon. I am guessing when I say 30 degrees up. But they do go up higher by midnight. They stay all night. 

A tourist on a cruise ship should be able to see them as ships often arrive before dawn and go to a nearby port.
