Lights in the evening sky performing incredible maneuvers over Bonneville Salt Flats

Driving east on I-80 not long after sunset, I observed 15-30 lights appearing and disappearing (often in pairs or trios), flashing dimly, pulsing irregularly sometimes dimly (with a blue-white to white light, occasionally with a sparkling, deep ruby-red finish), sometimes brightly (with a white to yellow-orange light, glowing similarly to the appearance of household lights when a dimmer switch is manipulated randomly), accelerating from hovering, stopping on a dime, "swinging" in tight arcs, making 90° course alterations, circling each other as if "playing" with each other, pacing automobile traffic in both directions (eastbound and westbound at approximately 80 MPH) before stopping and descending.

The lights maintained their brighter glows for intervals of varying lengths, sometimes glowing steadily for 30 seconds or more (though there appeared to be a correlation between brighter glowing and gravity-/momentum-defying maneuvers specifically).

The lights' movements had a bizarre "on-rails" quality that suggested they were not affected by variations in the air column like an aircraft would be, or else had exceptionally advanced aerodynamic characteristics. Their movements seemed purposeful, yet (as mentioned above) had an almost "playful" quality to them.

This behavior continued until the appearance of stars in the deepening twilight made distinguishing the UAP from other sources of light too difficult.

Midway through the sighting, a slow-moving light fixed-wing aircraft (possibly a Cessna, Beechcraft, or similar) flew over the I-80 east, somewhat south of the highway (and consequently, apparently over Dugway Proving Ground airspace). This aircraft seemed to be patrolling, and did not noticeably interact with the UAP.
