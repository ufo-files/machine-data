Large, star-like object drops near PANTEX plant

In early Fall/1988, while living in Amarillo, TX, sometime in the very early a.m. hours, was on security [removed personal information to Investigators report section/cms/LLL]
 Partway through the evening's security foot-patrols, was headed northeastward one the plant's roadway access routes, when glanced upwards into the distant night sky and noticed rapid movement of a bright, white, unwavering, radiant-glowing, star-like light source approaching my general direction, causing me to halt and observe. Object's star-like size was similar to that found among the largest of celestial stars visible at night, and remained so throughout the observation, similar to the unchanging level/intensity of its radiant glow, which was equal to the brightest of stars. When first noticed, the object was traveling along a linear E,NE-to-W,SW trajectory at a rapid, sub-meteoric pace, was silent throughout the observation, and appeared to maintain level/horizontal trajectory and altitude of approx. 3,000'-4,000'. When first noticed, object appeared to be approx. 12+ miles in distance, was under observation approx. 5 seconds, and had approached to distance of approx. 5-7 miles in less time than that. When the object reached the approx. 5-7 mile distance area, it was observed to execute a small, rapid, downward arc in its trajectory, heading it into a drop towards the ground. Partway into its drop maneuver, the object appeared to noticeably slow its descent rate/speed, before observation of the object was lost due to blocking buildings/terrain. Throughout the observation, the object did not leave any kind of visible trail/residue, was silent and lacked sonic boom despite the speed, and was observed to pass through, or behind, a few small, thin, scattered, multi-elevation clouds with no obvious disruptive effects, during its descent. Hesitated a bit before resuming patrol, straining/concentrating to detect any associated noise, explosion-report, or impact tremors, with negative results. Consulting a map to check on the object's distances, locations, and trajectory, noted that the object appeared to travel in close proximity to the PANTEX plant, and may have dropped down approx. 3-5 miles to its SW. The night was seasonally warm, little-no winds, with the aforementioned scattered, small, multi-elevation clouds, and an unnoticed moon phase to the general south. Sketches available.
