Small white flash near the ISS

My wife and I were standing on our back deck in Frostburg, Maryland, about 6:07 p.m. on Tuesday, Jan. 19, watching the International Space Station move across a cloudless night sky. The ISS was about 40 degrees above the southwest horizon, a bit below the moon, which was bright though not full. An eastbound passenger plane was visible elsewhere in the sky, but only by turning our heads. As the ISS passed beneath the moon, we both saw, for only a second, a small white flash between the ISS and the moon, at 45 or 50 degrees above the horizon. It was perhaps the size of a penny held in an outstretched hand. We've seen meteors before, but this lacked any arc, trail or shower of sparks. It also looked nothing like the continuously moving and blinking lights of an aircraft. It looked instead like the gleam of a lantern when the shade is briefly opened, then shut again; or like a dark moving object briefly illuminated in the moonlight. We both said aloud, "What was that?" -- so we know it wasn't just our imagination.
