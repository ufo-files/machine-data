  I, XXXXX, being first duly sworn, state that:

1. My name is XXXXX. My birth date is XXXXX. I attended XXXXX State University, graduating with a Bachelor's degree with a major in Criminal Justice Administration and a minor in Psychology.

2. I enlisted in the Air Force on my 17th birthday (XXXXX) and reported to Lackland AFB for training on XXXXX. I was assigned as administrative, AF specialty code 70230, during my basic training. My first duty station was at XXXXX, on the "high line." The high line was the civilian name for the area between the Canadian border and US Highway 10. The official military designation for this area was "pine tree line." My second duty station was XXXXX on the cadre team, which included 6 AF members and 1 army corps engineer, tasked with prepping the air base for military acceptance and activation.

From there, I transferred to Headquarters of the 7th Air Division at High Wycombe, England dealing with Top Secret sensitive information. I remained there past the normal rotation to close down/deactivate the 7th Air Division and 4 of the satellite bases, except RAF Upper Heyford. My duties were to read and disseminate Top Secret Information to the U.S. General staff and our British counterparts. This included preparing and shipping top secret material.I was married to the daughter of XXXXX who commanded the RAF Signals command and later XXXXX. My subsequent transfer to XXXXX was the result of international politics, which had a bearing on my reassignment to ARFCOS.

3. I served as a Transportation Squadron Clerk at XXXXX. As a Transportation Squadron Clerk, I was involved in the scheduling and dispatch of UH-1 "Huey" Helicopters to the missile silos at Minot. The helicopters primarily carried launch crews but they were also used to transport Air Police Emergency Response Teams to shoo away UFOs from the missile silo doors. This was a fairly frequent occurrence at Minot; conservatively speaking, this happened more than once a month. I did talk with returning Air Police teams when they returned and asked what was going on. The enlisted point of view was to keep your mouth shut and say little, if anything, because they didn't want to be ridiculed. I would say that it was common enough knowledge to create a peer group silence. The only thing which would have been in writing would have been flight and fuel logs, which were not in my area.

4. From 1966 to 1968, I was a member of the specially selected Armed Forces Courier Service operating out of Operating Location No. 1 at McChord Air Force Base in Washington. As an ARFCOS staff member, I held a Top Secret, Extremely Sensitive Information, Extensive Background Investigations clearance (TS-ESI-EBI). Part of my duties with ARFCOS was to transport damaged/nonfunctional nuclear warheads from across the USA "highline" to the repair facility at Fernald. I was not told why the devices weren't working; that information would have to be obtained from someone in munitions. Fernald was a short distance from Wright Patterson, through Amish lands. I have no idea who owned the big brick building as the occupants didn't wear clothing with insignia other than dosimeters.  I don't even recall seeing identification badges. The ARFCOS Operating locations took orders from headquarters, room 1c36 in the Pentagon. The Station Commander would receive an order and assign the mission to couriers. I volunteered to take all eastbound airplane flights. We would fly commercial to an Air base, meet our LOG air transport and have Munitions maintenance staff transport the canisters to the hot pad (a remote area away from buildings and other aircraft) and load them. 

During my assignment with ARFCOS, I traveled over 100,000 miles on Eastern Air and Delta Airlines in addition to Military flights. I made an indeterminable number of flights during which I would escort between 2 and 6 canisters which contained damaged devices. The canisters were made of aluminum, were about 6 feet in diameter and about 4-1/2 to 5 feet high with electrical plugs to maintain the temperature and humidity inside. I used to put my arms and legs in the netting around the canister and sleep. Due to their size and weight, we only moved 6 at a time in a DC6, DC7 or Argosy, along with a limited amount of other restricted items. Six was also the limit for a semi-truck. What we moved always required an enclosed transport.  

I flew commercial and military flights both to and from Wright Patterson. I would pick up the canisters via LOG (Logistical Air Command, the predecessor of Material Air Command) Air and then fly with them to Wright Patterson Air Force Base in Ohio, making intermediate stops as necessary. I never flew on a LOG flight with insignia nor did the crew wear uniforms. Once in Columbus, Ohio I would escort the canisters via semi truck with class "A" placards to Fernald. I knew what was in the canisters by comments made by custodians when they transferred custody of the freight. Accompanying documents did not specifically state the contents of the canisters but the radiation stickers were a darn good visual clue. I kept a record of these flights for a long time but destroyed them after I communicated by e-mail with XXXXX and my application for disability was denied.

Regarding the weight on a DC6 and DC7, the loadmasters used a tool referred to as a stick. This was a long metal pole with a 90 degree short probe on one end. The aircraft had a small bump on the rear bottom; the crew would take the stick and hang it from a hole in the bump. Then they would load cargo until the front wheel came up and the tail went down on the stick. Then they removed the last pallet. If the nose wheel went back down the crew still considered that as being good to go. I only saw that happen once in Denver on a really hot day.  Once our plane was loaded, we flew around stopping by various places and transporting "stuff" from there to other bases. For instance, we might land at Denver and rendezvous with persons from Rocky Flats. On several of those trips, Rocky Flats personnel told me that the cargo was nuclear warhead triggers. A direct flight from point to point to point (i.e. Malstrom to Wright Patterson) was rare but I did participate in more than one of those. 

5. In 1967, I flew from Malstrom with 6 canisters eastbound to Fargo where we were to rendezvous with another LOG Air and receive some hand cargo. It was very unusual to fly with 6 canisters and I surmise, although I have no proof, that this occurred following the March 1967 episode when the UFOs took a number of missiles offline.  We were on the "hot pad" waiting for the other plane when I and three other flight crew saw a light about our 12 o'clock position. I caught a ride to the Tower with the Air Police and asked about this light. The Radar Team in the Tower showed me blips, or targets, showing radar returns indicating that there was an unidentified craft (at 25,000 feet if I recall correctly) and another one south of us at 5,000 feet inbound. I then returned to the airplane. We completed our rendezvous with the other airplane and continued eastward to Minneapolis-St. Paul or possibly Duluth. 

6. In Minnesota, we were on the "hot pad" transferring cargo from a DC-7 to an Argosy for approximately 3 hours in the dark. The pilot, co-pilot, myself and one other courier then departed flying southbound towards Wright Patterson. Along the way, we were again followed by an unknown light which escorted us until somewhere north of Wright Patterson. Then, in the early first light, we were hit by something which damaged the instrument panel electronics and lights. The intersection with the light was not felt but created a spectacular light show. From my position in the cockpit, it appeared that something resembling ball lightning entered the right wing tip, then proceeded along the leading edge of the wing, into the right outboard engine nacelle, jumping to the inboard nacelle then through the cockpit; it exited the aircraft across the left wing in the same manner. Not being part of the flight crew, I cannot even guess the extent of the damage. We lost radio, navigation, altimeter, compass and as I recall, the flight crew had difficulty getting the gear down. Throttles did appear to function; at one point the pilot had found a long straight line of lights so he descended thinking it was a runway. At some small altitude, we observed a freeway overpass ahead. The pilot shoved the controls to full power which enabled the plane to gain enough altitude to clear the overpass. He then found a line of blue lights which he lined up towards and descended again. At that point, the flight engineer asked me to hand crank the rear door when we got to the ground. I started cranking when I heard the engines stop. I then saw three flight crew rolling on the ground away from the front doors. I evacuated also, injuring my butt when I hit the runway. I asked the crew why they jumped and was told they were afraid of all the high explosives we were carrying. 

7. I have no direct information re the cause of the damage to the devices which I was transporting on that day but I did overhear a couple of technicians at Fernald discussing Electro Magnetic Pulse. Their conversation, which I overheard but which was not directed to me, could have been in regard to the items in the canisters which I had transported on that occasion.

8. At no time during this day, or any other duty period, was I under the influence of liquor, drugs or any medication which would have affected my ability to comprehend, analyze or interpret the incidents which took place.

I know this because XXXXX admitted it to me after I sent him questions by e-mail. 

10. My security clearance was pulled in 1968 without explanation although I have long suspected that it was punishment for reporting the presence of UFOs. In that era there was an Air Force Regulation, in the 205- series as I recall, which contained a reporting format and an office of responsibility to submit the incident report to. As a loyal and dedicated fool, I complied with regulations.
 
11. I left active duty on XXXXX, partly because of my personal feelings about loosing my security clearance and partly to unsuccessfully try to save my 7 year marriage. I joined the XXXXX Air National Guard around Thanksgiving, 1973 and worked for years as a Federal Title 5 full-time Air Police Technician with dual AF specialty codes. I worked as an Air Police Supervisor and Weapons Systems Security Supervisor. I was sworn in to the XXXXX Police Department and the XXXXX County Sherriff's Department. I also served over a year as a XXXXX reserve police officer and three years as a correctional officer. I completed a total of 21 years between Regular AF and Air Tech. 

12. I also experienced a personal encounter with a UFO in 1976. I was on a prison gun tower at the XXXXX State Correctional institution, 5 miles south of XXXXX, in the late afternoon, facing east with the lowering sun behind me. An object appeared from the north, heading south toward XXXXX. I observed it through 7-15X50 binoculars.  It was a small spindle-shaped object, moving at jet speed while maintaining its horizontal trajectory/altitude. It appeared to be internally illuminated with a rosy-orange color. There was at least one other tower guard who saw this. He was a close hunting and fishing friend until his untimely death by heart attack. We were very familiar with weather balloons out of XXXXX airport and never saw anything similar to this. At the time, I pressed the speaker control which notified all other towers and central control. Control was rather snippy re tower officers who looked elsewhere than into the prison compound. I found a picture that portrays a vehicle similar to the type I saw at the 1976 prison on renderosity.com.  I have not undergone hypnosis because I haven't wanted to know more. I'm under the fearful impression that occupants of these vehicles are not very friendly.

We took as much as 600 "r's" (as per XXXXX staff) on any given occasion. I and my physician, for whom I have the utmost confidence and respect, believe that the radiation I received caused me physical damage.
