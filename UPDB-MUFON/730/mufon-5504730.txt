{rjl} I saw a formation of lights that were stationary and that changed in numbers and configuration.

My husband and I were leaving our house to go to our Grandson's birthday party. I looked at the clock in the pickup as I got into the passenger side as I didn't want us to be late. It read 7:12 pm (1912). As my husband was backing out of the driveway, I looked out of my opened window and noticed in the southwest sky, a formation of five very bright, larger than stars, objects in the sky. Three of the objects formed a triangle with two more at a distance, one to the right and above, and one to the right of that one and below it. The clock read 7:13 pm (1913). My son's house is about eight minutes from my house. During the drive I observed these objects until they disappeared completely at 7:19 pm (1919). During the drive the first five objects were joined by two more. The objects changed configuration five times during the drive from the original sighting at 7:13 pm. However, the triangle stayed the same the whole time. Each change was just an appearance of another object, usually at the right point of the triangle, kind of like a tail of a kite. There was no movement when this happened, they just appeared. So there ended up being the triangle of lights, two additional lights at the right end of the triangle and the same two lights as described above (at 7:13), further to the right, one above and the other one to the right and one below. There was never any sound or movement during these changes. I observed a total of seven objects. At 7:17(1917), the lights started just blinking out at the right (tail) end of the triangle, one by one, until they all disappeared by 7:19pm (1919). It was a clear night with no clouds. You could see the stars clearly. That is why these objects or lights were much larger and brighter than the stars were. I did make a sketch of the configurations and the times that each change happened. In 1997 my husband and I lived in northern Arizona. On several occasions I observed the same type of lights although in different configurations.
