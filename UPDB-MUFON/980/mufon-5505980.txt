I was stationed at McMurdo Antarctica with the US Navy operation deepfreeze program from Oct 1969- Oct 1970. Sometime during the Antarctic winter (24 Hrs. days without sun), I was on top of a fuel tank taking a reading. It was obviously cold but very clear. I gazed up at the stars and observed a star like object moving parallel with the ground in a northern direction. I believe everything from that point would be basically north. At first I thought it to be a satellite but it was moving too fast to be a satellite or a weather balloon. It was obviously not a meteor so that left only a possible aircraft. It is highly unlikely that aircraft fly over the south pole during the Antarctic Winter. The light was slightly in front of me when I first spotted it. It continued in the same direction until it was about overhead when it ascended and accelerated at a much higher rate of speed at what looked to be a 45 degree angle and quickly disappeared. I have never seen anything behave in that manner and I would be surprised if we had that technology in 1970.
