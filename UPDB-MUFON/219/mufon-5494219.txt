{nd} While stargazing watched what appeared to be typical satellite make a 45 degree turn

Last evening was a perfect night for stargazing. Dark, cloudless and bright stars. I was on our back deck shortly after 9 PM. At about 9:15PM I spotted a typical satellite or pin dot of light moving South to North and just below (east of) the Big Dipper. As I watched it pass directly overhead the object made a smooth, but tight turn to the Northeast. The turn appeared to be between 40 and 45 degrees which would also be the new heading. The turn was made as the object was nearest Alioth on the Dipper's handle. After the turn I checked the time. It was 9:16 PM. The light continued in a Northeast direction until it faded from view about 30 seconds after the turn. The object looked and acted like the typical satellites that pass over us on any given night. Almost all follow the same South to North trajectory and appear as smooth moving pin dots of reflective light in the night sky that fade into and out of view. As soon as the object made it's turn, I looked at it through binoculars and noticed nothing different. No other colors or blinking. It remained a steady moving pin dot of light until it faded from view. My feelings were rationally curious as soon as I witnessed the turn which prompted me to pick up the binoculars and write down a note or two. Prior to that it was just a beautiful night sky to gaze at.
