{td} Brightest light in sky, moved slowly, then stationary, then rapidly disappeared.

I am an amateur astronomer. Because there was a (rare) period of clear skies on Friday morning,
I found myself taking astro photos in my backyard observatory between 2 and 5 AM.
Even 30 minutes I would go to observatory, check my laptop and telescope progress, and manually rotate my dome. Before so doing, I would look up at Ursa Major, which was near the location of my target, to see if any clouds had rolled in. When I went out at 4:03, I looked up and immediately saw something that should not have been there. It was a light, circular in shape, or perhaps a sphere,
brighter than any object in the sky, certainly brighter than Jupiter. It was moving slowly towards my target and at first I thought it was an airplane or satellite and was worried it would mar my current photo. Then I realized this object was too steady and quiet to be a jetliner, and too bright to be a satellite. While I was watching it, it stopped moving and became completely motionless. As I absorbed this startling event, it disappeared, in place. I thought perhaps, it had gone behind a cloud. But the sky was completed clear; no clouds anywhere. I watched for several minutes, and scanned the remaining of the sky, to see if it would reappear. It did not.
