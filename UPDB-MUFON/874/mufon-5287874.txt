Close encounter with aerial object that made no sound

Camping at (Name of ranch moved to Investigators Report Field/cms/LLL) with my longtime friend (Name removed to Investigators Report Field/cms/LLL) and his family, and fishing on the east bank of the Pedernales River around 2 am on Sunday July 18, (or a different Sunday in July '76) 1976. Billy's family was asleep. Object first looked like a plane in the distance, but was moving toward us in a Z pattern. Billy noticed it first as odd, but I initially dismissed it until it got closer. It stopped still over the middle of the river, and straight out from us at 45 degrees, maybe 50 feet up. There was enough moon/star light to see gunmetal gray color, and the shape as a classic saucer, flat on bottom with sloped on top towards dome in the middle, 30-50 feet across, 15 or so feet tall at the center. About 10 solid amber color lights were evenly spaced along edge on bottom. There was no sound whatsoever. I stood there mouth agape, taking it all into my memory. Billy started shining his flashlight on it, and I got really scared at that point, thinking that it would react negatively, and he would not stop doing it. We could see the flashlight beam of light playing on the bottom. Billy said later that he was trying to signal it to come pick us up for a ride, ha. Not too long after that, it made just a very slight movement towards the east as if to gain direction/momentum, then in an instant was gone over our heads. When it left, we heard a swishing sound like a sword going through the air. During the encounter, I was somewhere between fearful and dumbfounded. I was amazed and excited beyond description afterward, and did not sleep that night. We both had been wanting to see something like this, and I feel very fortunate to have had the experience of a lifetime. We did not report this encounter to any authorities, and some of my friends gave me a hard time. I think I remember seeing a related newspaper article in the Houston Post the next day/Monday, but have not been able to track it down with Rice University Fondren Library's assistance.
