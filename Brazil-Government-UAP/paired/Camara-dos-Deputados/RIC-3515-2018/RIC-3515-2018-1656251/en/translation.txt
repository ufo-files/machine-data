Mr. President,

In accordance with the rules, I request that Your Excellency, after consulting the Desk, ask the Minister of State for Defense for information on the identification, location, and concise summary of all documentation containing probative, operational, functional, or personal information, generated by military or civilian personnel under the custody of the Brazilian Army, originating and classified between the years 1996 and 1997, and in subsequent years if correlatable. Specifically, I request information on generation and collection under the orders of the Military Command of the East and in the lower instances of the 4th RM/4th DE, from which the Military Organization Sergeant's School of Arms (ESA) is subordinate.

I also request information that may have derived from orders issued by superior instances, up to the Army Command in Brasília, if they refer to any extraordinary occurrence or operation recorded in that OM during those years. That this list of documents, to be sent to this deputy, include all classified information according to the legislation in force at the time, those reclassified, and those with extended secrecy periods from these cited years until the entry into force of the current Access to Information Law (LAI - Law 12.527/2011), or access reservation, as provided by the same LAI, subsequent decrees, portaries, and instructions. I further request that such data be provided, as per the examples in the attached tables (I and II).

The highest representation of the Ministry of Defense and within what, according to the Forces, is the
true account in the files of their subordinate agencies, have confirmed not only the
existence of such information, but also some occurrences involving alleged UFOs and their
crews, as well as the seriousness with which at least the Brazilian Air Force (FAB)
treats the matter. We currently have over 10,000 pages of documents and other types of
information in various formats, in batches collected and in the possession of the National
Archives in Brasília and Rio de Janeiro, entirely sent by FAB. This without
counting the procedures of this Force, regarding the process of collection, registration, and
archiving, with subsequent collection of the material to public archives. Such procedures are
outlined in Portaria 551/GC3, of August 9, 2010, published by FAB and known among
scholars of the subject and in specific media as the "ET of Varginha Law."

However, despite the demonstrated effort by the Brazilian Air Force, many issues raised
by previous requests remain pending. Over time, almost seven years since responses to the
last RIC were given, there is no discernible same disposition from the other forces,
the Army and Navy, to provide data to interested citizens, as the same phenomenon that
justified FAB adopting and publishing its procedures for registration and archiving
occurs not only in the air space, but also over and under Brazilian territorial waters, and
its activity is also recorded on land, in over 8.5 million km² of Brazilian territory, making
it then a matter to be addressed not by one, but by the three defense forces of the national
territory.

In particular, the justificative motive of this Request builds its bases on the search
for information about a specific case, of great national impact and equal worldwide
repercussion, known in the media as "The ET of Varginha Case," or among ufologists as
"Varginha Case," because it occurred in that mining city at the beginning of 1996 and its
press coverage lasted for at least another year. This case is of singular importance for a
number of reasons, but two are of paramount importance to scientific knowledge and public
transparency. First, because, according to ufologists, it involves the crash of an unknown
technology UFO and the capture of at least two of its crew members, and second, due to the
impressive cover-up operation of the facts, for reasons that are not yet very clear.
Suspicions also lead to another serious conclusion, due to a possible breach of national
sovereignty, embodied in the final destination of the crashed ship's material and the seized
bodies, which, according to ufologists, according to the military involved, went to the
United States of America against the wishes of several officers, for obvious reasons.

The lack of public information about the case, considered true by a large part of the
population of Varginha, results in prejudice, ridicule, embarrassment, pain, and doubt among
witnesses and relatives of the people directly involved, such as the family of military
policeman Marco Eli Chereze, who would have been a fatal victim of direct contact with one
of the crew members.

Already under the LAI (Law of Information Access), which was sanctioned after the
aforementioned RICs were answered, new questions about the procedure, especially
from the Army, were raised. Since 2012, several requests have been made to the Federal
Government's Electronic Service of Citizen Information (e-SIC), all denied or partially
granted.

The documents that remain classified, particularly those from 1996 and 1997, should be made available in accordance with Article 45, Section II, of Decree No. 7,724, which requires that all classified documents be identified by code on a specific list on the electronic pages of the responsible public bodies by June 1st of each year. If these documents indeed exist and were generated between 1996 and 1997, as numerous circumstantial and testimonial evidence suggest, they can only remain classified as "Ultra-Secret" with a 25-year secrecy period.

One of the main military witnesses in the Varginha Case has categorically stated in a recorded deposition that the operation to transport the unknown biological entities, the crew of the crashed UFO, from ESA to Campinas (Unicamp) on January 23, 1996, was treated as "Ultra-Secret" by the commanders and thus classified in that category. Therefore, its records could only be made publicly available on January 24, 2021. However, the CIDIC (Document Indexing Code for Classified Information) that identifies it should already exist and be available to the interested parties.

Moreover, it is observed that the designated location for publishing the "list of classified and declassified information of the Army," at http://www.eb.mil.br/informacoes-classificadas-desclassificadas, does not follow the suggestions of the "Guide for Publishing the List of Classified and Declassified Information and Statistical Reports on the Access to Information Law," available at the e-SIC (Electronic Information Request System) of the Ministry of Transparency and Control General of the Union (CGU), accessible at: http://www.acessoainformacao.gov.br/lai-para-sic/sic-apoio-orientacoes/guias-e-orientacoes/guia-informacoes-classificadas-versao-3.pdf.

In this CGU-drafted guide, under subitem B.2.4 - Content of the "classified information list" - it clearly states: "It is also recommended that the agency or entity disclose the subject matter of the classified document, with the aim of increasing transparency regarding its content, as well as allowing social control. It is emphasized that including the subject in the list of classified documents aids in identifying future reference, as provided in Article 30, II, Law No. 12.527/2011. To do so, the agency should inform as broadly as possible the content of the document, without, however, revealing the restricted information." This recommendation is simply ignored by the Army.

It is also noteworthy that in this list of classified documents, only those whose secrecy periods expire or in the previous year, or in the year of the list's publication, up to June 1st, are published. Thus, as of June 1st, 2017 - the last update was made in 2016 - all ultra-secret documents were close to losing their secrecy period, i.e., those with their origin registered up to 1992, 25 years prior. There is no document in this category classified after 1992. It is also noted that all those classified in this category already have the approved suggestion for reclassification, meaning an additional 25 years of secrecy. There is no report of any document being declassified or made public since 1997.
