Mr. President,

In accordance with the rules, I request that Your Excellency, after consulting the Desk, request from the Minister of State for Defense information about the identification, location, and concise content of all documentation containing probative, operational, functional, or personal information, generated by military or civilian personnel, under the custody of the Brazilian Army, originating and classified between the years 1996 and 1997, and in subsequent years if correlatable. Specifically, I request the
information regarding generation and collection under the orders of the Military Command of the East and in the lower instances of the 4th RM/4th DE, from which the Military Organization Sergeant School of Arms (ESA) is subordinate. I also request any information that may have originated from orders issued by higher authorities, up to the Army Command in Brasília, if they refer to any extraordinary occurrence or operation recorded in that OM during the mentioned years. That this list of documents, to be sent to this deputy, includes all classified information according to the legislation in force at the time, those re-classified and those with secrecy deadlines extended from these cited years until the entry into force of the current Law of Access to Information (LAI - Law 12.527/2011), or access reservation, as provided for in this same LAI, subsequent decrees, ordinances, and instructions. I request that such data be provided, as per the examples in
annexed tables (I and II).

This is the case, for example, with the strange absence to date, on the Army's website, of the Indexing Codes for Documents Containing Classified Information (CIDICs) originating in 1996 and 1997. Art. 45, Section II of Decree No. 7.724, one of the decrees introduced to supplement and give effect to the LAI, requires all documents that remain classified to have their identifiers made available in code format in a specific list on the websites of the public bodies responsible for them, by June 1 of each year. If these documents do in fact exist and were produced between 1996 and 1997, as countless pieces of circumstantial and witness evidence show us, they can only remain secret if they were classified at the time as TOP SECRET, a category with a secrecy period of 25 years. Indeed, one of the principal military witnesses in the Varginha Case categorically stated, in recorded testimony, that the operation to transfer the unknown biological entities, crew members of the crashed UFO, carried out on January 23, 1996 from ESA to Campinas (Unicamp), was treated as top secret by the commanders and was thus classified in that category. Accordingly, its records could only be available for public consultation on January 24, 2021, although the CIDIC identifying it should already exist and be available to interested parties. But if Decree 7.724 came into force on May 16, 2012, why has the Army still not made the CIDICs for this and other events in the Varginha Case available in its “list of top-secret information,” six years after the LAI came into force?
In addition to this issue, it can be seen that the location designated for publishing the “list of the Army's classified and declassified information,” at http://www.eb.mil.br/informacoes-classificadas-desclassificadas, does not follow the suggestions in the “Guide for Publishing the List of Classified and Declassified Information and Statistical Reports on the Access to Information Law,” included in the e-SIC of the Ministry of Transparency and the Office of the Comptroller General of the Union (CGU), available at: http://www.acessoainformacao.gov.br/lai-para-

In this guide prepared by the CGU, subsection B.2.4. - Content of the “list of classified information” area clearly states: “it is further recommended that the body or entity disclose the subject of the classified document, with the aim of increasing transparency regarding its content, as well as enabling public oversight. It should be emphasized that including the subject in the list of classified documents helps identify it for future reference, as provided in art. 30, II, Law No. 12.527/2011. For this purpose, the body must describe the document's content as broadly as possible, without, however, revealing what the restricted information concerns.” The Army simply ignores this recommendation.
Also striking about this list of documents classified as top secret is that only those whose secrecy periods expire either in the preceding year or in the year the list is published, up to June 1, are published. Thus, as of June 1, 2017 - the last update was made in 2016 - all top-secret documents were about to reach the end of their secrecy periods, that is, those whose origins were recorded up to 1992, twenty-five years earlier.
