Mr. President,
In accordance with the rules, I request that Your Excellency, after hearing the Bureau, request the Minister of State for Defense to provide information on the identification, location, and concise content of all documentation containing evidentiary, operational, functional, or personal information, generated by military or civilian personnel, under the custody of the Brazilian Army, originating and classified between the years 1996 and 1997, and in subsequent years if correlatable. Specifically, I request information regarding documents generated and collected under the orders of the Military Command of the East and lower instances of the 43rd RM/4 3 DE, from which the Military Organization Sergeant's School of Arms ESA is subordinate.
I also request information that may have derived from orders issued by superior instances, up to the Army Command in Brasília, if they refer to any extraordinary occurrence or operation recorded in that OM during those years. I request that this list of documents, to be sent to this deputy, include all classified information according to the legislation in force at the time, reclassified documents, and those with extended secrecy periods from the cited years until the entry into force of the current Access to Information Law (LAI - Law 12.527/2011), or reserved access, as provided for in the same LAI, subsequent decrees, ordinances, and instructions. I further request that such data be provided, as per the examples in the attached tables (I and II).

--- seg-215cb57334e55e5f477fd702 [needs-review] ---

Both answered in parts by the three Armed Forces, in greater representation of the Ministry of Defense and within what, the Forces affirm, is the truth reported.

From the strange absence until the moment, on the Army's website, of the Document Indexing Codes (CIDICs) containing secret information originating from the years 1996 and 1997. According to Article 45, Item 11 of Decree No. 7,724, one of the decrees that emerged to complement and give effect to the Access to Information Law (LAI), all still classified documents must have their identifications in code format made available in a specific list, on the electronic pages of the public bodies responsible for them, by June 1st of each year. If these documents indeed exist and were generated between 1996 and 1997, as numerous circumstantial and testimonial evidence show; to remain secret, they could only have been classified at the time as ULTRASECRET, with a 25-year secrecy period. Indeed, one of the main military witnesses in the Varginha Case affirmatively stated, in a recorded deposition, that the transfer of unknown biological entities, UFO accident crew, implemented on January 23, 1996, from the ESA to Campinas (Unicamp), was treated as ULTRASECRET by commanders and thus classified in that category. Therefore, their records could only be available for public consultation on January 24, 2021, although the CIDIC identifying it should already exist and be available to those interested. Yet, if Decree 7,724 entered into force on May 16, 2012, why has the Army not yet made available in its "list of ultra-secret information" the CIDICs for this and other Varginha Case occurrences, six years after the LAI's validity?

In addition to this issue, it is noticed that at the location designated for the publication of the "list of classified and declassified information of the Army," whose address is http://www.eb.mil.br/informacoes-classificadas-desclassificadas, the suggestions of the "Guide for Publishing the List of Classified and Declassified Information and Statistical Reports on the Access to Information Law," available at the e-SIC (Electronic Information Request System) of the Ministry of Transparency and Control General of the Union (CGU), accessible at the address:
http://www.acessoainformacao.gov.br/lai-para-sic/sic-apoio-orientacoes/guias-e-orientacoes/guia-informacoes-classificadas-versao-3.pdf. In this guide, prepared by the CGU, in its subitem B.2.4 - Content of the "classified information list" - it clearly states: "it is also recommended that the agency or entity divulges the subject matter of the classified document, with the aim of increasing transparency regarding its content, as well as allowing social control. It is emphasized that including the subject in the list of classified documents aids in future reference identification, as provided in Article 30, Item 11, of Law No. 12.527/2011. To do so, the agency must inform as widely as possible the content of the document, without, however, revealing the restricted information." This recommendation is simply ignored by the Army.

It is also striking in this list of classified documents as ULTRASECRET that only those whose secrecy periods expire or in the previous year, or in the year of the list's publication, up to June 1st, are published. Thus, until June 1st, 2017 - the last update was made in 2016 - all ULTRASECRET documents were close to losing their secrecy period, i.e., those that had their origin registered up to 1992, 25 years before. There is no document in this space that has been classified as ULTRASECRET after 1992. It is also noted that all those classified in this category already come with an approved reclassification suggestion, i.e., with an additional 25 years of secrecy. There is no news of a document classified in the highest degree of secrecy that has not been reclassified, and even less that has not been declassified.

I refer to the Ofício 13 Sec/RI/E/n°2201/18, dated May 17, 2018, which concerns
1.
Request for Information No. 3.515/2018 submitted by Deputy Chico Alencar (PSOL-RJ) to the Minister of Defense, requesting information about the identification, location, and concise content of documentation containing probative, operational, functional, or personal information generated by military or civilian personnel and in the custody of the Brazilian Army, originating and classified in the years 1996 and 1997, and in subsequent years if correlatable.
2.
Regarding this matter, I must inform the honorable Deputy that, after consulting the Army Command, the following response was drafted:
I - "Under the scope of the AI (Law on Access to Information), which was sanctioned after the aforementioned Requests for Information from the Chamber of Deputies (R/Cs) were answered, new issues regarding the procedure, especially that of the Army, have arisen. Since 2012, several requests have been made through the electronic system of the Federal Government's Citizen Information Service (e-SIC), all of which have been denied or fulfilled in a way that does not reveal what may still be under secrecy. This is the case, for example, of the strange absence, to this day, on the Army's website, of the Indexing Codes of Documents containing Confidential Information (CIDICs) originating in 1996 and 1997. According to Article 45, Item 1, of Decree No. 7724, one of the decrees that emerged to complement and give effect to the AI, all still classified documents must have their identifications available in a specific list, on the electronic pages of the public bodies responsible for them, by June 1st of each year."

Response:
The lists of classified/declassified information are published annually in accordance with the provisions of Portaria no 012, dated January 9, 2015, which approves the Instructions.

Response:
Classified documents at the ultra-secret level are listed in the classified documents registers. The first register was published in 2014, covering the years of 2013/2014, as prescribed by the General Instructions for the Assessment and Control of Classified Documents - (EB10-IG-01.015), and published after the entry into force of the AI.
Classified documents at the ultra-secret level are subject to extension of classification for up to 25 (twenty-five) years by the Joint Commission for the Reassessment of Information (CMRI), in accordance with Article 35, paragraph 1, item (UI) of the AI:
"Article 35 (VETTED)
§ 1 A Joint Commission for the Reassessment of Information is established, which shall decide, within the scope of the Federal Public Administration, on the treatment and classification of secret information and shall have competence to:
I - request from the authority that classifies information as ultra-secret or secret an explanation or the full or partial content of the information;
II - review the classification of ultra-secret or secret information, either ex officio or at the request of an interested party, in accordance with Article r and other provisions of this Law; and
III - extend the secrecy period for classified information at the ultra-secret level, always for a determined period, while its access or disclosure may cause an external threat to national sovereignty or the integrity of national territory or a serious risk to the country's international relations, observing the period provided for in Article 24, § 0."
