EXTRACT FROM THE REPORT OF THE COMMISSION'S END OF POST REPORT
     OF THE OCEANOGRAPHIC STATION ON TRINDADE ISLAND - PERIOD FROM
     1 NOVEMBER 1957 TO 16 JANUARY 1958 OF CC CARLOS ALBERTO FERREIRA BACELLAR
--- seg-9e20c2ad29580fc6b6dfeff6 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Incidents:
--- seg-06e9563e5cdd48b1bffc5ac1 [needs-review] ---
On December 31st, a coded radio message was sent to EMA, informing DHN, that an unidentified flying object (UFO), in the shape of a flattened ellipsoid, colored stainless steel, was sighted at an altitude of approximately 1600 meters, moving at a vertiginous speed in a northeasterly direction, without making any noise. The object was observed by Dr. 1st Lieutenant Ignácio Carlos Moreira Murta, 1st CI-SI Sebastião Soriano de Souza, and five workers from Companhia Moraes Rego, one of whom, the first to spot the object, claimed to have seen it previously on December 5th and reported it to his colleagues, but was not taken seriously. Both times, it was close to 8 a.m.

The following day, January 1st, all attention was focused on the sky when something appeared, observed by around twenty men from the garrison, which was confirmed to be the same object; I was also alert and it seemed to me, however, to be a seagull. The object—or seagull—was flying overhead, making depth perception impossible; moving in a northeasterly direction, it suddenly shone intensely at some point in its trajectory, perhaps for less than a second. If it was a seagull, its speed would have been considerable but within reason; if it was indeed the UFO, it should have been at a considerable distance, and in that case, its speed was incredible. This incident was not reported to EMA due to the lingering doubt.

Finally, on January 16th, around 1100 hours, I was already on board the NE "Almirante Saldanha" after the change of command at the post, when I was notified that an unidentified aerial object had been sighted from the ship over the island. I was in my cabin at the time and immediately went to the deck, where I found several people, including Mr. Almiro Baraúna, a professional photographer, who had accompanied several scuba divers to the island of Trindade to take underwater photographs. Mr. Baraúna was one of the most excited as he had taken several plates of the object and was eagerly awaiting positive results. I did not leave his side to verify the authenticity of the negatives. I saw him remove the film roll from the camera and went to the darkroom to watch the development, as it was too hot outside, so I stayed outside during the operation time, about 10 minutes. Leaving the darkroom, still with the film in the tank, I saw him remove it and witnessed his disappointment as he, visibly nervous, assumed he had failed. I took the film, examined it more closely, and in three negatives from different positions, I noticed the presence of a strange stain that was later perfectly identified. The film remained in Mr. Baraúna's possession, who, upon my insistence, agreed to come to EMA if necessary. Upon my arrival in Rio, I went there and dealt with the case with CC José Geraldo Brandão, providing a verbal statement. From that point on, everything that was done was at the exclusive initiative of EMA.
