Justification
          On December 26, 2007, and January 2, 2008, a group of seven scholars of the phenomenon of unidentified flying objects (UFO, or OVNI in Portuguese), known as the Brazilian Commission of Ufologists, requested from the Federal Government the declassification of confidential information on the topic, generated and archived by the Federal Administration, as well as its availability for public access.
          As mandated by Law 11.111/2005, in conjunction with Decree 5301/2004, the signatories of the document submitted it to five Federal institutions, including the Office of the President (Casa Civil) and the Ministry of Defense, specifying the desired information and its location, providing extensive legal argument to support their claims, and emphasizing that these documents are "permanent, of historical value, probative, and informative, which must be definitively preserved." (Law 8.159/91, Article 8, Section 3).
          The Brazilian press, across various segments, including Rede Globo's Fantástico program on May 22, 2005, Jornal Hoje on January 28, 2008, Folha de São Paulo newspaper on January 11, 2009, IstoÉ magazine on July 22, 2009, and UFO magazine in dozens of monthly issues starting from April 2004, published notable articles primarily focusing on the dissemination of ufologists' requests and their practical outcomes.
          In 2009, the three last-mentioned press outlets described, with front-page headlines, the opening of over two thousand pages of confidential documents sent directly from the Aerospace Documentation and History Center to the National Archive between October 2008 and September 2009, as well as an additional two thousand obtained through donations from civilians and military reservists. All documentation is official and is available for public consultation at the National Archive's Regional Coordination in the Federal District (COREG), a division under the Office of the President.

According to the articles, even though the Air Force has provided most of its declassified files due to the expiration of the secrecy period, documents considered fundamental for obtaining concrete data on the presence of UFOs in the national territory have been illegally omitted. One of the cases that exemplify this illegality is the absence, in the declassified information, of over sixteen hours of super 16mm films, hundreds of photographs, and numerous reports, originating from a secret operation of the Brazilian Air Force in the outskirts of Belém, which occurred between October and December 1977.

Therefore, with over 30 years of reservation, these documents should already be available. The former National Information Service, now the Brazilian Intelligence Agency, participated, to some extent, in this operation, which was given the code name "Operation Plate." The Folha de São Paulo, ISTOÉ magazines, and UFO publications even name some witnesses to the existence of the material, as have ufologists in their document submitted to the Executive.

In both this case of Operation Plate and others that were similarly not sent by the Ministry of Defense to the National Archive, witnesses are categorical in stating the existence of documents within the three Armed Forces.

To conclude, we inform that two bills are currently being processed in this House that are directly linked to the issue raised: Bill 5141/2009, authored by Dep. Guilherme Campos, which "Establishes the National Ufology Day"; and Bill 5228/2009, authored by the Executive, which "Regulates access to information as provided in XXXIII of Article 5, II of § 3, Article 37, and § 2 of Article 216 of the Constitution, and provides for other measures." Both bills, with the support of ufologists, are currently in the Constitution, Justice, and Citizenship Committee.

Bill 5141, of which I am the rapporteur, aligns with the aspirations of the community that studies the UFO phenomenon, and aims solely to establish an honor, respect, and seriousness for those seeking the truth, distancing prejudiced views of a national and planetary study that has already completed 60 years.

Bill 5228 aims to perfect the mechanisms for accessing public information. And that is exactly what researchers of the Brazilian Commission of Ufologists are also seeking with their questions to Minister of Defense Nelson Jobim.

Therefore, this request enables the Ministry of Defense and the Federal Government, by extension, to fulfill the requests of the scholars supported by an already existing legal framework.
