1. PLANNING
OF THE WORKFORCE
--- seg-28d7b00f84643a2e1f89a349 [failed-protected-token-check] ---
Workforce Planning (WFP) is the dimensioning that articulates human resources management with strategic planning and budgeting, public competitions, and mobility policies. Its objective is to ensure that the public administration has, in a continuous and sustainable manner, servers and collaborators with the necessary profile, quantity, and competencies to execute public policies and achieve institutional goals.

The System for Personnel Dimensioning (Sisdip), a technological tool provided by the central agency of the Civil Servants System (Sipec), supports management, registers data, stores information, and performs dimensioning calculations through deliverables, such as aggregating qualitative and quantitative indicators regarding the workforce in federal public institutions.

The system is based on evidence, demographic scenarios, capacity analysis, and inclusion and diversity policies, ensuring a balance between efficiency, equity, and fiscal sustainability. It was developed by the Ministry of Management and Innovation in Public Services (MGI) in partnership with the University of Brasília (UnB), with data processing by Serpro, featuring a robust database that enables faster mapping of activities.

The Workforce Dimensioning Manual provides strategies and best practices for institutionalization and replication of WFP in various institutions. It includes methodologies for diagnosing and projecting personnel needs (quantitative and qualitative), such as workload analysis, retirement scenarios, critical competency mapping, and identifying institutional capacity gaps.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) about:

**Server Allocation Based on Workforce Dimensioning**

This document provides answers to common questions regarding server allocation based on workforce dimensioning, in partnership with the University of Brasília (UnB).

**1. What is Workforce Dimensioning?**

Workforce dimensioning is a process to determine the optimal number of personnel needed for specific roles or units within a public service organization. It considers factors such as workload demands, strategic goals, and available resources.

**2. How does server allocation relate to workforce dimensioning?**

Server allocation, or resource allocation, is a crucial aspect of implementing workforce dimensioning. It involves distributing IT resources, such as servers, based on the identified staffing needs and workload demands.

**3. What are the benefits of server allocation based on workforce dimensioning?**

* **Improved efficiency:** Allocating servers according to staffing needs ensures that resources are used effectively, avoiding underutilization or over-allocation.
* **Enhanced service delivery:** By aligning server capacity with workload demands, public service organizations can provide faster and more reliable service to citizens.
* **Better resource management:**  This approach promotes transparent and data-driven resource allocation decisions, leading to better financial management.

**4. What information is needed for server allocation based on workforce dimensioning?**

Key information includes:

* **Workload data:** Detailed information on the volume and nature of tasks performed by each role or unit.
* **Organizational structure:** A clear understanding of the reporting lines, roles, and responsibilities within the organization.
* **Strategic goals:** The organization's short-term and long-term objectives, which can guide resource allocation decisions.
* **Available resources:** Details about existing server capacity, network infrastructure, and other IT resources.

**5. What tools are available for server allocation based on workforce dimensioning?**

Several tools and methodologies can be used, including:

* **Workload analysis tools:** Software that helps quantify and analyze workload patterns.
* **Resource planning tools:** Applications that facilitate the allocation of resources, including servers, based on identified needs.
* **Data visualization tools:** Software that presents data in a clear and understandable format, aiding in decision-making.

**6. How can I learn more about workforce dimensioning and server allocation?**

This document provides foundational information. For in-depth guidance, consult:

* **Ministry of Public Management and Innovation (MGI) publications:**  Access relevant books, guides, and reports on workforce planning and resource allocation.
* **Partnership with the University of Brasília (UnB):** Explore joint research projects, training programs, and expert consultations.

**7. What are some common challenges in server allocation based on workforce dimensioning?**

Challenges may include:

* **Data availability:** Obtaining accurate and complete workload data can be difficult.
* **Organizational resistance:** Change management and resistance to new allocation methods can arise.
* **Dynamic work environments:** Workloads and organizational needs can fluctuate, requiring ongoing adjustments to server allocation.

**8. How is this process evaluated and improved?**

Regularly review the outcomes of server allocation against established performance metrics. Collect feedback from users and stakeholders, and adjust allocation strategies as needed based on lessons learned.

**Subject:** Anistia for Federal Public Servants and Related Procedures

**Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs):**

**1. Law No. 8.878/1994:**

This law grants amnesty to federal public servants who were dismissed or discharged between March 16, 1990, and September 30, 1992, due to violations of the legal order or political motivations, allowing them to return to their previous positions.

**2. Interministerial Decree No. 5,115 (2004):**

Established a commission to review administrative acts related to the anistia processes provided for in Law No. 8.878/1994.

**3. Decrees No. 1,498 and 1,499 (1995) and No. 3,363 (2000):**

These decrees refer to the anistia processes addressed in Law No. 8.878/1994, and were revised by subsequent legislation.

**4. Decree No. 9,261 (2018):**

Defines the competence and procedure for handling residual issues related to the former Interministerial Commission (Decree No. 5.115/2004), specifically for processing dossiers of those anistied by Law No. 8.878/1994.

**5. Decree No. 6,077 (2007):**

Regulates the return to service of servants and employees anistied by Law No. 8.878/1994, outlining procedures and conditions for their reintegration, including returning to their original positions, and amends Decree No. 5.115/2004 by stating that the process must respect budgetary needs and availability.

The Special Commission of Former Federal Territories (CEEXT) is a temporary commission overseen by the Ministry of Labor Relations, with the primary objective of analyzing and ruling on applications for inclusion of civil servants from the former territories of Rondônia, Amapá, and Roraima into the public service payroll upon its dissolution.

Its main responsibilities are outlined in the following normative acts:

1. **CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS (EC 60/2009, 79/2014, and 98/2017):** These amendments establish the constitutional basis for the Union's obligation to absorb these civil servants.

2. **LAW No. 13.681/2018: **This legal instrument translates the amendments and establishes salary, wage, and benefit tables applicable to civilians, military personnel, and employees on the dissolution payroll.

3. **DECREE No. 12.261/2024 (Decree on Structure):** This administrative act organizes the CEEXT, outlining its functioning, establishing its structure (including chambers for each former territory), and reorganizing leadership positions to ensure the Commission has the necessary resources to implement the law and conclude enrollment processes.

4. **SRT/MGI MINISTRY OF LABOR RELATIONS ORDER No. 5.393 of July 4, 2025:** This order sets out criteria and procedures for the CEEXT to follow in analyzing, processing, and ruling on applications for transfer and enrollment in the federal public service upon its dissolution, including deadlines, workflows, document submission, and more.

**Dimension 6 - Retirement and Disengagement**

**6.1 Retirement**

**Systematic Manuals**

This dimension encompasses the set of policies, processes, and instruments aimed at managing the functional transition of a public servant, from retirement planning to the end of the employment relationship. Federal public service retirement involves different modalities and rules established by Constitutional Amendment 103/2019 (Pension Reform), which includes voluntary retirement, retirement by age and contribution time, and mandatory retirement at 75 years of age.

This dimension aims to ensure sustainability, institutional continuity, and dignity in the transition to inactivity, recognizing retirement as a legitimate stage of the work cycle and establishing guidelines for granting, maintaining, and paying retirement benefits. Additionally, Instruction Normative 2/2024 provides guidelines, criteria, and general procedures for retirement benefits for public servants who opted for the Complementary Pension Regime, while 96/2021 defines norms and flows for pension compensation between the Own Social Security Regime (RPPS) and the General Social Security Regime (RGPS).

In the international context, studies indicate that the absence of planning for disengagement and succession can lead to risks of discontinuity and loss of critical capabilities. The Referential Labor Cycle Model reinforces that the retirement phase should be treated as part of the servant's life cycle, not as a separate event.

In the context of public sector human resources management, reducing bureaucracy and litigation, streamlining processes, and ensuring that decisions are made based on clear, uniform, and updated norms is fundamental. These transversal themes permeate and influence all stages of the employment cycle, impacting both individuals and organizations. They are not confined to specific moments but rather shape the experience of public servants and the effectiveness of human resources management.

There are five transversalities:

1. **Normative Update and Legislative Consolidation of Personnel**: This involves reviewing and updating laws and regulations governing public sector employment to ensure clarity and consistency.

2. **Digital Transformation and Integration of Structural Systems**: Utilizing digital technologies to optimize processes, automate tasks, and facilitate access to services and information for employees.

3. **Generation and Transparency of Evidence and Data Security**: Creating and sharing reliable evidence and ensuring data security throughout the employment cycle.

4. **Institutional Dialogue and Public Governance**: Establishing effective communication channels and structured dialogue between employees, management, and relevant institutions to address concerns and improve practices.

Digital transformation enhances efficiency, agility, and communication in human resources management, leading to better policies and practices that benefit all stakeholders in the federal public sector.
