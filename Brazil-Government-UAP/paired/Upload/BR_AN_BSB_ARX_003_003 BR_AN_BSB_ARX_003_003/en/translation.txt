--- seg-91d2d047df5fad4208cebace [needs-review] ---
April 13 to 16, 1991, the Brasília newspapers reported an incident involving the sighting of an Unidentified Flying Object-UFO in the Papuda region, with a large dose of sensationalism, as is unfortunately usual for much of the press.
In an attempt to clarify the facts, the Paranormal Phenomena Study Center of the Multidisciplinary Advanced Studies Center at the University of Brasília (NEFP/CEAM/UnB) created the UFO Study Group-GEU. The Group went into the field to collect statements and gather data from the responsible agencies and to study what became known as the "Papuda Case."
Thus, having obtained several statements and important documents, and believing the amount of data collected to be sufficient, we begin what is intended to be a broad and open assessment of the incident.
The specific statements about 11.04.91 were given by Lieutenant Jorge Luiz Fideles Damasceno and Private Reinaldo Sergio Oliveira. We then sought confirmation of the data from their colleagues. In the meantime, some of them decided to reveal other incidents occurring at different times, mostly in the Papuda region. Although these incidents will not be analyzed in depth at present, they will be cited and briefly described for illustration.
Considering the witnesses' credibility (see Evaluation of the Incident, p. Jtf-^*), we will omit the full presentation of the statements concerning the incident of 11/4/91. They will be used at different points as the analysis proposed here calls for or requires. These statements are available in our archives for consultation.
First, the specific statements about the incident of 11.04.91 will be used to cross-check information against documents supplied by the Ministry of Aeronautics Social Communication Center-CECOMSAER, the Brazilian Air Command and Defense Center-NUCOMDABRA, and the Aerodrome Meteorological Center-CMA-BR.
We will then briefly report the incidents occurring on other occasions involving Military Police guarding Papuda prison.
Finally, we propose to assess the incident and undertake a general analysis of the explanatory hypotheses for the phenomenon studied, before drawing our conclusions.

Although rare nowadays, some works rich in definitions and historical presentations of ufology can still be found (see bibliographical references).
It is worth recalling the original meaning of the term, which, although young, already seems somewhat forgotten and therefore distorted.
The expression Unidentified Flying Object-UFO designates only a category of phenomena whose study within the scientific method has not been possible in a way that identifies it, as happens in other areas of knowledge where the object of study is isolated from its environment and compartmentalized. Quantitative or qualitative analysis leads to a deeper understanding of the object studied. With the UFO phenomenon, this has so far been at least very difficult, if not impossible. Through negligence, indifference, or who knows what, the scientific community has not dealt properly with the subject. In this context, specific behavioral characteristics must also be considered, such as the phenomenon's ability to evade attempts to approach it, reinforcing its intelligent aspect and sending researchers in search of new methods*. As we shall see later, such behavior by both the researcher and the object may justify an interdisciplinary approach, which, in the author's view, is the only way to obtain coherent results in UFO research using the scientific method.
It therefore characterizes something little known that should not necessarily, and from the outset, be interpreted as an object of alien origin. Its origin is still unknown and its nature unidentified, except regarding intelligent aspects. These represent the probable cause of its ability to violate clearly known physical laws. However, the limited knowledge of UFOs does not invalidate attempts to study them or rule out the need to do so. Quite the contrary: it encourages and provides sufficient reasons to undertake such study seriously and urgently**.

The Internment and Reintegration Center-CIR currently accommodates approximately 700 prisoners serving sentences ranging from 10 to 15 years. The Brasília Custody Center-NCB accommodates about 400 prisoners awaiting trial.
It is isolated from the urban environment, and most of its area has no lighting. Only the interior of the maximum-security prison, CIR, is lit by 10 high-power floodlights. It has 10 elevated guard towers and ground guard posts.
The entire security area covers 5km². CIR occupies an area of 1200m² on a single level. This area contains the Custody Center-NCB, the Internment and Reintegration Center-CIR, and the 3rd Independent Military Police Company-3a CPMInd.
The 3a CPMInd is in a favorable position for observing and guarding the security area: it is elevated and less brightly lit. NCB is between it and CIR.
For a first-time visitor, the atmosphere is tense, different, quite unusual. Even the police officers and agents who work there, accustomed to dealing daily with a wide variety of situations, feel this "charged" atmosphere.
