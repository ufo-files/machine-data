The Brazilian military regime investigated UFOs and ET activity in the 1970s.

MAURICIO STYCER
Local Reporting

The Brazilian military regime (1964-1985) investigated, in the 1970s, cases of alleged UFO sightings and spied on the activities of Brazilian experts in ETs (extraterrestrials).

Documents from the now-defunct Dops (Department of Political and Social Order), currently stored in the State Archive of São Paulo, show that so-called intelligence services wasted time and money investigating the "abduction" of a paulista merchant by crewmembers of an unidentified flying object (UFO).

The documents also show that Dops called two ufologists, people who study UFOs, to testify and even infiltrated an agent to attend the regular meetings of a group of UFO enthusiasts.

Located by historian Cláudio Tsuyoshi Suenaga, the documents confirm something that Brazilian ufologists have always suspected but that the scientific community only saw as another symptom of the obsession that afflicts many of these scholars: "The military always worried about the UFO phenomenon," says Suenaga.

"X-Files"

The historian, who is preparing a master's thesis on the subject at Unesp (Paulista State University), goes further: "It's clear that there is a 'X-Files' in Brazil. Until today, there is concern from the Brazilian government on this topic."

Suenaga is referring to the American science fiction series "X-Files," aired in Brazil by TV Record and Fox. The show tells the story of two FBI agents involved in the investigation of unexplained cases (UFOs, ETs, paranormal phenomena, etc), archived under the rubric "X-Files."

On April 28, 1974, in the vicinity of Guarantã (423 km northwest of São Paulo), an incident occurred that led the state police (Dops) to investigate. That day, according to the report sent to the police by the then-director of the Dops, Lúcio Vieira, Onilson Patero traveled in a flying saucer from Guarantã to Colatin.

The investigation into the "extraterrestrial" incident was conducted by a Dops agent, who, disguised, attended a meeting of ufologists in January 1975. The agent's report, sent to Romeu Tuma, then-director-general of the Dops, revealed that Onilson Patero had indeed encountered a UFO, but that the encounter was fabricated.
