IV - Therefore, the COMDABRA registers in its files the report or testimony of any person, pilot or not, civilian or military, Brazilian or foreign, who has seen or believes they have seen an OVNI in Brazilian airspace, provided this information is officially sent to the COMDABRA. Currently, the report must be on the HOTEL Traffic Control Form (annexed model), prepared by the control bodies of the SISDABRA and sent to this Combined Command. (Answer to questions #1 and #2).

V - The reports and other OVNI news in Brazilian airspace that come to the knowledge of the COMDABRA are usually cataloged through the completion of the HOTEL Traffic Control Forms. (Answer to question #3).

VI - The occurrence in Brazilian airspace most similar to the OVNI overflight from Mexico is on May 19, 1986, in the cities of São José dos Campos (SP) and Anápolis (GO). The COMDABRA has in its records the Incident Report of the Command, which includes the reports of the pilots and flight controllers involved in the episode and copies of journalistic publications that covered this event. (Answer to question #4).

VII - The Brazilian Aerospace Defense Command does not possess information regarding the French Government's initiative to ensure its citizens free access to information about OVNI incursions into French airspace. (Answer to question #5).

VIII - Regarding the possible investigation of the existence of UFOs by the FAB, the COMDABRA is unaware of any evidence to that effect. There is material in the COMDABRA files related to reports of possible OVNI occurrences, however, the existing documentation, classified as confidential/secret, does not present conclusive opinions regarding the existence or non-existence of such objects. (Answer to question #6).

IX - Regarding the event of May 19, 1986, in the cities of São José dos Campos and Anápolis, the former Minister of Aeronautics, Ten-Brig.-do-Ar OCTÁVIO JÚLIO MOREIRA LIMA, ordered that all facts established be fully disclosed to the media so that, officially, the Brazilian society would be aware of these events. Thus, the individuals involved in the episode were instructed to declare everything they experienced, which was widely publicized by the media at that time. This event was investigated by the Air Defense Command (COMDA), and the results of the investigation are found in the summary of the Incident Report, annexed.

Finally, the COMDABRA is unaware of any study within the Brazilian government aimed at investigating phenomena related to UFOs. (Answer to question #7).

APPENDIX 2 TO INFORMATION NO. 01/04/COMDABRA, DATED JUNE 30, 2004
SUMMARY OF THE INCIDENT REPORT
        REGARDING THE EVENT OCCURRED ON MAY 19, 1986
--- seg-03e87709774796d797974635 [needs-review] ---
**INTRODUCTION**

This report is intended to inform the authorities of the Ministry of Aeronautics, in particular the Honorable Minister, about the events that occurred on May 19, 1986, regarding the information provided by air traffic control and air defense agencies, as well as the intercepting pilots involved in the incident.
--- seg-2a2b9d61fbb2063dd117233c [needs-review] ---
2. INITIAL CONSIDERATIONS

   The existing radars in the area of the incident were basically intended for air traffic control, not being specifically used for air defense. In this case, their technical limitations made air space surveillance deficient in detecting targets. Thus, tracking the aerial movements detected at the time was greatly hindered, as there was no condition to maintain radar visualization, as the contacts obtained were not strong enough to form an initialized track.

   The information provided to the pilots by the Military Operations Center of CINDACTA 1 (COpM1) was carried out through personal evaluation and experience of the air defense controllers.
--- seg-e1bc4374c9b98e862ba235c6 [needs-review] ---
3. DESCRIPTION OF EVENTS

   Events are described in chronological order.

   1. 12:15Z       The Brasília Area Control Center (ACC-BR) informs COPM1 that the tower controller in São José dos Campos (SJC) had observed yellow, green, and orange lights moving over the city. Simultaneously, the controller informs the São Paulo Approach Control (APP-SP), which confirms radar contacts in the SJC area;

   2. 00:08Z        The pilot of PT-MBZ spots lights at a position on the 150° radial of the SJC VOR, moving from E to W. According to the pilot, the lights appeared as large red stars;

   3. 00:39Z         The air defense alert (Alerta DA) aircraft from Santa Cruz Air Base (BASC) was activated. The Alerta DA was activated due to radar detections of several "plots" in the SJC area. Although these detections did not constitute clear radar echoes, they were used to vector the alert aircraft, with the intention of allowing them to continue in the final interception phase using their onboard radar;

   4. 01:10Z       The Anápolis Approach Control (APP-AN) reported detecting some echoes on its terminal radar. These echoes were not being picked up by the COPM1 radar. The CODA ordered the activation of Anápolis' Alerta DA alert due to APP-AN reporting receiving forward and speed definitions from the radar echo.

   5. 01:34Z        The first Alerta DA aircraft from BASC took off. This aircraft was vectored to the western sector of SJC, an area where some radar echoes were being received. While level at FL 170 (approximately 5,200 m), the aircraft's pilot reported seeing a white light below their flight level, which subsequently rose and positioned itself 10° above the aircraft. Tracking was maintained until FL 330 (approximately 10,000 m). It was also reported that the light briefly changed from white to red, green, and then back to white, remaining in that color.

6. 0148Z         Takeoff of the first DA alert aircraft from Anápolis Air Base (BAAN). The aircraft was vectored to an area where radar detection was being obtained by APP-AN, which transmitted its information to COpM 1, who controlled the DA alert from these data. This procedure was adopted because the air defense radar was not picking up any traffic. Five presentations (intercept procedures, where the alert aircraft is directed at the target) were conducted, and although the pilot made radar contact, there was no visual contact. In one presentation, the aircraft got as close as 2 miles to the target, whose trajectory alternated between zigzagging and making sharp right turns. The target's speed fluctuated suddenly, sometimes allowing the interceptor to close in, and other times increasing significantly, resulting in distances as the interceptor flew at supersonic speeds. Contact was lost, leading to the abandonment of the pursuit.

7. 0150Z         Takeoff of the second DA alert aircraft from BASC. This aircraft was vectored to the southern sector of S J C. The pilot reported seeing a red light at the position provided by COpM 1, confirming land-based radar detection. After a few minutes, it was determined that there was no approach towards the light, and it then went out while radar contact was lost. During this presentation, thirteen "plots" briefly appeared in the rear of the interceptor. A 180-degree turn (on the horizontal plane) was commanded so the pilot could check the rear sector, but there was no visual or radar contact.

8. 0217Z        Takeoff of the second DA alert aircraft from BAAN. The aircraft made no visual or radar contact.

9. 0236Z      Takeoff of the third DA alert aircraft from BAAN. Similar to the previous DA alert aircraft, it made no visual or radar contact.
