 Now, what have we here?    The UFO Sighter editorial actually saying something
about UFOs?

 As incredible as it may seem, though, here it is.  The Sighter has of late
left the direct saucer reporting to others, devoting ourselves to philosophic
and administrative affairs.  But at the heart of things is still the UFO
mystery itself.

 At the Sighter office in Atlanta (which serves also as the office of kindred
organizations) a skywatch was held in March of this year.  It was a one night
affair held under the auspices of Project:  UFO Skywatch, a local and more­
or-less ad hoc organization, on a city-wide (area-wide) basis.

 The fokywatch, in case you're wondering, was not held in the office (that
might be carrying "armchair UFOlogy" a bit too far) , but this did serve as
the communications headquarters. The program was fortunate enough to receive
some radio and television publicity, as well as a follow-up broadcast on a
local radio station with one of the skywatch organizers.   In addition to this
the two major local newspapers carried a classified ad on the watch in ad­
vance of it.

 The last time I know of a major skywatch being mounted in this area was
during the furor of the March, 1966 flap.  That was a huge success, but it was
such in the wake of one of the biggest flaps in history. This time it was
more-or-less being done cold. Other than convenience the date was to a con­
siderable extent random.   ----


 Which brings us around to an interesting point.  The publicity and the
weather were with us, but on this largely randon date so were the saucers.
I speak now not of the quality of the reports, but of the fact that we d1d
get UFO reports.  A considerable number at that.  One could even class 1t as
a flap.

 There is an interesting word: Flap.  It is an integral part of UFOlogy ter­
minology.  And yet, is there really any such thing as a flap in terms of in­
creased sightings in a basic sense, or are there only publicity flaps?  In
recent discussion of this possibility with James W. Moseley, the prominent
UFOlogist, I believe my trend of thought on the subject has tended more in
this direction. The Atlanta skywatch, it seems to me, adds once again to this
thesis.

 Experiments on random days in other areas should also be tried.  Perhaps
there are flaps, but perhaps the flap is only another probable UFOlogical myth
that should go the way of the five year cycle.

                                AND, AS I WAS SAYING   •   •   •


                               by Eugene R   Steinberg,
                               Program Coordinator, Radio Station   KDOM

         Some two years have passed since my writings graced the pages of this
magazine.  Now, with thousands of miles of travel behind me, I have time to
sit back and reflect over many of the things I. said in days of old. Perhaps
the few readers who·do recall my earlier writings in "The UFO Sighter," un­
doubtedly with considerable pique, are awaiting the occasion when I shall re­
tract many of the sweeping pronouncements I made.


         Alas,   that occasion is not goint to arrive in the foreseeable future    •   •   •


       Frankly, I spent two years content to read other people's words, and
if I felt a bit hot under the collar at times, I managed to hold my tongue     •   •   •


or should I say typewriter.

       My most consistent adversary has been Steve Erdmann.  Though often I
have attempted to reach some sort of understanding with him, he has come to
regard me as the personification of Satan himself.  My letters are banned
from his domicile; thus, only in print am I afforded the opportunity to make
my views known to him    .•


       So it was not entirely unexpected that Mr. Erdmann would be the one to
force me to break my long silence.  Perhaps it was just as well.

         I really thought I had said just about all that could be said about
NICAP.    They have plodded along for some 12 years now, and the ups and downs
of NICAP seem directly related to the comings and goings of the saucers them­
selves---if only because the comings of the saucers arouse us kooks to a
fever pitch, and naturally the members of the news media  with a flair for
melodramatics can surfeit the newspapers with fanciful stories of the coming
of the aliens.  Memberships pour into NICAP headquarters, and the powers­
that-be in that troubled organization pronounce the imminence of congressional
hearings, and the end of Air Force secrecy, but    •   •   •


         The UFOs are off again to who-knows-where, and NICAP has to implore
those members who remain in the fold to cough up some more cash, to keep Keyhoe
and his' clan afloat until the saucers invade· us again in force.

         Does the reader see a pattern emerging?

       NICAP seems to survive only because a flap comes along to perk things
up.  NICAP cannot do a single thing in and of itself to keep flying saucers
on the front pages of the nation's newspapers, and foremost in the minds of
the public.  Right now Vietnam and race riots take the honors.


       Yes, Mr,Erdmann, NICAP is an abysmal failure, and so is every other
flying saucer group in the world!
                                                                               4

        O.K.,   so it's another one of those sweeping pronouncements.


       But, is there any doubt in anyone's mind of the accuracy of this
"sweeping pronouncement?"


         I know most of you will point to the great strides made by NICAP,
APRO,   and other groups in alerting the public of the existence of flying
saucers.   But where is it that most people learn about saucers in the first
place?   Naturally, television, radio (the medium which provides my bread,
as it were), and the press.   - The saucer press excepted, of course.


       It's the major flaps the succeed in showing everyone that flying
saucers are skipping across the skies   that the aliens are among us.
                                        •   •   •                       The
total circulation of all flying saucer magazines combined is probably less
than 150,000, yet the most recent Gallop poll on the subject confirms that
many millions of people believe in flying saucers; extraterrestrial, inter­
dimensional, or what have you.  These people, for the most part, didn't
learn about flying saucers from flying saucer magazines.  They learned about
it from their local news media.  Books help, I admit, but it usually takes
less than 100,000 in sales to put a book at the top of the best seller lists.

       Frankly, Ian Fleming, Edgar Rice Burroughs and the Beatles reach far
more people than flying saucer magazines!

       I may have precipitated considerable dissension in the ranks of UFO­
logists by the foregoing remarks.

         When I single out NICAP to criticize,      I do so because,    of all the UFO
groups that have come and gone in the past twenty-one years,           they have shown
the most promise to separate saucer-fact from saucer-fiction.  Yet despite
the grandiose ideals that are professed by all UFO groups, large and small,
and considerably greater resources to back them up, NICAP is just another
flying �aucer club.  The only reason they have not sunk under entirely is the
fact of Size and prestige.
           '.;--
       Mr. Erdmann replies to my arguments by rendering the same quilty ver­
dict on "Saucer News", and on me due to my past association with that magazine.
I am not in\ favor of guilt by association, and furthermore, "Saucer News" does
not claim to\be the nation's sole authority on flying saucers.   NICAP does!


       One of the numerous publications dedicated to the memory of Mr. Webster
defines "Egotist" as a conceited, boastful person.  Despite Mr. Erdmann's
efforts to compile his own dictionary, I prefer to stick with the experts,
for otherwise what little unity we have in the English language would be lost
in a moras9 of confusion.


        Ego is the one thing that gives us kooks the nerve to speak up and lay
-bare our unpopular ideas.

         I thought it was c-ommon knowledge among readers of "The Sighter" and
                                                                      5

"Saucer News" that I spent many hours as a welcome visitor to NICAP, prior
to the period of my association with "Saucer News". Moreover, I was active
in their recently revived New York Affiliate for several years.

       In concluding these ramblings about NICAP, et cetera, I should like
to mention the current issue of "Saucer News, " in which Mr. Erdmann retracts
much of his earlier sentiments about NICAP.   Let Mr. Erdmann's own words
stand in rebuttal to his earlier remarks, as published in the Fall-Winter
issue of this magazine.

       The rise or fall of NICAP. will have no influence on our search for
"The Answer."  Also, it would seem that the political implications of the
rampant inter-club squabbling have, if anything, seriously hampered our eff­
orts to get somewhere.

        So, let these words stand as my final critique of UFO organizations,
per se.   I've given. up hope that any ·of them are willing or able to make any
progress.   It's high time we dispense with such trivia and work toward getting
some facts, and getting them soon.

       For, when we stick with the status quo, we are aiding in the pre­
servation of the age-old tradition of secrecy in UFOs and related mysteries.
In-fighting ought to be left to the political parties, who seem so much more
adept at this ancient art.  Let's try to find out why UFO groups can't or
won't succeed.  Is it our own incompetence, or the immensity of the task we
choose to tackle that makes us fail?  Or, fellow fans of AR, is it true that
persons unknown seek to stop us in our tracks?

       There is a saying that there are some people who make things. happen,
some who watch things happen and others who have no .idea what's happening.

       Which are you?

berg may write to him at 242 Tenth Street, Windom, Minnesota  56101.)
