FASTER THAN LIGHT : Interstellar Travel in the New Physics .                            by D . A. Newton .

        At       the   beginning of   this century      two   scientific          theories quickly           emerged
from the ruins of the Newtonian world view , and changed forever the science of
physics:         Relativity and Quantum      theory .    Of   these        theories ,      Einstein ' s      Special
Theory of Relativity is the mos t well known .                    Most     people have heard of E=mc2 ,
t h e Twins Paradox or Time Dilation , even if they don ' t understand what these terms
mean. But          General Relativity ,   Einstein ' s    later      theory ,      and Quantum        theory     are
l i t t le known by the general populace . And even physicists , after all these years ,
are still making new discoveries in the fields of General Relativity (G.R.) and
Quantum Mechanics ( Q.M.) .
        The possibility o f time travel has              been circulating in scientific ci rcles
for some          years now ,   and has even     gained a         certain        respectabi lity .    Last      year
                                                                                                                   1
Scientific American carried an article on "The Quantum Physics of Time Travel"
explaining the paradoxes that can arise when time travel is allowed. The popular
                                                                               2
American magazine Astronomy carried an article entitled "Tunnels through Time"
back in 1992. The Daily Telegraph ,              last September ,          ran an article by Dr Michio
Kaku of the City University , New York , explaining how all we need to do to travel
in t i me is harness a huge energy source; we have all the equations we nee d . 3
                                                                4
Even Jenny Randles has a book out at the moment on time travel.
        Most of these ideas for time machines rely on what objects called wormho les .
These are tunnels that connect two regions of space and time . By travelling through
a wormhole , you can go from A to B without passing through any point in between .
Handy as this is , wormholes are             not easy to find and manage .                   But in theory at
least , it can be done. Black holes can be used as wormholes , and miniature q uantum
wormholes also exist according to theory . Some mechanism could possible be devised
to stretch a quantum wormhole into a usable macroscopic wormhole.
        While travelling through time seems to be all the rage at the moment ,                                 there
is also the other exciting possibility : travelling vast distances in short periods
of    t i me .    Relativity shows us     that   space and time            are    not    separate quantit ies .
The equations deal with spacetime.               Not only is time travel possible in theory ,
but so is t ravelling faster than light .
        But hold on . Doesn ' t relativity forbid anything from travelling faster than
light? Yes , Special relativity does. But as the name suggests , Special Relativity
is a special case . I t deals only with flat , Euc lidian spacetime and inertial frames
of reference. Neither exist in nature . Matter and energy bend spacetime , and when
you    deal with huge amounts of matter and strong gravitational fields ( such as
in a black hole ) all sorts of wonderful things become possible .

S pacetime travel through black holes .

        Black holes are regions of space where the gravitational field is so strong
that even light is not fast enough to escape , i . e . the escape velocity from the

have formed in the moments after the Big Bang when the density of the primeval
universe was high enough for them to be c reated. Large black holes are formed

star rushes inwards under the force of gravity and gets crushed in the centre
