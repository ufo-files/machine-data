Pseudoscience
 and the U.S.
Patent Process
Mark Banash, Ph.D.
Did you know that an invention doesn't have to be described in a scientifically accurate way to be granted a U.S.
Patent? Learn why respected companies will invest millions in a startup that cannot explain its processes and
products rationally. And hear how companies can sell such products without understanding how they work.

Mark Banash has a Ph.D. in Chemistry from Princeton and a B. A. with a major in Chemistry and minors in Math
and English from the University of PA. He is currently employed as a research scientist at Millennium Inorganic
Chemicals in Baltimore as well as at Howard Community College as adjunct faculty. He also writes science fiction
and serves as the producer of the audio series “The Funnt File” which often deals with skeptical themes.

                                                 FESTSCHRIFT 2000
This Saturday, October 7, the Skeptics Society will be holding its annual conference at the Beckman Auditorium on the

                                     EVOLUTION IS STILL IN THE NEWS
A report evaluating the teaching of evolution in public schools gave six states scores of 100 for their science education
standards.
        California, Connecticut, Indiana, New Jersey, North Carolina and Rhode Island all received scores of 100 and
grades of A.States that scored in the 90s and received A's were South Carolina, Delaware, Hawaii, and Pennsylvania.
        Grades of B went to: Colorado, Minnesota, Vermont, Washington, Michigan, Arizona, Idaho, Massachusetts,
Missouri, Montana, Oregon, South Dakota, Utah and the District of Columbia.
        Grades of C went to: Maryland, New Mexico, Nevada, New York, Nebraska, Louisiana and Texas.
        Unsatisfactory grades of D went to: Arkansas, Kentucky, Wisconsin, Virginia, Alaska and Illinois.
        Failing grades of F went to: Wyoming, Maine, Ohio, Oklahoma, New Hampshire, Florida, Alabama, North Dakota,
Georgia, Mississippi, West Virginia and Tennessee. Kansas's grade was F-minus.
        Iowa was not included because it does not have statewide standards. Each district creates its own standard.
