                  11THE U.F.O. EXPERIENCE..

                     By J. Allen Hynek.

     In 195 2 Dr. Allen Hynek, while Director of Ohio State      l1i\
University 's McMills Observatory, was invited to join the
organisation of Project Blue Book as an astronomical consul­
tant. As most of us know, Project Blue Book came into being
in 1948 when the United States Government appointed an Air
Force panel to investigate whether or not the world- wide
reported phenomena of U.F.O.s could be verified and if so,
whether or not this constituted a threat to the United States
security.

     When Dr. Hynek joined Project Blue Book he was deter­
mined to show the public that the true scientific method was
certain to expose 'flying saucers ' as unscientific. However,
as the book sets out, this intention never materialised.
For a period of 20 years, he remained a consultant to the Air
Force and naturally he had access to all reports of the Blue
Book files. He also testified several times before congres­
sional groups which expressed considerable interest in the
subject.

     It is obvious from the very beginning that Dr. Hynek is
a thoroughbred investigator who understands scientific pro­
cedure; he certainly is not carried away by emotional impul­
ses. It must be this quality in him which made him aware of
the emerging patterns of the sightings. He reasons that if
U.F.O.s are indeed figments of the imagination, it is strange
that the imagination of those who report U.F.O.s from all over
the world should be so restricted to definite patterns! In­
deed, he says: 11Precisely because the spectrum of reports
of strange sightings is narrow can they be studied. If each
strange report was unique and their totality ran the gamut os
all conceivable strange accounts, scientific investigation of
such a chaotic panoply would be impossible. Scientific study
presupposes date patterns ahd a measure of r�peatability, and
                          - 8 -

by and large U.F.O. reports lend themselves to classification
within their domains of strangenesso It is these we shall
pursue ...

     Dr. Hynek' s first purpose in writing this book, then,
was to construct prototypes of major observational categories
of U.F.O.s rather than to present detailed accounts of indi­
vidual sightings. As a consequence he rests the U.F.O. prob­
lem on the prototypes of the following categories: nocturnal
lights, daylight discs, radar/visual sightings and close en­
counters of two kinds.

     His second purpose is to expose the unscientific way in
which the Air Force handled the Blue Book Project and the
prejudice of its investigators. He is up in arms that the
scientific world through the publication of the Condon report
and the United States Government through the closing of Pro­
ject Blue Book, has officially put an end to the speculation
about U.F.O.s.

      In October 1966 the University of Colorado and the U.S.
Air Force entered into a formal agreement to establish a
scientific committee to study the vexing problem of U.F.O.s
with which the Air Force had been saddled for 20 years.
Dr. E. Condon headed this committee, and two years later the
Condon report (937 pages) was published. This report was
responsible for the United States Government terminating all
official investigation into the problem of U.F.O.s. When
project Condon was only three months old, Dr. Condon has re­
portedly said: 11It is my inclination right now to recommend
that the Government get out of this business.. (of U.F.O.s) .
1 My attitude right now is that there is nothing to it... The
Condon report seems to be spiced with the same spirit.

     Dr. Hynek exposes the century- old reaction of the scien­
tific world with the words - 11It can 't be, therefore it isn 't...
On the other hand, he explains that most U.F.O. reports are
frustrating in the extreme, they contain so few facts that
this lack alone has deterred several scientists from devoting
time to the matter.
                               - 9 -

     It remains regrettable that people often remarked to Dr.
Hynek, after their contact with Project Blue Book officials,
that if they had an encounter again they would not report it.
What investigations were carried.out by Blue Book, and what
questions were asked, were almost always aimed at establishing
a misconception. Dr. Hynek has sometimes wondered if Project
Blue Book could be a front, a cover- up of the real work being
done by another agency secretly. For instance, it became
clear that no Blue Book case had been given F.B.I. treatment,
i.e. no case was followed through until every possible clue or
bit of evidence was obtained. On the contrary, reports came
in and were handled in a completely routine manner. Also, if
the project was a full- fledged serious operation, the Air Force
headquarters would have appointed high- ranking officers, but
there were none.

     However, although the Condon report and the subsequent
ending of Project Blue Book closed the official U.S. door on
investigation, there are many groups which will carry on trying
to unravel the mysteries of U.F.O.s.

     Dr. Hynek is confident that now the comedy of the so- called
official projects are finished, serious work should begin.
He says that for a directed objective study of the phenomena,
the available data requires major organisation, systematiza­
tion, and the adoption of a uniform terminology for their des­
cription and evaluation. Only with such a system in operation
will it be possible that the new phenomenon may direct man 's
consciousness to understand new universal principles.

     11The U.F.0..!. Experience.. is an important book - a • must •
for ai those trying to ma1n ain a balanced outlook on the
U.F.O. question. And what a pleasure to be able to show this
book by such a prominent scientist to any who still regard
ufologists as 'cranks '!

                    SIGHTINGS   OVER     SOUTH   AFRICA.

18.6.1973.   DURBAN:

     Mr. Peter Mostert, his wife and two sons, observed a large
bright yellow circle of light at about 6.15 p.m.   It rose
above Howard College then drifted over to Westville, rose up
at right angles and disappeared.   Afterwards a fairly bright
light travelling from South West to North East moved across
the sky - travelling very fast - and this object was followed
by six others, evenly spaced one behind the other.   One of
the objects seemed to be slightly above the others, and they
flew in formation and veered off out of sight.

     On the same evening a couple living at the Bluff observed a
bright object travelling across the sky at about 6.00 p.m.

28.6.1973.    SPRINGS:

     Mr. and Mrs. I. Roberts of New Township, Springs, returned
home at 9.15 p.m. and found a saucer- shaped object, with a dome
on the top, apparently resting on the lawn in the back yard of
his house.   It was about three metres high, and even wider.
As Mr. Roberts reached the back gate and heard its low- pitched
droning, it rose into the air emitting a thick smoke and shining
with a bright orange light it disappeared into the sky, sur­
rounded by a haze of some sort.
