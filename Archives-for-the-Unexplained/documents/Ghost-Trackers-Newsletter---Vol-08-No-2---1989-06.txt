                                          MACABRE LONDON


                                                     By

                                              Tom Perrott


       Any city with over two thousand years                             of       history              to    its
credit     must          be      the     proud       possessor     of        many       secrets,            some
accumulating the dust of centuries and some                                  of     a    more           recent
origin.
        In an article of this length it is impossible to do justice
to     more  than  a  few  of  them, but it is to be hoped that the
following accounts will be of interest.
        On the East side of the City of London, close to  the  site
of     one of its many ancient gates, Aldgate, stands the Church of
St.     Botolph,         which was founded in 1125 and rebuilt                               in        1741-4.
Several       years           ago    I    had       heard that this ancient edifice was
        .
believed to house the mummified head of the Duke of Suffolk,                                                 who
was beheaded in 1554.                     He was the father of                the       hapless             Lady
Jane     Grey,         the      pathetic        grand-daughter of Henry VII,                       who upon
the death of Edward VI,                   was proclaimed Queen of England,                             but who
wore the Crown for only nine days,                         before she was               arrested             and
sentenced          to     death          by the followers of Mary Tudor,                      afterwards
Xary I.        I accordingly              visited     the church one day,               when       I was       on
business in         the area.              Inside     the building       I found             an        elderly
lady     who       was busily engaged in dusting the pews.                                   I asked her
whether the head was still in existence,                            whereupon            she           invited
ae    into the         Vestry,       where she solemnly removed a plain cardboard
box     from       under         the Vicar's table.               I took off the lid of the
box,    and there glaring balefully at me,                         was tbe          head.              It    was
not a grinning skull,                    but a perfectly perserved head,                      pickled by
the     elements           to a leatherlike consistency,                     as a result of                 many
years of exposure to the air,                        while impaled on one of                      the       many
spikes     which crowned the ancient Aldgate.                                There were even one
or two wisps of straggling hair,                          still   attached              to    the           ducal
cranium.
        Since       the        day       of    my    memorable visit a more enlightened
incumbent has made it possible for the head to be                                        given          decent
burial.       May the good Duke of Suffolk rest in eternal peace!
        Another          city        church not far from the Thames Embankment is
the Church          of        St.      James,       Garlickhythe,        which           at       one        time
contained          the     mummified remains of a Mediaeval                        gentleman,            which
were discovered here during                          excavations        in     1839.          His           exact
identity,              although          he    was     affectionately             known           of    "Jimmy
Garlickhythe",             was      unknown,        but as he     was    buried              in    a        glass
coffin,       a    status      symbol in        the Middle Ages,             it was      believed that
he     must       have        been a person of note.                Some considered that he
might have          been      one of the six           former     Lord       Mayors           of       London,
believed          to     have        been      buried in the church,              possibly Richard
Rothing,          builder of the original church                    in        1326.          Hie        coffin
bore     an       inscription             carrying        the following salutary warning;
"Stop Stranger,               stop as you pass by. As you are now,                            so once was
I.     As I am now,            You will be,           so pray prepare to follow me. "
     In 1942 during the German Blitz on London, a bomb hit  this
church, but it luckily failed to explode.   However it seemed to
have    the     effect           of    awakening             Jimmy      Garlickhythe             from     his
slumbers,        because          from       then onward s,           his ghost was alleged to
have been seen on several occasions.
       Once    when         an    American          tourist          and    her       two      sons      were
visiting       the      church,          the       elder        boy claimed to have seen the
figure of a d ried -up cor pse standing                            at      the    top       of    a     stone
stairway leading to the balcony.
       Bow     the remains of Jimmy are no longer to be seen,                                      because
he,    like the Duke of               Suffolk,              also   enjoys        eternal         sleep     in
hallowed      ground.
       Jeremy         Bentham          <1748-1832) was a political theorist and a
Radical,  who           wrote   treatises  on  prison  and  judicial   and
parliamentary           reform,   many  of his recommendations having been
since permanently adopted.                         He        was   one      of     the      founders       of
University           College,      London,         to which he was deeply attached,                        so
much so in          fact,    that he left            instructions           that    upon his           death,
his     body     should          first be dissected for the benefit of Medical
Science,       and     then       that       his      skeleton           should       be     permanently
stuffed        and mummified.                This was done except that his head was
found not to mummify satisfactorily, so an  artificial  one  was
placed  on his corpse instead.   He is now preserved permanently
in a glass case,             d ressed in one of his original                          suits,          wearing
white     gloves,           and       carrying          his faithful walking-stick,                     which
rejoiced in the name of "Dapple".                               He is to be seen sitting                   on
his     favorite chair and beholding the world in a most benevolent
fashion.         When I used            to    attend lectures at the college,                          I used
to     always        have to pass Jeremy                    in his display case.                 He always
appeared to regard                me     approvingly,              as      if    he      was     perfectly
satisfied with the course of studies that                                  I had chosen.
        As     an     ad ditional            check          upon   the      upkeep of his beloved
college,       Jeremy is sometimes said to descend from his                                      case     and
to     proceed        down the corridor to the Library,                            the tapping noise
of Dapple,          being distinctly heard                    en route.
        It used to be said that "the Streets of London                                         were     paved
with     gold",        which          might have been the case before the suns of
B mpire had         forever set.              However         one can safely             say     with     all
sincerity,           when        walking          around       the      crowded       streets          of the
Metropolis,          that one never has to proceed very far before coming
across a fascinating story.

                    THE     S IGHING GHOST OF C ARPENTER STREET


                                                    By


                                            Bob Barraco


        As we followed the real estate agent into the house we were
going to rent, we had no idea that a ghost was part of the deal.
Sure the place looks like it could harbor a spirit but, so  does
every        other       inbuilding
                             the  historic    district  of   Saelm,
Massachusetts.  Salem is one of the oldest . ports in the U. S. and
was first settled in 1626.   For a small city with such an early
and  notorious history,                     everyone expects to have a haunting here
and there.
        At first we experienced nothing unusual,                                at     least       nothing
that     couldn't           be     attributed            to    the age and condition of the
house.       The main house              itself was built in 1807.                     It is a lovely
three-story,             low- hipped- roof brick               Federal dwelling.            We live in
another      part of        the house         that was added             in 1860      to serve         as    a
kitchen and a second floor bedroom.                                 The next stage built on to
this is a garrison revival,                       constructed   to  emulate  the  early
homes  of the 17th century.                         This " carriage house" as it is now
referred to,         was        built in 1930         by      one   man,   a former owner              whose
name    I have       not    yet been         able    to discern.              This is an important
facet in this case as                   I suspect that              this      ·is    the   person           who
remains here -             in spirit.
        As     I    have         said,       my     wife       and       I encountered no unusual
occurrences,             until one night about two months after we moved in.
We had been watching a movie which had just ended, when directly
next to my wife on the couch came the most explicit sound  of  a
very heavy sigh.                The hair on the back of our necks stood up,                                 we
were     taken           a little by surprise but kept calm to watch for any
further development.                     To our      astonishment we saw in front of us
a    shadow of a partial figure walk                           by us and into the               next room.
I was not going to pursue it that night.
        The next month yielded                      nothing          spectacular,          although         we
were     most        keen        to watch for any paranormal events.                              We would
hear the sounds of                 closet         doors        opening        and     light       switches
turning,           but     when we would            check both doors and light switches
everything          remained           in    the     usual          positions.         This       activity
persisted           for     several          months but other sounds were added like
that of someone walking heavily on the floor above us.
        Then it happened,                what      I had been waiting for                  for     a    long
time,       a full blown ghostly- type apparition.                                  I had just got out
of bed on a bright April morning and was sitting  in  my  living
room  looking  out of the window when there appeared a man.   He
looked to be about age 60 or more and was                                  dressed         in     dungaree
clothing and work boots.                      He was walking down the brick walkway.
At first       I    thought he was an intruder,                       after all we are not near
the street side walk..                   He would have to walk past the main house
to get here.              Then    I saw his facial expression.                        It was not that
of     an     intruder           who     would look cautious,                 but he had a look of
belonging,          like he had every                right          to   be    where       he    was.         I
thought        that        he might be a sales person selling raffle tickets
or boosters.               So    I waited by my front door before opening it to


                                                      9
give him      time to       get to the door.               Just at the moment he should
have arrived at the door,                 I opened it finding an empty stoop.                                   I

left     the     property          without ·    me        seeing         him      as       the        yard    is
surrounded by a high fence.                    He had vanished!                   This has been the
only time I have seen this apparition.
        I decided to do some research for background information on
former     owners and for any                 significant events that might account
