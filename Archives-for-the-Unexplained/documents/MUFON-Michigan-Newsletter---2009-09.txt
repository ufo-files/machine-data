                                         By Roger Marsh UFO Examiner

         A Michigan family experienced a low flying triangle UFO April 5 with the typical three points of light, quick move-
ments, and silence, according to witness testimony from the Mutual UFO Network (MUFON) database.
         A man and his wife, his mother, and adult son, were traveling south on U.S. 127 after leaving Soaring Eagle Casino
when the encounter occurred.
         The encounter began when the man and his son noticed a red light in the sky at the 11 o’clock position. The light
seemed to be growing brighter and getting bigger at the same time. Then it suddenly "flashed to a brilliant, large white light."
         "I have never seen anything so bright before," the witness said. "It was a piercing white light."
         As the object moved across the highway directly overhead, they could make out three distinct white lights at its three
corners - forming the shape of a triangle.
         The object moved away from them and hovered over a field as they moved along at 70 mph.
         The following is the original and unedited witness statement. Please keep in mind that most UFO reports can be ex-
plained as something natural or manmade. If Michigan MUFON investigates and reports back on this case, I will update this
page. You can read my selected cases by type of UFO report at the UFO Traffic Report index page.

                                                Richard H. Hall:
                                       December 25, 1930 - July 17, 2009
                                            Richard H. Hall was a leading UFOlogist and proponent of the extraterrestrial hypothesis to
                                   explain UFO sightings; he had also written numerous books and articles dealing with the role of
                                   women in the American Civil War. On the morning of July 17, 2009, Richard Hall passed away due to
                                   a long battle with cancer.
                                            Hall was born on December 25, 1930 and held a bachelor's degree in philosophy from Tulane
                                   University in New Orleans. He lived most of his life in the Washington, D.C. area.
                                            From 1958 to 1969 he worked for the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenom-
                                   ena (NICAP), one of the most prominent and influential UFO civilian research groups in American
                                   history. He began as executive secretary, and eventually became NICAP's assistant director. In this
                                   role Hall was both an eyewitness and participant to much of the early history of the UFO phenomenon
                                   in the United States. Working with NICAP director Donald Keyhoe, he helped lobby the United States
                                   Congress to hold public hearings and investigations into the UFO phenomenon.


          In 1964 Hall researched, edited, and wrote much of The UFO Evidence, a compendium of the best
UFO sightings and incidents of the 1940's, 1950's, and early 1960's. A copy of The UFO Evidence was sent to
every member of Congress in 1964, and the book is still regarded by many UFO researchers and historians as
one of the best UFO books ever published.
          Following Keyhoe's ouster as NICAP director in 1969, Hall left NICAP to work as a technical writer
and editor. He continued to work in the UFO field. He served as the director of the Fund for UFO Research,
which provides grant money to legitimate researchers working in UFO studies. He also was the editor of the
MUFON Journal, the official publication of the Mutual UFO Network (MUFON), the largest civilian UFO
group in America today.
          In 2001 he wrote a sequel to The UFO Evidence; it covered major UFO incidents from the mid-1960's
through the 1990's. He is also the chief editor of the Journal of UFO History, which is published six times per        Major Donald E.
year. He was a strong proponent of the theory that UFOs are extraterrestrial spacecraft from an advanced alien         Keyhoe (left) &
civilization. Hall was an active member of the "UFO Updates" message board and website.                                Richard Hall (right).

          To supplement his income as a UFO researcher, Hall worked for many years as an abstractor-indexer for the Congres-
 sional Information Service in Bethesda, Maryland. A member of the Authors Guild, Hall also published numerous books and
 magazine articles dealing with the role of women in the American Civil War, and he maintained a strong interest in Civil War his-
 tory through his life.
          For the last few years Hall worked closely with MiMUFON member Jean Waskiewicz on the historical team on the NI-
 CAP website and was a source of inspiration and guidance to her and the team.
          Richard Hall was a true pioneer in the field of UFO research and will be greatly missed.

                                Lou Gentile Dies
                                       On June 28, 2009, paranormal investigator and syndicated radio show host Lou Gentile lost
                             his battle to cancer. Lou Gentile was about 40, and is survived by his wife and three children. He was
                             born in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania and grew up in 2 haunted houses. Lou personally experienced
                             poltergeist activity for over 20 years and became an investigator, before doing his own radio show.
                                       Gentile had been featured on MTV's "Fear," FOX Family Channel's "Scariest Places On
                             Earth," The Learning Channel, The Discovery Channel, "Unsolved Mysteries" and many more pro-
                             grams in this genre.
                                       With a lifetime of experience and being trained by America's only Roman Catholic Lay de-
                             monologist, Lou strived to help people who are afflicted with negative, demonic or diabolical entities
                             all free of charge.
                                       The Federal Communications Commission on November 18, 2007, fined Philadelphia talk
                             show host Lou Gentile $10,000 for allegedly operating an unlicensed radio station. Gentile ran the
                             "Lou Gentile Show," a streaming Web site that broadcast talk about "paranormal, alternative, unusual

and other-than-mainstream topics." At the time, Gentile insisted that the charges against him were false.” It’s BS," he told a re-
porter. "It's just ridiculous."
          He contended that although pirate stations in the Philadelphia area rebroadcast his programs, often aired on licensed AM
outlets, he never ran a station himself.
          When Brad Steiger heard of Gentile's death (he) said that there was a side of Lou that no one saw. Steiger said that as he
was just completing a radio program, Gentile mentioned that immediately after he said "good night," he was headed for New Or-
leans to see if he could be of any help with the destruction caused by Katrina. Steiger remembered that was right after the monster
storm had struck, because they had begun the program by extending condolences to those in that listening area.
          Services for Lou Gentile were held on July 7th in Philadelphia at the John F. Fluehr & Son's Funeral Home.

          “Mysteries” Seminar --
          As promised, I have more information on the October 24th Mysteries of Space and Sky UFO Seminar, the sixth of these
events orchestrated by Dr. Peter Resta. This year’s event has a new venue, an expanded speaker roster, and a variety of extra options
that in my opinion retain its great value per money.
          First, the date, time, and location details: “Mysteries 2009” will take place on Saturday, October 24th from 9:30 am to 9:30
pm at the Ramada Inn in Hanover, Maryland, near the Baltimore-Washington International Airport. Saying “UFO Group” should
give you a discounted room rate if you call the Inn. A free shuttle from the airport and Amtrak accessibility provide some conven-
ient alternatives from the chauffer-yourself-from-Michigan (which Linda and I have found not to be a challenging drive at all). The
considerably longer timeframe accommodates a confirmed nine-speaker schedule, with Budd Hopkins, Colonel Charles Halt, Captain
Robert Durant, Rosemary Ellen Guiley, Peter Franks, Nicholas Roseler, and “Mysteries” co-hosts and regular presenters Sue and Rob
Swiatek, plus Dr. Resta himself.
          By moving the conference site Peter Resta has addressed the main problem I had mentioned with the two Mysteries seminars
Linda and I have attended, that being the room and sound system at the community college. He has also attacked the issue of meals,
a detail always appreciated by myself. The conference will provide a free lunch buffet for those who register early enough. Plus,
for attendees who want to stay beyond the regular speaker schedule, there are a Happy Hour and plated evening banquet, with what’s
described as “an opportunity to explore the human mind with Peter Franks” and a tribute to UFO legend Dick Hall. Check out the
website http://mss-2009.eventbrite.com for more information, especially on all the options for registration. Prices run from $30 for
just the night session to $70 for the whole event, but there are price advantages for signing up early and you should read and under-
stand all your options. It still seems to me, given the larger speaker list and extras that simplify other essentials (food!) plus provide
more opportunity to interact with other serious ufologists, that “Mysteries 6” still offers the best combination of value for money out

           From The Editor’s Desk                       ABC’s “Disinformation Machine” Strikes Again
                                                               A couple years ago, the ABC Network presented the TV special “Peter
                                                      Jennings: Seeing is Believing”. Nuclear Physicist Stanton Friedman dubbed it a
                                                      “mockumentary”. On August 17, 2009, ABC outdid themselves by presenting
                                                      “Primetime: Outsiders” with Juju Chang. This one made Seeing is Believing look
                                                      like an episode of “UFO Hunters”.
                                                               This program went beyond attacking the UFO phenomenon in general by
                                                      concentrating their venom on the abduction phenomenon. Juju Chang admitted on
                David Twichell                        camera that she was a skeptic, which is not what is typically expected from an objec-
                                                      tive reporter yet is far too typical in the case of UFOs and alien abductions.


          As usual with this type of program, they spent the first forty minutes presenting the mystery and the last twenty explaining it in
conventional terms – and, as usual, omitting rebuttals that would have shot big holes in the conventional theories.
          UFOlogy’s nemesis Susan Clancy of Harvard University, successor to the late John Mack, reiterated her assertion that all alien
abduction scenarios could be explained by “sleep paralysis”, a condition that may occur in normal subjects or be associated with narco-
lepsy, cataplexy, and hypnagogic hallucinations. Of course there was no mention of cases such as Travis Walton (Fire in the Sky) where
six witnesses watched in horror as their friend was abducted in a brilliant, blue beam of light emanating from a gigantic, silent, disk-
shaped UFO hovering at treetop level. Nor was there any mention of Linda Cortile (Budd Hopkins’ “Witnessed: The True Story of the
Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions”), where no less than twenty individuals witnessed a woman being floated through a closed tenth
story window into a waiting UFO. None of these witnesses were asleep at the time. It is no wonder these cases weren’t mentioned.
They would have made Clancy look ridiculous.
          Michigan MUFON member and alien experiencer Ann Huff wrote to ABC saying (in part): “I have seen them in the daytime, at
11pm at night on the lawn and in my bathroom at 2pm. I was not asleep, not even in bed. I remember seeing hands at my windowsill
when I was about 10 years old. Then the little gray came up to the window and into my bedroom. When I saw the show tonight I was
very frightened. I remembered the time it happened. I hope that your editors realize that this is real and that people who have experi-
enced these things are not having a sleep disorder. I could tell you of times that weren’t even at nighttime and I wasn't in bed.”
          Longtime researcher and hypnotherapist Leo Sprinkle was personally attacked in an attempt to make all hypnotherapists appear
to be “leading” their subjects into believing they had been abducted by alien beings. Nothing was mentioned about the Betty and Barney
Hill case where Dr. Simons attempted to lead them both away from the alien abduction scenario by suggesting their experience may have
been something else. Nothing was mentioned about similar experiments by Budd Hopkins. In those cases, the abaductee would have no
part of the hypnotist’s leading suggestions and stuck to their story while under hypnosis. Hopkins has further asserted that thirty percent
of his cases did not require hypnosis – the subjects had conscious recall of most or all of their frightening episodes.
          MiMUFON State Director, Bill Konkolesky, wrote to ABC with the following critique:
          “I was surprised and appalled at the mean-spirited demeanor of Juju Chang on the Tuesday, August 17th episode of Prime-
time. She was barely able to restrain her personal contempt for the people she interviewed who were invited on the program to share
their stories of being the victims of alien abduction. Regardless of one's views on the controversial subject, the controversy shouldn't be
how aggressive the interviewer can get without bringing the guests to tears.
          “Possibly the worst was Juju spitefully telling a supportive wife of one abductee that the woman was "the biggest fool" and that
she should leave her husband. This woman was someone who was sought out by ABC to appear on the program, only to be am-
bushed and humiliated.”
          Bill passed this email on to MUFON members and added: “If you also watched this awful program, I encourage you to share
your thoughts with ABC, as well. This is the page to submit comments to them on their news programming: http://abcnews.go.com/Site/
page?id=3271346
          If you missed this show, you were lucky. If you wish to torture yourselves, you may go to http://abcnews.go.com/Primetime/
story?id=8330290&page=1”
          Interestingly enough, at the above website, ABC offered a poll asking the question “Do you believe in Alien Abduction?” As of
September 9, 2009, there were 1,260 responses:
          Yes = 716.
          No but I believe in extraterrestrial life = 369.
          No, UFOs and crop circles are bologna = 175.
          Whoops! It looks like Juju Chang and the ABC Network have missed the mark again!
           David Twichell
           MiMUFON Newsletter Editor
