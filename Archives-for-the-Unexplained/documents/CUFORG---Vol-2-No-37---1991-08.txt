                       The absence of information about asteroids
                     caught Clifford Cunningham's attention in 1976. An
                     astronomy aficionado since childhood and a free­
                     lance writer, the Ontario resident had just bought
                     a 141/.l-inch (36 em) telescope and was seeking an
                     avenue for meaningful application of the· instru-           �


                     ment's capabilities. He began his serious asteroid
                     observations from the roof of the 17-storey apart­
                     ment building where he lives in downtown Kitch­
                     ener. With the bright lights of the city beneath him,
                     Cunningham tracked down hundreds of asteroids
                     and measured their positions. Knowing exactly
                      where they are at any given time makes it pos­
                      sible to calculate more accurately where they will
                      be in the future.



                      FLYING MOUNTAINS

                        The nights on the exposed roof, however, proved
                      to be a test of Cunningham's physical and mental
                      stamina. Between observing sessions, he had to
                      store his telescope in the furnace room, which

       With respect to the dynamically developing nature of our experience
with the modern UFO-Alien presence, and to the numerous and profound
ramifications of this experience, I think that certain events in the turbulent
present call for the making of an updated estimation of our situation. A brief
account of my previous, relevant works is given at the beginning of the
reference section of this paper.

        It is evidently true that, at least since the outset of the second World
War, major instances of military activity tend to be accompanied by peaks of
the UFO-Aliens' observed activity. One can cite, here, WWII, the Korean
War, the Vietnamese War, and the Egyptian-Israeli War of 1973 (see ref. no.
1, pp. 18-20). In my opinion, for the proper understanding of this funda­

mental correlation, we should be cognizant of the presumption regarding the

words, whereas there exist very significant correlations between our military

the general estimate, concerning the UFO-Aliens' leading role in .the takeover
of this world, with the presumption that their decisive move will be triggered
by our time of unprecedented--nuclear--tribulation. So, especially in view of
the nuclear capabilities of the U.S.S.R., one must contemplate a causal
relationship pertaining to the recent political volatility involving the U.S.S.R.
as this occurs together with enhanced UFO-Alien manifestations.

        Of course, based on this same empirical reasoning, one can anticipate
the actions of the UFO-Aliens; and clearly the Soviet's condition is not the
only subject of pertinent interest. In fact, it should be readily understood that
as of the writing of this date; Sunday, Jan. 13, 199 1; the incredible military­

reaction. Furthermore, an outbreak of hostilities there, of the type that is
commonly foreseen, should logically be expected to precipitate a substantial
UFO-Alien reaction. Indeed, this particular, complex state of affairs presents
an important test case with respect to our basic understanding of the UFO­
Aliens' actions in the modern period: simply put, if an intense war breaks out
there, we should perceive the UFO-Alien response; and that response could
very possibly take place in various parts of the world. One can say that, for
our predictive considerations, about the only ingredient that the Kuwaiti
problem lacks is that the U.S. and U.S.S.R. are, we hope, not on opposite
sides.
