                    During the past few years a great deal of progress has been
            made in relation to the function and metabolism of various anatomical
            diyisions of the human central nervous syst~m. This progress has
            to a great extent been fostered and abetted by the study of the effects
            of drugs on this very complex system. These drugs have mainly been
            utilized as tools in attempts to develop the relationships between
            cerebral metabolic processes and brain functiot1 as expressed by
            individual mood and behavioral changes.

            little attention-paid to the general pharmacology of the drugs concerned.

            basis with little theoretical background.
