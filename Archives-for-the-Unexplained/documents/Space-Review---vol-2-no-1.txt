             The Dichotomy of Venus During 1962
                       by C. M. Pither
      The dichotomy of Venus is the time when from the earth we see
the pl unet as a half phase and this, of course, occurs twice during
one Sidereal period of Venus .       The first occurs when the planet is
approaching the earth and the phase is diminishing ; the other when
Venus r ecedes from the earth and the phase i s increasing.

- cal and observed dichotomy; each being exa ctly what its name sugg-
ests. Theoretical dichot omy is the time for wh i ch it is ~redicted
that half phase will occur, while observed dichotomy i s the actual
time at which the ho lf~phos e was seen i n the telescope .     There con
be as much as 4 days ' difference between the two and this differ-
ence i s termed the 1 Schr6ter Effect . ' ~reason f or this effect is
not , at the moment , fully understood, but work on this problem i s

 thick atmosphere of the planet . ~

 -ing star, observed dichotomy is always earl y , and with a western
 elongation - when Venus is an evening star, observed dichotomy i s

       Thus, during the eastern elongati on of 1962 when the date of

have heated the interior of the Moon to blast furnace tempera-
tures . This heat is said to be more powerful than the radio-
active materials present in the Moon .
     Kopal pointed out further that the gravitational pull of
earth on the Moon would be 100 t i mes greater than the lunar
force which controls our tides •••
Abundant Water Supply on Moon ?     Professor Kopal told space
scientists of the American Rocket Society that water supply
on the f.1oon was so great that it could cover the surface to a
uniform depth of 984 feet . He conti nued , " ice close to the
lunar surface could form glaciers covered by dust and debris .'

minor bulges numerous astronomers have observed in certai n
regions of the Moon.
     Kopal agrees with the theory that the Moon was formed by

'Solar Research' by Giorgio Abetti (London: Eyre & Spottiswoode ,
1962) Pp. 173 Net Price 18s.
      'Solar Research ' is the fourth book in the series ' A Survey
of Astronomy' edited by C . ~ . Ronan , the aim of which is to form a
link between books upon elementary astronomy and those which
assume the reader to have specialised knowledge . Professor Abetti's
book fulfils this aim very well indeed; the mathematical formulae
are kept to a minimum; there is a useful g lossary of terms and the
index is adequate . There are sufficient line diagrams and graphs ,
which are particularly necessary for this subject . A few of the
pla tes could be improved on in the quality of their reproduction .
      Chapter V is entitled ' Total Eclipses of the Sun 1 , but in fact
its content is much wider than suggested. About two-thirds of the
chapter is under the heading ' Solar Activity' , sufficient, surely,
to warrant a separat~ chapter with this title?
      However , Professor Abetti has made a very useful contribution
to the series , ' Solar Research' will make an excellent addition to
the book- shelves of those taking a serious interest in modern solar
astronomy , whether as an amateur astronomer or as a university
student.                                    Carole Jordan B.Sc.
