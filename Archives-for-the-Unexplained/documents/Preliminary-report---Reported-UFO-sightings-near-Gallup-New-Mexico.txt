National Institute for Discovery Science
                                     Preliminary Report of Investigation
                                                  October 22, 1999

Subject:           Reported UFO sightings near Gallup, New Mexico.


                                                       PREFACE
NIDS does not generally publish reports of investigations in the absence of laboratory analysis. NIDS is dedicated to
the research and study of aerial phenomena and related topics, focusing on scientific exploration and professional
investigations. However, because of the spate of erroneous reports being generated over the recent reported UFO
sightings in the New Mexico area, NIDS has decided to publish its preliminary report of investigation into the
sightings. Rather than point out where others have erred, either by intent or carelessness, NIDS will present the facts
as obtained by our investigation. NIDS will offer no conclusion. The readers may draw their own conclusions based
on the information presented. In accordance with NIDS policy of confidentiality, all names of persons or places
which might identify witnesses have been deleted.

Background: On about 10-11-99, a DJ for a radio station in Gallup, New Mexico, called and told NIDS there
had been several UFO sightings by members of the Navajo Nation in the past two weeks. He told NIDS the
sightings were reported in the Gallup Independent Newspaper. For two days NIDS Investigator 2 tried
unsuccessfully to reach the reporter. On the second attempt, NIDS was given the name of another reporter for the
newspaper. He said that he worked in the Dine’ branch of the Gallup Independent Newspaper, located in Window
Rock, New Mexico. He said that many members of the Navajo Tribe, including tribal police officers, reported
seeing strange lights in the sky on October 2, 1999. He also mentioned that there had apparently been a missile
launch of some type from Vandenberg AFB, in California on 10-02-99. The launch occurred at the same general
time as many of the reported sightings in New Mexico. Though some of the UFO reports may have been sightings of
the launch, several of the reports had elements not consistent with the missile launch. On 10-11-99, Investigator 1
called the NIDS resident field researcher in New Mexico and asked him to investigate the happenings reported on
10-02-99.


                                                  INVESTIGATION
(Investigator 2’s narrative is given in first person singular)
On 10-17-99, Investigator 1 called me at home and said that Investigator 3 had uncovered enough relevant
information to warrant an investigative trip to New Mexico. Apparently, the amount of potential witnesses was
extensive and demanded a hands-on investigation. Before our arrival in New Mexico, Investigator 3 arranged for a
radio show interview with Investigator 1 on a radio station in Gallup, New Mexico. Investigator 3 also contacted an
administrator of the Navajo Tribal Police and arranged to have the two officers of that division who had witnessed
the UFO present for interviews. On 10-18-99, at approximately 1030 hrs, I received a phone call from the reporter,
returning my original call from the previous week. I explained to him that I had already discovered that the UFO
sightings were reported by another reporter. He said that he was interested in becoming a NIDS field researcher in
the New Mexico, Gallup, area. He said that he had extensive contacts in the occult and also the Navajo tribe. I
explained that we already had a field researcher in New Mexico, and further, that issue was something with which I
had nothing to do. Somehow, the reporter was aware that Investigator 1 and I were on our way to New Mexico.
Investigator 3 met us upon our arrival at the Albuquerque airport at 1600 hrs, Mountain Time, on 10-18-99. He said
that the reporter was insisting upon being present during our scheduled interviews with the police officers. The
reporter was citing his interpretation of the freedom of information act, saying the public had the right to know the
facts. Investigator 1 called the reporter from the airport and told him that he was not welcome to be present during
our private interviews with the police officers. The reporter refused to concede the issue, and became difficult.
Investigator 1 called the police administrator and explained our concerns about the necessities of interviewing the
officers privately. The administrator assured Investigator 1 that the reporter would not be allowed into the
interviews.

Investigator 2 Sighting of an Aerial Object on I-40:
Between 1800 and 1900 hrs, while we were traveling west on I-40, from Albuquerque to Grants, I saw a light in the
west sky directly ahead of us at about a 25 to 30 degree angle above the horizon. It was very bright, and at first I
assumed it was a star. But then I realized that even though the sun was below the horizon, it was just barely so, and
the sky was still very lighted by daylight. Then, assuming it was a plane or some type of aircraft I turned away from
it to look elsewhere. About five minutes later when I looked forward through the car’s windshield I saw the light
still where I had last seen it. It appeared closer, but I got the impression it was because of our movement along the
highway in it’s direction, rather than the object’s movement that we were closer. I could detect a slight elongated
shape to the object. I reached into my bag and retrieved my 8X20 binoculars. The object, when viewed through the
binoculars was definitely elongated, or “cigar” shaped. To me, it appeared to be glowing as a result of sunlight
reflecting off it’s surface. I brought the object to the attention of Investigator 1 and Investigator 3, who said they had
been observing it also. Investigator 1 said he thought it was a plane. I gave the binoculars to Investigator 1 who still
had the opinion it was a plane, though he said he didn’t observe the object in detail. I continued to view the object
with the binoculars. As the sun set lower beyond the horizon the light reflecting off the object began to diminish.
The object appeared to be positioned in the sky with one end pointing predominately west, the other pointing east.
As the light diminished, the west end, which was lower than the east end, became darker, though it was still visible.
As the light faded from the whole object, it became more difficult to see. Then for maybe two seconds the object
became bright and distinctive again, revealing a more slender, sharper focused object, before fading completely
from sight. Once it faded, the object was no longer visible in any way with the binoculars or with the naked eye.
Though the sky was much darker now, there was still enough light to see objects against the lighter backdrop of the
sky. At no time during my intense, magnified observation of the object did I see wings or rotors. I spent six years in
the USAF, usually on or near the flight-line, observing the approach, take off, and flight of military aircraft of all
types, including F-5s, F-4s, F-15s, F-16s, F-111s, A-10s, B-52s, KC-135s, KC-10s, C-130s, C-141s, C-5s, and the
U-2/TR-1. I’ve also observed many helicopters and foreign aircraft. The only aircraft that could come close to the
characteristics of the object that I saw would be a helicopter or possibly a blimp. But there were no visible rotors and
blimps don’t disappear.
