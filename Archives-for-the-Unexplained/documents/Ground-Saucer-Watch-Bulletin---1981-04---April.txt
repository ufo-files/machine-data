                         DIRECTLY SPEAKING
                             By: W.H. Spaulding


Another enemy related to the effectiveness of UFO research is inflation.
During the past five years all of us have seen a tremendous rise in
prices on just about every conceivable purchased item. Inflation
hurts every business, large or small, the profit or the not-for-profit
variety, because of soaring operating costs. UFO organizations are
no exception, we also have operational expenses. Some of these
expenditures, which are common to all organizations include; secretarial
costs, postage, printing, stationary, long distance telephones, rents,
computer time-sharing charges, etc.
These costs vary from organization to ·organization, but generally
represent the sources for outgoing cash flow. Of course, it has been
said, it's not how much you make, it's how much you spend that determines
the degree of poverty and/or wealth. And simply stated, that is the
problem with UFO funding, there are only a few viable methods of
obtaining monies for this endeavor which include; lectures, contributions,
membership dues, sales of printed/photographic materials, etc. Even
the most recent funding to a UFO group, via a grant to pursue government
documents not released during the GSW lawsuit, is considered miniscule.
Grant funding although attractive, will never be a primary source for
revenue. Common business sense tells us that . the larger a group becomes
the greater the operational overhead grows. If UFO organizations could
obtain a membership of 5,000 dues-paying members, many new problems
and programs could be tackled. Additionally, the monetary gain obtained
from a large membership dues base would certainly take care of all
operating expenditures.
What type of individual requests and/or shows interest in UFO organizations
and purchases materials? Unfortunately, and most UFO organizations
will agree, that many of the requests come from teenagers or persons
working on a school project. The scenario of a typical letter in this
category asks for "free" photographs, case studies or documents related
to the phenomenon. To add insult, the requester does not send any postage
and in over fifty percent of these incidents, does not purchase any
materials, most likely due to their own lack of funding.
Another category of individual who requests materials/information is
the flash-enthusiast. Their span of interest generally parallels the
publicity generated by the media during a high cycle period of UFO
"activity". They will write letters, subscribe to a UFO periodical
and when the subscription terminates, you will never again hear from
these individuals.
With all of the problems of obtaining money to research new programs
in the UFO field, it's no wonder that great strides of progress have
not transpired. Inflation, lack of funding, and a negative public
opinion are all factors against new break-throughs and is one of
ufology's biggest enemies.

During the late summer of 1980, Williard Mcintyre, a director of a
Maryland-based UFO organization, forwarded to GSW, two photographs

highly cropped, shaped like a small near-parallelogram. The image
contained on this negative appeared as a "small burned creature".
The following is a copy of the GSW photographic analysis of these

graphic analysis. The negatives, although extremely age-dated, contain
photographic images of a severely burned humanoid-like creature/animal
lying on the ground and what appears to be wreckage, consisting of
twisted metal.
Although GSW's computerized enhancement technique works well on photo-
graphs of purported UFOs, only some sparse data can be gleaned from
photographic images, relatively close to the camera, of the conventional
variety. When analyzing photos of UFO's, such items as verification
of the time of day based on image shadows is obtainable, details of
lumen dispersion, information on surface reflectivity and the approxi-
mation of distance-to-camera based on the effects of the atmosphere to
groupings of pixels (picture cells}.

reports of nocturnal UFO's could easily be the electro-plasma effect
caused by fault-lines in the earth's crust, and when the pressure on

rock would release plasma. Since these plasma look like night-time
UFO's, the theory was valid!
