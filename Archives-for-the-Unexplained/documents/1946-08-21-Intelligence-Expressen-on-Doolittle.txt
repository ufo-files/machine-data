Doolittle means to discuss gasoline – knows about the occurrence of ghost bombs

August 21, 1946

General James H. Doolittle, vice president of Shell Company and Captain Douglas Bader, contact person at Shell’s Aviation Department in London, arrived in Bromma on Tuesday by extra plane from Oslo.
The General stated that his trip was purely commercial and that he means to leave Stockholm on Thursday, moving on to Copenhagen and then throughout Western Europe, finally returning to the States via Cairo. Regarding his meeting with General Nordenskiöld and the leadership of the Swedish Air Force, he replied with a gracious smile that he wasn’t really aware of the program his Swedish hosts had organized for his short visit.
His visit to Sweden is just another destination in his travels, but even so, it may be quite likely that discussions will be made with the Swedish housing management regarding the proposal on the nationalization of petrol.
The new tetraethyl lead is probably subject to discussion, for according to specialist statements it should be a far more economical alternative to regular gasoline.
- I have read mentions of your so-called ghost bombs in a few reports and I have no idea how to explain the occurrence of these strange objects. I would be very interested in getting to see them myself, the general said.
Group Captain Bader, one of England's most skillful hunting aviator during the war and writer of several articles in Expressen, was modesty personified. He chose to not utter a word regarding his attempted escape from the German prison camp where he had ben held during the war, but did acknowledge that the Germans had "irritated" him considerably.
- They had both good and bad points, but unfortunately the bad usually outweigh the good, he said, smilingly. Personality issues really doesn’t matter in this kind of situation, for if you were held captive by the world’s most gracious people you still wouldn’t like them - and the Germans are not the world's best.
