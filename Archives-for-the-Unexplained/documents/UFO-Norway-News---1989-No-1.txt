                                  UFOs AND MYSTICISM

                                 By Hans Kristian Moe

In this article, written by one of the leading members of UFO- Norge,
means and goals for the "common ufologist" are reviewed. Some critical
remarks about ufology during the last 40 years are presented. The article
may have something to say to all active ufologists, especially when
considering both former and present state of ufology.

In what ways should ufology try to get other people interested in UFOs?
Is it neccessary for the ufologist to "prostitute " himself ?

Serious ufology is going through hard times these days. By "serious"
mean a ufology which uses accepted, scientific methods when UFO-phenomena
                                    3


are observed, investigated and eventually explained. Ufology has during
the last 40 years fought more or less strenuosly to get the UFD-
phenomenon accepted as a scientific problem,      "the most significant
scientific problem in our time". However, the ranks of ufologists
themselves have not been sufficiently diciplined, members have sometimes
abandoned scientific principles, and we don't need many fingers to count
those members of the scientific community who think that the UFO-
phenomenon merits status as a scientific challenge.

  think that one of the reasons for science's fear of ufology is that
the majority of UFO-interested individuals constitute an undicipl ined
gang, where it seems almost impossible to structure the collection of
data and coordinate statements to media, among other things.

Because UFOs are not yet "brought down to earth" and studied methodically
like any other anomaly, grassroots members of the UFO community are apt
to let their fantasy run wild. The most incredible hypotheses regarding
the behaviour and origin of UFO's are uncritically put forward, with the
excuse: "We must be open-minded and allow for all possibilities. There
may be something we haven't thought of yet". With the consequence that
science, with its resources both technical and economic, is frightened
away even mor-e.

Any organized movement needs money in order to be active. Enthusiasm and
voluntary work can achieve much - but not enough. It costs money to
investigate an observation and to disseminate information.
Several UFO-organisations have, in order to improve their economy and to
disseminate more or less biased information, published magazines for
their members.

One of the main problems concerning the UFO-enigma during the last 40
years is that nothing significantly new has happened. With the term
"new", I mean something that would bring the UFO-enigma closer to a
solution. We have observed, and are still observing, the same lights
crossing the sky, the same "motherships", "telemetry-discs", humanoids,
landingtraces, etc. until boredom sets in. What sustains our interest,
however, are intriguing nuances in the new material. However, these
nuances are more apt to stimulate unrestrained fantasy than to lead
ufology nearer to a well founded understanding. Nonetheless, the new
material provides us with a significant reason to continue our data
collection: it tells us that the same phenomenon is still present - and
that it is still as unresolved as ever'

Most people would feel sorry for an editor who has access only to new
material almost identical to older already used. Especially if this has
been going on for the last 40 years. Hence, the ufological "grassroots"
are now getting bored and the UFO-magazines lose their buyers. This can
be observed today throughout the entire international UFO community.

Then the inevitable question is raised: what should an organisation or-
movement do when the supporter begins to dwindle?
Investigation shows that the great majority of UFO-interested individu-
als are also interested in other phenomena to which established science
doesn't have clear-cut answers. One may spculate whether it is the Rebel,

ihe Inquirer or the Doubting Thomas which dominates each and every one
of us.

A major part of the UFO community doesn't seem content with the "pure"
UFO-material, e.g. the observation and observer. They want something
more, new horizons to fantasy . So they wonder : are UFOs telepathic
projections which can be sensed by one or more observers? Are projecti-
ons emitted by superintelligences from outer space or from other
dimensions'>
Alternatively:
- UFOs are visitations from the past. Formerly, the earth was inhabita-
ted by civilisations which mastered the technology behind UFOs. And why
shouldn't our ancestors be able to visit us ? We do have sensitive per~ons
who via impulses claim to get messages from that same past.           ·
   UFOs are visitations from our own future. If you can believe the
tabloid press, we are also receiving signals from the future.
- UFOs are connected to pyramid power or vice versa. Were the pyramids
built using humanoids as consultants? Are UFOs able to neutralize gravit y
in the same way as the old Egyptians apparently could?
   The lost Antlantis, could it be the cradle of the ancient UFO -
civilisation?
- The Bermuda Triangle , could it be a porthole in which UFOs can jump to
and fro other dimensions?
- Do UFO- occupants watch after mankind in such a way that we aren't
allowed to commit too many stupidities on our already polluted planet '>
- UFOs are vi s ual manifestations of divine powers, a proof of God.

The abov e mentioned examples show that some parts of the UFO community
are to a large extent engaged in mysticism and the paranormal. And what
is wrong with that'> Ufologists have certainly for years accused science
and the authorities of being blind to the UFO-reality and failing to
realize that the UFO-phenomenon represents a real challenge demanding a
solution. I t is right th.at ufologists should be open to all kinds of
hypotheses even if they turn out to be vastly fantastic. However, the
hypotheses must be founded on observations and at this very point a major
o bstac le arises: the way some ufologists select their data, in such a way
that they fit into preconceived, fantastic hypotheses, giving other
ufologists goose-flesh!

We will give an example:
Two friends, designated A and B, are out walking a dark, clear autumn
night. !:/uddenly A c atches sight of a spherical, yellow-lit object some
10 0 metr e s a head, hovering above some trees in the background. A can hear
a faint, whi s tling s ound. He yells and points to the objec t, explaining
he sees. However, B sees and hear s nothing at all . A o bserves the sphere
for about 15 seconds .

We will try to explain the event using two hypotheses:

!.  The whole thing has been going on in the head of A; a halluc ination,
    a moment of madness.
2 . The s phere is a telepathi c projecti o n s ent by an unknown intelli -
    g e nce.

Of course, there are more than the abovementioned explanations:
B may for instance suffer from temporarily deaf-blindness, and hence is
unable to perceive the stimuli from the phenomenon .
It is a wellknown scientific pr i ciple that the most conventional
hypothes i s should be the first one to be tested when a c ase is to be
explained. In this case, I would assume that the first hypothesis could
give an acceptable result; however, not without resource-demanding
investigation; a closer look at the mental state of witness A being o n e
example. Unfortunately, it is just this aspect which is lacking or at
least very insufficiently covered in most investigated UFO-observations.
Furthermore, once again we encounter the dismal question of resources.
Personally, I have the opinion that the only acceptable way for ufology
is to make use of generally accepted scientific principles and theories
in our treatment of the UFO-phenomenon. Serious ufology must not
prostitute itself by being tempted to exploit the mystical a n d specula-
tive parts of the phenomenon. Even though many ufologists today cash i n
on such tendencies in order to .better their economy, hoping that this
will help them to be viewed as more serious, I think this will ultimately
turn out to their disadvantage.

Our need for the mystical must be satisfied in other ways than including
it in ufology.

                             By Per Ottesen


   Date: Friday 6 November 1987
   Time: Between 02.00 and 02.30 am
   Duration: Ca. 30 min.
   Place: Between Tretten and Favang in the Gudbrandsdalen valley

During the last 5-6 years we have intermittantly received reports from
the Gudbrandsdalen valley, indicating small and large LIFO "waves". For
a prolonged period, there have been very few reports from this area.
However, during the fall of 1987, several observations were registered,
including this extraordinary sighting made by a mother and her daughter.

Anny and Line Hammervold were driving up the Gudbrandsdalen valley after
a foreign visit. This valley is one of Norway's longest and is situated
in the southeastern part of the country. When they had reached a location
between Tretten and Favang <about 4 km north of Lillehammer), Line
observed that her mother was driving more and more slowly. She asked if
something was wrong and Anny then directed her attention toward a light
in the sky. She told that she had kept an eye on it for a while, because
it had shifted position and also had come closer. In the beginning she
thought it was a plane, because she could see windows on the side of it,
but finally she became convinced that this it couldn't be. Now they both
observed the light. It moved in a stepwise manner, e.g. it made fast
movements and then made intermittent halts. After having come closer, it
appeared as a "hat" or a "soup plate" being inverted. The witnesses could
now see that the object's shape was made up of lightpoints, with about
two lights on top and four underneath Cthe witnesses are not sure of the
exact number).

Underneath the object two "feet" were hanging down. They were a bit bent
and one of the witnesses compared them with "crabclaws". The feet were
sprinkled with tiny lights, in such a way that they glittered as if
mounted with diamonds. They blinked regularly about the same frequency
as that ol a direction indicator on a car or 'a little slower.

Suddenly the object lit up and got a strange, dark green colour. During
this phase of the sighting they observed very clearly the single light
points which made up the whole object. Then it shifted back to the
original white light. During this part of the observation the object had
shifted position between south and southwest, and was now standing in
such a way that they could observe it above a ridge. The cloudcover was
dense and relatively low, and this sets limits to the object's altitude
during the observation. They both think that the object was closer than
the ridge, which at this particular place is at a distance of about 1 km.

           DID A MAN FROM OUTER SPACE DIE IN SWEDEN IN 1955 ?

This story is of a rather controversial nature, and has evidently
been retold several times through various sources. Although this is a
common feature for many extraordinary reports in the UFO-community, it
doesn't rule them out as, at least, possible genuine cases. We have
included the report Cor "story" to · put it bluntly> in this magazine,
merely to indicate that events reflecting the more bizarre aspects of
ufology have been reported even in Scandinavia. Taken at face value, it
must b~ characterized as a classical CE-Ill report •

. During the exhibition "Kirlek eller kaos" ("Love or chaos") in Charlot-
  tenborg in Sweden, the Danish UFO organisation Frit UFO Studium <FUFOS)
  had marked their presence with a large stand. This was in 1977 and every
  day several hundred people visited the stand to watch a non-stop
  automatic lecture accompanied with slides. Due to the many questions
  raised among the public one could see that the interest for the UFO-
  phenomenon was steadily increasing.
  One quiet and calm day when we were visited by school classes, I noticed
  a modest man in his sixties who attended the lecture several times and
  became especially alert when the slides were showing pictures of UFO-
  occupants. This particular sequence of pictures had been prepared

according to the testimony of many witnesses.
Even if the lecture was interesting, I couldn't understand why an
ordinary visitor would attend it several times. Hence, I became curious
and sought to have a chat with him.
Initially, after having established contact, he was very reserved and
refused to go further into details. However, noticing that I didn't laugh
or shake my head, he began telling me his entire story, while I took
notes. It turned out to be a very strange story indeed.

The story of the anonymous witness

- Once upon a time I actually talked with such a man as is depicted in
the slide series. In 1955 I had a Job as a . forest worker near the Gulf
of Botnia in Vasternorrland. I was employed by two brothers whb delivered
timber to a sawmill situated in the middle of Sweden.
It was an early morning in July,. the time was about & am, and we were in
full swing cutting down trees in the wood when we heard a creaking sound
as from an animal which forces itself through the woods or from breaking
branches. In the next moment we saw a cigar-shaped object which flew in
a zig-zag pattern, seemingly uncontrolled between the treetops, while·
leaves and branches fell tumbling to the earth. My first thought was that
it could be a small plane which had lost its wings and was in the act of
making a forced landing. A river was situated 300-400 m away and it was
evident that the object was heading in that direction.
We all understood that the object would plunge to the earth during the
next few seconds, and we ran in the same direct ion. 15-20 m before we
came out of the wood, the object made a crash landing 30-40 m from the
river.
We   had expected an ear-deafening roar when the fuel-tank exploded;
however, that didn't happen. Instead a tremendous light lit the entire
area as from a gigantic lightning. The light was so powerful that the
sunshine almost disappeared. It was so intense that we virtually could
see through the trees. I could observe the growth rings inside a tree
which had a circumference of more than a meter; Just like examining an
X- ray picture. A couple of seconds later we experienced a vacuum wave,
which sucked everything towards the center of the light.
We were thrown forwards all three of us,      while branches and leaves
whirled around us. Probably it didn't last for more than a split second,
and still I can remember being thrown towards a tree - the time standing
still - and my entire life passing before me. All sounds around me were
suddenly nonexistent.
After we had calmed down a bit, we went towards the landing place to see
what had happened.
There was nothing to see at the place of the presumed disaster, save some
stacks of 1,.1ood blown down by the wind. We became very surprised and
decided to go back to work.


A dwarf wearing a uniform

When we arrived the edge of the forest, one of the brothers suddenl y
exclaimed: "Hey, a dwarf in a uniform is lying here!". The flying machine
had evidently crashed into the river and he must have been thrown out of
it .

Suddenly I became totally relaxed, and watched him carefully. He was not
a dwarf. His body was well proportioned with broad shoulders and normal
shapes. His skin was yellowish as that of an asian. The eyes were deep
and were totally black. He had two large wounds on the cheekbone and in
the forehead. The wounds didn't bleed, but were in a way "wet". His hair
was almost white.
His earlobes were grown together towards his neck and resembled shark
 fins. His lips were wrinkled, narrow and colourless.
When he laughed - which he did quite often - he revealed a row of small
teeth in both upper and lower jaw. I observed that his canine teeth were
flat and just as wide as our incisors.
His hands were small, with five slim fingers without nails, and when he
moved the hand, it seemed as if the ring finger was grown together with
his little finger. His uniform was made of reddish metal, and was very
tight to his body.
The head and hands were not covered with clothes, but by the feet the
uni form in a way transformed into a pair of shoes. The size was about
35-37. The footsoles were full of grooves and were vibrating, and for a
moment I thought of caterpillar belts on a tank . The stranger looked at
me and nodded slightly. There was no doubt he knew what I was thinking.
With his shoes he could roll forwards and backwards without moving his
legs. Around his waist he had a wide, self-illuminating metal belt
equipped withan unusual large b.uckle. This buckle shone weakly with a
light blue colour, which later on, when he was dead, became dark blue.
In the middle of the buckle there was some kind of a sign with a yellow
colour - as the characters U and V - and it seemed as if the V was
located inside the U. He knew that I was studying him, and he said: "It
is due to ' the suit that I'm still alive. Inside I am totally crushed".
His right hand disappeared inside the suit at the waist, but I didn't
see any pocket. He took out a rectangular object. It was the size of a
matchbox and had 12 small depressions. Using a metal fork, which was
fastened to the object, he stuck it several times into the different
holes. When he was finished, he tried to throw it some distance away. He
smiled and said: "Don't touch it.     It will give a message of what has
happened, so they can can come and look for me. Where I come from there
is also someone waiting". The stranger lay for a while as if he was
sleeping. His hands were clenched, and evidently he had great pain.
Suddenly, the two brothers looked a bit confused at each other, and then
they just went back into the wood without saying a word. Lateron
several years afterwards - I have figured out when reconstructing this

moment that the stranger must have telepathically asked the brothers to
go away, without saying a word. I sat and talked with the stranger for
two more hours before he died.


Contact with the earth

The man wouldn't let me know the exact content of the next two hours'
talking. I did only get some fragments of information: - The stranger
came from a place in the vicinity of the constellation we are designat-
ing the Eagle <Aquila). We are visited by several beings from the
universe, and some of them have developed so far that we can observe them
only when they materialize or dematerialize in order to visit a parallell
universe in the orbit of the earth. Some visit the earth in order to take
specimens of it, considering a future immigration to the earth. Some
others also have contact with human beings here, a contact being maintai-
ned for several hundred years.
This information is identical to that given by other witnesses.
The old man proceeded: "Just before the stranger died, he gave me a
folded sack from the invisible pocket and said: - When I die, the light
w i 11 disappear from my body and then you must pull this sack over my
body. Together with the two other men, you must dump me into the river.
Then I will disappear. Afterwards you have to wash yourselves properly
with the water in order not to get ill.
He could hardly breathe, and I figured that he didn't have much time
left. The light around his body gradually became weaker, and disappeared
towards the suit . The light blue buckle became darker and darker.


His last Swedish words

He looked at me and smiled. Then he said something in a language I have
never heard, never before or later. Then he said something in Swedish,
and his last sentence I was able to apprehend: "YOU HAVE COME AGAINST
YOUR WILL AND PASS AWAY WITHOUT WISHING IT, OUR LIFE IS AS STEAM . . . . . He
spoke a couple of words more, but his voice was so weak that I didn't
comprehend what he said. But I am convinced that he prayed to some sort
of deity before he died. I was moved by the solemn situation.
With the help of the two brothers I managed to pull the sack over him,
and after which we laid him in the river. The sack smelled of sulphur,
and our hands were itching. He was quite heavy, between 90 and 100 kgs.
When the sack came in contact with it, the water began to seethe an
bubble, and we understood that some sort of chemical process was taking
place. After five minutes everything had disappeared.
He had probably wished to crash in the river experiencing a sudden death,
instead of laying for two hours beside the river in great pain. He would
evidently die alone and think of his home, several thousand light years
out in the universe.


Recognizing the stranger

"That was everything", the old man said. "I worked for a couple of years
more with the brothers, but we seldom said anything ab o ut what happened.

The old man presented me with a piece of metal. "Look", he said. I stared
dumbly. It looked like a large hairpin. "What is it?". He laughed self-

on the location. The small box which the stranger had thrown away was

An anonymous person, who with his story and his proof could ha v e been on

told was true since he stuck to it for so many years. A psychologist

story. The man kept his secret deeply hidden afraid of being ridiculed.
Perhaps there are sev eral similar stories which never become public -
