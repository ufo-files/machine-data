STRATEGY FOR THE NEXT TEN YEARS

Ten years in ufology is    a     long time.     In ten years,         ten hypotheses can be
considered and thrown out of ten different windows for ten different reasons.
Out of ten 'experts', perhaps only one survives after ten years - and out
of ten groups, perhaps only one survives.

One of those survivors is the Surrey Investigation Group on Aerial Phenomena.
The reasons for its survival are harder to ascertain than the fact of its
existence.  But Sigap has played a major role in both the local and national
ufological scene.  It has investigated local sightings with an enthusiasm
undampened by the prevailing winds of public opinion and political expediency.

Much of the group's success has been due to the loyalty and strength of those
who have supported the group since its inception in                   1967.             Under the chairmanship
of one man for eight of those years, it has weathered the storms of public
ridicule, and private power struggles within the existing organisations in
this country.

It is inevitable that in studying a subject as elusive as ours, personalities
play an important part in the organisational structure.                                Unfortunately,
the pursuit of this charismatic form of leadership often ends suddenly, and
leaves a bitter taste in ones mouth.           Many of us have seen the demise of
many a group because of the inflexibitity of its representatives.                                  More
sadly, the cause of ufology has been advanced not one iota.

The strength of Sigap lies in its flexibility of approach.                                   It floats with
the tide and drifts with the wind.            It does not commit itself to rigid
policies which could later prove embarrassing.                It co-operates with those·
who wish to co-operate.        After all, its primary purpose is to investigate
reports of unusual aerial phenomena - and this it has done steadfastly for
over ten years.  Let's hope that in ten years time, ufologists have ironed
out many of their differences - differences which,                if channelled in the
right direction, could be utilised for the good of our cause.

Richard Beet    (0483 37322)

WHAT IS THE MOON?

By now we know that the moon is not just a romantic golden disc in the night
sky.        It would seem that the moon is not a fragment of the earth but probably
came from outside the solar system.

This is the astounding theory recently produced by two Soviet scientists,
Michael Vasin and Alexander Shcherbakov.

Information from the Apollo manned landings ·shows that the age and composition
of rock samples on the moon are total.ly different from those on earth.  Does
this mean that the moon is an artificial satellite sent to orbit the earth
millions of years ago by alien beings?        If this is not the case, why did the
moon vibrate for       3� hours when a rocket booster was deliberately crashed into
it?

The scientists have an answer to this.        They maintain that the moon is a
hollow sphere with a soft outer shell two miles deep.           They also believe that
·there is an impenetrable hull which even meteorites cannot pierce.                   The types
of me�� found in lunar rock suggest that they may be the remains of the
outer skin of an artificial satellite.        These were chromium,       titinium and
circonium.

If modern scientists were to duplicate this feat, these are the metals they
would use.

Perhaps the man in the moon is not so far fetched after all!
