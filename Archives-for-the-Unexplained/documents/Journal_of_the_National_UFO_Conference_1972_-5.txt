Code of Ethics.
Whereas, the members of the permanent organizing committee are cognizant of the need for
a clear-cut statement of principles to guide member organizations and individual
ufologists, the following official Code of Ethics was adopted by the majority at a
meeting of the committee in Cleveland, Ohio, 25th June, 1965.
Revised June 24, 1972
Whereas, cooperation among member organizations and individual ufologists is neccessary
to the furtherance of high standards in UFO research, and
Whereas, the free and universal exchange of information is a prerequisite to the eventual
solutionof the UFO enigma.
It is the sense of this Conference that no member organization or individual ufologist
shall arbitraily inhibit or indiscriminately bar the free exchange of information.
NOR shall any such organization or individual take any action which shall have the intent
or effect of suppressing such free inquiry.
Neither shall any such organization or individual, whether by word of mouth or in writing,
say or write anything which shall have the purpose or effect of discrediting, defaming,
libelling or slandering other organizations or individuals.
NOR shall any such organization or individual attempt in any way to impose any specific
set of values, whether his own or that of others; or take any steps which shall have the
purpose or effect of dominating, controlling, monopolizing or tending to create a
monolithic sphere of influence which shall inhibit, restrict, or maliciously interfere
with the operation or activities of such organization and individuals.
Supplement "Ar - For Editors
We urge that publishers of UFO periodicals refrain from publishing, under the guise of
fact, material which they have reason to believe is false. Editors should make every
reasonable attempt to determine the true facts in each case. Deliberate falsification
is unworthy of responsible UFO researchers.
We disapprove of the use of unsupported assertions, except as these may occur in the
course of general discussions clearly labelled as personal opinion. In extreme cases,
editors may find it advisable to use such material with special disclaimers.
we recognize the fact that editors, as individuals, are obliged to use subtantial amounts
of material which they are, themselves, personally unable to verify; specific reference
citations should be prominently featured where possible. This option is especially
applicable to material which the editor believes is most likely to excite unusual
controversy.
We do, furthermore, regard the use of pseudonyms as unfortunate while reluctantly recog- *
nizing that they are occassionally necessary.
Discussions of personalities, as opposed to issues, must in no case contravene the Code of
Ethics approved by this Conference. Firmly believing that honest controversy is essential
to the advancement and progress of ufology, we nevertheless oppose gratuitous conflict.
