as early as 1912.         The sightings seemed to correspond with the early flying days when.

which sparked off many of the reports seen in the United States during the years 1951-3.
When this 10�million candlepower flare was first tested secretly in 1951 near Windsor
Locks, Conn., police and other officials were besieged with phone calls reporting

Douglas B 26 bomber, with a blinding flare streaming out from one wing.

magnesium powder was burnt at extremely high temperatures.          The powder contained in
a t?rpedo-shaped canister 12 feet long, would burn for four minutes with intense light.

SECRETARIAL   REPORT    1971-2.


Unfortunately it has been another difficult year for SIGAP due both to the inactivity
on the local scene - making it again difficult to maintain healthy membership numbers
and also increased financial expenditure incurred in the groups administration.


Up to Journal Vol 3 No 2 the magazine and previous SIGAP Newsletters had been produced
very cheaply with the production facilities given to Omar Fowler at work.


However now,·as well as not being able to use the Plastic Coatings Factory canteen for
our meetings, we have had to produce the magazine on a commercially viable basis.


To cope with the expenditure incurred with the magazine we have had to cut down our
issue of f�ee exchange magazines to other UFO groups by 75%.  In fact we used to issue
60 copies to members and nearly 50 copies on a free exchange basis to other groups
both at home and abroad.  Also due to recent postal changes affecting the 2 and 4 ounce
limits the magazine has to go for 3� instead of � and increase of 4�fo.


In fact with the re-introduction of the Journal on a bi-monthly basis if we cost each
Journal at its selling price of 15p+3� postage the total cost per annum comes to
£1. 11 without any further expenses like meetings and other .circulars etc. In fact
with the present £1    annual subscription we are actually paying people to join!


To continue the magazine on a bi-monthly basis we must have a sales drive to enable all
copies to be sold as soon as-possible to provide the funds for the next issue.  To
effect this we offer anybody a commission of 3� if they can sell 10 or more magazines.


The other major change this year is that we are now using Guildford House as our
venue, which means that we no longer have the free use of a meeting room�  With the
low attendance figures it is impossible to make any realistic admission charge to cover
the full expenditure incurred.  However I am sure that you all prefer the new surround­
ings with our break for coffee and biscuits preferable.


We plan to hold our meetings bi-monthly instead of quarterly as before and the Executive
Committee is presently drawing up a list of speakers for forthcoming lectures.  The
Meetings will be held on months where the magazine is not due.


During the year we have lost our Chairmari and Editor due to personal and business
comrnittments and this was basically the reason for no Winter magazine - November '71.
However we now have an Editorial Board consisting of four members of the Committee to
decide the content of PEGASUS.  You will see from the current JouXnal that we now
include SIGAP affairs - at last - as well as the usual articles.
