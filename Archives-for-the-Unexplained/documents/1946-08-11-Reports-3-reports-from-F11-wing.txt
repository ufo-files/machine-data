KUNGL.
SÖDERMANLANDS FLYGFLOTTILJ
         Flottiljexpeditionen.

Department 1
No. 189

To the chief of the Defense Staff.

I hereby respectfully report the statements from a number of eyewitnesses regarding a supposed "rocket-like fireball."
The following people have provided information.

1. Colonel Birger Schyberg, Tufkärr, Vadsbro:
At 20:50 o'clock, initially writing it off as an example of some major fireworks display, Mr. Schyberg came to observe a lightning clad torpedo-like object. The phenomenon lasted about 5 seconds, during which it traveled across the sky.

2. Mr. Nils Eriksson, Gustavsdal, Björkvik. Three other people had been present.
At around 21:00 o'clock, a brightly lit object arrived from the south-southeast, changed its course and ultimately disappeared in north-northeasterly direction. No fuselage could be observed. The phenomenon had initially shone white, but became redder as it went and seemed to be fitted with a tail unit. No sounds could be heard. It passed at a constant height of about 1000 meters. The object seemed to have been slightly faster than a normal flying machine; its speed did not resemble that of a shooting star.

3. Mr. Jerm, Fada, Enstaberga:
At 20:45 o'clock, a luminous object had arrived from the east. It disappeared to the north at about 200 meters altitude. No fuselage could be observed. It was fitted with a bright tail and had been completely soundless. Mr. Jerm figured that the object might had landed in an area called Bergamon.
He chose to describe the radiant glow as reminiscent to car lights of a stronger kind.

Nyköping, August 12, 1946.

Sten Dalborg
squadron adjutant
