August 10: Report from the Naval attaché in Helsingfors

From The Naval Attaché in Helsingfors, number 135/1946, message nr 71.
To the Commanding Officer at the Defence Staff, Foreign Dept

Country: Finland-Russia
Classification: Confidential

Re: Guided missiles   

1. The French Navy Attaché in Scandinavia, Capitaine de Vaisseau Bienaymé, is at present visiting Helsingfors. He is showing great interest in observations concerning guided missiles. 

He is convinced that the space phenomena observed lately can be referred to V-2 bombs launched at Peenemünde and guided in a wide trajectory over Sweden-Finland and over the Baltic countries.

This opinion is confirmed by observations made in Finland on June 9 concerning about 30 sightings on basis of which it was possible to watch the trajectory from Björneborg with an easterly course towards the Lahti area and then with a south-easterly coursee towards Kotka and further onto the Finnish bay.

From a specification of observations made the conclusion has been drawn that the average speed of the missiles would be 2.700 km/h, to be compared with the speed of the V-2-bombers which is about 3.000 km/h.

2. Except for light phenomena described in memo no 68 of August 6, a report has also been received from a group of officers who observed a white-yellowish fireball at an altitude of abt 5000 m. This observation was made on Aug 7 at 09.40 pm at Sommaröarna off Esbo.

The fireball was visible during 2 seconds in south-easterly direction over the Finnish bay. It was moving at very high speed in a faintly curved trajectory from southeast towards west, reminding of a ballistic curve close to its maximum altitude. No smoke could be noticed.

3. News items on "ghost rockets" are not very often seen in Finnish newspapers nowadays. However, it is not prohibited to publish such observations, according to several publishers asked.

Helsingfors Aug 10, 1946

/signed/ G Sjöberg-Silfverling
Commander 1 degr., Naval Attaché
