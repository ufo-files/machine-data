            The 20th annual National U FO conference (NUFOC) is now history, hav­
     ing taken place as scheduled in Tucson, Arizona on the weekend of May 14th.
     Local chairman Ed Biebel, formerly of Cleveland, did an outstanding job of
     planning three days of interesting events for the assembled delegates -
     including a scenic dinner Thursday evening.; a tour of Kitt Peak National
     Observatory Friday afternoon; a scenic dinner & skywatch Friday night;
     delegate sessions Saturday morning & afternoon (the so-called "closed ses­
     sions"); and a public session Saturday evening. There was also a press
     conference Friday morning, which was well attended by the local media, and
     a special delegate breakfast Saturday morning, sponsored by Allen Benz of
     the Foundation for U FO Research - a Tucson-based UFO organization.
            About forty delegates attended the "closed sessions", which were held
     in one of the meeting rooms of Tucson's Plaza International Hotel. In our
     opinion the discussion that ·took place there was very likely the most pro­
     found & worthwhile of recent years, even though no formal resolutions were
     adopted. Every aspect of ufology, and the problems it faces due to public
     apathy, were discussed in frank detail. Not surprisingly, no over-all con­
     sensus could be reached, since the delegates represented many different
     approaches to the UFO problem, from scientific /conservative to contactee.
     But everyone present benefited from the exchange of views, and that     was
     about all that could be expected.
            The public session, held in a high school auditorium in an obscure
     part of Tucson, had a disappointing attendance in spite of considerable
     local radio, TV and newspaper publicity.    (Exact attendance figures are
     Classified.) Your " Smear" editor acted as master of ceremonies, and pre­
     sented our annual "Ufologist of the Year" award to researcher Hal Starr
     of Phoenix, who was our featured speaker this year.
            Also, since this was our 20th annual meeting, we decided to present
     a "Ufologist of the Decade" award, which went to Dr. J. Allen Hynek     of
     CUFO S, who in our collective opinion has done more than anyone else to
     make ufology viable and even respectable. Dr. Hynek was informed of the
     award by telephone several weeks ahead of time, and dictated a short· but
     elo quent acceptance spe�.ch over the phone - the text of which has dis­
     appeared unmysteriously at the moment. In this lost text Hynek made graci­
     ous reference to the NU FOC and our long-time efforts in the field of ufol­
     ogy. Since Dr. Hynek could not attend the convention in person, his award,
..   in the ;form of a pla que, was accepted by his friend and former co-worker
     Allen Hendry, who now lives.in Tucson. UFO fans will recall that Hendry
     was formerly the chief investigator for CUFO S, and is the author of "The
     UFO Handbook".
            The most impressive lecture of the evening was·by Hal Starr, a vet­
     eran investigative reporter & newsman who, among other activities, runs an
     internationally syndicated U FO show from Phoenix. His theme was "The KGB,
     CIA, and UFOs - The Cosmic Watergate".    Starr has that professional qual­
     ity of delivery which is all too rare in UFO lectures, and his material
     was dramatic, whether or not one chooses to believe all of it. One      of
     Starr's main themes was the somewhat mysterious deaths of Morris K. Jessup

and James McDonald. He also discussed the strange interest that govern-
ment agencies seem to have taken in his own ufological activities. As an
example of this, he beguiled the audience by describing how he had found a

enough, your humble " Smear" editor was present earlier that day when the

      Another of the speakers at the public session was Kal K. Korff, a
young man from California who has distinguished himself greatly in the UFO
field by various papers & tracts, including a book which purports to be
the definitive work exposing the pitfalls of Billy Meier and the Pleiades.
Your editor was very favorably impressed by Korff's scientific bent, only
slightly distorted by the exeessive confidence of youth. Korff clashed
briefly with pro-Pleiadean Wendelle Stevens during the question & answer
period, when Korff showed a series of anti-Pleiadean slides. It is to the
credit of both men that the confrontation was kept brief and within rea­
sonable bounds.
      Final speaker on the public program was none other than     Timothy
Green Beckley ("Mr. UFO"), who came all the way from New York City to give
a well-rehersed run-down of current contactee claims. With him on the po­
dium was a charming young lady named Diane Tessman, from California, who
told of her own experiences as a contactee.
      Rick Hilberg, a co-founder of the NU FOC, had expected to fly in from
Cleveland, Ohio, to give a lecture at the public session, but was unable
to do so because of unforeseen business problems. Incidentally, Rick and
the rest of the "Cleveland Fe3:ction" of the NUFOC will be sponsoring the
glorious 21st annual convention, to be held the weekend after Labor Day
next year.
      It should be noted here, for those few who do not know, that Tucson
is the head quarters of AFRO (Aerial Phenomena Research Organization), al­
legedly the oldest and certainly one of the most prestigious of the many
American flying saucer research groups currently in existence.   AFRO is
run by its founder, Coral Lorenzen, and her husband Jim.   As has been not­
ed repeatedly by such groups as MU FON, APRO refuses for their own reasons
to cooperate with other UFO organizations. Thus, in spite of overtures
to the Lorenzens months before the NU FOC Tucson convention was held, we
received no official cooperation whatever from them! Whereas many of
their local members attended our conference on their own, there was no
word at all from the Lorenzens. One can only assume they resented the
"invasion" of their "turf". At least they did nothing to mess us up, as
far as we know, so at least we can be grateful for that!
      To your editor, the fondest memories of the 1983 NUFOC are not from
the speeches or even the "closed sessions" ravings, but from the new ufol­
ogical friends that-we made. To name but a few: Hal Starr, the showman
supreme; Greg Cushing, a vague CIA-type fellow who good-naturedly haunted
many of the private conversations (Did he plant Hal Starr's "bug"???); Al­
len Hendry; Ronald Story of UFO Encyclopedia fame; Wendelle Stevens; Allen
Benz; Winfield S. Brownell & his wife, well-meaning New Age workers from
California; Joanne Cusack of Kirlian photography fame; and many others,
including and especially the unforgettable, inimitable J. Richard Green­
well of Tucson - a Cryptozoological delight, whose next expedition will
be off into the wilds of the South Pacific, in search of an unmentionable
(he asked us not to mention it) sea animal of legendary fame!
      To sum up: Poor planning of the date and location of the public ses­
sion was the only flaw in an otherwise well-done (as well as rare!) per­
formance by local chairman Biebel, to whom the NU FOC and ufology in gen­
eral owe a debt of thanks.
