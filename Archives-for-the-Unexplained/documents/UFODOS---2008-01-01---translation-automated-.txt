                                        UFOLOGICAL LITERATURE


 Ufology does not study aliens or hypothetical extraterrestrial life forms (there are other sciences for this).
 Ufology studies flying objects that cannot be identified with anything known.


 There are about fifty hypotheses about the origin of UFOs, and an alien one is just an ordinary one. The
 assumption that UFOs are alien spaceships is tantamount to claiming that UFOs are time machines of our
 descendants.

 The first photograph of a UFO in history was taken on August 12, 1883. Mexican astronomer Jose Bonilla,
 photographing sunspots, noticed strange objects against the background of the Sun (in 2 hours he counted
 283 objects).

 The complete catalog of observed forms of UFOs includes 250 drawings. The classic, that is, the most
 frequently observed forms of UFOs are 32.

 The UFO invasion of the Soviet Union in the late 1980s was not related to their intensification. Just in
  1989, the Office of the Glavlit of the USSR, with the assistance of the Council of Ministers of the USSR,
 lifted the previously existing restrictions on the publication of information about UFOs.

 Terms such as unidentified radar target, anomalous aerospace phenomenon and the like are used in official
 sources to refer to UFOs (unidentified flying objects).

 experience is the most reliable source of the reality of UFOs and anomalous phenomena.
