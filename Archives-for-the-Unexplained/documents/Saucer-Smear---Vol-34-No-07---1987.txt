validity. But - suppose for a moment that just one such vision, of a
plane crash for instance, came out precisely as predicted. Don't we know
for a fact that the person making the prediction would be arrested  for

who, following a psychic vision, led police to the precise location of a

      In this, the fortieth year since Kenneth Arnold's classic UFO sight­
ing, there has quite naturally been much talk about Mr. Arnold and    his
friend Ray Palmer, co-founder of FATE Magazine.  An interesting  article
about these two departed gentlemen appears in the June "MUFON UFO Jour­
nal", written by Greg Long.
      Thus it is timely for us to tell our readers a little-known story
about the man who coined the term "flying saucer". In the summer     of
1967 we ran a huge UFO convention, commemorating the 20th anniversary of
the Arnold sighting, and we made a serious attempt to get both   Arnold
and Palmer to participate. Palmer would have been willing, but     Arnold
wanted a great deal of money; and when Palmer heard about this, he decided
to hold out for a large sum too.
      Incredibly, Arnold claimed he was about to immigrate from   Boise,
Idaho to Australia, and would be living there by the time of the conven­
tion.  He wanted a first-class round-trip airplane ticket from and back
to Australia, which would have cost thousands!  We continued correspond­
ence on the matter, and as the convention date grew nearer, it became ap­
parent that Arnold would still be in Idaho at that time. Arnold     shot
back that it didn't matter where he was - he wanted a sum of money equal
to the cost of the ticket, anyway. We wrote back inviting him to go to
hell.  That ended the correspondence.
      Ten years later, when we met Arnold and his wife at the 1977 FATE
Convention in Chicago, he pointedly refused to spea� to us, and walked
away every time we approached him.
      Ray Palmer, on the other hand, was a gentleman. We met him once
at his home in Amherst, Wisconsin, and had a long talk with him. He came
off as a bright, very articulate, though somewhat eccentric individual.
Quite likely the fact that he seldom appeared in public was caused by his
dwarf-like physical deformity. However, he did speak at the above-men­
tioned FATE Convention, and his lecture was most interesting indeed. He
died a short while later.
      Incidentally, two of our colleagues in the National UFO Conference
(NUFOC), namely Charles Wilhelm and Ron Schaffner, had planned to release
an "expose" of Kenneth Arnold to coincide with the 40th anniversary   bit.
We were looking forward to seeing what they would come up with;  but for
some reason the project fizzled.
