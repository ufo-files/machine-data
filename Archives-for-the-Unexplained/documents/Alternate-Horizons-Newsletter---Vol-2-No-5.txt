  In analysing this incident, it is first well to note the considerable possibility of a
hoax of one sort or another.    This type of phenomenon            •   •   •   an anonymous voice on the tele­
phone during a publicly advertised event    •   •   •   could well be a prank of some sort.                       Several
direct - if somewhat remote - possibilities have occurred to me.


  There is, however, some evidence tending in the opposite direction.


 We have previously noted the similarity to the Delong tape.                          It is possible that the
similarity is only due to the similarity of many such messages. This in itself could be
interpreted in more than one way.     But, whether this is true or not the fact remains:
They are quite, quite similar.


  It is not inconceivable that someone knowing about the Delong tape and perhaps my own
interest in it could have made the call, but the circumstances are such that I tend
strongly to doubt this.


  There is also an admittedly mild structural reason for rejecting the hoax thesis.                                       This
structural matter involves a particular term allegedly used by the caller which somewhat
lessens the likelihood of a hoax.


  It wouldn't stop at least some people, to be sure, but it is also appropriate to mention
that making threatening calls such as this do bear some potential consequences for the
caller.    Perhaps legal; perhaps moral;   perhaps UFOlogical.
