July 12th, 1975 in Dunedin, New Zealand
July 12th, 1975 in Dunedin, New Zealand
"Richard Williams" (a pseudonym) was having lunch with three of his friends (names were
withheld as they were well known in the area at the time) at a coffee shop called Stewarts
on the south side of Dunedin's octagon. All four of them were feeling under the weather
with migraines, etc. Williams noted the group was having unusual difficulty sustaining
conversation. They quietly ate their lunches when a sudden silence fell through the
previously noisy café filled with about 80 people. The patrons and staff froze mid-
movement, their positions comic and unstable. Even the steam billowing from the espresso
machine froze in place in a dynamic configuration. The four witnesses felt that they were
the only ones in the café who were unaffected by whatever it was. (Of course, it was very
likely the opposite was true)

At the top of the entry stairs (which the group was sitting across from), an unusual man
appeared. He was wearing clothes of "high taste and elegance" and appeared to be around
20 years old. He glided down the stairs (floating slightly above them) and moved across
the café over to the counter, where he stood first in line. The witnesses reported
something of a white "glow" surrounding his upper body/head. This glow appeared to be
emanating not from him, but from the air around him.
As soon as he stood first in the queue, time resumed, the steam continued flowing from
the espresso machine, people resumed their movements from where they left off.

The odd man ordered a glass of juice and sat down at the last free chair at a 12 person
table. He acknowledged the witnesses' attention with a smile. They wanted to talk to him
but they found they could not move. When they finally managed to stand up, they directed
themselves up the stairs and out of the café, seemingly against their will.
They saw this man about six weeks later, but this time he was surrounded by a green
glow, and nothing unusual happened. He walked down the stairs normally and ordered
another glass of juice, again taking the last free chair at the same table.

Another witness, Mac X, as he called himself, (who claimed he had never met the four
