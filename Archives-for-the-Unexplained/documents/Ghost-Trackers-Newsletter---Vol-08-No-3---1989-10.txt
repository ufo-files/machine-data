                 AN EXERCISE IN PHOTOGRAPHIC DISCRIMINATION:
                       THE HAUNTED CEMETERY AT STULL,          KABSAS

                                            By


                                      Maurice Schwalm

             The  rural   cemetary  at  Stull,   Kansas                is   legendarily
        possessed  by   demonic  werewolves,   vampires,              and other Satanic
        proxies that materialize  at   the  Spring and  Fall  equinoxes.
        Massive  vandalism  and/or   poltergeistic damage  has    occurred
        there.   The malign quality of the area is usually attributed to
        murder  in the graveyard at the time of its foundation <which in
        folklore allows demonic access to the grave population) .
             Such an explanatory incident would  be   appropos:   Legends
        always claim an historical and logical basis.   In this case, the
        usual claimed historical basis is readily disproven.   The legend
        states  that    a  stable boy killed the mayor in the graveyard in
        the 1850's.     The cemetery wasn't plotted until 1867  and  Stull
        was,  and  is, an unincorporated village that never had a mayor,
        nor apparently was there any later such incident.
              At least one verifiable phenomenon occurs   there:  Covens
        regard the cemetery as sacred and conduct rites there which have
        been,   once  at  least,  photographed.  Satanists attribute the
.   �
        presence to a "Child of the   Devil"  by a  local   witch buried
        there.   Again,  there  is  no           historical        evidence--nor is any
        likely.
             These phenomena have been           reported     in     detail   in   Daniel
        Fitzgerald's   Ghost  Towns  of  Kansas <Gossip Printing, Horton,
        Kansas, 1979>.    Since 1974, at least six accounts  of  multiple
        student  experiences  at  that   location  have appeared  in the
        University Daily Kansan  <Kansas   University>, as   well as  an
        account on Kansas City's Channel 5 in October, 1984.
             In photos taken by the writer in 1986 with a Polaroid SX-70
        camera,  the matter is illuminated somewhat differently.   There
        is a distinct golden glow around a cemetery marker <somehow        the
        name  is  not on the D. A. R. 's 1940 list) which proved to be that
        of a seventeen-year-old boy who died in the       1890's  of  typhoid
        after being crushed and weakened when a wagon overturned.        Also,
        a  photo  was   obtained   of   a   werewolf-like   boy standing in a
        cemetery tree.    He appears to be holding out a mask.
             This  suggests     that    the    seventeen-year-old    boy    is
        identifiable   as   a  "werewolf    ghost"  of the kind described by
        Peter Haining in hie Dictionary of      Ghost  Lore   <Prentice-Hall,
        Inc.,  Englewood Cliffs, BJ. , 1984) : A ghost that by preference
        changes to  the  shape  of   a  werewolf.   If  so,  photographic
        discrimination here displaces shamanic discernment.
