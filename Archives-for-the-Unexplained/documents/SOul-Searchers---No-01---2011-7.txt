All too often you hear the horror stories of
ghost hunters trespassing on properties or
deliberately provoking a spirit in order to get
that great picture or experience that
adrenalin rush. They care very little about
the poor soul that is stuck between the
worlds and very often they don‟t consider
the health & safety aspects of the people
conducting the investigation.

So if you are conducting an investigation at
a site it is good practise to do an initial site
investigation in daylight hours so you can
see the actual landscape and can note
possible hazards. Also this site visit can
give you clues regarding possible hot spots
for paranormal activity. If you only conduct                  Investigating Picton Viaduct
night time investigations then you are
missing out on vital phenomenon, because
paranormal activity can occur throughout the night and day.

Give the site a thorough once-over. Note any bushes or tree limbs that have contact with the
house and could cause banging or scraping noises. Look for visibly loose boards, loose roof tiles,
rain gutters, or shutters. Note also the position of any street lights etc. that could be reflecting
inside the house. While outside, stand in several different locations and observe if there are any
sound amplification points. House angles, trees, and terrain changes can affect how sound travels.
Check all the windows and doors for drafts and look for any animals that could be nesting in the
roof and walls.

                                           To the average paranormal investigator the digital
                                           camera has become an essential tool for their
                                           investigations. Using digital cameras in an investigation
                                           is an easy and time effective way of getting the right
                                           images for research purposes but often there is a
                                           question on the authenticity of the images that are
                                           being photographed.

                                           Digital cameras are no doubt an excellent tool for
                                           documenting a location and the investigation procedure;
                                           however a problem arises when the digital camera
                                           becomes the primary source of the investigation
                                           justifications which can lead to some questions being
                                           raised on the credibility and integrity of the paranormal
                                           investigation.


Unfortunately with the technology available and the ability to „Photoshop‟ images it makes it more
difficult to prove that the image is authentic. Even with all of the technology that we have
available, digital cameras should not be used as the single tool in an investigation. Investigations
results should include detailed research and corresponding activity incorporating reputable
photographs, video, evp and substantiated witness reports.

Basic Guide for Paranormal photography

Flash: Use a digital camera that allows you to adjust the flash strength. Turn it up all the way to
increase your depth of field.

Night scene or night shot: cameras with this function should be used with a tripod. Without one
you stand a great chance of blur or light trails because night scene gives your camera a longer
exposure time. Do not use a long exposure unless you are shooting off a tripod. The longer the
exposure the more movement in your picture becomes see through. See through apparitions and
light trails all could be movement with long exposure.




SOuL Searchers eMagazine – Issue 1               5
ISO: ISO speed affects the shutter speed / aperture combinations you can use to obtain correct
exposure. The higher the ISO the more pixilation you get. If using a flash at night you want an
ISO that is appropriate. 400 to 640 are good all-around settings. If you have a 12 mega pixel or
higher camera you can even go up to an 800 ISO. Do not go higher unless you are shooting off a
tripod without a flash. Also do not drop to a 200 or 100 ISO unless you are shooting off a tripod
with flash.

Foreign objects/Orbs in your picture: when using a flash any foreign particle, from lint to pollen
to insects, even moisture appear as objects in your picture if they are near your lens, they often
give the illusion of being further away from the lens. Remember you are shooting in 2D.




Shadows: Many times a dark shadow or spot is misperception. A finger blocking a portion of a
flash will appear as a dark shape. When taking pictures an object off the side can appear as a
larger shape when the flash creates a shadow.


Paranormal Mist: Are they spirits? They must be paranormal as I don‟t see it with my eyes.
First look at exposures on your digital camera. A long exposure can create a mist like appearance.
Also look around the environment, there might be lots of highly reflective surfaces from windows,
mirrors, chromes, glass tables, vases. A flash off many of these can create what looks like a mist
on your digital camera.




When outside mist captured on the camera can be anything from atmospheric haze, yours or
another‟s breath, to temperature changes. When its cold out we can see our breath, even when its
warmer we don‟t see our breath but it still can be accentuated by the camera flash. Often these
anomalies can be easily explained with critical thinking and common sense.

Samhain weekend at Monte Cristo, a homestead which has the notoriety of being the "most
haunted house" in Australia. With the veil between the worlds being especially thin at this juncture
of the year what better time could there be to conduct some serious paranormal research. Armed
with cameras, handycams, and a variety of paranormal testing equipment the SOuL Searchers
team departed Sydney early in the morning with great expectations and perhaps some trepidation.

Monte Cristo is situated in the quaint country township of Junee which is approximately 470 kms
South West of Sydney. It was built in 1884 by Christopher Crawley, a wealthy local farmer. He was
married to Elizabeth, a hard and ruthless woman who was rumoured to have ruled the household
with an iron fist.
