Case xxx.
A yard covered with smelling slag

Time: Sunday, September 1, at about 20.10
Place: 1,2 km N of the Strömtorp railway station (county of Örebro)

Someone at the Staff pencilled a note of this interesting case on a slip of paper noting that 
slag had been recovered on September 3 and sent to FOA 1 (the chemical section of the Research Institute of National Defence). The police report on the case:

”Degerfors police district. REPORT. Tuesday, September 3, 1946. On Monday, September 2, 1946 at 21.00 hours, editor Ragnar Jansson, Degerfors, reported by phone to this police station that he, shortly before, from painter Gösta Johansson, with address Box 1411, Degerfors, had received a report that Johansson on Sunday, September 1, 1946, in the evening, just north of the Strömtorp railway station in Degerfors, had observed a light phenomenon and that Johansson later, at the place of the sighting, had recovered coke-like objects of a considerable number. 

”Editor Jansson stated, on being questioned, that he had no intention of reporting the sighting in the press.

”For this reason and in accordance with the contents of the circular from the County Government Board of Örebro to the police chiefs of July 8, 1946, concerning measures in case of reported sightings of light phenomena, etc, an investigation has been performed, and whereby the following persons have been heard and stated:

”Painter Gösta Axel Johansson, born March 30, 1920, registered and living in Degerfors, postal address Box 1411, Degerfors:

”On Sunday, September 1, 1946, shortly after 20.00 hours he had cycled along the main road from the Strömtorp railway station towards Storegården in the Degerfors community. When it was about 20.10, the time he couldn´t state more precisely, and Johansson was about 100-125 m SE of the estate Tallhaga, Strömtorp, he had on the ground, immediately close to, and east of, the said estate, observed a fiery light, the size of which Johansson estimated at 2 x 3 meters. The light, which had a yellowish colour, had been completely invariable for about 10 seconds, whereafter it suddenly disappeared. The whole sight had seemed to Johansson as if there was a fire inside a room and the door all of a sudden was closed. 

”After about 5 seconds Johansson had sighted a complely similar light, in the same place, which after further 5 seconds vanished as suddenly as the previous one. Johansson had then left his bike and ran towards the place, but when he was only 30-40 m therefrom, it seemed to Johansson that there was an explosion without any sound. In the immediate vicinity of the estate in question, all of a sudden a glowing mass had started to be thrown around the yard [of the estate] and [had] fallen down in an area of about 15 x 20 meters, whereupon everything went dark again. 

”Johansson had then hurried up to, and into, the estate, where he told the family Arvid Pettersson, who live alone on the estate, about his sightings. None of the family members stated that they had seen anything unusual and had thus not seen anything of what Johansson had observed. When Johansson, then in the company of the estate owner, worker Arvid Pettersson, went out into the yard, a weak smell of gas could be noticed, but Johansson could not state what kind of gas it was the question of, if there was one. 

”Johansson states, on being questioned, that he during the whole time of his sightings not heard any sound. Neither had he seen any other persons in the vicinity. At the occasion there was darkness and had a rather heavy rain been falling, which had been the case without any interruption during the whole day of September 1. The ground had therefore been completely soaked with water. 

”Johansson had then, in the company of Pettersson, searched the ground where the phenomenon was seen, whereupon they found some coke-like objects, some of them like slag. Of these objects Johansson had brought along some to his house, since he had been interested in it. The objects had upon recovery been completely cold, since a good while had passed since ”the explosion”. There was, however, a clearly discernable gaslike smell from the objects. 

”On the next day, after having ended his work in the evening, Johansson reported his observations to editor Jansson of the Karlskoga-Kuriren. He had never thought of reporting to the police. On being questioned Johansson finally states that he had noticed nothing in the air before he first spotted the light on the ground.

”Mill worker Arvid Emanuel Pettersson, born on July 15, 1892, registered and living in Degerfors, postal address Tallhaga, Strömtorp: 

”He, who was living alone with his family on the estate Tallhaga, outside which the forementioned Johansson said he saw the light phenomenon in question, had on Sunday, September 1, 1946, been sitting in his house reading and listening to music on the radio. Pettersson, or someone of his family, who likewise were in the house, had not noticed anything unusual and had thus not known anything about Johanssons sightings until he, at about 20.15 hours, came into the house and told, in complete conformity to what he [Johansson] here previously has stated. Pettersson had followed Johansson into the yard, where they then made the observations reported by Johansson. Also Pettersson says he noticed a gas-like smell out in the yard, but could not either state what kind of smell it was. 

”Even on the following morning Pettersson had noticed the smell in the yard. The smell had, however, no harmful effect neither on Pettersson nor on any of his family members. After sunrise on Monday, September 2, Pettersson had seen that the whole of his yard and the fields of gras were covered with small coke-like objects, of which some were like slag. Such objects were also found on the steps to the house. Neither Pettersson nor his family members had not touched any of the found objects or in any way changed the appearance of the yard after Johansson had made his noted observations. No damage had occured on the estate by the incident. Neither Pettersson had thought of reporting the matter to the police.

”On Tuesday, September 3, 1946, the undersigned police sergeant, performed, in the company of the forementioned Gösta Johansson and Arvid Pettersson, an investigation of the place of the observation. There was found and recovered a multitude of coke-like objects, totaling about 3 cubic decimeters. Some objects were burnt and slag-like. The area of the yard, where the objects were recovered was measured at 25 x 15 meters. Any marks on the ground, which consisted of a graveled yard and lawn, could not be noted. Pettersson restated his earlier information that no measures had been taken neither on the yard or the lawns after the sighting by Johansson. Pettersson said he knew firmly that no such objects had been found in the yard or the lawns before. 

”Johansson was given the opportunity to show the places where he was when he made the different observations. It was then evident that he had a completely unblocked view towards the place close to the Tallhaga estate where he claimed to have seen the incident. 

”Any other persons, who possibly could have any information in this matter, has not been possible to find. Those objects found in the place, as well as those found by Gösta Johansson shortly after the sighting, which he brought to his house, are now in custody at this police station pending further advice.

”According to the General Staff map II.Ö.33, the Askersund area, the sighting has been made about 1.200 meters N of the Strömtorp railway station... Degerfors police station as above. signed /Gustaf Johansson/, police sergeant.”

The report was distributed up the chain of command, via the County Government Board and the Regional Staff to the Defence Staff, where Captain Birger Olin, of the Air Defence department, on September 10, ordered an analysis of the coke-like substances. FOA I found the following:

”The sample is made up of a large number of coke- and slag-like pieces which, by appearance, match completely with Sample 17 [case xxx] from Rynninge. The find can without further investigation be classified as ashes from a boiler, or similar. All sample objects are impregnated with a grey-brown powder, probably powdery ashes. Nothing in the find points to it having originated from a space projectile.”

Status: Unidentified (sightning) 
Sources: Police report, Sept. 3; Analysis report nr 27 from FOA I.
