                                         The UFO Incident
                                  Movie Review by Bill Konkolesky
         For the morning session of our upcoming January 27 meeting, we will be showing the movie “The UFO Inci-
dent” based on true events of the infamous 1961 Betty and Barney Hill abduction event. The film originally played
on primetime television during the Halloween season (surprised?) of 1975 and is pretty difficult to find a copy of,
nowadays.
         “The UFO Incident” is a rare treat in many ways and works so well on many levels. It’s superbly-acted,
sticks exceptionally well to the facts, knows its special effects budget, and even works as a period piece, highlighting
the social and political issues of those times.
         Barney and Betty Hill are played wonderfully by the great James Earl Jones and Estelle Parsons. Jones and
Parsons are highly believable as deeply stressed UFO witnesses and have an authentic chemistry as a devoted couple
under the strain of heavy psychological duress.
         This film earns tremendous praise for sticking to the facts, too. Later Hollywood efforts like “Communion,”
“Fire in the Sky,” and “Intruders” suffer embarrassingly from an attempt to spice up the original abduction accounts
on which they’re based (especially the first two of these films). “UFO Incident” came out well before these other
films and it is evident how carefully the film-makers tried to preserve the factual integrity of Hills dramatic encoun-
ter. It shines because of this.
         “The UFO Incident” wisely knows and heeds the limits of its special effects budget, as well. The film is told
primarily in flashbacks, much of it through the Hills’ hypnosis sessions with Boston psychiatrist Dr. Benjamin
Simon (played in the film by Bernard Hughes). During moments where the film-makers could’ve pushed cheesy
UFO special effects, they wisely pulled back and opted to let the strong acting abilities of Jones and Parsons as
Barney and Betty, eerily recounting the events while sitting in the psychiatrist’s office, carry the narrative.
         This movie is so deep and well-acted that even if it didn’t have a UFO abduction element, it would still work
as a moving insight into the issues of what a black man and white woman faced as a married couple in New England
during the early 1960’s. Throw in the Cuban Missile Crisis as a backdrop and the tension is palpable on these issues
alone.
         “The UFO Incident” is a well-made and moving account of the Hill’s historic UFO abduction event. A true
classic. We’ll be showing this 90 minute film at 10am at our upcoming meeting.

           RODRIGO BRAVO GARRIDO
           Since the beginning of Chilean history, there have been reports of unidentified phenomena, sometimes called UFOs, ob-
served in our skies.
           Over the years, we have increased our capacity to explain many of the sightings, but some continue to remain without a
scientific or logical explanation.
           For that reason, in October of 1997, the Department of Civil Aeronautics, the Chilean equivalent of the American FAA, set
up the Committee for the Study of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena, known as the CEFAA. This agency handles the best reports of
unidentified aerial phenomena in cooperation with aeronautic specialists.
           In 2000, the Aviation branch of the Army of Chile assigned me to research anomalous aerial phenomena, which they knew
were real, and their affect on aerospace security. I then graduated to become a military pilot, and learned that the Aviation Amy had
in its files many reports of UFO incidents from military pilots.

mented. This has helped to create awareness among the flight crews about UFOs, and they now cooperate with flight security to
report any abnormal situation.
           One of our most important civil aviation cases occurred in 1988, showing that unidentified flying objects can be a danger
for air operations. A Boeing 737 pilot, on a final approach to the runway at “The Tepual” Airport in Puerto Montt City, suddenly
encountered a large white light surrounded by green and red. The light was moving in the opposite direction of the airplane, coming
straight at it. The pilot had to make a steep turn to the left in order to avoid a collision. The phenomenon was observed by the con-
trol tower personnel.
           In 2000, the crew of a Chilean plane from the Aviation branch of the Army, flying South of Santiago, observed a long ci-
gar-shaped object, a brilliant gray. It flew parallel to the right side of the aircraft for two minutes and then disappeared at a very
high speed. This object was detected by the radar of the Control Center of Santiago which notified the crew minutes before the inci-
dent.
           Sadly, the UFO subject has been contaminated with false information, out of touch with reality, provided by unqualified
people to the media. This has moved the issue away from a scientific and specialized study where it belongs.
           In light of that problem, we now make the call to all the countries that are presently working on this issue in a serious and
factual way, to unite their efforts. We would like this to include the United States of America.
           This cooperative effort would help all of us counteract the potentially unsafe effects of UFOs on air operations around the
world.
           New cases are being documented by pilots, traffic controllers, operations staff at the world’s airports, and by many others
with the proper training to determine if a flying object is something unusual.
           The true origin of these UFOs is unknown, yet they do affect aviation all around the planet, and this must be addressed.

ington Post; this may have contributed to the turnout, in spite of torrential rains that morning that broke a major drought in the area.
Sue attacked the “UFO abduction mythology” that abductions always occur in the middle of nowhere, to just one, uneducated per-
son – and that, ultimately, the location itself is nonexistent. Sue also countered the notion that the Hills had had contact with the
“emerging data” on abductions, and averred that the recent book by Kathleen Marden and Stanton Friedman demonstrates that the
Hills had many conscious memories – that many details of their recounted experiences were not solely the product of hypnosis.
          After a break for lunch Carl Feindt, whose catalogue of approximately 1100 underwater UFO reports may be accessed at
www.waterufo.net, opened with a study of UFO physical influences on water. Feindt proposed a model to explain four specific
types of phenomena apparently generated by the ionized force fields he postulates surround some UFOs: 1) a water column; 2) a
“bulge” of water; 3) depressed water or “holes” in water; and 4) mounding of water. Basically, the relationship of the UFO to the
water surface – particularly its height below, at, or above the surface -- causes one or more of the phenomena described. My wife
Linda, with her training in engineering, feels that this intriguing explanation could be further explored mathematically.
          Feindt’s presentation was followed by a trio whose ufological careers have helped define, and still to inform, the most rig-
orous approach to the subject. All three are alumni of the fabled National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena
(NICAP), having joined in the 1960s. That these scholars are still contributing mightily to the field is evidenced by an article by
Dr. Maccabee in the September and October UFO Magazine on the failure of “prosaic” explanations at “explaining away” several
“classic” UFO cases, Dick Hall’s Journal of UFO History (check out his website www.hallrichard.com for this and other quality
items), and Don Berliner’s appearance on the radio version of the Bill O’Reilly Radio Factor the very next week after the seminar.

           Don Berliner’s fact-filled presentation “From Arnold to Roswell: Discs & Saucers & Mass Confusion”, concentrated on the
hectic first months of the “modern UFO era”. Berliner, who is the Chairman of the Fund for UFO Research, had actually heard a
live radio announcement that the Army Air Force had come into the possession of a flying disc near Roswell, New Mexico, and sees
in the Roswell event a transition from the June 24th, 1947 Kenneth Arnold sighting of “discs at a distance” to later ufology, with its
focus on physical influences and trace cases, occupants, and purported crashes. Arnold’s discs sighting itself marked somewhat of a
departure in terms of the physical appearance of the perceptions, from the “glowing balls” that were the “foo fighters” of World War
II and the tubular “Ghost Rockets” of Scandinavia soon after the great conflict. Mr. Berliner also observed that the Arnold sighting
was almost singular in its precision, as the aviator used a stop watch and two points of reference in his speed calculations for the ob-
jects that he saw. Don further noted that the Army Air Force (AAF) press release about the Roswell “flying disc” being “loaned” to
the AAF would not have made sense if the recovered material was felt to belong to the AAF in the first place – another fascinating
little piece of evidence for the utter strangeness of the event.
           After Don’s talk a short break transpired, which helped in digesting what had been presented so far, and then Richard Hall
took the podium. Called the “godfather of ufology” by Dr. Resta, Dick Hall did not disappoint, as he sketched the history of the Na-
tional Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena’s role in the modern era, which Hall, as Donald Keyhoe’s right-hand man and
Secretary for NICAP, was particularly well-placed to observe. Dr. Resta had justly termed Hall’s great works, The UFO Evidence I
and II, as the “Bible of ufology”; Hall noted that the first of the two compilations was developed partly to make money as NICAP
was running short of funds. Hall observed that the April 24, 1964 Socorro, New Mexico sighting by police officer Lonnie Zamora
marked the beginning of the 1964-1967 wave; how the March, 1966 Dexter and Hillsdale, Michigan sightings got then-Congressman
Gerald Ford interested in getting the subject investigated; and that after the publication of the “Condon Report” things went sour,
with media interest and favorable treatment of the subject taking a nosedive from which they have not recovered to this day.
           Lastly, Bruce Maccabee gave a justly-titled “60 (or more) Years in 60 (or more) Minutes” overview of the major UFO
events in the U.S., with reference in numerous instances to cases which Maccabee had personally investigated, and also noting rele-
vant treatments of these on Bruce’s website, www.brumac.8k.com. Maccabee believes that the five years from 1947 to 1952 estab-
lished the “tradition” of scientific, military, and societal response to the UFO sightings. Bruce’s presentation contended that 1)
something strange is going on – AFC (Alien Flying Craft) are real; 2) there are lots of theories about them, which won’t be answered
until the “ufonauts” tell us – and probably not even then; and 3) how we study the subject has to come to grips with the different
meanings “history” and “true facts” have when applied to ufology. A fuller exposition of these points will have to await another arti-
cle. Maccabee listed several 1947 UFO “events” that predated the Kenneth Arnold sighting, and mentioned his belief that the initial
Roswell “cover-up” was done to disencourage locals, especially kids, from going out to the crash area and looking for material for
themselves.
           Overall, “Mysteries of Space and Sky IV” had some of the quality of an academic symposium, with the emphasis on solid
fact and logical observation rather than on racy speculation. As with some academic symposia, the timing was not perfect. Speak-
ers held to the task of providing a beginning, a middle argument, and an end summary in their presentations, rather than attempting to
adhere to a locked timetable. As a result, the presentations ran almost until 7 pm, nearly an hour longer than scheduled. The lecture
room, while comfortable, was barely adequate to hold the crowd and, if the speaker lineup is as good next time, may not be sufficient.
Vending was handled on an informal basis, with tables in the back of the room and at its entrance holding the books, CDs, and other
buyables. Bruce had the most merchandising savvy of the group, offering a combo deal on his “faction” book Abduction in My Life,
some really cute note-cards created by his daughter, and his music CD “The Joy of Ivories”. This latter is an eclectic compilation of
synthesized music ranging from classical takeoffs to honkytonk and cowboy-western sounds. On this disc I particularly liked “Flight
of the Bumble Boogie”, an updated version of Rimsky-Korsakoff’s “Flight of the Bumblebee”, the eerily-beautiful “Dark Asteroid”,
and “New Age Rising”, a major piece with Mendelssohnian overtones which Bruce composed for and first presented at the Grand
Rapids, Michigan, 1997 MUFON International Symposium.
           A couple of side notes: the availability of the presenters before and after their presentations and their willingness to answer
numerous questions not only added to the value of the event, but it speaks well for the speakers, who truly are professional in the sub-
ject and evince a desire to spread credible knowledge about it. I also was taken by the historical role which Michigan sightings and
Michiganders have had on UFO history, with of course special consideration for the March, 1966 Dexter and Hillsdale sightings and
the subsequent role which then-Congressman Gerald Ford played in getting governmental hearings held, leading to the formation of
the notorious Colorado Project.
           I would highly hope that Dr. Resta and the Swiateks are able to continue to put on future “Mysteries of Space and Sky” sym-
posia, and commend Anne Arundel Community College for furnishing the venue for the event. In a field that too often elicits quips
by late-night comedians and light and uninformed media comment, there is a crying need for professional, scientific, and rigorous live
treatments of the subject being available to a general audience. In my opinion “Mysteries” does the best job of any U.S. symposium
at filling this need. Located as it is on the outskirts of Annapolis, the city of Arnold, Maryland is within chip-shot distance of Wash-
ington, D.C., Baltimore, and the Chesapeake Bay, themselves exceptional travel destinations. Consider making this event part of a
short, medium, or long vacation to this historic and beautiful part of the country next year!

                                                The UFO Incident
                                         Movie Review by Bill Konkolesky
          For the morning session of our upcoming January 27 meeting, we will be showing the movie “The UFO Incident” based on
true events of the infamous 1961 Betty and Barney Hill abduction event. The film originally played on primetime television during
the Halloween season (surprised?) of 1975 and is pretty difficult to find a copy of, nowadays.
          “The UFO Incident” is a rare treat in many ways and works so well on many levels. It’s superbly-acted, sticks exception-
ally well to the facts, knows its special effects budget, and even works as a period piece, highlighting the social and political issues
of those times.
          Barney and Betty Hill are played wonderfully by the great James Earl Jones and Estelle Parsons. Jones and Parsons are
highly believable as deeply stressed UFO witnesses and have an authentic chemistry as a devoted couple under the strain of heavy
psychological duress.
          This film earns tremendous praise for sticking to the facts, too. Later Hollywood efforts like “Communion,” “Fire in the
Sky,” and “Intruders” suffer embarrassingly from an attempt to spice up the original abduction accounts on which they’re based
(especially the first two of these films). “UFO Incident” came out well before these other films and it is evident how carefully the
film-makers tried to preserve the factual integrity of Hills dramatic encounter. It shines because of this.
          “The UFO Incident” wisely knows and heeds the limits of its special effects budget, as well. The film is told primarily in
flashbacks, much of it through the Hills’ hypnosis sessions with Boston psychiatrist Dr. Benjamin Simon (played in the film by Ber-
nard Hughes). During moments where the film-makers could’ve pushed cheesy UFO special effects, they wisely pulled back and
opted to let the strong acting abilities of Jones and Parsons as Barney and Betty, eerily recounting the events while sitting in the
psychiatrist’s office, carry the narrative.
          This movie is so deep and well-acted that even if it didn’t have a UFO abduction element, it would still work as a moving
insight into the issues of what a black man and white woman faced as a married couple in New England during the early 1960’s.
Throw in the Cuban Missile Crisis as a backdrop and the tension is palpable on these issues alone.
          “The UFO Incident” is a well-made and moving account of the Hill’s historic UFO abduction event. A true classic. We’ll
be showing this 90 minute film at 10am at our upcoming meeting.
