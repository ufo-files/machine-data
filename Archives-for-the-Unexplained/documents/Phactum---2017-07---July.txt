                                Ark Encounter




          In August of 2017 the humble editor of Phactum decided to travel to Williamstown, Kentucky,
      the site of Ark Encounter, a full size and accurate representation of Noah’s Ark and the brain-child
      of Ken Ham, an Australian evangelist and Young Earth Creationist. The Ark shown here suppos-
      edly conforms to dimensions and descriptions as related in the Holy Bible and interpreted by schol-
      ars. Whatever the degree of accuracy it is an impressive structured 510 feet in length, 85 feet wide,
     Page 2                                               Phactum                              July/August/September 2017



and 51 feet high. A massive wooden structure. The picture on the front page was the only one where I managed to get a view
of the entire length of the Ark, unfortunately obscured by some trees. I have other pictures but due to lack of a 35 mm lens I
was not able to photograph the entire length from a clear viewing point. The mashup of two photos below do show the entire
Ark from the port side (left), a quite handsome image from a distance, perhaps two football fields away. Unfortunately I have
no sophisticated photo handling software, but I suspect that readers get the idea. The starboard side is less picturesque and one




doubts that Coast Guard scrutiny would be favorable. Nonetheless, there it is; an accurate replica of the Ark that saved all
humanity and all species except for those that it didn’t save.

has also become more doglike--mottled coats, floppy ears,

Reported by Jennifer Cortese
     Dr. Offit’s lecture on scientific inventions that changed the world for the worse was very thought-provoking. I thought
that his discussion of pain and addictive medicine was the most intriguing. It seems that humans have been trying to treat pain
without causing addiction for thousands of years. It is interesting that at one point in time the use of opium and morphine was
believed to be acceptable, even for children, for many different common ailments.

     I did not realize that the invention of the hypodermic needle by Alexander Wood was actually an attempt to combat the
addiction that often follows the use of drugs such as morphine. Furthermore, I was surprised to learn that doctors would even
instruct patients how they could inject morphine themselves at home!

      As a Chemistry major, I liked that Dr. Offit explained the steps by which drugs like heroin and opioids came about. I did
not know that heroin was a derivative of morphine that was created specifically in an attempt to diminish the addictive proper-
ties of the substance. Adding an acetyl group did not help to make the drug any less addictive, and yet chemists continued to
search for ways to modify components of morphine! These chemists had good intentions, yet the chemicals that they created,
such as heroin and oxycontin, became widespread and caused many people to become hopelessly addicted. It is understandable
that pain relief and addiction often come hand in hand, as the relief of pain in and of itself can be an addiction that people seek.
Adding in the addiction caused by chemicals only makes the creation of these drugs more dangerous.

     Learning about the progression from opiates to morphine, heroin, and opioids makes me wonder what new, exciting drug
will be produced next that results in mass addiction. Researchers have been trying to separate pain relief from addiction for
hundreds of years, so I do not believe that they will stop now. There was once a time when heroin was sold over the counter
while aspirin required a prescription, which demonstrates how little we initially understand about the chemicals that are pro-
duced. I wonder if one day researchers will truly be able to understand the underlying causes of addiction and ultimately get
rid of the addictive properties all together. Somehow, I doubt that this will ever happen, but I would be surprised if chemists
ever stop trying to create this ultimate pain relief drug!

                            Useful Definitions of Ideologies
                                                   By Paul Schlueter III



     In the April/May 2017 Free Inquiry, p. 44, article writer James J. Hughes quoted some interesting definitions of ideolo-
gies. The original source cited is Understanding Libertarian Morality: The Psychological Dispositions of Self-Identified Liber-
tarians," in PLOS ONE 7, No. 8 (2012). The co-author of this article which Hughes named is psychologist Jon Haidt, who was
researching moral intuitions (particularly those which seem to be innate and instinctive to people). First Haidt lists morals:
 Care/Harm: Protecting others from harm.
 Fairness/Cheating: Treating others in proportion to their actions.
 Liberty/Oppression: Judgements about whether subjects are tyrannized.
 In-Group Loyalty: To your race, group, family, nation.
 Respect for authority/heirarchy.
 Sanctity/Purity: Sanctity/degradation, avoiding disgusting things, food, and actions.

    Haidt found that Conservatives, Liberals, and Libertarians differ in their relative sensitivity to (or observance of?) these
foundation moral traits:
 LIBERALS are more sensitive to the first two.
 CONSERVATIVES are less sensitive to the first two, but more sensitive to the final three.
 LIBERTARIANS are most sensitive to the third, and less sensitive to the other five.

      For more of the underlying research and reasoning, please refer back to the original article by Haight, et al. Keeping this
list handy could prove useful when considering how to best categorize ourselves and others, and when deciding how to frame
discussions we hope will appeal to others. Because these characteristics appear to be inborn, it would seem to be unlikely that
one would readily change his/her ideology in response to argument. However, knowing the ideals which appeal to such indi-
viduals might sway their specific support for/opposition to a proposal.

      My own informal and non-scientific survey (discussions with friends) indicates strong disagreement with these findings
among (approximately conservative) evangelical Christians and Correctional Officers. The primary objection is that "Science
is unreliable anyway." There is a ready response: "Show me the gene that is responsible; they can't point it out." (Note: Scien-
tists admit this - the traits are probably the result of many genes interacting in complex ways.) Many direct the conversation
toward their opposition to "Determinism" (the idea that our ideas and behaviors are inherited), and specifically the argument
that "Gays aren't bom that way; they learned it from their environments." Often cited is the argument that "Liberals want to
make us believe that nothing is anybody's fault, but we all have to be responsible for our choices." I found it interesting that not
one evangelical person held out the argument for evangelization: the idea that people's beliefs and behaviors are subject to their
being convinced that the Church/God favors a position which the believer should adopt. Indeed, if we accept the moral founda-
tions described above as a guide, then the heavily religious (and particularly the evangelical) would mostly fall under the Con-
servative ideology.

support, and conservative opposition. As such, it would offer little value in terms of legislation or social policy, causing more
of the same old partisanship and division.

search that studies these ideas in the context of race/culture.

by the individual's underlying wealth and power. The thug is essentially lacking in wealth and authority, while the bully tends to
have one, the other, or both.

   What thugs and bullies have in common is their apparent enjoyment of the use of violence or authority to force others to
bend to their will. Thugs are characterized by a relatively high level of physical strength and ferocity. Bullies enjoy the support
of underlying support networks (gangs, police authority, powerful relatives, etc.) which guarantees their dominance in conflict.

    Both thugs and bullies seem to use a mental algorithm of consequence in selecting their victims. First, is the victim likely to
submit? Second, if the victim fights back, is the victim likely to prevail (either through his own strength/ferocity, or through the
support of his own network)? Third, is there a good chance that the victim can be intimidated against reporting the abuse he suf-
fers? If not, then fourth, how well does the thug/bully believe he can withstand the consequences of being caught abusing the
victim?

   Thugs and bullies both seem to have a very good sense of how best to demonize and alienage the victim from his support
network. In prison, calling someone a "child molester" is all but guaranteed to cause him to be shunned by anyone who would
otherwise support or assist him; offenses against "the weak" are also cited as derogatory character stains (though it is notable
that thugs and bullies thrive on intimidating "the weak," it is "manly" to victimize any other man to his face, no matter how
poorly equipped he might be to stand up for himself).

   Thugs and bullies also seem to share an overblown sense of entitlement, especially in regard to their entitlement to victimize
anyone who can be victimized. This is, in fact, the defining characteristic. A thug/bully takes what he wants, dehumanizes and
belittles the victim as part of the offense, and invariably does so from a position (imagined or real) of his own superior power. If
successful, he will return to his victim again and again, with ever-increasing demands.

   Another factor thugs/bullies have in common: If they are shown that the victim is not worth the consequences he'll suffer for
offense, then the thug/bully will seek out easier prey instead. Where the old concept of judging a man by how mighty his ene-
mies may be defines his strength and courage, observing that one's victims exhibit weakness and cannot reasonably compete on
the abuser's level is the mark of a thug's or bully's cowardice.

   Thugs and bullies rarely respond to shaming, because that requires them first to value the opinions of others, and they uni-
formly lack such concern. Thugs and bullies tend to "gang up" and lend one another support and backup, but only to increase
their own likelihood of domination. Thugs and bullies exhibit little or no loyalty to anyone, outside of their immediate benefit,
and can be relied upon to readily turn in their own ("snitch") as a means to reduce their own punishment when being held ac-
countable for their abuses.

     Both thugs and bullies are adamantly enraged by the "offense" of one of their victims reporting their abuse. Interestingly,
among those in authority (or empowered to exert authority), the best way to identify those with a tendency to bully is to observe
their response to the idea that their actions might be reported to higher authority for review; people with integrity are usually
willing to face review and confident that their actions will be upheld as valid, while bullies know that their superiors are likely
to recognize the inappropriate nature of their actions.

   As one might expect, prison is a great place to observe thugs and bullies in their never-ending hunt for their next victim.
