                                                    The Plain Dealer
                                                      March 1 5, 1 9 7 4

The Soviet Union suffered a setback in the most ambitious attempt
yet to explore the planet Mars when the descent capsule from an
unmanned spacecraft, Mars 6, lost radio contact before landing
on the planet on Tuesday.

The descent capsule of a second spacecraft, Mars 7, shot past
Mars by about 8 00 miles on March 9 because of what was described
as a malfunction in "one of the onboard systems . "

The pair of reverses, which were disclosed yesterday by the of­
ficial Soviet press agency Tass, occurred at the end of a seven­
month trip by four Soviet spacecraft that covered a distance of
about 3 00 mi11ion miles .

Tass reported that during its descent by parachute, the Mars 6
module was able to transmit some informatio�l about the planet's
atmosphere before falling silent .

Both spacecraft have since swung into orbit around Mars, as did
the third-spacecraft last month . The fourth Soviet craft failed
to go into orbit at about the same time .

The Soviets had previously landed two capsules on the planet .
On December 2, 19 7 1 , the Mars 3 craft parachuted a capsule into
the southern hemisphere of Mars .    The capsule transmitted signals
for only 20 seconds before going dead .    A month earlier, a
capsule was hard-landed on the planet by the Mars 2 craft .

At least one of the current capsules was believed intended to
transmit back television images .

Despite the reverses, Tass yesterday contended that the spaceships
had gathered "new important data" about the planet, but offered
few detai Is.

French and Soviet instruments aboard the Mars 6 module were re­
ported as automatically analyzing solar wind and cosmic and solar
radiation.


Analysis of the planet's radiation had provided "new information"
about the surface, temperature, structure and soi1 composition,
heat conductivity and atmosphere of Mars, Tass said.  I t reported
that the planet's atmosphere contained "several times more water
vapor" than had been thought.

Although the United States has not yet attempted a soft landing
on Mars, its Mariner 9 spacecraft sent back detailed pictures-of
the planet's surface during a successful mapping mission in 1 9 7 2 .

The United States has planned to dispatch a Viking spacecraft in
August or September 1 9 7 5 to look for signs of life on Mars.
