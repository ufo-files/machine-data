Midland Reporter-Telegram - Midland, Texas…
22nd January 2008…

If Aliens Ever Visit Midland, Let's Hope It's A Slow News Day…

   When I think of people reporting UFOs, images of individuals who drink beer all day in remote areas come to mind.
   But that certainly wasn't the case recently in the Texas town of Stephenville, population 15,000, as those who described the unexplained were by all accounts credible.
   When news of possible UFO sightings first posted on The Associated Press wire, I grinned and directed my Web browser to the Stephenville Empire-Tribune, figuring a hometown paper like that would surely have the scoop. It certainly did with careful, honest and dare I say brave reporting by Angelia Joiner.
   That's when I started wondering about conversations going on in that newsroom.
   A few days after the story published, news trucks started rolling into town, among them CNN's Larry King Live Show. International interest sparked as other mass media outlets  converged upon the town and I faithfully visited the Empire-Tribune Web site daily.
   Then the paper's editor, Sara Vanden Berge, wrote a priceless column with an online headline that read:
"UFOs, camera crews and a skeptical editor."
   I laughed out loud and immediately liked this woman.
   Berge went on to describe her newsroom predicament, in a column for their Jan. 18 edition.
   "Those of us who work for the newspaper are still in shock from all the hoopla the story has created - especially me - who had a hard time even agreeing to do it in the first place."
   She would later tell me it was a "slow news day" in Stephenville.
   In her column, Berge explained she wasn't excited about running the initial story of the mysterious sightings and was even "less excited" when she opened the paper the next day and saw the word "UFO" in a headline after having left early the day before.
   Upon seeing that headline, the editor in fact "came unglued," she told me while recalling the whole ordeal over the telephone.
   She immediately worried her paper would be laughed at and look like a tabloid, as she confessed to readers of her column.
   But people didn't laugh, she said, and instead bought every single paper printed that day.
  And no one is laughing at those who saw whatever it was they saw.
   Most of the good people of Stephenville are having fun with the story, even capitalizing on it like some high school science club students who are selling T-shirts in their dairy town, with  an image of a cow being beamed up to a flying saucer. "They're here for the milk," the design says.
   Wearing foil hats, residents at a nursing home were all smiles when recently photographed.
   As the paper tried to go about its business of reporting daily civic events and such, news organizations from all over the country kept calling, as did people from far away wanting to  report their own UFO sightings. It was difficult for anyone to get anything done, Berge explained.
   But as of late Monday, things were perhaps slowly getting back to normal in Stephenville.
   If you ask Berge, it comes not a moment too soon.
   "We've got a paper to put out," she said as we both chuckled.
   Thanks Empire-Tribune for reporting the story so many of us loved, even those of us who share your editor's skepticism.
   And if aliens ever decide to visit Midland, let's hope they do so on a slow news day.
