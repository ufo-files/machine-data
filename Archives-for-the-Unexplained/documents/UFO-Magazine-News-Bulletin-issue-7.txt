   It was the culmination of many months of planning and work by many
individuals, but Mysticon, the first mixed media convention ever held
in the Cleveland area, proved to us that such events don't have to be
stuffy,strictly "scientific" in order to inform the public, but can
really be funl Not only that, but contact with the public during the
various lectures and presentations allows the "experts" to get ideas
that in many instances can help "put a new slant" on things related
to their studies.
   So it was this interchange of ideas and opinions that made the first
Mysticon, held at Parma Memorial Hall this last May 30th and 31st, a
successful first venture. The public was treated to a series of lectures
and slide presentations Friday and Saturday evenings that ranged from
such topics as the "Bermuda Triangle'' to an illustrated history of pulp
adventure magazines of the past. Not to be left out, the science fiction
movie fans were treated to the showing of two classics Saturday afternoona
"The Day the Earth Stood Still", and · "Things to Come." These two fine
movies were preceded by a nostalgic look at the past by local radio per-
sonality Archie Rothman.
   Experts on many topics were on hand to lecture and answer questions
from the public, and eo generally get together with old friends and
discuss tHeir latest theories and findings. A list of those attending
Mysticon sounds like a "who's who" of UP'Os, the occult and science
fiction t Robert s. Easley, James w. Moseley, Allen H. Greenfield, Eugene
R. Steinberg, Curt Sutherly, Edward M. Biebel, Allan Manak, Tom Nealings,
Robert Lawrence, Fred G. Cook and many others.
   Plans are now in the formative stages for a 1976 event, but you can
bet that we will keep you inform•d so that we can add your name to the
above list for next year!

       The speculation of life on Mars, which started when man first looked
    into the sky and is now more intense than ever before hopefully will
    be solved on our nation's 200th anniversary when the $950 million dollar
    Viking Mars space probe lands on the surface of the Red Planet in the
    first direct search for extraterrestrial life. Whether the probe finds
    evidence of extinct, expected, or existing life, the implications of it
    will be staggering.
       Although scientists are split on the question of life on Mars, there
    is pretty much agreement as to life elsewhere in the universe. Astron-
    omer Carl Sagan, the foremost expert on exobiology, feels that there
    could be one million civilizations in our galaxy. One of these civili-
    zations could very well be on the planet Mars.

    the word 'evidence' refers to observations which suggest intelligence
