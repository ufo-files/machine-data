MISCELLANEOUS RAVINGS:
       Jerome Clark, now editor of FATE Magazine and the CUFOS "Interna­
tional Reporter", writes an editorial in the June "MUFON UFO Journal",
taking exception to remarks made by Jenny Randles in an earlier issue of
said Journal.
       The arguments are long-winded, but essentially Randles believes that
"the vast majority (of ufologists) ....really have an inner longing to see
their belief vindicated that UFOs are advanced alien visitors who have
come here, will change the world, and maybe help us get out of the mess
we are in."
       Clark takes exception, and responds, in effect, that saucer believ­
ers fall rather neatly into two distinct categories: (1) ufologists, whom
he calls "secular scientists"; and (2) followers of the contactee move­
ment. Clark believes that religion, and wishful thinking about benevolent
aliens, are almost totally absent among the ufologists, who presumably com­
prise the membership of research organizations such as CUFOS and MUFON.
       We're basically on Jenny's side in this argument. "Secular scien­
tists", indeed!   There is more wishful thinking among ufologists than any
ufologist would ever want to admit!   We know that at least one MUFON state
director believes that UFOs are "God's missionaries and their associates
from other worlds". The line between "secular scientists" and crackpots
is not a line at all - but a continuous variation.   We are all at least a

when you used to believe in fairies, Jerry?)

      It's really too bad that John Lear is so mad at us, . when we have
done more than almost anyone else to publicize his views. Before he got
mad, he sent us at least two tracts. One is 45 pages of typed text, writ­
ten by someone with the pseudonym "C. H. Krill", and called "A situation
report on our acquisition of advanced technology and interaction     with
alien cultures; January 1988". The other is a 9-pager called "The Dulce
Papers", consisting of text and some groovy drawings of alien     fetuses
(feti??) in various stages of development in a secret underground lab
somewhere beneath Dulce, New Mexico, or some such.
      We haven't actually read all of this stuff, and what we have read
we don't believe. But, as a Public Service, we will lend either or both
of these remarkable documents t.o anyone who asks, as one of our Fans was
kind enough to make up several extra copies for us to pass around. We have
more copies of the 9-pager than the 45-pager; so, first come, first served.

      Through another Fan, we recently received a tract solliciting funds
for the defense of Charith Pendragon, a Hawaiian parapsychologist who was
apparently fired from various teaching jobs because of pressure from the
local CSICOP branch. Pendragon is, as we have known for some time, suing
CSICOP for over a million dollars.
      The tract states: "Self-appointed 'skeptics' seek to control what we
see, hear, and learn. 'Skeptics' have acted to limit the scope of intel­
lectual research, discussion, and investigation of parapsychology.    If
'skeptics' can censor the teaching of parapsychology in Hawaii, they will
soon be coming to other states to condemn other sub jects which are not in
their narrow and limited view of reality."
      Believe it or not, we sympathize with Pendragon. However, we did
not send any money to his Cause ....

      Eugene Steinberg, who published a UFO magazine in the early 1970's
called "Caveat Emptor", has decided after all these years to get back in­
to the fray. He will shortly bring out a new quarterly zine with the same
name, priced at $3 per issue or $10 per year. The first issue, due out in
September or October, features (among other lesser things) a very frank
interview with your humble "Smear" editor. Steinberg can be reached at:
8 Gate House Lane, Edison, N.J. 08820.
      We might add that Steinberg has been involved in ufology for over
25 years. He has long been associated with the National UFO Conference,
and he worked with us at the old SAUCER NEWS office in New York City back
in the late 1960's. He is a very bright guy, which is another way of say­
ing that his ufological views are very close to our own ....
