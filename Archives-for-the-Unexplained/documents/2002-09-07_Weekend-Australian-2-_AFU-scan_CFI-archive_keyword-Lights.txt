    Blast from
    the vast
    unknown
    Bruce Montgomery
    A FIREBALL that streaked
    across South Australia's
    Fleurieu      Peninsula     on
    Thursday evening, followed
    by a thunderclap as it
    smashed through the sound
    barrier at 20km a second, was
    almost certainly a small
    meteorite speeding to Earth.
      Cheryl Johnson was show-
    ing visitors the koalas and
    peacocks at her Urimbirra
    Wildlife Park at about 6pm
    when she saw the blazing
    object shoot through the sky,
    arcing westwards towards
    the towns of Yankalilla and
    Normanville.
      "It sped across the sky like
    a falling star," she said.
      Ms Johnson only had the
    object in view for two or
    three seconds. The visitors
    missed it.
      Senior Constable Robert
    Fisher of the Goolwa police
    heard the blast at home.
      "It shook the window and
    vertical blinds," he said.
      "It was a loud report and
    then nothing. I was waiting
    for someone to report a gas
    outlet going up."
      Meteor astronomer Gra-
    ham Elford, a research fel-
    low at the University of Adel-
    aide, said that from all the
    descriptions he was certain it
    was a meteorite.
      "My guess is it would have
    been tens of kilograms, per-
    haps the size of a football,"
    Dr Elford said.
      "You don't need a large
    mass for this effect, they
    come in with enormous
    speed."
      Dr Elford said if it had
    landed intact, the crater
    would be 2m to 3m wide.
