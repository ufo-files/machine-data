  National UFO conference:

  Two members of the Project, Debbie Payne and Dominic McNamara, attended the 2004 national UFO conference
  held in Sydney in June, to present a paper on the progress of the Project’s work.

  Deb, aided by Dom, gave a one hour PowerPoint presentation on Disclosure Australia. The talk was designed to cover
  the project’s methodology; unveil some previously unknown data and stimulate discussion on the topic. A copy of the
  text of the talk, together with the PowerPoint presentation, can be found on the Project’s web site at
  http://disclosure.freewebpage.org

  The talk also called for Australian UFO research groups to consider initiating an Australian wide collection of UFO
  reports on the Disclosure Australia website. Currently this site holds a collection of South Australian reports between
  1932-2004, together with extensive catalogues of observations from air crew; physical trace cases and photographic
  events. It would be great to see a listing from all states on this site.

  Feedback from audience members about this presentation was extremely positive. Comments to Deb and Dom centred
  around the amount of work which had been undertaken by members of AURA; the audience member’s lack of
  knowledge of the large amount of material which the Government had compiled over the years, and the openness and
  transparency of the Project’s methodology in sharing the material it had uncovered. Naturally, this response is most
  rewarding to the members of AURA who have spent much time and money over the last year undertaking the work.

  One of the conditions placed upon our file inspection, given that the files were transferred to Adelaide for us, was that
  our researcher was not allowed to take down any personal details of witnesses from the papers.

  A look through the files revealed a number of interesting items including:

  · Original police interview notes with the witness of the 13 June 1985 Lake Clifton WA car pace case
  A report from two Army SAS members who sighted a UFO while on a mission, and during which observation their
  radio equipment would not work
  A file titled: “Operation Close Encounter” when RAAF aircraft were placed on alert to investigate the source of radar
  returns
  A file containing lots of pictures and a report from the Hessdalen area of Norway where UFO investigators made some
  very interesting observations using diverse monitoring equipment including radar
  The set of UFO policy files 554/1/30 parts 1-3
  Several more parts of series 580/1/1 files which contain raw reports.

  Following this inspection visit, when we were awaiting advice that photocopies had been made for us, we received a
  telephone call form RAAF Edinburgh to say that the volume of work was too much for their insufficiently staffed
  office and that we were not going to receive any photocopies at all! Advice was given to us that now access was to be
  granted solely allowing us to take written notes (no personal details) about the contents of the files. Arrangements have
  been concluded for two members of the Project Secretariat to make a series of visits over coming weeks to examine
  the files in detail.

  apart from a “probable file” on a still living UFO researcher. This response from ASIO is now several months old, so
  we have submitted a further letter to the NAA asking that the ASIO search for the file be expedited.

  Mysterious light in 1915:

  Recently we uncovered a file (series MP16/1/0 control symbol 1915/3/1288) which discusses an investigation of
  “mysterious lights at night” by the Intelligence section of the General Staff in 1915!

  A complaint was made by a Mrs E Osborne of Wallacedale, Victoria, to authorities. The complaint advised that Mrs
  Osborne and her family had reported “mysterious lights at night.” Mrs Osborne and other local residents were
  interviewed and told of moving night time lights “high up in the air in the direction of Mount Napier.”

  As documented on the file, investigation by the authorities revealed that the cause of these lights was “…trappers
  visiting their rabbit traps” and that “…being very hilly lights seen from Wallacedale at night time would appear to be
  higher than they really were, especially at a distance.”

  Project strategic plan revisited:

  Almost a year has gone by since we commenced this Project, so it is timely to revisit our Mission Statement, aims and
  stages.

  The Project’s Mission Statement is:

  To ascertain the extent of official Australian Government knowledge of the UFO phenomenon; then to document both
  this and civilian knowledge on the subject.

  The Project’s aims are:
  To collect, collate, analyse and disseminate information about what is currently known about Australian Government
  knowledge of the UFO phenomenon.
  To document and make available the “best” Australian UFO cases held by both civilian and Government sources.
  To ascertain what knowledge of the UFO phenomenon may not currently be in the public domain, and seek to
  document and disseminate this information to interested parties.

  Stages:

  1. To utilise the Federal Archive Act to locate and examine relevant file holdings more than thirty years old.

  2. To utilise the Federal Freedom of Information Act to locate records available in this way.

  3. To create an archive of “best” Australian UFO cases drawn from both Government and civilian holdings.

  4. With this knowledge to then lobby the Australian Government to fully reveal                    the extent of its knowledge of the
  UFO phenomenon.

  Progress to date:

  1. We have located and examined almost 110 possible relevant files in the NAA; summarised many of these and made
  these summaries available in a number of ways; had the NAA examine and electronically open a number of files;
  prepared listings of raw UFO reports made to the RAAF and published our findings in this Newsletter, on our web
  site; through the “Australasian UFOlogist” magazine and via talk at the June 2004 national UFO conference..

  2. We have just commenced our work in submitting FOI requests to the RAAF. So far we have received 600 pages
  under FOI. We will be shortly submitting FOI requests to other Government agencies. However, this is a time
  consuming and costly process.

  3. We have commenced work in building our archive of “best” cases on our web site.

  Summary:

  The Disclosure Australia Project is the most comprehensive exploration ever undertaken of the interest that the
  Australian Federal Government has/had on the UFO phenomenon. As such, it is anticipated that it will take 3-5 years to
  complete.

  Modified Hynek classification system
  DO - Daylight disk or other shape
  NL - Nocturnal light- a point source of light
  CE - Close encounter (within 500m) at any time of day or night
  RV - Radar visual
  DL - Daylight light only – a point source of light
  NO - Nocturnal object where the UFO had a defined size rather than being a point source
  Photo - Photographs or video were taken
  Trace - Ground marking
