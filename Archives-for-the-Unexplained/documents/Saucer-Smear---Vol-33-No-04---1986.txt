A SCIENTIFIC PIONEER IN EXPLORING THE UNKNOWN

with the Comet. " His death on April 27th was on almost the exact                        day

    Bluebook. From the point of view of UFO believers, Hynek's job was to
    successfully debunk all well-publicized UFO cases, in order to keep the
    public calm. The high point came in March, 1966, when Hynek was called
    in to try to explain a series of close-up sightings near Dexter, Michigan.
    Under pressure from the press to come up with an explanation, Hynek men­
    tioned "marsh gas ", and then the Shit hit the fan, because the explanation
    simply did not fit. For months after that, Hynek was ridiculed as "Dr.
    Swamp Gas " in cartoons and editorials throughout the U.S., and public in­
    terest in UFOs soared to an all-time high. (It was Hynek's "marsh gas "
    that led to your humble "Smear " editor being called upon to lecture    to
    over one hundred colleges around the country, 1966-1975.)
          Eventually Hynek, as an intellectually honest man, came to realize
    that some of the UFO cases he was called upon to identify really were
    strange and not easily explanable in conventional terms. He lost his Air
    Force contract and eventually founded CUFOS, where he tried to walk     the
    tightrope between scientific objectivity and advocacy, i.e., Belief. Some
    fault him for having been less than perfect in performing this almost im­
    possible task. Your editor knew Hynek as an open-minded mild-mannered
    gentleman who did the best he could. Though he tended to believe in UFOs,
    he was not wedded to any particular theory - which was entirely to     his
    credit, though it greatly annoyed some interplanetary Believers. Actually,
    Hynek was quite sympathetic to the "4-D " (or "Jt-D ", as we call it) The­
    ory, & stated so publicly many times, as well as privately to your Editor
    and others.
          Our only quarrel with Hynek was a mild one, just a few months ago.
    As noted in "Smear " at the time, Hynek's last signed editorial in the "In­
    ternational UFO Reporter " came down hard on "Smear " for having printed a
    letter he didn't agree with, written by Jerry Clark, his own editor. We
    never took this up with Hynek directly, because we assumed that he was al­
    ready a victim of the terrible disease from which he soon died. His mem­
    ory and no doubt his sense of proportion were already failing, and we are
    told that by the time of his death he could not even physically recognize
    his family and friends. Death was a merciful release.
          Hynek's last years brought him, sadly, a sense of failure, for he
    fully realized that he had not solved the Mystery, nor had CUFOS attract­
    ed significant funding, nor, in general, had other prestigious men of sci­
    ence followed his lead, to become openly involved in UFO investigation.
    Someone with a more forceful personality might have done better, but only
    by a terrible lowering of standards.
          Who will take Hynek's place as "Numero Uno " in the American UFO move­
    ment? We know of no one on the scene who can match his qualifications.
    Phil Klass, who has always accused Hynek of having "switched sides ", tells
    us facetiously that he too would be willing to switch if the price     was
    right. Few if any ufologists would even consider accepting Klass in such
    a role, though in reality he would probably do a more objective job than
    most people. But no, we are not advocating Klass as leader of ufology!
    Let us just wait and see what happens in the next few months.
