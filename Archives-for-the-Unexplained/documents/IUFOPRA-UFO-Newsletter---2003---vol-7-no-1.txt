The Pacific Northwest has long been the location of myth and mystery. Stories of
Sasquatch, the Yakima Earthlight, and Batsquatch have lingered through its
history. It was here in 1947 that the Kenneth Arnold sighting of nine objects
over Mt.Rainier opened the door to our current enthusiasm and study of
ufology. Through public lectures and websites many of us are already familiar
with the following case study and media event.

                          The Scene of the Crime

The case of Jonathan Reed begins on October 15, 1996 when Reed, hiking on a
trail "some 15 miles northeast of Snoqualmie Pass, 60 miles east of Seattle" had
an alien encounter, which would change his life. His dog Suzi, runs up over a



                                                                                   4
knoll, grabs an alien by the arm and is instantly pulverized. Reed in anger takes
a branch striking the alien dead. Or so he thinks. He takes out his camera and
camcorder, which he conveniently carries in his backpack and photographs an
object- spacecraft/doorway to another dimension, which is hovering at the
scene. In his fear and dismay, he picks up the alien who is about 4 feet long and
45 lbs. And wraps it in a solar blanket he is carrying. Bundling the whole
package he frantically runs to his Jeep which is parked at the trailhead parking
area and makes a beeline to a ranger station located in a suburb of Seattle. It is
closed so he takes the alien home, puts it in his freezer and goes to bed. He calls
his best friend Gary and the next day they go back to the scene but find
everything is back in place as if nothing had occurred.

Three days later - we have no idea if Reed has checked on the alien in the
interim, Reed hears a noise coming from the freezer and finds that " Freddie"
who he now has named is indeed alive and seems to enjoy his rather chilly new
home. Hence the media name of "Alien Popsicle". Freddie spends the next
couple of days briefing Jonathan on the mysteries of the universe and presents
him with a prize. The link artifact. A bracelet worn on your wrist that makes
you invisible. My mind flashed back to times when my home and work life was
making demands and how handy that would have been! Don't we all wish to just
"vanish" at certain moments in life?

Enter the "agency". Jonathan is out one day and he comes home to find his
house is ransacked, his belongings thrown about and "Freddie" has been
kidnapped. Jonathan fearing that his life is in danger takes a few vials of alien
tissue to his friend Dolly for safekeeping. Reed gives a couple of samples to Gary
as well. It is unclear when the tissue samples were actually taken and if Freddie
is a live participant for what we have seen on the video and photos circulating at
lectures and on the Internet, Freddie is photographed as a very dead alien. We
never see a "live" Freddie or even a dead Freddie at the scene of the crime on the
Rachel Lake Trail. Why didn't Reed take photos of the alien while he lay on the
forest? How convincing that would have been.

My first knowledge of this case was two years later in 1998 when Peter
Davenport of the National UFO Reporting Center called and asked if I heard of
this event. Reed had appeared on Art Bell's Coast-to-Coast unfolding to the
public his alien tale. Reed had stated that he had called Peter Davenport as well
as MUFON two days after the incident. Being an avid hiker myself, I consulted
my "Washington State Gazetteer" a detailed topographical book containing
information on the state. I was lost, it didn't make sense. This would have put
the location of Mt. Stewart, a high alpine mountain with a few public trails. It
was later determined that Reed had been hiking the Rachel Lake trail which is
located approximately 12.5 miles from the Snoqualmie Summit and put our
investigation in a different location altogether.

                        Rachel Lake Trail and the Weather

The Rachel Lake Trail begins at 2790 feet graduates to a high point of 5154 feet,
has a total length of 4.8 miles and an effective length of 8.1 miles. Effective length

approximately 3 hours. In his book Reed says" My mind captured by the

One critical fact to look at in investigating a story or even hiking a trail is to
check the weather conditions in that area especially knowing the Cascade
Mountains can receive snow year round. Historical weather records of October
15, 1996 in Snoqualmie Pass show the weather conditions as follows:

Mean Temperature: 34.6 degrees F, Max Temperature: 37.4 degrees F,
Dew point 33.7 degrees F, Precipitation 1.5, Wind speed 12.5 mph, Events:
Rain, Snow, and Fog.

week or season. What happened to the yellow and gold of fall?

The Bible is a book of UFO stories. Some may construe this statement as

nervous.

In 1947 "flying saucers" was a description coined by the press following Pilot
Kenneth Arnold's famous sighting of nine disked shaped objects over the
Cascade Mountains in Washington State on June 24th. Arnold was a
businessman from Boise, Idaho. While flying his private plane, Arnold witnessed
nine flat, shiny objects flying in formation near Mt Rainier, and he compared
their peculiar motion to a saucer skipping over water. A newsman inspired by
the description, coined the term "flying saucers", and this term became a
household word as waves of reports came in of strange objects seen in the skies.
(Ref. No 5).

Reports were being made by qualified observers, such as military and civilian
pilots, air traffic controllers, and others whose jobs depended on their ability to
identify objects in the sky. Needless to say the authorities were alarmed. The FBI
and the U.S. military investigated Arnold's sighting. An FBI agent's comments
on the reliability of the report are worth quoting: it is the personal opinion of the
interviewer that [Arnold] actually saw what he states         he saw in the attached
report. It is also the opinion of the interviewer that {Arnold} would have much
more to lose than gain and would be very strongly convinced that he actually
saw something before he would report such an incident and open himself up for
the ridicule that would accompany such a report. (Ref.No 6).

CIA SAID TO RUE ITS LONGTERM COVER-UP BY ROBERT SCOTT
MARTIN
SPACE WRITER (SPACE.COM) JUNE 2002.

Central Intelligence Agency- http://www.cia.gov
CIA's Role in the Study of UFOs, 1947-90
http: //www.odci.gov /csi/studies/97unc las/ufo. h tml


Not only has the U. S. Central Intelligence Agency admitted its role
in trying to "correct" public opinion about UFOs over the last half
century, it now believes the policy caused "major problems" in
dealing with the public.
In an internal report entitled "CIA's Role in the Study of UFOs,
1947-90", agency historian Gerald K. Haines portrayed the CIA as
consistently and deliberately working to suppress reports of
unidentified aerial phenomena since modern UFO sightings began
with the Kenneth Arnold case of 1947.




                                                                                   18
Still, even in a paper filled with covert attempts on the part of both
the CIA and the Air Force to "persuade the public that UFOs were
not extraordinary, " Haines himself continued the suppressive
policy, perhaps unconsciously, by writing that the CIA "paid only
limited and peripheral attention to the phenomena" since the early
1950s.
This tension in the report, written at the request of CIA Director
R.James Woolsely in 1997, is a telling reflection of the government
agency's troubled broader relationship with UFO sightings and
literature. Haines' history is studded with deceptions of the CIA not
only repressing UFO reports and reviewing recommendations that
agents monitor UFO organization for subversive activities, but also
trying to hide its own interest in the matter. Indeed, the struggle to
"carefully restrict" and "forbid" any public awareness of CIA
involvement in UFO investigations eclipses the actual investigations
as the major thrust of the agency's UFO efforts. Even though the
agency had accepted the Air Force's conclusion that there was only
"a remote possibility" that UFOs were interplanetary aircraft as
early as 1952, investigations of the "massive buildup of sightings"
went on, just in case.


CONCEALMENT OF CIA INTEREST.


However, after 1953, when negative findings from a civilian panel
motivated the CIA to " the entire issue of UFOs on the back burner"
entirely, Haines said the agency became almost exclusively
concerned with covering up its own involvement in the world of
unidentified flying objects. This aggressive policy of public non­
involvement was important to the CIA for many reasons. First, a
number of agency officials and study groups over the years urged
the CIA to "conceal its interest" because such attention would seem
to officially sanction to the existence of UFOs. Although the agency
itself, like the Air Force, believed the chance of flying saucers
posing a direct threat was minimal, the fear that even unfounded
public belief in the phenomenon, if encouraged by government
interest, could be enough to "touch off mass hysteria and panic".
Particularly in the 1950s, the Cold War heightened this somewhat
obsessive concern with hiding any evidence of the CIA's
involvement, said Haines.


Although the agency's UFO study group did not see any security
threat emerging directly out of flying saucers themselves, even they
actually existed, the CIA was deeply worried by the possibility that
Soviet agents could use UFOs as "a possible psychological warfare
tool" or cloak a more Earthly attack with fake UFO reports.
Tantalizingly, Haines also noted that at least one CIA Director,



                                                                       19
Waiter Bedell Smith, "wanted to know what use could be made of
the UFO phenomenon in connection with US psychological warfare
efforts. " The report does not mention whether the agency followed
up on this opportunity to manipulate UFO reports I a more
sophisticated manner for its own purposes.


As the 1950s wore on, the CIA became even less interested in UFOs
in themselves and more concerned with covering up its own early
involvement with the phenomenon. In 1955, only the possibility
that the Soviets would eventually develop a flying saucer of their
own kept investigations from ending completely. Meanwhile,
ironically, the CIA had built its own "unidentified flying object", the
U-2 surveillance aircraft, and sightings of these plans needed to be
kept out of the media. According to Haines, Air Force investigators
were "careful not to reveal the true cause" of U-2 sightings.
However, having no other means of explaining the encounters, it is
likely the field agents were forced either to lie or retreat into
suspicious silence. The return of the repressed Haines argues that
his suspicious was not a good strategy for the agency, but the
established need for secrecy left the CIA with little choice while
fervor over the government's role in "covering up" UFO information
grew.


Even though the agency itself " had declining interest in UFO cases"
by the late 1950s, it was still spending considerable resources
looking out for "the more sensational UFO reports and flaps" in
order to suppress them.


Ultimately, this policy backfired by highlighting the CIA's role in
investigation - or the ominous cover-up thereof - only to "add fuel
to the growing mystery surrounding UFOs".      UFO researchers
blamed the agency for starting the UFO flap of the 1950s for
psychological warfare purposes, and the idea proved so persuasive
that even CIA Director Stansfield Turner asked his staff whether the
agency was "in UFOs" after reading a 1979 New York Times article.


At the end, Haines concluded, the tactics of silence and repression
were a failure. "The UFO issue will not go away soon, no matter what
the agency does or says. The belief that we are not alone in the
universe is too emotionally appealing and the distrust of our
government is too pervasive to make the issue amenable to
traditional scientific studies of rational explanation and evidence".
Indeed, much of that "distrust" was the CIA's own doing, and the
benefits appear to have been limited. Despite the agency's best
efforts to keep UFO reports out of the media, according to Haines,
