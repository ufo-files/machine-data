   To have missed the recent upsurge of UFO sightings reported
in recent months by the news media, one would have to have been
either a blind, deaf mute; a cave dweller; comatose; or dead.
Even the good old 'Beeb' gave the UFOs top of the bill on their
6. 00pm and lO. OOpm news programmes on Old Year's Night; sending
us into the New Year with a festivity of flashing irridescence
as they scooped ITV for the film report on the Auckland sightings.
Certainly Page Three readers of the "SUN" on the 2nd January could not
have helped but notice the UFO report and 'still' on the page next to
the shapely 'earthly' body.

  Are we entering the year of the UFO? (When have we heard that before!)
If they are anything to go by, then certainly. the first few hours of
1979 seem to indicate so. With UFO activity rife in both hemispheres
it looks as if we could be in for another 'flap'.

  In Northern Ireland we had our fair share of sightings and ignoring
the recent Queen's University Rag Week hoax calls, these have come
from virtually all parts of the Province.   From as far away as Beleek
to Limavady, reports have been continually streaming into the Centre's
Hotline. On early Sunday morning the 1st January an RUC patrol was
asked to look into a reported sighting in Glengormley (Co. Antrim)
and during the afternoon several people reported seeing a cigar-shaped
UFO traverse the sky from east to west.   On the same date a surgeon
in England reported seeing a similar object travelling in the same
direction - a possible correlation.

  Needless to say official comment ou this recent burst of UFO activ­
ity varies from the planet Venus to space flotsam re-entering the
earth's atmosphere.  Undoubtedly some of these reported sightings can
be explained away in rational terms and indeed the New Year's sightings
were first classified as a 'fireball' and later explained as the
remnants of a Russian rocket burning up, however quite a large number
of the reports filtering into our organisation have yet to be explained.
On the subject of explanations, I would love to know why so many,
supposedly learned people come out with the Venus and 'flotsam' ex­
planations without appearing to have given the subject some thought.
Having had an interest in astronomy for many years, and having spent a
lot of nights and mornings observing the heavens (the Moon, Venus,
Mars, Jupiter, Saturn; you name it, I've seen it!), I have yet to see
them loop the loop, spiral towards the ground, move horizontally faster
that a jet aircraft, or speed away at impossible angles to fizzle out
and disappear; especially in broad daylight! And as for space flotsam;
hasn't the Government told us that anything short of Skylab ( which
would break up anyway) would burn out long before it reached the lower
layer of our atmosphere.  Nonetheless, there is a lot of debris (man­
made or otherwise!) floating around above our heads and by the laws
of probability some of it is bound to make an appearance. However,
bits and pieces of 'space junk' will burn brightly and then fizzle out
like a Halloween rocket and its path is naturally downward.   It will
not go out and light up again in differing colours and it will certainly
not behave like Barnes Wallis' famous "Dam Buster" bomb.

will revolutionise flight.  It's called CCV (Control Configuration

including flying sideways.  I thought only UFOs could do that!

  I have written these two seemingly irrelevant pieces into this ed­
itorial to amplify two very simple arguments.  In the field of
technology and extra-terrestrial observation, the sci�ntist's yard­
stick or frame of reference is constantly changing.  Changes in
circumstances and development take place at such a rate that no
sooner is one concept accepted, another quite radical comes along to
render it "old fashioned". H G Wells, one of the forerunners of
modern science fiction, thought of satellite-type weapons which were
totally inconceivable in his day. Yet in this age the US and USSR
confront each other with proposals of satellite vehicles which could
devastate entire cities on our planet.

  Today we do not know what UFOs are; those that do not fall conven­
iently into our present-day frame of reference - but what of tomorrow?

  A historical approach to the UFO phenomenon carries with it advantages
and disadvantages.  If we study a period going back six decades or so,
we can be reasonably confident that no startling new revelation about
our subject will come to light; as some general, politician or scient­
ist publishes his memoirs; alternatively as some new document is de­
classified.  In other words we can be reasonably sure that the source
material for the time is complete. The other side of the coin is of
course, that we have to rely almost totally on these records and can do
no useful investigation work-one cannot interview dead witnesses.

  A particularly interesting body of UFO related data dates from the
last decade of the nineteenth century and the first fourteen years of
this century.  These reports, today daubed 'Phantom Airships' diffe r
in one importnat respect from the modern UFO phenomenon: they were
interpreted at the time, by those who took them seriously, as airships
(powered lighter than air craft) constructed by either a 'foreign power'
or a 'secret inventor'. Like the modern UFO phenomenon, the 'airship'
reports seem to occur in geographically localised waves. The waves
isolated so far (it seems likely that present research has only scratch­
ed the sufrace) a re: 1896 in the US (5), 1909 and 1913 in Britain (1,
3,4) and 1908 in Denmark (2). The reports are also simila r in profile

strange lights in the sky and a few stories of encounters with craft
and their occupants close to or on the ground. The'close encounters'
invariably describe human occupants and 'Heath Robinson' mechanical
constructions with wings, propellers, sails, etc. For those readers
