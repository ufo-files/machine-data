Diverse are the images of the state of New Mexico: the physicist in a Los
Alamos laboratory cooking up a superconductive ceramic in the wee hours
of the morning; the Navajo sheepherder propelling his flock across ahigh-

New Mexico has often been described by the many great artists and photo-
graphers who have been drawn there as a place where the light is "diff-
erent"; where vision seems more acute and colors seem more vibrant some-
how (away from the smog of Albuquerque, anyway). The proliferation of
spiritual seekers seems to indicate that some clearer inward vision is
more accessible there, as well.
aeyond the mesmerizing blue skies and surrealistic sunsets, NewMexico is
 "different" in more easily acknowledged and recognizable ways. It features
 an intriguing cultural mix: Hispanic, Anglo and Indian (3 major groups:

from their tribes and living as Hispanics) - plus a more recent influx
of spiritually-oriented groups: Sikhs, Moslems, Buddhists and lesser-
known or more obscure factions. The population of New Mexico includes a
greater percentage of minorities than any other state.
Economically, the state presents unique contrasts, with high technology
