                      Stacked neatly in a locked drawer in a special­
                   ly designed laboratory at Duke- University in
                   Durham, North Carolina, are sets of black and white
                   cards the size of ordinary playing decks. Each set
                   contains 25 cards; five marked with stars, five with
                   squares, five with circles, five with wavy lines, and
                   five with crosses. The cards were designed to test an
                   individual's mental telepathy-the mind-to-mind
                   transmission of images, thoughts and ideas.
                      Originated in the 1930s by Dr. J.B. Rhine who
                   directed duke's parapsychology ce�ter, the test has been given to many
                   people over the years-some showing remarkable results. For instance, not
                   long ago, the card test was given to an unnamed 26-year-old woman in New
                   York City. She was tested by Professor Bernard F. Riss of Hunter College.
                   The woman's score was fantastic-she correctly guessed 1,349 out of 1,850
                   cards (73 percent). The remarkable results were recorded in the Guinness
                   Book of World Records. The average person would have had about 73 right (2
                   percent).
                      But as any parapsychologist knows, the experiments taking place outside
                   the laboratory-spontaneous, and unrehearsed, are the one which grab the
                   headlines.
                      Fantastic results have been achieved by such well known psychics as
                   Fredrick Davies who predicted that Israeli leader Golda Meir would resign in
                   1975; Maris De Long. who accurately foresaw Southern California being hit
                   by an earthquake of considerable magnitude during the first three months of
                   1971, and Joseph DeLouise, whose psychic clues led to the identity of the
                   Sharon Tate murders in 1967.
                      Two recent psychics however are not limiting their abilities to predicting
                   future events. They are utilizing their precious talents to aid law enforcement
                   agencies hunt for criminals and in finding missing persons.
                      The two are notable psychics,Dorothy Allison of Nutely, New Jersey, and
                   Kathleen Rhea of Cupertino, California. Both have used their" ·powers
                   recently to help police and relatives of missing persons. Dorothy Allison
                   found the killer of a New York cab driver who had been murdered and
                   dumped in the East River. Kathleen Rea aided Churchill County authorities
                   in finding the wreckage of a single engine ·plane containing the bodies of a
                   father and son.
                      Indeed, police are learning (and it's about time) to rely more and more on
                   the work being performed by psychics-many of whom we hope to interview
