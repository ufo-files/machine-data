                                       ARASTRA BARN

         (SOUTH OF PORTOLA VALLEY, NEAR PALO ALTO BORDER: OFF ALPINE

                                 AND ARASTRADERO ROADS)


           North on Laurel (towards San Francisco) to Ravenswood--left onto Ravenswood.
   Get in right lane and cross El Camino. Continue on this street (which is now Menlo Avenue)
   until it deadends at University--tum right onto University, and then make a left onto Santa
   Cruz Avenue. Continue on Santa Cruz---Santa Cruz will veer to the left at a stop sign after
   a mile or so, and, at a signal light shortly thereafter, Santa Cruz again veers toward the left.
   The next signal light/intersection is Sand Hill Road--go through this intersection, but get in
   the right lane, so that you can follow the signs to Portola Valley. Stay in the right lane, and
   it becomes Alpine Road, going towards Portola Valley (see map). Continue on Alpine past
   Ladera, then take the first available left tum--there is a left-tum lane. If you go past the
   Alpine Inn (formerly Rossetti's, I believe), you've gone too far. Take the 4th right tum,
   which a narrow road marked with a yellow "15 mph" sign. The first right tum is to the
   Alpine Inn, the second is a narrow road, the third is a driveway leading to a private house---
   take the 4th right tuml Conintue on this road---it will tum into an unpaved, gravel road,
   which has a sign on the left saying, "HORSE BARN STRAIGHT AHEAD"---this is your
   target, so continue until the road ends in a parking area in front of a horse bam, which will
   be slightly to your left as you drive up. Park away from the bam, on the south side of the
   parking area.

           The target is the exterior of the wooden bam, plus the small fenced enclosures for
   horses on the east side (attached to the barn) and the area with a number of trees on the
   west side.

           Walk all around the bam, and the area with the horse enclosures and the area with the
   trees. Note the design of the bam, its condition, and what it is made of. What are the
    dominant patterns? Then stand off to the side of the main entrance, near the office (with
   the tubs of plants). Make friends with any resident felines (unless you are allergic); visit with
    a nearby horse (if so inclined)--enjoy the open air and the views!
