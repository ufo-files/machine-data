I hear about such things, the more I think that possibly there is. Take this Saturday just gone, the 1Oth

close encounter. What follows is the log of what we saw, but first.... When we arrived, we went armed
with binoculars checking the local fields for Crop Circle formations. We walked up the road, and
scouring the distant fields, Bill said 'look over there'.. in a distant field we saw what looked like a
marking in the centre of a 'virgin' field of green. We walked along a public right of way for some 2
miles (It felt like 4 ! ! !) and we could see what looked like 2 circles with a central path (dumbbell) and
another path coming off of one of the circles. Suddenly we lost sight of it behind trees. So, Bill and
Andy fought through barbed wire (I got stuck and did not follow ! ! ! ) , brambles and trees, but were
unable to locate the alleged formation (The time now was around 21.55 hrs and the light was fading
fast... ). So we returned to the Cheesefoot Head car park rather disappointed. I reported what we saw to
Paul Vigay and I received an e.mail from him saying that Lucy Pringle and Colin Andrews could not
locate any formations in the Cheesefoot Head area. (Were they looking in the right direction I ask
myself??).

  My name is Matthew Williams and I am referred to in Paul Vigays (Enigma magazine) recent report
on the Country File program dealing with Crop Circles.
  I just want to state that whilst Pauls article is the most accurate on this particular program I have seen
there are subtle problems which I need to explain which wilJ help the reader understand why parts of the
program appeared the way they did.
  Firstly before I go on to the details of the program let me say that over the years I have been inv<;)lved
with TV programs and documentaries on both sides of the camera, I know how the TV game works and
the ways that the program makers often make assumptions and cut corners in order to give the "feel"
they want a piece to work. I often found program makers encouraging people to say things "their way''
and also to stage situations which are obviously unnatural to the witness. This ends up making a slick
piece of TV but does not represent the true facts of a situation. In essence - coming from somebody
who knows - don't trust anything you read in newspapers or see on TV. It is often lies or gross
exaggeration ! ! !
  In the case of the Country File show, it appears that the makers had wanted a particular slant on what
crop circles were about and had done a "pre-treatment" which gave an outline of who they would like
to see in the show and the types of things they expected people to say.
  Having picked the people and contacted them, filming commenced and suitable comments and
statements were picked and earmarked for the edit. These comments may not be a true reflection of the
whole conversations which really took place because after all the segment was only about 20 minutes or
so. This defines the word "Edit". Participants rarely have any choice over how they are edited into a
program and often sign "release forms" to allow their interviews to be used in programs. These release
forms are a moral disclaimer which basically means that the program makers are allowed to make a
person say or appear to do anything they want in their editing and re·jigging   . .   and that you have no right
to redress. In fact, these release forms hold no real legal basis because someone can still complain that
the material was defamatory or lied in some way and seek redress through courts or TV ombudsman,
but this rarely ever happens.
