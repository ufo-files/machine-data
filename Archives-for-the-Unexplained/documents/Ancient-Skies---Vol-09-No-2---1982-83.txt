LAKE MIRAMAR
   The casual visitor to Lake Miramar in southern
Chiapas, Mexico would never know that 400 years ago
a hundred thousand Lacandon Indians flourished in
the area. There were over 100 stone dwellings on
the principal island in the lake and hundreds more
along the shores. Today only one family of latter-
day Lacandons lives on the shore and a few crumb-
ling stone walls on the island are all that remain
of the once - great civilization. There are two stone
"idols", small representations of human figures,
on the island, but these have been mutilated by
having their heads and hands cut off. The severed
parts are missing.
   At the edge of the lake at the site called "Cueva
del Mono" there is a huge stone boulder rising out
of the water,into which has been carved in deep re-
lief a human figure (see accompanying photo). The
style is Olmec,or at least Olmec - inspired . The posi-
tion of the carving is truly baffling because it is
situated high up on the face of the boulder, about
40 or 50 feet above ground level, and the face of
the stone slants inward towards the bottom.There is
no way the sculptor could have gotten to the site
 to carve the image, unless the water level then was
 considerably higher than it is today, or unless the
boulder was moved after the carving was made, by
 some sort of upheaval , such as a volcanic eruption.
   Aside from the few rock walls,the two "idols" and Rock -fa ce carving.Lake Miramar(Photo:Gene Phillips)
 the rock-face carving, no other vestiges of civili-
 zation remain at this now-idyllic spot, completely
 lost to the outer world . Time has forgotten Miramar
 and its 1,200 foot deep, crystal clear, warm waters
 that extend five miles across from East to West . No
 roads lead to the lake and the nearest landing strip
 is at San Quintin, a two hour march through the
 thick jungle growth to the West.
    But unlike some other past civilizations, we do
 know what happened to the Lacandons - they were an -
 nihilated by the conquering Spaniards. In the late
 16th Cent~ry , the conquistadors arrived at Miramar,
 saw the flourishing civilization and determined to
 conquer the people in search for gold. After two
 years of stubborn resistance, the indians were van-
 quished when the Spaniards brought a warship all the
 way from Spain, dismantled it, carried it piece by
 piece through the jungle , reassembled it at the
 shore of Lake Miramar, then sailed across the water
 to the island and killed all who failed to escape.
 After the Spaniards quit the place, the indians
 p.ever returned .
