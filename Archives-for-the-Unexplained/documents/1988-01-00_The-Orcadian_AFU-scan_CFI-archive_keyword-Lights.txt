   Three red flares sparked off a
full scale search in the Pentland
Firth on Tuesday.
   The air-sea search in a gale,
involved a lifeboat, helicopter,
plane, and two coast rescue
companies.
   However, the emergency oper­
ation drew a blank. No vessel in
distress was located and a coast­
guard spokesman said: "It's .a
complete mystery."
   The alarm was raised when a
coast watcher at Barswick in
South Ronaldsay reported two
red flares. He sighted a third as
he was speaking to Pentland
Coastguards at Kirkwall.
   The flares appeared to have
been put up somewhere between
Stroma and South Ronaldsay.
   The Longhope lifeboat was
launched and was joined by a
Nimrod aircraft from .Kinloss
and an RAF helicopter. On land,
Broughness coast rescue company
combed the South Ronaldsay
coastline.    Their     Duncansby
counterparts were also turned out
on the other side of the Firth but
a combined sweep of the entire
area proved fruitless.
    A coastguard spokesman said
afterwards that there had been
no reports of any vessel in
distress. He dismissed a hoax as
unlikely and said that even if
someone had got into difficulties
and then found they no longer
 required assistance, a      report
 would have been made to the
 coastguards.
    A scaled down search involving
 the coast rescue units at Brough­
 ness and Longhope was con­
 tinuing, as we went to press .
