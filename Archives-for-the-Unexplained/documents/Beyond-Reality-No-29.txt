   THE CIRCADIAN RHYTHM
     AND ITS HALF-WAVE
   For all living creatures on
Earth, and within each of their
individual cells, there are chem­
ical and physical processes that
rise and fall with a rhythm that
averages about 24. 8 hours (the
lunar day) and is called the
''circadian rhythm,'' from the
Latin words for ''about one day.''
   The ebb and flow of blood sugar
levels, liver cell enzymes, body
temperature, seed germination,
wake-sleep activities and respira­
tion, to name just a few, are all
circadian. If plants are placed in a
constant environment (black box),
their leaves and petals will rise
and fall, or open and close, in
circaQian rhythm, without any
known external cue. And if we
lived in caves, without clocks, our
living rhythms would creep way
from the regular 24-hour solar
cycle, and become circadian.
   Its origin is a mystery. Among
biologists there are deep differ­
ences of opinion as to whether it is

an internal clock, based on chem­
istry, or whether it's generated by
cosmic fields. But until recently,
researchers noticed only the re­
peating events at these cycles,
ignoring what happened in be­
tween, at the half-cycles. They

       During the last 1.5 years over two million porpoises­
   friendly, air-breathing mammals of the sea, have been killed

   catch tuna.
       The fishermen use the porpoises to locate yellowfin tuna­
   the kind called light meat in the cans--and then set their
   nets around both the tuna and the porpoise in order to
