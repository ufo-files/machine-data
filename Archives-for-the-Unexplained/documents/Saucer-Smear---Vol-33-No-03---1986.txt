      The NAUFOF has now officially disbanded. What is the NAUFOF,    you
might ask? Short for North American UFO Federation, the NAUFOF was born
at a MUFON convention in Toronto, Ontario, a few summers ago, and was in­
tended to be an "umbrella" organization for ufology in the United States
and Canada. Due to endless personality & doctrinal disputes among its var­
ious board members, the organization seemed doomed to failure almost from
the start; and thus it has now gone the way of many similar organizations
in the past. In ufology, everybody wants to be a "chief", and nobody wants
to be an "Indian": ....

      Well, it's official now:  Your editor has been Purged from the ranks
of the prestigious Society for Scientific Exploration. As noted in previ­
ous issues of "Smear", we managed to slip through the cracks, so to speak,
and for awhile it looked like our membership was assured. In fact, we re­
ceived a letter to that effect from one of the high officials of said or­
ganization. But alas, saner heads prevailed, and now our membership fee
has been returned with apologies. (See Charles Tolbert's missive in the
letters section of this issue.)

      Non-subscriber Douglas Curran of Canada has blossomed forth with a
beautiful soft-cover book called "In Advance of the Landing: Folk Concepts
of Outer Space� This, tome is a really high-class photographic and socio­
logical look at the UFO and New Age movements throughout North America, and
the author is to be congratulated for his middle-of-the-road approach to
the sub ject. There are 62 illustrations, many in full color, showing vari­
ous contactees, cults, earth-made representations of flying saucers,    and
such. These pictures are the result of the author's 90, 000 miles of tra­
vel during a period of several years!
      Your humble editor is mentioned on Page 123, in Curran's description
of the 1982 UFO convention in Reno, Nevada. This short paragraph deserves
to be quoted here in full:  "James Moseley, the lean and eccentric former
editor of Saucer News and now editor of Saucer Smear, elicited the only
negative reaction from the crowd during the conference. The self-styled
'bad boy of Ufology', Moseley took the position that after thirty years of
UFO sightings and testimony there still does not exist one single piece of
conclusive physical evidence in support of extraterrestrial visitation. Mos­
eley repeated his long-standing offer of $1, 000 to anyone who could produce
physical evidence of flying saucers. The offer was met by a disturbed
rumbling from the stands, coupled with a renewed interest in the stalls
and tables selling psychic-communication crystals, metaphysical tracts, and
health preparations...."
      The price of this soft-cover tome is $16.95, but it will be well worth
it to-those who see the UFO contactee movement as (whatever else it may also
be) one of the more fascinating sociological phenomena of the twentieth cen­
tury. The publisher is Abbeville Press, Inc., of 505 Park Ave., New Y ork,
N.y.   .   .   •   .
