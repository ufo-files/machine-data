June 12: Call for military reports 

By Wednesday, June 12, the situation at the Defence Staff called for more and better reports on the mysterious heavenly phenomena. The Eskilstuna case (June 8) and the Finnish meteor (June 9) had a decisive effect on the military bureacracy. It seems that reports of fast-moving objects (which the military system in Sweden as well as in Finland failed to identify as meteors) were of greater significance than reports of (relatively) slow-moving derigible-like objects, like in the Stora Mellösa case. Mr. Enhörning, the technician at Eskilstuna, had reported ”intermittent bursts” which was a sign of the V-1. 

The Defence Staff decided to make a call for reports through the military organizations. The following memo was dispatched in 221 copies to national and regional staffs and units in the army, air force and navy: 

”Headquarters, Defence Staff. Department L (Air Defence) nr 7:49. June 12, 1946. Reports concerning light phenomena.

”In the latest weeks certain light phenomena etc have been sighted over Swedish territory. It cannot be ruled out that these could be connected to tests, by a foreign power, of types of remotely-piloted weapons. To get better information, than what presently can be achieved through newspapers and from single observers, personnel at staffs and military units, first of all sentries, night-guards etc, should, until further notice, be notified to observe and report sightings in the air, which may seem peculiar. For this, the following instructions concerns reporting, and could also serve as a guidance for questioning civilians who report their sightings to military authoritíes.   

”1. Report should include:
     the reporters location at the time of sighting,
     the time,
     direction where the phenomenon was observed,
     angle of height, course and
     description of the phenomenon.
     The place should be stated according to field map,
     The time should, if possible, be fixed within 1
     minute, and the duration should be estimated, e.g.
     ”0254, light 20 seconds thereafter smoketrail 50 
     seconds”. The observer´s clock should be          
     compared to Swedish standard time and the time   
     should be stated accordingly. 
     The direction is decided over a certain point in the
      terrain, whereafter the direction later should be 
      measured by a compass.
      The angle of height and course should be stated
      only when this can safely be done. Especially, 
      any observed movement in height should be 
      reported and whether the course was straight 
      or zig-zagged (curved). 
      At the desciption the objects appearance (shape)
      and the colour of the light (smoke) should be 
      reported, stating whether the light (smoke) was
      regular, intermittent, etc. Sound phenomena 
      (noise, bangs, hissing etc) should particularly
      be stated. If possible the time difference between      
      visual appearance of the phenomenon, and when 
      the sound was heard, should be reported.
   2. Report should be sent by unit chief (or similar) 
      as soon as possible directly to the Defence Staff´s 
      Air Defence department (tel. ”military staffs”), in 
      non-office hours by the officer on duty (or similar)
      to the officer on duty at the military staff building, 
      Stockholm (tel. ”first line of the military staffs”). If
      an exensive number of sightings should occur 
      they should be mailed by post.       
   3. Reports should not be released for publication. 
      On being asked for this reference should be made
      to section III of the Defence Staff. 

On order of the Supreme Commander
/signed/  T.Bonde
 acting chief of the Defence Staff 
/signed/ Nils Ahlgren

We know, from the archives, that the order was widely circulated at the regional level. For instance, we know that about 60 copies were made and dispatched (on June 26), by the North Swedish regional military staff, to chiefs of local police, Home Guard chiefs and customs outposts. On July 8, the County Government Board [Länsstyrelsen] at Örebro sent a similar reporting order to all police chiefs in that county.
