      An excellent response to the CSICOP position app.e.ars in the March-April
issue of "International UFO Reporter", written by its editor, Jerry Clark.
Jerry and your "Smear" editor have disagreed on many subjects at many times,
including about the merits of CSICOP as a counter-balance to ufological and
parapsychological nonsense. But at this stage of the game, we cannot help
but agree with him 100% in his remarks, which we quote below in part:
      In case you didn't know it, CSICOP, aka "the lone voice defensiing ra­
tionality" (Chairman Paul Kurtz, "Skeptical Inquirer", Winter 1992, page 117)
- is in dire straits these days, at the receiving end of a series of lawsuits
filed by Uri Geller. Depending on your point of view, Geller is the most re­
markable psychic of his generation or a charismatic sleight-of-hand artist.
CUFOS and IUR are not required to have an opinion, or even frankly to care
very much, about which of these more accurately characterizes Geller.
      CSICOP, of course, has always cared deeply, to the point of obsession,
about Geller's method of bending spoons, and managed to elevate a question
which ought to be of interest only to parapsychologists and magicians into
an issue sufficiently threatening to demand both the urgent attention    of
Western civilization and the rapid infusion of as much cash as you can spare
into CSICOP's bank account. CSICOP long ago eschewed "scientific investiga­
tion" for itself ("Skeptical Inquirer", Spring 1982, page 9) , though retain­
ing its middle name so as to continue to wow the rubes - CSICOP's    use of
"scientific" is as calculated as the Moral Majority's is of "moral" - but
it just as long ago demonstrated that what it lacks in scientific seriousness
....it more than compensates for in its extraordinary ability to promote it­
self and to maintain a fat bank balance.
      Currently, even as Chairman Kurtz frets that the Lone Voice Defending
Rationality may be stilled (thus necessitating a new round of fund-raising
letters) , CSICOP is busily husrling millions to construct a huge Center for
Inquiry from which CSICOP, "Skept}cal Inquirer", and related heresy-bashing
enterpreneurial enterprises will be able to combat the irrational hordes in
space and comfort - all the while' keeping up the pretense that they are dis­
playing immense courage and making enormous sacrifices against vastly super­
ior forces.
      This is what the experts call a "rhetorical strategy". In the     real
world, of course, the odds are stacked hugely in CSICOP's favor. Most maga­
zine and newspaper articles on CSICOP read like press releases; and "Science",
the "New York Review of Books", "Nature", "Scientific American",   "Science
News", "Time", "Washington Post", and practically every other elite organ
hold the line, which is that any sympathetic consideration of anomalous phe­
nomena, however cautious, is pseudoscientific by definition. Few of the au­
thors of any of these have ever heard of the "Journal of UFO Studies", the
"Journal of Parapsychology", the "Journal of Scientific Exploration", or
"Cryptozoology" - all sober, refered scientific periodicals - and CSICOP is
not about to enlighten them....
      One did not have to be a psychic to predict that eventually CSICOP
would get into serious legal trouble, given the extravagance and reckless­
ness of its rhetoric, about which even CSICOP Fellow and founder Ray Hyman -
though no other significant figure within the leadership - has complained on
occasion. Hyman once remarked to me on a "frightening fundamentalism" he
had seen in the debunking ranks. Until its recent crisis CSICOP evinced lit­
tle interest in moderation in speech or printG   The one exception involved
the quick squelching of a nobody who circulated a letter which, purporting
to speak in CSICOP's name, sought to organize those who wanted to "get dirty"
against "those who have no demonstrated·facility to reason". In this in­
stance Kurtz sternly admonished the nobody to cease and desist.
      But when somebodies - in other words, debunkers with names, public as-

        Here's an update on a subject one seldom hears about any more -    the
mysterious "hush-up" of Albert K. Bender, the man who in 1952 founded one
of the very first flying saucer clubs� It was pretentiously called the In­
ternational Flying Saucer Bureau, as they had a few members overseas.      Ex­
actly four issues of Bender's little quarterly magazine were published before
he made his famous claim to have been warned, by three men in black who vis­
ited his apartment, that he had come too close to the Truth about UFOs and
must therefore cease & desist. He did.
        Although there were a few hundred subscribers to the zine, the active
group - as with all organizations - was very small indeed. Among the hard
core were Dominick Lucchesi of New Jersey, now deceased; August C. Roberts
of New Jersey, now in failing health; and the late, great Gray Barker      of
Clarksburg, West Virginia, who made the Bender story the heart of his best­
known book, "They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers". About a decade after
the "hush-up", Barker persuaded Bender to write a book about his ufological
problems. This little-known tome, first published in 1962, is called "Fly­
ing Saucers and the Three Men". It tells of an absurd race of aliens       who
are coming here for natural resources that are absent on their home planet.
        For many years Gray Barker kept in touch with Bender, who eventually
moved to California from his original home in Connecticut. Recently we learn­
ed that August Roberts is still corresponding with Bender, forty years after
they first made contact with each other. Bender has had physical and mental
problems through the years, and is entirely off the UFO subject. He       now
lives in Hawaii, we are told, and is planning to move to Australia,      where
relatives of his English-born wife are living. Will· the real answer to the
so-called Bender Mystery ever be learned? Probably not!.-.-.-.-
