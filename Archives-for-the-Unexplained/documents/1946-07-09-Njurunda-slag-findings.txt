A report on slag findings  (dated July 11, 1946)

Personnel: Colonel Rudberg, Captain Westlin, Captain Ljungdahl.

The investigation revealed:

1. That slag which looks similar to previously discovered material could be found along the entire beach, even far away from the "point of impact", along with driftwood, bark, etc.

2. That the "point of impact" couldn't be observed from where airline pilot Lindén and Mrs. Söderlund were standing.

3. That the alleged "point of impact" consisted of a small pit, around which a number of slag pieces were found. At the time, two girls were sunbathing  on a beach 20-30 meters from this point, but the phenomenon hadn’t caught their attention. They didn't observe the impact, nor did they hear any sound and weren't made aware of the phenomenon or anything in connection therewith.

Thus, it must be assumed that the discovered slag is a product derived from ships and that the "point of impact" didn’t occur on Björkön. The impact range area must have been much greater than already estimated by the observers.
In addition to previous reports, we received a message from the town of Stöde. Apparently, in the middle of May, a slag-like mass (around which the grass was burnt at one meter radius) had been discovered in a field (approximately 100 meters from the main road).  This glowing mass had probably been hurled through the air. Portions of this mass were sent in separately.
