       Did Bomb or Saucer Put Hole
       in ·a British Potato Patch? · .··
The small townlet of Charlton, England (population 423) was suddenly placed on the map
during July, 1963, when a strange 8 foot wide crater appeared in a potato patch on the
farm of Mr. Roy Blanchard.
Two members of parliament demanded an investigation. An army bomb disposal unit
appeared on the scene on July 24th, stating that they were tackling the crater as a
"normal bomb disposal job."
Speculation zipped · around the country. Some thought it was a meteor, while others
speculated that it might have been the result of a World War Two Bomb, which deterio-
rated for twenty years before finally going off. Still others, including Blanchard,
the owner of the farm, thought it was the imprint of a landed "flying saucer."
Hundreds of curious sightseers began to appear in the area around the farm. Among them
was a man claiming to be an Australian Scientist. He claimed that the crater was made
by a spaceship from the planet Uranus. However, a check with local scientific circles
showed that they had no knowledge of the man.
On July 26th it was announced that . the Army unit, which had been digging at the site,
claimed to have found a meteor of tennis ball size. Maj. H.P. Quartrough, speaking on
behalf of the Army unit, stated "Our interest in the incident is at an end."
Despite the apparent explanation, sightseers continued to peer into the . roped-off
crater. Why? Several points do not fit the meteor explanation.
First, there was the fact that in a neighboring barley field, a fifty-foot circle of
barley had been beaten down, coinciding with the appearance of the crater.
Also coinciding with the advent of the crater was the fact that one of Mr. Blanchard's
cows began to shed it's skin, as if it wer~ burned. Could this have been caused by
the meteor? Probably not without killing the cow. It was also noted that both barley
and potatoes were missing from Mr. Blanchard's field, apparently unaccountably.
What does Blanchard think? ''As far as I'm concerned''. he said, "something strange
landed in those fields. And when it took off again, some of my potatoes and barley
went with it."
In the last issue of Saucer Album, we discussed the censorship of information related
to unidentified flying objects by the United States Air Force. Could the British Army
have a similar policy? Is it possible that the "meteor" was a hoax? Even if this is
not true, how do we explain the vanished vegetables, burned livestock, etc. in the
context of the meteor theory?

                                    By Allen Greenfield
I guess over the course of years every ufo investigator comes into contact with unusual
experiences; sometimes explainable, at other times not. I, for one, have been admitted-
ly hesitant to mention any of the several unusual events that I have observed. In the
first place, I don't have much evidence to shed a great deal of light on any ~f these
occurrences. For another, I don't want my readers to mistakenly believe that I've "gone
contactee."
But now I have decided to discuss these events as best I can. I'm not trying to~
any of them; certainly none are unbelievable in light of the many far more fantastic
events that have been reported within the realm of. ufological investigations. I can
only present them in good faith; I do not claim that any of these events are ufo sight-
ing~ , nor do I claim that they have a special value of any sort. I'll report it
straight , you decide their worth.
                                       Spider ~

As ,any ufologists ·do, I have the habit of going out early in the evenings of the warm
months, during the· hours when the earth is dark while the sky is still light, in order
to "skywateh" for· ufos. This practice is a 'hold-over' from the time several years ago
when various ufo groups attempted to set vast "sighter-networks" into operation. The
plan was a flop each time it was tried, but some ufologists got into the 'habit' of
"skywatch", mys~lf included.
But on one such occasion, several years ago (exact date unknown), I got the fright of
my life. When I went out, at about 6:00 P.M. during late autumn, I got that uncomfor-
table feeling that everyone knows; that disturbing feeling of "eyes on the back of your
neck." As I write this -late at night -I have that feeling again, but this is, of
~ourse, only power of suggestion -- I hope!

Anyway, I had this feeling, so I was a little jumpy in the first place. I started my
observations looking · at the early evening stars. Nothing took place, and so, after
about an hour, I decided to give up, as usual. Taking a quick sweep of the now dark
sky, I caught, out of the corner of my eye, a view of what I can only describe as a
"black area" about 5 feet around, perched on the edge of the roof, defined by the glow
of a neighbors spotlight.
Panic! It was unreasoning, unscientific, cowardly, et cetra. But nevertheless Allen
Greenfield was gone, moving as fast as his now wobbling knees would allow. Back in
my office, I cooled off. It was rather obvious to me that I had seen some sort of
shadow, and that, · coupled with the "eyes" feeling, had produced the 'black area'. So,
fully armed (kitchen knife), well equipped (movie camera - unloaded, as I later dis-
covered, spotlight, binoculars), nerves steadied (I hoped), I returned to the scene of
the 'sighting'.
There it was , still outlined, although not what you could call sharply defined; it was
like looking at something through heat waves, such as you see on highways on hot days.
Extended from the 'area' was a glistening gossmar-like substance; a single strand of
a spider web - or angel hair.
I was still scared, but I was calm enough to stand my ground - so long as it didn't move.
I thought to myself that if this was the reaction people would have if the ufos, peace-
ful or otherwise, were to land, well, we were in trouble. Of course, as a ufologist I
hear stories of ufo kidnappings, etc., much more often than the average person, so I'd
be more likely to get seared than John Q.

I stood there for a few minutes, gaping. The object did nothing, and I did nothing to
make it do anything. Had I been with other people, I am certain my reaction would most
likely been far different - as of that time I have been an opponent of one man obser-
vation teams. A person seeing an object he can't identify while by himself feels
awfully alone and unsure.
So I stood and looked. By this time I was certain of one thing; this was no illusion.
After awhile I decided it wasn't going to do anything more. I don't know why I didn't
have the presence of mind to call someone else to see the 5 foot circle of 'whatsit'
••• but, as it must be obvious to you by now, my mind wasn't working too well that night.
The occultist buffs might say that the object "had the whammy - the evil eye .. on me.
I wish I could say that was what it was, but it wasn't~ ••• 1 was just p1ain scared.
I left, deciding to check and see if it was still there the next day.   When I checked,
whatever it was -----wasn't. You figure it out.
                                   Ball Park Incident
It was during the summer of 1962. Hundreds of baseball devotees had tur.ned out to
watch a not-very-interesting game between Atlanta and Toronto. Since Atlanta was
losing rather badly, no one was really concentrating on the field. Someone glanced up
at the sky. Then everybody was looking. I happened to be at that game and I also
looked up. I have a sneaking suspicion even some of the players on the field secreted
a glance. I was pretty excited. There, after all my waiting - my first look at a ufo:
It was a circular object with a glowing beam. It circled the field for several minutes,
disappeared, then reappeared to repeat the cycle several minutes later. If it was a
~ ufo, I theorized, it might be attracted to the strong stadium lights.

But there was an "if" in my mind. In the first place, the object did not look like
what you would expect a ufo to look like. To be sure, it was unusual, but it did not
fit the "standard" ufo pattern. Then again, there was the slightest suspicion of a
motor noise, heard faintly above the crowd. There were a lot of comments thrown around
among the group of people I was with, but nobody mentioned the possibility of a flying
saucer, ufo, or somesuch. Now, although I'd never seen a ufo, I have followed the
phenomena closely. Usually, when someone~ a ufo, he calls it fust lh!l· There
was considerable doubt in my mind, so I just chalked this one up as "insufficient
evidence for evaluation".
A few months later, Mr.         of radio station         telephoned me a similar report.
I didn't connect the two at first, but as investigations were conducted, I remembered
my experience at the Baseball Stadium. I also recalled noted research Frank Edwards'
ufo report. The night after his sighting, a plane with a special gadget for advertisi ng
went up with a message reading something like ..Hello Frank Edwards", or some similar
'cute' message of questionable humor. ~lr. Edwards noted that, while the "real" ufo was
called 'saucer' by many who saw it, no one was fooled by the subsequent hoax.
Then I got word from the above mentioned man, who hosts a well-known local radio program,
that the thing as described in the report he gave me might have been an advertising
plane. Further investigation confirmed this.
In theory, this also explained the ball park incident. · But there was -and still is -
some room for doubt. Firstly, the second incident was not definitely proven to be a
mis-identified plane, although the evidence pointed this way. Second, it could be
argued that- the two incidents, though seemingly similar, were totally unrelated. I
don't think so. I think that both incidents can be attributed to the advertising plane.
Others may disagree, but from now on, if I .receive any report~ of this nature, I'm
going to check for these optically-tricky advertising planes as my first point of
reference.

                                      Fireflies

John Glenn and other astronauts who've seen the so-called "space fireflies" haven't
got anything on me. As a matter of fact, I've seen 'em too. Not spots before the
eyes, but real, honest-to-goodness fireflies •••• and I didn't even have to leave the
ground to see them.

It started with a phone call during the spring of 1Q62. A friend of mine who, incident-
ally, doesn't believe in ufos, gave me a ring to calmly tell me that "hundreds of little
specks" were flying over the area. I went outside, looked in the direction he indicated,
and there they were. Specks, specks, and still more specks. High in the sky. Somebody
suggested that it was a fire; the particles floating into the air. A quick check with
the local press disclosed "no fire".
The friend who called me in the first place called again to tell me that the local
weather bureau offered the explanation "it's a plane." Yeah, it's a· plane ..•.•• that
looks like hundreds of particles moving around in all directions. I understand that
a few angry comments passed between the inquirer and the .weather bureau official before
they hung up.

When night fell, the sparkling whatsits disappeared. Later, my original informant
phoned again to offer his solution. "They were high-flying fireflies". I hung up. So
they were (A) a fire, or (B) an airplane, or (C) high-flying fireflies. Oh well, t~is
doesn't really fall under the category of 'ufo' and therefore I'm not duty bound tq
investigate further. S~ch things fall under the rather all-encompassing title of
"Fortean Phenomena". I let the whole thing drop.


Here is the most mysterious phenomena of all. Before describing it, let me state that
I live a good distance from any industrial area that might produce such a phenomena.
I'm a nightbird. I'm probably as alert at 3:00A.M. as you are at 3:00P.M. At about
the quietest period of the day- between 2- 4 A.M., there occurs a strange muffled
booming sound almost every night. It usually comes more than once each night, though
I haven't been able to determine any set pattern of any kind.
The booms are generally rather low, and might go unnoticed. I don't know how I first
came to notice them; I don't think they've ever been noticeable. At any rate, they are
noticeable to me now, because I am alerted to hear them. I don't know what they could
be, certainly not the unmistakable 'boom' caused by sonic booms, explosions, or the
like. They are a unique, wee-hours noise. What are they? You tell me.
And so ends my strange tale. As I said, none of these events are earth-shattering
in scope, or revealing in the content. But you must admit, it's all pretty strange.
As a matter of fact, as the British might say, "Highly irregular."

Early in the last century a Russian Trapper called a certain thickly-wooded mountain in
Northern California "Chas-tcha" -- "A thicket" in Russian. The name of the mountain
has not changed much - it is now called Mt. Shasta, a corruption of the original Russian.
Mt. Shasta~ The name strikes a magic cord in the minds of even the most conservative of
ufo investigators. Since its discovery, and before, if we are to believe the stories of
the local indians, Mt. Shasta has been a location for the strangest of events; the un-
usual, the weird, the unexplainable.
The indian legends cannot be accurately traced, and therefore are not discussed in this
article. But the settlers in the area can attest that, up until the most recent of
years, strange occurances have taken place in the area around Mt. Shasta. It is note-
worthy to mention, in fact, that the famed Arnold incident took place over a sister

The 14,000 foot peak was the subject of a book, "A Dweller of Two Planets", published
in 1884. Although this book was undoubtedly largely fancy, its backdrop could have
some basis in fact. The book discusses the strange people who live on the mountain.
When this area was first settled by the Spanish, the Shasta people were considered
indians. But despite their aloofness, the Shastites were soon known to be white men,
dressing in "long white togas." This is recorded fact. This indicates that the dwellers
are not, as some have maintained, just another California ''Quack cult." They were here
before the European, and yet are ~ indians.
Ignition trouble is often associated with ufos. This is proported to be in connection
with elector-magnetic (E-M) effects associated with ufo propulsion. It is noteworthy
to mention that similar effects have been noted in connection with cars approaching Mt.
Shasta. Even before electrification, in the middle 1800's, mysterious flashes were
noted on the brush-covered slopes of Mt. Shasta. These often occured in clear weather,
virtually ruling out natural electrical effects.
Richard Cohen, in an article in "Nexus", February, 1955, states "People living in that
part (near Shasta) of California also claim that strange cattle, unlike anything ever
seen in America, have emerged from the woods, but before going very far along the high-
ways and byways, these animals would be frightened by some invisible signal, and would
abruptly turn around and run back toward the places from which they came."
In 1931, a forest fire swept over the Mt. Shasta area. A mysterious fog suddenly covered
the mountain, completely stopping the fire's advance. The demarcation line of where
the fire stopped is still decernable. It is abrupt, drawing a perfect circle around
the central zone. On the lower side of the circle, you can see younger trees that have
grown up since 1931. On the upper side are taller, thicker, and obviously far older
trees, which were protected by the mystery fog.
A. Tomas records two notable incidents of ufo sightings near the Mount. "In the late
twenties golfers in Northern California watched a silver, wingless air ship going
noiselessly over the mountain tops. It was unlike any plane or dirigible of the times.
In October, 1956, David Williamson observed 14 lights over Mt. Shasta making unusual
sky maneuvers. Then one descended from the sky, right on. top of Mt. Shasta."
