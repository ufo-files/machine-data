                                     The Uplifting of Jane Lead

Jane Lead was a British Christian mystic who lived from 1623 to 1704. According to her own writings,
during a dance at a Christmas party, when she was a fifteen years old, Lead heard a miraculous
disembodied voice. It said "Cease from this, I have another dance to lead thee in, for this is vanity." She
interpreted this as a sign that she should devote her life to a spiritual cause, and in later life this decision
led her into the study of theology, philosophy and alchemy. Fascinated by the works of Jacob Boehme
(1575-1624), the German seer and theosophist, Lead began to interpret her own visions and experiences
using his methodology. She started to write down what she saw in a diary and it was upon these writings
that she went on to base her teachings. By 1693 she had her own established group of followers, the
Philadclphians, which met regularly in Lambeth. London.
         The structure of Lead's alleged experiences docs not stray far from the model that one is
accustomed to reading about today in books by John Mack or Budd Hopkins. Even the 'voice' she heard
at an early age could be interpreted as a telepathic command, a very common experience among
abductees. Years later she would write about actual sightings that she had and several 'abductions' by a
group of beings who were neither angels nor demons. (Lead often uses the term 'Magia' when referring
