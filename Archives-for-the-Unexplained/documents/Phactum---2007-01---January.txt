       The first PhACT lecture of 2007 will be on Saturday, January 20, at 2:00 PM, at Phila-
delphia Community College. This meeting will have a quick recap by Eric Krieg, PhACT Presi-
dent, about failed legal efforts to shut down his skeptical web pages. But our main speaker is ACLU
chapter board member, Bill Ewing, will speak on Issues of Free Speech.
       Our constitution limits majority power with the bill of rights and 13th, 14th, 15th and 19th
amendments. The American Civil Liberties Union is committed to preserving these rights, espe-
cially freedom of speech, freedom of the press, separation of church and state, equal protection un-
der the law, right to due process and right to privacy. The ACLU was founded in 1920 is nonprofit
and nonpartison, has half a million members and handles around 6000 court cases yearly.
       This lecture is free and open to the public. All are welcome. Bring a friend or better yet,
bring two friends.

                Your input to this newsletter is important and very much wanted. Original articles, letters, and
                book reviews are desired. We welcome criticism and suggestions for improving this newsletter
                and PhACT in general. Submitted items should have some general interest for Critical Thinkers
                but we certainly can relax that rule for member written poetry. We are not a political forum and
                therefore prefer not to get into factional party or sectarian debate, but we are not at all shy about
                presenting multiple points of view about more narrow issues such as Global Warming, malaria,
                Life on Mars, the Nature of God, and a Universe of other topics that might interest you and other
                Critical Thinkers. Later in this newsletter Dave Leiter urges all to read. I urge all to write.
                Send your thoughts, including denunciation of the editor, by e-mail: phactpublicity@aol.com.

                                       A Skeptic's Thought
                            It seems inconsistent with all human reason,
                           That God in his infinite goodness and love___
                           His wisdom, his power and mercy unending,
                               Would quietly sit in his mansion above,
                          While over the earth dire misfortune is pending,
                           Dissension and discord are rife thru the land,
                              Injustice prevails, and the innocent suffer,
                           And fairest and loveliest, fall at Death's hand.
                          Could he calmly sit, high on his throne in the sky
                          While pain and distress to his children drew nigh?

                                                 - Colfax Burgoyne Harmon,

                                              Poems Of Sentiment
                                          Colfax Burgoyne Harman
