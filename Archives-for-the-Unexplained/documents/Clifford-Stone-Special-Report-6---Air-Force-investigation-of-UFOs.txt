                            SPECIAL REPORT 6

                THE U. S . AIR FORCE ' S REAL INVESTIGATION
                    OF UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTS
                                    By

                            Clifford E. Stone




    For many years , it was believed that Project Blue Book was
    the u. s . Air Force ' s o nly of ficial investigation of UFO ' s.
    However , new evidence has c ome to light that seems to rule
    this out . In fact , this new evidence seems to indicate
    that Project Blue Book did not investigate UFO ' s at a l l ,
    other than to operate as a public relations program fo r the
    Air Force on UFO inouiries.
                           -           The Air Force ' s real
    investigation of UFO ' s was conducted by the Air Defense
    Command. To wit : the 4602d Air Intelligence Service
    Squardon (AISS) .

    The 4602d AISS has been known by many names over the
    years . It has been known as the 1006th AISS , 1127th USAF
    Field Activities Group , 7602d USAF Field Activities Group ,
    and is currently known as the u.s . Air Force Special
    Activities Center at Fort Belvoir , Virginia . This unit has
    a wartime mission , " of exp loiting downed enemy people ,
    papers , and hardware. " This unit also had/has a peacetime
    mission of providing intelligence teams to , ''recover or
    perform field exploitation of unidentified flying objects ,
    or known Soviet/Bloc aerospace vehicles , weapons systems ,
    and/or residual components of such equipment. "
    In order to form some ki n d of understanding of the 4602d
    AISS ' s involvement in the Air Force ' s UFO investigations ,
    we need to look at some early Ai r Force documents . The
    first of these documents will be Air Force Regulation (AFR)
    200 - 2 , dated August 12 , 1954 , entitled Unidentified Flying
    Objects (short title : UFOB) .
    Air Force Regulation 200-2 is an interesting document for
    many reasons . One it was an Intelligence Regulation other
    than a Research and Development Regulation (AFR 80-17) , as
    it would become after the formation of the Condon Project .
    Two , it defined what the Air Force ' s definition of a UFO
    was, separating UFO ' s f rom what was called " familiar
    objects '' . This was to change also , as a result of the
    formation of the Condon Project , as shall be shown later in
    this report . Also , it outlined responsibilities for the
    investigation of UFO ' s or UFO Reports . This changed too ,
    with the introduction of the Condon Project . AFR 80-17
    required UFO Reports to be forwarded to the University of

Colorado, while at the same time admitting some reports
would be classified and not fully available to the Condon
Project. In cases such as these, according to AFR 80-17,
as much of the information as possible was to be provided
to the Condon Project. Here we have another problem.
Many of the documents released under FOIA clearly show
classifications higher than Secret for which the Condon
Project was cleared. Those cases classified at the Top
Secret level and/or restricted under such programs as SCI
would not be available to the Condon Project. This
required someone to review UFO reports before their
transmission to the University of Colorado. Once again,
that job fell on the 4602d AISS (known as the 7602d USAF
Field Activities Group by this time).
As stated above, the definition of what was considered a
UFO had also changed. AFR 200-2 defined a UFO as , ''any
airborne object which by performance, aerodynamic
characteristics, or unusual features does not conform to
any presently known aircraft or missile type, or which
cannot be positively identified as a familiar object." AFR
80-17 defined a UFO as, "any aerial phenomenon which is
unknown or appears out of the ordinary to the observer."
We can see from these two definitions that one is serious
(AFR 200-2) and the other is no more than a joke (AFR
80-17). The AFR 200-2 definition assumed that the observer
was factual in their reporting and the object was real and
unknown based upon certain features of the object reported;
while AFR 80-17 ' s definition made the assumption that the
observer did not know what he/she was talking about and no
unknown objects were involved.
According to AFR 200-2 , the Air Force's interest in UFO's
was twofold. These were; '' First as a possible threat to
the security of the United States and its forces, and
secondly, to determine technical aspects involved. '' The
regulation was quick to point out that to date no UFO had
imposed any threat to the security of the United States.
However, it pointed out that, '' the possibility that new air
vehicles, hostile aircraft or missiles may be regarded as
flying objects by the initial observer is real. " Surely
this would hold true even in this day and age regardless of
what the U.S. Air Force might wish us to believe.
AFR 200-2 made it perfectly clear that the Air Force was
not looking for space vehicles from some other world.
This, to me, is interesting in light of the fact that the
Air Force always concluded there had been no evidence
indicating sightings categorized as unidentified were
extraterrestrial vehicles. Of course, the U.S. Air Force's
most unscientific and official stand on extraterrestrial
vehicles has always been that they cannot exist, therefore,
they do not exist.

I      Air Force Regulation 200-2 also made it clear the the Air
       Defense Command had a direct interest in the facts
       pertaining to UFO reports and had , " in the 4602d Air
       Intelligence Service Squadron (AISS), the capability to
       investigate these reports." All Air Force activities were
       authorized to conduct preliminary investigations that might
       be required for reporting purposes; however,
       " investigations should not be carried beyond this point,
       UNLESS SUCH ACTION IS REQUESTED BY THE 4602D AISS." It is
       important to note that the base Project BJue Book officer,
       at the base receiving a UFO report, would conduct the
       initial preliminary investigation into the report.
       However, any other contact or follow on investigation into
       the case required the 4602d's direct approval and not that
       of Project Blue Book Headquarters at Wright-Patterson. To
       be sure , the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC) was
l      not to become involved in any case until, ''after the Air
       Defense Command has exhausted all efforts to identify the
       UFOB (UFO Report) ."
       All UFO reports of any intelligence value were to be
       submitted to the Director of Intelligence, Headquarters
       USAF, Washington, D.C. by the 4602d. Also, all UFO reports
       from outside of the United States were to be directly
       submitted to the Director of Intelligence, Headquarters
       USAF prior to their transmission to ATIC . Once again we
       know that many of the overseas cases never made it to
       Project Blue Book after receipt by the Director of
       Intelligence, United States Air Force.
       Now one might say that all this is well and good. However,
       the fact remains that if the Air Defense Command was unable
       to identify any UFO reports, then those cases were to be
       turned over to Blue Book for analysis according to AFR
       200-2. While this is in fact true, one needs to keep in
       mind several points. One, while the Air Technical
       Intelligence Center , Foreign Technology Division housed the
       Aerial Phenomena Branch (Project Blue Book) , it also housed
       a unit of the 4602d AISS . Today this unit is known as
       Detachment 22, u.s . Ai r Force Special Activities Center and
       reports directly to its Fort Belvoir , Virginia
       headquarters . Secondly, we are aware of reports that neve r
       made it to the Blue Book files. Some of those reports have
       surfaced in current years from the Office of the Director
       of Intelligence, u. s. Air Force indicating that the 4602d
       AISS was aware of these cases and did not forward them to
       the Blue Book Office as required. To be sure, the existing
       group still analyzes and evaluates those UFO reports
       considered to have some form of vital intelligence interest
       to the United States .
       Suspected or actual items of material from alleged UFO ' s
       were to be collected and safeguarded in such a manner as to
       prevent any defacing or alteration which might reduce its
       value for intelligence examination and analysis . AFR 200-2
       and Intelligence Collection Instructions (ICI) , dated June

                                   CONCLUSIONS



         The 4602d AISS carried out the U. S. Air Force's real
         investigation of UFO ' s. Those cases which contained vital
         intelligence of interest to the U.S. Government were
         routinely sent forward by electrical means to interested
         intelligence agencies, bypassing Project Blue Book. Should
         the public or media become interested or have any knowledge
         of any of these cases, then only enough information
         (sometimes disinformation or out-right lies) would be
         provided to Project Blue Book to satisfy the media's
         questioning. AFR 200-2 states, '' Headquarters USAF will
         release summaries of evaluated data which will inform the
         public on this subject. " AFR 200-2 further states, "In
         response to local inquiries , it is permissible to inform
         news media representatives on UFOB's when the object is
         positively identified as a familiar object •.•• For those
         objects which are not explainable , only the fact that ATIC
         will analyze the data is worthly of release, DUE TO THE
         MANY UNKNOWNS INVOLVED ."

         Over the years, many documents (raw intelligence data) have
         been released indicating the 4602d AISS's involvement with
         UFO's. While these documents reflected such units as the
         1127th, 7602d , and USAFSAC they are all the same unit and
         carry out the same mission; just different names. Many of
         these documents show that this unit had an interest in
         UFO's long after the closure of Project Blue Book , such as
         the documents concerning UFO sightings in the USSR and i n
         Be lgium. Also , these documents clearly show that the U.S.
         Air Force still has an interest in UFO ' s today and that
         that interest is highly classified for whatever the reason.

         It has been said that we have enough evidence to prove the
         existence of UFO ' s in a court of law . However , UFO ' s are
         not a legal problem but a scientific one. Therefore, we
         must approach it in that light . A scientist must have
         concrete evidence before he is willing to offer an opinion
         and absolute proof before he is willing to offer any
         conclusions . I believe that evidence and proof can be
         found in the classified vaults of the U.S. Air Force
         Special Activities Center at Fort Belvoir , Virginia. The
         unit that to this very day carries on the Air Force ' s real
         investigation of Unidentified Flying Objects, classified to
         the extreme that the Air Force may neither confirm nor deny
         the existence or non-existence of many programs apparently
         related to UFO recoveries , in the interest of national
         security. Programs concerning the existence of much of the
