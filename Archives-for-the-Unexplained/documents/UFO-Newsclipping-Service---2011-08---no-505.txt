                                                           The Loch Ness Monster is far from being a hoax- rather it is 'plausible'
        Metro                                              and may exist after all according to one oi the UK 's leading dinosaur
        Eng land                                           experts, Dr. Darren Naish.
        July 28th , 2011




                    By Aaron-Spencer Charles




         A paleontologist at the University of Portsmouth, Dr Naish said the sheer number of sightings suggests there is some truth to the
         ancient legend.

         'The huge number of 'sea monster' sightings now on record can't all be explained away as mistakes, sightings of known animals or
         hoaxes,' he said.

         ' At least some of the better ones - some of them made by trained naturalists and such - probably are descriptions of encounters
         with real, unknown animals.

         'Because large marine animals continue to be discovered, and various new whale and shark species have been named in recent
         years, the idea that such species might await discovery is, at the very least, plausible.'

         The Loch Ness Monster has been 'seen' by many people since 1933.

         The body of water itself measures 22 .5 miles long and 1.5 miles wide, with a depth of 754ft, with its sheer size giving credence to
         the rumour of a plesiosaur-like creature living below the surface.

         However, despite admitting there may be some truth to the Loch Ness Monster myth, Dr Naish, along with Dr Charles Paxton
         from the University of St Andrews, both debunked the theory it is a dinosaur.

         Speaking at the 'Cryptozoology: Science or Pseudoscience?' conference in London, Dr Paxton said: 'If there are prehistoric
         animals alive today it would imply that there's something very wrong with our understanding of the fossil record.

         ' But in 1995 , a benthic ray , which lives on the ocean floor, was found that measured 3.42 metres and eight large marine species

Dear subscriber,

I regret that due to a number of circumstances , I will not be able to maintain
publication of UFONS.

Since becoming Editor in January of 2009, I was able to maintain monthly
publication of the paper. This allowed UFONS to reach its sooth issue and
40th Anniversary. These are certainly landmarks in the field of UFOiogy! Not
many other publications (if any) can boast this track record. Of course ,
credit goes to Rod Dyke and Lou Farish who kept this publication alive for
decades prior to my brief time as Editor.

I hope to send out issue #505 in August. This will be the last issue of
UFONS, unless a willing and suitable replacement for Editor is found. If
you , or someone you know, is interested in becoming Editor, please
contact me via email at: ufo.news@hotmail.com or write to the address
above.

In early September, refund checks will be issued to all subscribers who
were due future issues. Again, this will be the case unless UFONS
continues under another Editor. I ask for your patience while this takes
place and ask that you contact me, if you have questions relating to your
refund. Both the UFONS email and P.O. Box addresses will remain active
till year's end to insure proper correspondence with subscribers.

I must thank you for your support of UFONS while I was your Editor. It has
been challenging , fun , educational, and a thrill to work for all of you. My
activities in the field of UFOiogy will continue as a lecturer and historian.

Good luck to all of you in your future endeavors.
