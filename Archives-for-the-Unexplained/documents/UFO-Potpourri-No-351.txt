                                                         UFO       HEATS      CAR!


          A 26 year old woman was driving alone enroute to West Hampton on
          Long Island on February 4, 1990.  About 11:15 pm a large luminous
          oval shaped object appeared to the front of her car at treetop
          level and paced her car as she drove.   She said the object was as
          large as the width of the four lane highway.   The light coming
          from the object was intense and she had  to squint to see the
          road.  The heat coming from the object was very warm and she
          could feel it through the windshield of the car.   It flew just
          slightly ahead and above her.  She described it  as having a
          smooth surface with no doors, windows or appendages.        At one
          point, the object  suddenly  rose and  sped  off to the  southeast .
          Throughout  the incident  there was no  traffic  on the   highway.   She

          was alone  and frightene d.  This case  is  particula rly  interesti ng

          because a  number of factors  are similar   to those  found  in the
          December 1980 Cash/Landrum case which took place near                                                            Houston,
          Texas.  Further details may be found in UFO UNIVERSE,
          August/September 1991, page 5.

                        'DOOMSDAY' ASTEROID MAY HIT EARTH


The HOUSTON CHRONICLE, dated June 18, 1991, carried a headline
story entitled "Scientists scanning the heavens for 'doomsday'
asteroid".          It said:   "Somewhere in space at this moment,            hurtling
toward Earth at roughly 16 miles asecond,                  is the doomsday rock.
The question of growing interest to scientists and engineers is
exactly when it will approach our planet and whether anything can
be done to avoid a catastrophic collision,                  such as nudging the
rock off course with a nuclear blast or two.  The doomsday rock
is an asteroid large enough to severely disrupt life on Earth
·upon impact, lofting pulverized rock and dust that blocks most
sunlight.          Agriculture would virtually end,          and civilization
could   wither and die,        just as the dinosaurs and many other forms
of life are         thought by some to have       been    wiped out by a massive
object from outer space 65            million years ago."


As far as we know at this time, no astronomer has located the
killer asteroid.  But, given enough time it is inevitable that
one will appear.  And the odds are that the moment could be
relatively soon,         in celestial terms.


Experts have calculated the odds,               estimating the risk of death
for an American over a          50-year period and found that the risk
from an asteroid impact is            1 in 6,000.        That means the risk is
higher for being killed by an asteroid than it is from an
airplane crash,         from tornados,    or from fireworks.


Very little is being done to spot the killer asteroid.                    NASA is
now spending less than $1 million a year to search for Earth�
crossing asteroids.  More telescopes are needed.  Located in
space   or    on    the surface of the    Moon,    new telescopes could       provide
the early warning of an asteroid approach necessary to permit
something to         be done to avoid a collision.           That presupposes we
have the space systems           in our inventory that could prevent an
asteroid collision.




                        RARE ISOTOPE FOUND ON       NASA    CRAFT


The March      7,   1991 issue of     the THE TIMES,      reported scientists
from several U.S. government and university laboratories have
found the rare atmospheric            isotope    Beryllium-7   present on the
surface of NASA's Long Duration Exposure Facility                   (LDEF).


The LDEF was returned          from   space by    the    Space Shuttle Columbia in
January 1990 after nearly six years in Earth orbit.


The isotope is radioactive and produced naturally by cosmic ray
reactions in the         Earth's atmosphere.       The finding is unusual
because the isotope is mainly produced at much lower altitudes in
the atmosphere than where the LDEF was orbiting.  It appears that
some,   as yet undetermined,          process   efficiently carries the
isotope      to high altitudes.
