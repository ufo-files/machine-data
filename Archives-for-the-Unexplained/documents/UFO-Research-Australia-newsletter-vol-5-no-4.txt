One of the stumbling blocks, faced by an investigator of the UFO phenomena, is the in­
fluence brought to bare on the witness by his or her peers.              This pressure takes many
forms and produces varying,    mostly negative,     results which can cause modification of
witness behaviour and attitude, distortion of details, even the decision not to report
the event.   Generally, younger witness are influenced more by peer group pressure.


There are no set rules for dealing with the results of peer group influence. There
are, however, some tactics that can be utilized, and some attitudes that can be
adopted, when dealing with this influence.         Most important       is to be flexible in all
dealings with the witness.  Since peer group pressure affects people differently, no
two witnesses will respond to a UFO investigator in the same way.  Hence, it is up to
the investigator to vary his/her behaviour to match the situation.  The adoption of
a non-aggressive, non-authoritarian, attitude, especially during initial dealings
with the witness, is of great value.  Another useful point is for the investigator
to give the witness a full explanation of what is looked for in a report {how fine
a detail is needed},   how the report will be processed and who will see the report
{ the witness will need to be reassured of confidentiality}.             In dealing with a younger
person, it is necessary to communicate with them on an equal level, using their
jargon, if this seems necessary to put them at their ease.


A person influenced negatively by peer group pressure feels it is not socially accept­
able to see UFOs, much less talk about seeing them {unless that person is drunk,
drugged, insane, making it all up, etc.}.        This witness needs to be reassured that
sane and sober people see UFOs, that they are dealing with someone who is prepared
to listen to them without prejudice.


A witness who discusses a UFO incident with his/her peer group             absorbs     some of the
group's opinions, reasoning, and explanations for the event.  In such a situation,
the original details have been embroidered upon by others who did not witness the
event.   To remedy this situation, the investigator needs to get the witness to repeat
his/her narrative of the event a number of times to get the basic facts.                It is also
necessary to interview the witness alone to avoid further influence.


A witness can be more disturbed by their UFO experience than by harassment from their
peers.   This person has a basic emotional need to know what they saw.               The peer group
harassed witness, unlike the peer group repressed witness, will be more inclined to
report a UFO incident because of their greater need to identify and rationalise.
This witness needs lots of reassurance and fast action by the investigator - they
need answers quickly and preferably  in some tangible form.


Peer group pressure can also have a positive outcome when it pressures a witness into
reporting an event.  However, whether a witness is pressured, either for or against
UFOs, it is important to be mindful  of the influence of others when dealing with
