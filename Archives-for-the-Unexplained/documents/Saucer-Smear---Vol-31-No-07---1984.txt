PIE HIGH IN THE SKY OVER NEW YORK STATE:
       On August 25th there was a day-long UFO conference in Brewster, N.Y.,
as a direct result of a "flap" that seems to have been going on for over
a year in that area - a few miles north of New York City. The conference
was sponsored by "Smear" non-subscribers Peter Gersten and Philip Imbrog­
no. Among the 500 to 1,000 attendees were several saucer luminaries from
the New York area, including ace "Smear" reporter Harold Salkin. Sadly,
your humble editor was in Florida and unable to be present.
       The meeting ran from 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., according to our inside in­
formation. Speakers included the two sponsors plus Dr. J. Allen Hynek him­
self ( in the physical body) , who flew in from Arizona for the event. Also
speaking were Pete Mazzola of the SBI, Larry Fawcett ( co-author of "Clear
Intent") and several others. Many ufological luminaries, seldom seen in
one place at one time, were in the audience.
       Apparently there have been literally hundreds of UFO sightings in
the general vicinity of Brewster, seen by thousands of people. The most
commonly-seen object is a huge V-shaped thing, said to be anywhere from
one to three football fields in length. It sometimes flies as low as 200
feet over the homes of frightened residents, and it displays approximately
six lights of different colors. The strange part is that, unbelievably,
there are also about six planes in the area, which fly around in tight
formation several nights a week, deliberately imitating the appearance of
the "real" object. Little is known about these planes, but some people
suppose that they are piloted by people in league with the Air Force or
the CIA - the idea being to confuse the public into thinking that all the
sightings are of mere airplanes!
       It sounds to us like something truly weird may be going on here. At
least it should be possible to find out more about these terrestrial planes
and the people who pilot them. We've been hearing about this situation
from non-subscriber Harry Lebelson and others for a long time, but no one
seems to know what the hell is really going on. Stay tuned!

RE-HASH OF THE 1984 NATIONAL UFO CONFERENCE
      The 21st annual NUFOC convention was held as scheduled on Sept. 21-
22 at a motel in Cleveland, Ohio, co-hosted by Bob Easley and Al Manak
of the United Aerial Phenomena Agency. The crowd was somewhat smaller
than usual, but filled the conference room rented for the occasion.
      In regard to convention business, it was voted to add the names of
Kenneth Arnold, Edward U. Condon, and Joan O'Connell (recently deceased
editor of "The New Atlantean Journal") to our Honor Roll of ufologists who
have passed on. Pete Mazzola was given our coveted annual Ufologist of
the Year Award and was made an adviser to our NUFOC Governing Board. (This
Board is called the Permanent Organizing Committee, and consists of James
Moseley, Chairman; Al Manak, Bob Easley, Rick & Carol Hilberg, Charles Wil­
helm, Tim Beckley, Ed Biebel, and Hal Starr.)
      A bid was accepted from Kal K. Korff to host next year's convention
some time in May, in Freemont, California, near San Francisco. It is be­
lieved that the 1986 Con. will be held in Phoenix, Arizona by veteran ufol­
ogist Hal Starr, and your "Smear" editor has volunteered to hold the '87
bash in his new home town of Key West, Florida. The 1987 Convention will
thus occur forty years after the Kenneth Arnold sighting, and twenty years
after the glorious 1967 New York City Convention, which marked a high
point of sorts in the history of ufology.
      Getting back to 1984 - the lecture program in Cleveland was longer
and more varied than usual, and without a doubt gave the audience a good
cross-section of current thinking in Middle Ufology. Pete Mazzola gave a
spirited plea for unity in the field, while at the same time taking pot
shots at Dr. J. Allen Hynek and others. Mazzola waved a copy of the 1982
CUFOS tax return, which shows that they spent only about $400 for research
out of a $53, 000 income!
      Larry Peters (now nick-named "Old Red Eyes") gave a stirring account
of his adventures in Rome, Ohio a few years ago, playing shooting games in
the woods with mysterious red-eyed creatures that could barely be seen.
Ron Schaffner countered with a slide lecture, tending to prove that the
Rome case and many other Bigfoot-type incidents are hoaxes or misinter­
pretations.
      Madeline Teagle, a contactee lady from Cuyahoga Falls, Ohio, gave a
charming & mild-mannered defense of the contactee syndrome. Larry Blazy
gave a rambling run-down of ancient mysteries in the Bible and elsewhere.
Gray Barker spoke briefly on current ufological mysteries and showed a
trilogy of allegedly authentic UFO films.
      Due to a lack-of-time problem, Antonio Huneeus of New York had to
race through his collection of a hundred UFO slides from around the world,
but he did a good job anyhow. Al Manak gave a brief run-down of his com­
puter study of recent UFO cases, finding few if any meaningful patterns.

JERRY CLARK of FATE writes:
Dear Jim,

     By now faithful nonsubscribers are well aware of Phil Klass' effort
(in a phone conversation with a University of Nebraska administrator) to
portray ufologists as de facto Soviet agents. less well known is the fact
that the university withdrew its sponsorship of future conferences following
Phil's threat to sue the institution. Phil made the threat because he claimed
that a widely-disseminated internal memo paraphrasing his remarks misrepresented
his views.  (Phil distributed a partial transcript of his phone remarks which,
far from proving his point, only confirmed the memo's accuracy.  If anything,
the memo made Phil's charges sound a little less nutty than in fact they were.)

     Nonetheless, the university told organizer Ray Boeche it would not put
any money into the 1984 conference Ray had hoped to hold.  Ray tells me he
has been unable to get financing from other sources.  So there will be no
more Fortean conferences at the university.

     While all this was going on, I entered into correspondence with Phil
about the matter.    In the course of same, Phil lumped ufologists not only
with C ommu nists but also with Nazis and Ku Klux Klan members. When informed
that the university had cancelled sponsorship of the proposed 1984 conference,
he declared that he really didn't care all that much.    (After all, once again
the world had been saved from opinions Phil disapproves of.) He continued to
argue that those who express skepticism of official pronouncements on UFOs are
aiding America's enemies, thereby, in effect, flirting with treason. Phil's
letters got ever crazier until finally, at the conclusion of a communication
that was abusive even by Phil's standards, he made a crack about inviting me
aboard his yacht, murdering me and dumping my body into Chesapeake Bay.     I told
Phil that I considered his remark not only unfunny but grossly offensive, that
unless I had a full apology from him I would end our correspondence (which
spanned a period of years) forever.

     Predictably, Phil responded with an abusive letter which, among other
things, accused me of having no "sense of humor," in conmon (naturally)
with "most 'UFO Believers.'"  I might note here, by the way, that not long
ago Phil "joked" to Bill Moore about having him killed. Moore, a typically
humorless UFOP, was not amused.

     At the conclu.sion of the affair I described Phil's antics to a well­
known UFO skeptic, who observed that correspondence with Phil only encourages
him in his excesses, and he should not be encouraged.   He said that on oc­
casion it may be necessary to deal with Phil in a public forum but that on no
occasion should we have anything to do with him privately.   In this man's
view Phil's conduct is so outrageous as to be beyond the pale and it is best
for all of us, believers and nonbelievers alike, to keep as distant from him
as possible.

     After years of Klass-watching, I can only agree. So far as I am con­
cerned, the University of Nebraska episode is the last straw.  It is time not
only for ufologists to cease correspondence with Klass but also,for Klass'
debunker colleagues (who so far have maintained a tactful silence) to let us
know if they believe McCarthyite tactics should be part of the UFO debate.   If
they say nothing, we can only conclude that silence is assent. And if they
assent (by silence or otherwise), I for one will have nothing to do with them either.

     With the University of Nebraska incident, the UFO debate has sunk into the
sewer.  Those who wish to remain there are welcome to do so.  I will not be
among them.
