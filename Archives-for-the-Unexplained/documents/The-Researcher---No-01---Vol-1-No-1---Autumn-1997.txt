                                 About
    The Merseyside Anomalies Research Association was founded in February 1996. It
    is a non-profit making organisation which is aimed to encourage, promote and
    conduct unbiased scientific investigation and research into UFO/ paranormal
    phenomena. MARA provides support for college, university undergraduate and post
    graduate research. MARA also has a functioning Witness Support Group which
    helps those who have experienced close encounterI paranormal experiences.

    MARA is an informal group of people of all backgrounds and a variety of ages over
    18. The group is limited to a small number of people, composed of individuals with
    mixed backgrounds who want to actively investigate and research UFO/paranormal
    phenomena within Mersevside. MARA does not give public lectures at its regular
    meetings but often presents lectures for other groups and societies.

    Membership application to MARA is free and open to all members of the public.
    Places are limited and there is a waiting list. The Association is run jointly by its
    group co-ordinators who are amateur investigators and researchers in their spare
    time, and are members of well known national organisations. They are responsible
    for major decisions involving the group. The organisers are also responsible for the
    Association's membership, activities, functions and research.

    It is a policy of MARA not to have any fixed beliefs. MARA is not responsible for the
    opinion's expressed, or implied, by guest speakers, The Researcher or the by any of
    the Association's members.

    MARA is affiliated to the British UFO Research Association, Northern Anomalies
    Research Organisation, NLTf'ON, the Incorporated Society for Psychical Research,
    Halton Th1 UFO Factor, Merseyside Area Paranormal Society and works with the
    Association for Scientific Study of Anomalous Phenomena, Wirral Para..11ormaJ
    Investigations and the Wirral Saucer Group.

                                 The Voice
Tiris is my chance as editor to say a few words about the publication. It is basically a
voice for the 1\1.erseyside Anomalies Research Association. Of course, the magazine is
not just for MARA members but for those who have never heard of us as a group
and are interested in what is being reported from within Merseyside. .MARA has
become successful as a group, unlike some unfortunate groups, and we have been
able to become involved in some very interesting cases.·Also we are not a politically
orientated group, and that is an important thing to stress. This year has a number of
significant events happening such as the fiftieth year of modern ufology. We have
become witness to an era where the subjects of the paranormal have become part of
our culture, being found fashionable by youngsters, and great story          · by the
media. It has always been difficult to make the public aware of the complexities of
such a subject, especially when you cannot fit the answers of the paranormal debate
into small tidy boxes. Just so long as we, the researchers, do not get lost along the
way ( and some have, especially when enticed by the seduction of money ) then we
will have the opportunity to present our findings to the public, academic and
scientific circles.

It is unfortunate that a lot of the public will accept almost anything being written
in the glossy ''paranormal'' magazines and expensive videos instead of reading
genuine research, mostly available in book form. Yes, the business minded have
found the new niche of the nineties, the selling stalls of paranormal paraphernalia.
But I often ask myself, does possessing empty pockets actually buy you the
knowledge needed to learn about such a subject matter? With the multitude of
monthly literature and videos I am certain that people just cannot afford to buy
everything that comes out just to keep up to date. By judging the contents of these,
most of it seems to have very weak foundations when it comes to supportive
evidence or is made up, very little of it is actually written by genuine researchers.
Where there's a market, there's a way of telling a story that sells. To be honest such
business ideas has its good and bad points.

The UFO/ paranormal phenomenon has served the media by the production of
''good" stories to print or the background to television productions which have so
far failed to give an objective representation of anomalous phenomena. One
example is the "Strange But True'' live Debate, a cheap and comical event that was
the product of commercial television and not one concerned about the serious
research that goes on. A commercialism that has become successful where making
money is concerned. The effects of this can be seen in the types of letters .MARA
receives from the public where the majoriiy of letters reflect the American science
fiction C'..tlture that is loosely based on true lJFO/ paranormal incidents and that can
easily appeal to, and influence, the publics perception of such phenomenon. The
effects of this, I feel, can be reflected in the numbers of conferences organised this
year. This has been the Year of the Conference, and although it may have provided
an excellent opportunity to present a special event it has not happened to my
knowledge. Once people get too much of good thing then they get tired of it. This
year has seen a number of badly attended events. It is not necessarily the fault of the
organisers but the fault, it appears, of an over exploited phenomenon. You buy the
books, the magazines, the videos, car stickers, alien model kits, alien dolls and, oops,
you've no money left to buy an expensive ticket to go a conference. It's such a
shame because the idea of the conference is to listen to and meet the real
researchers who, at most times, do a lot of good work.

The 5th BUFORA Merseyside UFO Conference, organised and ran by BUFORA and
MARA members, was quite fortunate to have an average number of people turn up.
We had around a hundred and fifty people there on Father's Day! A success for us.
We had moved venue from a small room that encouraged people to sweat to a large
historic hall that encouraged people to freeze ( we'll get it right eventually ) .
Speakers included Margaret Fry, Stan Conway, Tim Matthews and Roger Ellison. An
experimental change of scene that was immersed in a background of structural
refurbishment, that was why there was no heating, and that was why no one could
fall asleep. All in all a good day was had by all, even if you did not possess a physics
degree!

The BUFORA International Conference held at Sheffield in August is an event I
normally enjoy going to. Lots of people, lots of stalls, lots of speakers and lots of long
lost friends. However, this one came with lots of trouble. Unfortunately certain
people became embroiled in a conflict turned personal because of unfortunate
politics. Take it from me, as someone who shared the organising of that event I was
disappointed because of what happened behind the scenes, and as a result the
conference failed to turn out the way it was originally planned the year before. The
conference itself consisted mainly of British speakers despite it being an
International Conference, the whys of which are not relevant to this publication but
it is matter needed to be seriously addressed by those responsible people within
BUFORA, if future conferences are to go ahead and become successful for the
promotion of UFO research and BUFORA. There were some good speakers there
such as Paul Devereux, Nik Redfern, Don & Vicky Ecker, Larry Warren, Peter
Robbins and Debbie Jordan, and new ones such as Brian Clarke the editor of
Enigma magazine and .:Man in Black' from "Funky Bunker''. The atmosphere was

involved ego struggles and a subject badly done to death by the media. To be fair all

fighting, the exploited subject a.nd the finances went against them. Hopefully next
year will prove differently, I have confidence in BUFORA to do just that.

                           To Bear W. itness
                             by Mark Glover (Sept. 1997)


The justification for the Witness Support Group concerns how anomalous
phenomena affect the witness psychologically. fundamentally it is a question of
necessity being the mother of invention, as it were. Due to the fact that .some
anomalous experiences invol\-~e traumatic events such as occur in the so called
''alien abduction'' type experience, and include what would basically constitute
physical assault or abuse if performed by human beings, it has become valuable for
both witnesses and · research/investigators as a facility for understanding and
coming to terms with the phenomenon and its effects.

Of course not all aspects of the anomalous or Extraordinary Human Experience
[EHE] (White, 1996) are necessarily negatively traumatic, nor need they be so in
order to justify their inclusion beneath the umbrella of a witness support group.
There are emotions and potentially physiological components of the EHE which may
be considered as being "positively traumatic''. How can something be positively
traumatic, one might hear people say? Well, this may involve for instance physical
and psychological stimuli (which can and do occur in the mundane daily lives of
individuals) having a invigorating effect on the individual as a whole. They may
call upon the individual to think more deeply about life and the significance of the
direction in which their lives are moving.

Some witnesses to frightening UFO experiences will say that the events of their
experiences have made them more courageous or confident in daily life. For
instance by encouraging them to increase their social circle or find work. The very
fact that EHEs initiate the need for some individuals who experience them to seek
out help and support in coming to terms with their experience, in itself represents a
widening or expansion of an individual's life. One member of the MARA WSG who
has undergone a close encounter of the fourth kind (CEIV) said that in life she had
always considered herself a coward, but going through her ordeal of an experience
has helped her become stronger as a person. As a married mother, she has had the
courage for instance to get a small part-time job and having to defend. her children
from what she considers is an intrusion (since they have also been effected by these
experiences) has taught her to respect herself as a valuable parent to those
children.                                                     ·

  The author here is not saying that the experiences, which the above and her family
  have had, are to be just accepted therefore and not challenged because they have
  produced this effect. Nor that the causal agent should hence be co:ri.sidered as
  necessarily non-malevolent, non-violating or non-abusive. What is being said is
  that the effects of EHEs can be to induce "healing" within the individual if they are
  dealt with in the correct way. It is all a question of perspective and attitude and the
  battle to understand - "why me!" This latter question has a lot to do with taking
  responsibility. Again the author is not implying resignation to the phenomenon but
  rather a sense of an individual rising to the occasion as it were and girding
  themselves up for a fight for their own self respect. It can therefore (because of
. this) be seen as a process of inner development or growth. Witnesses to these
  events often express a profound sense of powerlessness. "I can't control it (they), it
  (they) just comes and takes me over. I don't like that!'' or words to that effect are
  frequently voiced by witnesses and clearly demonstrate the need they have for some

                       A Pilgrim's Progress
                                  by Anthony Eccles

I received a case in 1994 from a local government officer who had experienced a
car crash. His injuries included a redamaged knee, shock and depression. I mention
this because, in my opinion, there is a paranormal link connected with this
incident. Up until that incident, the witness had always been a sensitive person ( I
have known him for fifteen years ) , artistic ( creative is a better word ) and one
who had suffered some abuse as a minor. He had, on occasion, a number of strange
experiences prior to a UFO sighting he was to have sometime after the crash.

Late one night, the witness was in bed with his back touching the wall behind him.
He felt something from behind and then felt movement upon the bed. The witness
describes the movement as a soft padding similar in nature to the movements of a
small cat. The hair on the back of his neck stood up and he instinctively felt that it
was a cat that he could feel. Sleep followed and he cannot recall any unusual
dreams.

Later, approximately one and a half years ago ( c.1992 ) the witness had a more
frightening experience. Lying in bed_on his back the witness observed something
like a whitish streak pass by his face, the witness heard laughter and believed that
'it' was trying to scare him. The witness shouted in reply "Oh f'** offi" Immediately,'
the witness could not move and felt something press onto his face, he felt
suffocated. He managed to get his hands to his face to push that 'something' off,
however, this happened again eight or nine times, the witness was still resisting.
The witness could not breathe and started to panic, he felt very frightened. Closing
his eyes he could see the image of an old woman's face and it had the likeness of a
varnished puppet face next to her. No body was seen with it. Opening his eyes, he
saw the woman and the puppet face but they slowly disappeared. The witness
started doubting whether he was asleep or awake his nose also began to feel as if it
had something shoved up against it. The witness was hot and sweaty, and when the
'presence' first appeared he felt that "it was there to test him". Earlier in the day,
the witness remembers talking about Chingle Hall ( a haunted hall near Goosnargh,
nr. Preston ) and mentioned that if he saw something very unusual he would have
been extremely curious about it.

A couple of weeks later, the same experience occurred for a second time. Again the
witness was in bed and lying on his side. It was late at night and he felt paralysed,
something similar to fists seemed to be hammering at his body. The witness could
not speak and managed to shout "get offi" the experience then stopped. This
experience was not as frightening as the previous one, he was unable to determine
what was happening and also if the 'presence' was going to harm him. Witness
very much believed that the 'entity' experienced on both occasions was the same
one. The witness remembers thinking "not again!"

So what do I make of these experiences. Well I do not believe that he was abducted,
that, I am certain of. It resembles more sleep paralysis. His encounters with the
white 'entity' appears very similar to the Old Hag Syndrome experienced by people
in Scandinavia. People have seen a figure resembling an old witch who cackles and
laughs at them and often screams curses. This may be a hallucinogenic part of sleep
paralysis. Firstly, the witness is always lying down in bed. At a time when the body

preventing the body from sleep walking and endangering itself. The witness

natural actions can be quite impossible and very exhausting. It may also reflect the
fact that the witness was almost strangled at birth by his own umbilical cord which
was tightly wrapped around his throat at birth. Therefore, early experiences,
defensive experiences might be present and overworking themselves to protect the
mind and body for survival.

So here we have the beginnings of some kind of instinctive mind heal and the
forthcoming account of a signal, a stimulated signal, one that encourages the mind
to survive and heal itself. An idea I had shared with the late Ken Philips and one
which he liked the idea of. So what brings me to this conclusion? And what of the
paranormal element to the case? Well, what is demonstrated is the receptive
conditioning already illustrated by the witness, from birth trauma to a reaction to
childhood and growing up which has made the witness an increasingly sensitive
person, something common to the majority of Close Encounter witnesses.

The car crash occurs in the early part of 1994. Two of them were in the car when
another driver carelessly drove the witness's car spinning into the other side of the
road. Both were not seriously hurt, the driver was fine but the -witness sustained a
knee injury ( the same knee that needed serious surgery some months before ) ,
shock and the forthcoming depression. The incident made the witness go through
what Mark Glover and I call ''The Pilgrim's Progress" Evolutionary Process from the
time of the experienced crash to the stimulated signal of the sighting and the
witness's full recovery. Furthermore, the witness could not get back into the car and
drive, he had developed a fear of driving in case he was going to crash again. Not
only that, but he was unable to go back to work, a place which was busy with
individuals who were not as educated as the witness. The witness did not feel like
he fitted in, he was isolated in his working environment ( he had lots of friends )
but he was not happy at work, it just did not feel right even though he had been
there for a number of years, he possessed status inconsistencv ( a common sign
amongst receptive individuals Ken Philips had disc_overed from the results of the
Anamnesis Project ) .

The process of self healing begins immediately proceeding the car crash when the
trauma affects the witness with new fears and depression. The witness sees a doctor
and receives two types of treatment, physio-therapy for his knee and prozac for his
depression, the witness does not receive counseliing. During this treatment, the
witness remained off work and refused to do any driving. Once his knee began to
improve he started Tai-chi which not only helped him physically but also improved
his confidence and probably regained him a sense of self worth ( being a very
sensitive person the accident may have questioned his own confidence ) .

Then on the 3rd August 1994 something happened which marked a complete
change to the witness's life. Just after midnight, the witness took his dog for a walk,
something which was done on a regular basis on a regular route twice a day. The
route led out of the witnesses road and finally onto the corner of Mostyn Avenue
and Ramsey Road in Allerton. The witness just happened to instinctually look up
( even though the witness did that every night as he enjoyed looking at the night
sky ) and there somewhat low above the roof of a house ( about 30 - 40' ) was a
stationary pulsati..ng red ball of light. He had not seen anything like it before and it
had made no noise. He noticed that the ball had pulsated from right to left of its

Since this is the first issue of MARA's magazine, I thought it would be a good idea to
briefly go over some of the paranormal cases that we have investigated. By far the

Research's 'Spontaneous Cases Committee'. Among the more unusual phenomena
reported by the family concerned were disembodied threatening voices, the 'ghost'
of a young girl wearing a cotton night-dress, maniacal laughter coming from empty
rooms and instances of physical violence.

John L. Hall and myself conducted the initial interview with the mother and the
daughter who were absolutely horrified by what was taki.n,g place in their home.
During these tape recorded interviews taken in the living room, noises were
recorded which some have compared to the sound of a horse walking on

                 Fighting the Secrecy War
                          by William Bimson ( Sept. 1997 )



Introduction

The purpose of this document is two fold. Firstly to inform people of the power of
information withdrawal and secondly to prevent the powers that be from using this
technique to keep us in the dark about information which is important to us all but
which they arrogantly believe we have no right to know about.

SECRECY TECHNIQUES

"Information withdrawal

Information withdrawal is simply the withholding of irtfonnatiort or secret
classification of information by authorities in order to keep that information from
the general public. This can be done by a number of means. -When the information
has not entered the public domain, it can be withheld under the official secrets act
in the UK. You may believe that this act only covers military personnel but in fact it
covers a .great number of government employees including all civil servants.
Therefore, somebody who works in the department of social security who's job is
mainly giving out milk vouchers to the needy, could be forced to keep silent about
the 20 foot diameter glowing disk which landed in their back garden the previous
evening. Threats of loss of job with no future prospect of being employed by any
government department would be perfectly legal. This would be an obvious abuse
of power as the official secrets act in this ~ is designe<!~~t the anonymity
of pe0ple who claim welfare benefits.

There may be good military reasons why the Ministry of Defence (MoD) would not
want the ,general public to know if lJK air space was penetrated by a potential
enemy. For example the MoD may not want the potential enemy to know that they
were detected by radar or other means and if the general public were alerted, then
the enemy would also acquire this knowledge. Hence all radar operators are quite
rightly covered by the official secrets act. However, if the detected phenomenon is
obviously something which can fly at speeds which are way beyond the means of
any conventional aircraft, it is probably safe to assume that its military capabilities
(if any) are far in excess of our own. The phenomenon is therefore not a threat at
all, or so great a threat that we should be given the chance to say our last prayers to
what ever gods we worship. I believe phenomena which fall into this class should
not be covered by the official secrets act.

Information withdrawal after exposure to the public domain.

There are a number of methods which can be used to withdraw information from
the public domain even after it has been shown in mainstream media. The methods
used fall into the categories of absolute denial, persuasion by government agents
that the information would compromise national security, personal threats, bribes
and misinformation.
                                         20



Absolute denial

Government agencies and the military use this technique to throw Ufologists of the
scent of an incident. They can get away with it because most people do not believe
that responsible people would lie about such a major event. Their strong credibility
is pitted against that of the "cranky" Ufologist who is perceived as a sort of cosmic
train spotter with a vivid imagination. In the general publics eye, the government
will always win unless absolute proof is retained by the Ufologist or witness.
Absolute denial in its most extreme case has been taken to the point where by the
government of the USA did not acknowledge that one of its own citizens had served
in the USAF despite the fact that he was stationed in England for a large part of his
service guarding top secret installations ( 1).

Persuasion

After a witness has gone public with a UFO sighting, the witness is often harassed
by the so called Men in Black (MIB). These are people who call at a witnesses home
to try and persuade them that it is in either the witnesses own best interest or their
countries best interest if they withdraw their statement about the sighting or claim
that it was a hoax. They are called Men in Black because they usually wear dark
suits and often arrive in a black car. They will sometimes show identification cards
with Ministry of Defence or some other agency written on them, but when the
MoD are contacted about them they deny that they ever sent any one to the witness.
Some witnesses have stated that the MIB have almost supernatural powers of
hypnosis and the ability to move very quickly from place to place without being
seen or heard. Some have suggested that the MIB are actually aliens who are trying
to hide information on operations which were not meant to be seen by human eyes.
There manner of persuasion will often take a threatening tone and has been known
to have terrified witnesses into agreement with there demands. other witnesses
claim to have been physically assaulted by people they believe to be MlB (2).

Bribes

It has been stated by some witnesses in other countries that they have been offered
bribes of money and property to keep quiet about UFO sittings. This works best on
witnesses who are short of money. The bribe is used in conjunction with other
techniques such as personal threats or ridicule from the general public because no
one would believe them any way.

Misinformation

This is indeed a potent weapon in the armoury of secrecy and one which is
surprisingly easy to implement. Probably the best example of this is in the Roswell
incident of 194 7 when a crashed UFO from which wreckage and possibly alien
bodies had been recovered was reported to the general public by the media. The
next day the USAF showed the public what they said was the wreckage and that it
was simply a crashed weather balloon with an aluminium radar target. The official
story....from the military constantly changed to hide further evidence and indeed
changed again quite recently when they claimed that the supposed alien bodies
where probably dumwies used on test parachutes which were used in that area at
that time (3). However, the information about the dummies was not available at
the time of the crash, and has probably been thrown in recently as further
misinformation because of the renewed interest in the incident due to the recent
fiftieth anniversary of the crash.

Theft of evidence

All of the above secrecy techniques are easy to implement when the only evidence is
simply an eye witness account. When other evidence is available such as video or
photographic evidence, it becomes more difficult for the various agencies whoever
they may be to perform a cover up. Their main priority then switches to one of
destruction or theft of evidence. This is usually accomplished by someone asking the
witness if they can borrow the evidence to authenticate it, i.e. make sure it is not a
hoax. The witness is usually glad to comply with the request because they believe
that they will finally have the evidence validated by an official agency.
Unfortunately, this does not happen. The evidence usually goes missing with some
or all of the usual secrecy techniques employed to discredit the witness.
Occasionally, a copy of the evidence is returned to the witness which is of such
poor quality that it is useless. Crystal clear photographs of aerial phenomena come
back as fuzzy blobs.

FIGHTING THE SECRECY WAR

It is obvious from the thousands of publications and eye witness accounts that a
secrecy war is being waged upon those who seek the truth, and the powers that be
are winning this war. The winners should come as no surprise when you consider
that on one side you have highly efficient, well trained and well funded
government agencies and on the other side we have an ill informed patchwork of
part time volunteers with little equipment and practically no funding. For any
Monty Python fans out there, the various UFO groups remind me of the Peoples
front of Judea, the Judean Peoples front and the Judean Popular Peoples Front.
Great news for the Romans. In order to have an effective policy which will at least
win some of the battles, we must recognise the secrecy techniques and implement
preventative measures to stop them being used. I have outlined some ideas for
preventative measures below.

Prevention of theft or loss of evidence.        -- - - ----   ·- ----- ----   - - ----   - -
Whenever a witness is contacted about a sighting they should be immediately
warned of the danger of theft. They should then be asked to keep any original
photographs negatives or video footage with them at all times until 10 good quality

group should agree to fund the reproduction of the copies. These should then be
quickly distributed to other members of the group and other research groups

agency to retrieve all of the copies. After initial analysis by the research groups to
make sure that the sighting is not something mundane or a hoax, the footage

the witness anonymous if they have requested this.

Prevention of misinformation.

the case of absolute denial, the answer would certainly be no and if this question

Prevention of bribery

This is almost impossible to stop. How would you convince someone who is short of

the incident. One should therefore never totally give up on a witness who has been
bribed.

sending other family members to a different part of the household by hypnotic

               Mystery of April UFOs sighted over
                Liverpool's Anglican Cathedral
                            bv Anthony Eccles and Susan McAllister
        This case was a joint investigation conducted by MARA and the British UFO
        Research Association. It came to the attention of the investigators atthe beginning
        of May. The event took place during the Wednesday afternoon of the 30th April and
        consisted of the sighting of two different objects. The first appeared at 12:55GMT
        and the second one at 13:05GMT respectively.

        A family of three had decided to go for a day out, as Wednesday was a beautiful
        sunny day. They had decided to relax in the large sunken grounds of St. James's
        cemetery, situated to the east of the Gothic style sandstone Anglican Cathedral. The
        area is filled with grass, bushes, trees and few surviving mausoleums and burial
        stones. The family were facing the awesome towering cathedral, behind them was a
        stone carved wall that rises to Hope Street.

        At approximately 12:55 a black oval object appeared from behind the cathedral. It
        seemed to be moving in a straight line from the north to the south until it had
        reached a point behind the cathedral when its course had changed and it headed
        off to the east. However, its reported motion was strange enough indeed, it
        appeared to be ''flipping over on itself'' very slowly like a coin if tossed into the air.
        The object passed directly over the heads of the witnesses who state that they had
        seen no wings, it did not produce ·any sound and it left no trail. The sighting lasted
        for only a five minutes.

        Fifteen minutes later, another object came into view, apparently moving in the same
        direction as the first object. However, this object appeared to be a very brightly lit
        cigar shaped object. Seen clearly as it passed above them and disappearing out of
        view to the east. Again the object first appeared from the north going south and
        suddenly turned to the east. It was observed for about six minutes. The witnesses
        had contacted Llverpool Airport and were asked to report their sighting to RAF West
        Drayton. The investigation found the witnesses to be sincere but it was also
        discovered that they had both experienced other paranormal phenomenon on
        previous occasions. The investigators feel that the witnesses are both receptive to
        various phenomena.

        :Enquiries to Llverpool Airport resulted with being advised to contact AIS (military)
        LATCC in accordance with current ATC regulations. The airport was able to
        confirm that they had not have any records regarding unusual aerial phenomena
        for that day. The Company Secretary of the National Air Traffic Services, due to an
        enquiry sent to the CAA, informed the investigators that although cargo aircraft
        were in the skies throughout the day none matched any specific radar track with
        the information provided to them. This further rules out a dirigible of any kind. RAF
        Valley confirmed that no military aircraft were in the area on that day. Weather
        details concluded that it was a hot and dry day. It is not possible to ascertain
        whether meteorological conditions could have caused an i11version or mirage. It is
        possible that a large helium balloon may have been responsible for the first sighting
        and that the two incidences are not connected. The case is presently unidentified
        due only to insufficient data.

         No. 1 St. Andrew's Presbyterian Church, Rodney Street.


For the first issue we are going to present a location that has been written about
many times by a variety of researchers. The church is being presently renovated by
John Moores University, rather than remaining a deteriorated ruin it is now being
given a new welcome leash of life as a fully functional academic building.

The church is called St. Andrew's Presbyteria...'11. Church, and it is situated on Rodney
Street. It was built in 1823-4 to the design of John Foster Jr. The body of the church
is a simple two storey design with arched windows and stuccoed walls, but the
stone facade is composed of massive Ionic columns flanked by the corner towers,
which are individually crowned with smaller Corinthian columns and domes. In
the churchyard to the north is the smaller Sunday School of 1872, its round arched
windows and classical details echoing the style of the church.

Within the cemetery stands an odd looking monument, known as McKenzie's Tomb.
He was Scottish and a wealthy promoter of the early railways, and a supporter of
science, but he was also an inept poker and a compulsive gambler. He had left
instructions for his burial. A large pyramidal tomb was built and his body was
embalmed and entombed within the structure seated at a poker table clutching a
winning hand of cards. The site is renowned for a number of hauntings, whether
they are caused by the restless spirit of McKenzie it is uncertain, but Rodney Street
has been the focus for a number of unusual sightings.
