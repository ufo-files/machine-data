         nablator wrote:Hoaxers are a lazy bunch.



  Not all of them. Some are rather skilled and willing to put a lot of time and effort into their hoaxes/artwork (e.g. some of
  the crop circle hoaxers).

  The really lazy hoaxers are almost as much of an insult to harder working hoaxers as they are to ufologists.

  In terms of recycling of hoaxes, more basic than the examples given above are occasions where a hoaxer has ONLY
  changed the title and date of an existing video and then gets publicity for a "new" video.

file:///D|/...ed%20forum/forum/viewtopic_php%253ff%253d14%2526p%253d38025%2526sid%253dd86280f8ee57c2a8e818f4f6fe04e7df.html[08/10/2021 20:56:14]
  Newly uploaded videos with a very recent date can get publicity as "new" videos, even if the same video has been
  online for YEARS with a different title and date.

  Because I find such lazy hoaxing rather, well, offensive, I've spent a bit of time over the years finding and exposing
  such examples.

  I thought it worth adding a couple of examples to this thread.

  For example, on the thread at the link below in 2009, I pointed out that a video associated with local media reports in
  England of a video accompanied by text such as this on newspaper websites: "Jamie Gardner, from Wolverhampton,
  said he captured a video recording of a triangular-shaped UFO floating over the Black Country shopping centre while he
  was walking to a friendâ€™s house near Netherton hill" had in fact been online for YEARS as a video filmed over
  Paris:
  http://www.abovetopsecret.com/forum/thread512535/pg1




  Similarly, press reports in 2010 of a video showing a UFO travelling at "mind-boggling speed near the River Liffey in
  Dublin" were in fact of a video that had previously been online with a completely different story attached to it (stating it
  was filmed above Fremont, California in 2008):
  http://www.abovetopsecret.com/forum/thread537284/pg1
