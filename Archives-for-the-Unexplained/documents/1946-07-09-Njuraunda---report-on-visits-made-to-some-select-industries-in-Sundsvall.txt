A report on visits made to some select industries in Sundsvall on August 3, 1946

The purpose of these visits was to confirm that the yellowish porous material, which was found in Njurunda, wasn’t related to any industries in Sundsvall.
I consulted multiple experts and went back and forth about the most logical explanation. Visits were made to research director Dr. Tydén at the Cellulose Ltd Research Laboratory, chief engineer Eidem at the sulphite factory in Svartvik, chief engineer Eidem and engineer Flodén, and chief engineer Fürst at the Stockvik factory.

Results: The substance is not derived from any of these factories. According to Dr. Tydén it couldn't possibly have originated from any cellulose factory, for that matter.
However, according to chief engineer Fürst, it has a certain similarity with so-called "cauliflower formations (in particular regarding the smell) that occur in the production of neoprene rubber at the factories in Stockvik. This substance is explosive.
I also sought contact with an airline pilot by the name of Lindén, who originally saw the impact and discovered the porous material, but he was unavailable on the 3rd on 4th of August.

Stockholm August 5, 1946

Eric Malmberg
Fli 3
