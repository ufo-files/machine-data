             As described in a recent issue of Life Magazine, most animals have
a mysterious ability to regulate their activities by means of some physiolog­
ical process which enables them to tell time. For example, turtles and pigeons
apparently get their directional fix by watching the position of the sun or
stars; but, as the sun and stars appear to be moving in the sky as the earth
turns, these animals must have some way of knowing how much the earth has
turned, Humans have chronometers to do this, Animals use mysterious biological
clocks which are almost as accurate!
             The most amazing thing of all is that these animal "clocks11 go on
functioning even when the animals are put into laboratories, under conditions
of controlled light and temperature, where they are hidden from all the obvi­
ous clues of the passage of time. For example, a certain breed of crabs habit­
ually hides in marshes near the sea at high tide, and runs out at low tide to
feed on the flats exposed by the receding waters. Every day, low tide comes 50
minutes later than the day before, and the peak of crab activity is corre­
spondingly later, In the laboratory, these crabs follow exactly the same pat­
tern as if they were still living in their natural habitat. Also, the crabs
living outdoors darken their skin as a protection against sunburn. The crabs
in the laboratory, who see no sun, darken their skins in the same way at the
same time!
             Scientists have found that under constant laboratory conditions,
animal clocks do not run exactly on a 24-hour day, but always very close to
it. In some animals, as many as 20 different processes have been studied, each
with an approximately 24-hour cycle, The observations made have be en very pre­
cise, but scientists are unable to agree on the cause of these cyclical
rhythms. One theory, advanced by Dr, Frank Brown of Northwestern University,
is that animals depend on some outside source of information such as changes
in barometric pressure, or in the earth's magnetic field, or in the intensity
of cosmic radiation. The alternate theory, advanced by Dr. Colin Pittendrigh
and a team of researchers at Princeton University, is that the clock is an in­
ternal self-contained timer, rather than a forced response to geophysical
forces, In this view, the clock is a property of protoplasm itself. The proto­
plasmic clock evolved with a period close to that of the tides or the sunrise
simply because this was an advantage to the life of the animal.
             Wbatever its cause, the importance of this biological clock is in­
disputable, The United States Navy and Air Foree, which helped to finance re­
search into the riddle, are interested because they would like to bring the
simplicity and precision of the animal timers to the bulky, complex machines
which guide their ships and planes around the world, Doctors are also inter­
ested, because health is apparently related to the proper functioning of the
internal clocks, It has been found, for instance, that chick embryos survive
more operations at one time of day than another, It has also been learned that
certain toxic drugs help mice when administered at one time of day, and kill
them when administered at another, This indicates that with further research,
Man1 s health can be improved by a better understanding of the mysterious in­
ternal clock which we too have within our bodies.
