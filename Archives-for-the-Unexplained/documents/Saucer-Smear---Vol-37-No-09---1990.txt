MISCELLANEOUS RAVINGS:

        We seldom publish book reviews (as we seldom read books!), but this
is a slow month, so we do want to tell you a little about a wonderful new
tome called "UFOs in the 1980s, Volume One", written and edited mainly by
Jerome Clark. Clark, as most of you know, is the former editor of FATE Mag­
azine and currently is vice president of CUFOS, thus making him a powerful
farce in the UFO field.
        Of the nearly one hundred entries to be found in Volume One, our fav­
orite, of course, is the one for "Saucer Smear" (Page 195), which we quote
in part: ". .. There probably was a time when James W. Moseley, the editor,
was interested in actual UFO reports and theories, but that was not recent­
ly. . . 'Smear' is. . . far more interested in the personal foibles of UFO buffs
and debunkers, sparing nobody.       Although hardly anyone will admit it, 'Smear'
is just about the only UFO bulletin everybody reads from cover to cover."
        Way to go, Jerry! We look forward to Volume Two, which, we are told,
will contain various entries pertaining to your humble "Smear" editor's ufo­
logical activities over the years.       There will also be a Volume Three even­
tually.
        We have not read the whole book (naturally!), but we were amused to
see the entry for "UNICAT Project" (Page 211), which of course is the pet
UFO investigative effort of Dr. Willy Smith.       Quoting directly from a pro­
fessional-level article published in 1989 in the International UFO Reporter,
Clark states: ". . .Unless UNICAT adopts a more representative sampling pro­
cedure;- the existing method of selecting cases introduces all kinds of bias
which distorts or masks real quantitative variations between explained and
unexplained events.       Even if UNICAT was adopting a random sampling procedure,
the method by which UFO reports come to the attention of ufologists        still
introduces statistical bias into the UNICAT database which at present we are
unable to evaluate.       For this reason alone any current database of UFO re­
ports cannot be used to infer characteristics of UFO reports in gen·eral, let
alone one that introduces all manner of additional bias into the        sampling
pro_cedure."
        The amusing aspect here is that Clark has recently been harrassed by
a series of very disagreeable letters from Smith, which understandably may
have caused a bias of his own. Clark is not the only ufologist who         has
clashed with the temperamental Smith in recent months.
        "UFo·s in the 1980s" is published by Omnigraphics, Inc. , Penobscot
Building, Detroit, Michigan 48226. Although no price is· given in the book,
it is rumored to cost sixty five dollars for Volume One.       (We received a
free review copy, fortunately.) Alas, wisdom does not come cheaply!.. . .
