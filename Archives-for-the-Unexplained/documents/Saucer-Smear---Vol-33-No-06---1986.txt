LETTER (S) ABOUT THE EDITOR:

      The following is the full text of a recent issue of a delightful
publicly distributed newsletter from an organization called ParaNet, also
known as the National Fringe Sciences Information Service.  (We kid you
not!) More about ParaNet further along in this issue:

      "Ufologist Erik Beckjord seems to have gotten a little carried away
with himself. At the recent National UFO Conference in Phoenix, Beckjord
gave a presentation on the New Zealand UFO which was photographed by news
reporters aboard a plane in 1979. The talk, which was based on research
done by Dr. Bruce Maccabee, included the film segments of what appears to
be, well, an indiscernable blob of light. Mega-skeptic Phil Klass opines
that what is depicted is a brightly-lit squid boat on the horizon at night
time. Fellow CSICOPer Robert Sheaffer has maintained that it is a fleet
of squid boats that optically coalesce into one, due to their distance and
tight formation. Beckjord's presentation tried to show that the object
was a bona fide unknown by deflating the squid boat theory. In this re­
porter's opinion, he failed miserably.
      "Regardless, in the latest issue of 'Saucer Smear', a tongue-in- cheek
newsletter put out monthly (sic) by convention chairman James Moseley,Beck­
jord writes: '. .. My lecture on the New Zealand UFO film.. . managed to ac­
hieve... the downfall of Philip Klass. Fellow Skeptic Robert Sheaffer open­
ly admitted that he felt Klass was proved wrong by Maccabee, and this was
seconded by the head of the Tucson Skeptics (Maj. James McGaha) . '
      "Well, Erik, not quite. Sheaffer never said Maccabee had proven the
squid boat theory wrong. He has always differed with Klass on the number
of squid boats sighted, but still clings to the basic hypothesis.        The
closest he and Beckjord ever came to agreeing at the conference was when
he was observed agreeing to let Beckjord buy him a beer, the bottle       of
which he later used as a prop in an impromptu demonstration of nighttime
optical illusions.
      "As for McGaha, no one was more surprised than he at his sudden con­
version to the UFO cause. 'If that's the best piece of (photographic evi­
dence) they can come up with, they're in trouble', he later told me. 'I
wasn't impressed.'
      "Lest anyone think that 'Smear' editor Moseley endorses Beckjord's
view, it is important to note that fully half the newsletter consists of
letters to the editor, some of which are published with wry comments from
Moseley. It seems to function as comic relief in an industry that takes
itself much too seriously; a release valve for some of the more       far-out
opinions. Moseley himself, while not quite a .skeptic, has his feet plant­
ed firmly on the ground and has slowly come to disdain the fringe elements
of the UFO 'religion'. He seems to be one of the few prominent in the
field to openly admit, 'I just don't know'.
      "Moseley is possessed of a dry, dangerous sense of humor. At the con­
vention, while he was seated at the dias with several of his colleagues, a
woman clad in the flowing white garb of the truly demented approached the
microphone and began reliving her birth trauma before our eyes. Claiming
to be a high priestess of the Brotherhood of St. Germaine, she singled
out Ufologist Stanton Friedman, a nuclear physicist, as an apostate       of
Atlantis who had single-handedly unleashed the horrors of Chernobyl on
an unsuspecting world. With tears in her eyes she pleaded with us to re­
pent and join the brotherhood before it was too late. Moseley's response
was to photograph her with a cheap camera in mid- spiel. The sight of this
poor woman desperately trying to save our souls while being photographed
with a toy camera       well, you had to be there.
