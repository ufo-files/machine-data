Persuading believers - What works?


  Subject: Persuading believers - What works?
  From: boospam@aol.com (Boospam)
  Date: 20/02/2004, 12:20
  Newsgroups: sci.skeptic

  I've been corresponding here for a few weeks, after an absense from message
  boards of several years. I'm a skeptic, and an atheist, and a Bhuddist,
  incidentally. I don't believe in any paranormal phenomena, and I don't believe
  in any divine intervention in the form of miracles, Fate, etc.
  It's interesting to note that in the more than 20 years that I have, on and
  off, been participating in message boards related to esp and religion that the
  arguments are pretty much still the same. I'm interested in finding out what
  people have found might actually be persuasive arguments that might
  occaisionally win people over to "our" side of the debate.
  I'll share some of my thoughts on the subject to get things started.
  First, note that I am talking about "persuasive" arguments, not logical ones.
  It doesn't matter how brilliant you are, or think you are.    If the other guy
  doesn't accept your argument, it is unpersuasive. Being persuasive is a lot
  more difficult than being right.
  A worthwhile observation is that most people are either religious, or they
  believe in some sort of psychic powers.   These people clearly have not been
  persuaded, which means that no argument will be persuasive all of the time. Do
  not think that these people who disagree with you have simply never been
  exposed to your brilliance. No argument will be persuasive all of the time.
  The audience has to be willing to listen and consider the possibility that the
  argument might be correct.
  So, to be persuasive, the first thing that has to happen is that you have to
  get into the frame of mind of the target audience, and see what might open up
  his mind. Likewise you must avoid anything that will cause him to shut off his
  mind. The most common mistake would be in assuming that all believers are
  deluded, stupid, uneducated, or insane. First of all, they aren't. Lots of
  normal, intelligent people who manage to lead contented and effective lives
  disagree with you about this stuff. To be persuasive, you can't inform them
  that they are all idiots.
  In the case of psychic phenomena, one line of reasoning that can work is to
  treat this as a legitimate area of scientific investigation, and discuss how
  one would go about testing for such things. Randi once marketed a "test your
  psychic powers" kit, with instructions on how to conduct a proper test of
  psychic abilities. I know that in my youth, I believed in esp, and
  occaisionally tried to find my abilities. Repeated experiments with negative
  results were part of what persuaded me that maybe this was all a bunch of
  nonsense.
  Another thing that will not persuade, along those lines, is any assertion that
  begins with "There is no evidence...." Hogwash! There is tons of evidence.
  From historical accounts to personal experience, to media presentations, the
  world is teeming with accounts of paranormal activity. Your job is to show
  that there is an alternative explanation for all of this evidence. Saying
  "there is no evidence" or worse, "your experience is not evidence", is a
  non-starter. It is both illogical and unpersuasive, because it puts the
  listener in a frame of mind where you have denied his validity, and shown
  yourself unwilling to listen. The evidence, by which I mean all of the
  accounts of paranormal activity, is your experimental data. You have to
  provide an explanation for it. You can't simply deny its existence.
  The "Randi has a million" argument can be persuasive, although it can also be a
  turnoff. "Real psychics" you will be told, will not be interested in material
  wealth. This is odd, since so many of them charge fees, but you will still be
  told that. Remember, this isn't about logic, it's about persuasion. To be
  effective, you have to show that greed is not the only motivator that could
  cause someone to accept the Randi challenge, and then note that still, no one
  captures the prize. Of course, few people will be persuaded, because they will
  insist that somehow Randi will cheat, or not let your data in. That's a hard
  nut to crack.
  If the topic is religious, you have to get inside the religion to be
  persuasive. I mentioned I'm a Bhuddist. Most Bhuddists believe in
  reincarnation, but if someone were trying to convince me that reincarnation was
  real, I would discuss what the Bhudda actually said about reincarnation. In

  On the subject of evolution, I would begin with "The heavens are telling the
