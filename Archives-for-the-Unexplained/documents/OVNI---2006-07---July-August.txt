                                Mysterious red cells might be aliens
                                                By Jebediah Reed


Scientists have yet to identify these unusual red particles. As bizarre as it may seem, the sample jars
brimming with cloudy, reddish rainwater in Godfrey Louis's laboratory in southern India may hold, well
aliens.


In April, Louis, a solid-state physicist at Mahatma Gandhi University, published a paper in the prestigious
peer-reviewed journal Astrophysics and Space Science in which he hypothesizes that the samples -- water
taken from the mysterious blood-coloured showers that fell sporadically across Louis's home state of
Kerala in the summer of 2001 -- contain microbes from outer space.



Specifically, Louis has isolated strange, thick-walled, red-tinted cell-like structures about 10 microns in
size. Stranger still, dozens of his experiments suggest that the particles may lack DNA yet still
reproduce plentifully, even in water superheated to nearly 600 degrees Fahrenheit . (The known upper
limit for life in water is about 250 degrees Fahrenheit . )
So how to explain them? Louis speculates that the particles could be extraterrestrial bacteria adapted to
the harsh conditions of space and that the microbes hitched a ride on a comet or meteorite that later
broke apart in the upper atmosphere and mixed with rain clouds above India.

If his theory proves correct, the cells would be the first confirmed evidence of alien life and, as such,
could yield tantalizing new clues to the origins of life on Earth.


Last winter, Louis sent some of his samples to astronomer Chandra Wickramasinghe and his colleagues at
Cardiff University in Wales, who are now attempting to replicate his experiments; Wickramasinghe
expects to publish his initial findings later this year.
Meanwhile, more down-to-earth theories abound. One Indian government investigation conducted in
2001 lays blame for what some have called the "blood rains" on algae. Other theories have implicated
fungal spores, red dust swept up from the Arabian peninsula, even a fine mist of blood cells produced by
a meteor striking a high-flying flock of bats. Louis and his colleagues dismiss all these theories, pointing
to the fact that both algae and fungus possess DNA and that blood cells have thin walls and die quickly
when exposed to water and air. More important, they argue, blood cells don't replicate. "We've already

"If it's true that life was introduced by comets four billion years ago," the astronomer says, "one would
expect that microorganisms are still injected into our environment from time to time. This could be one
of those events."


The next significant step, explains University of Sheffield microbiologist Milton Wainwright, who is part
of another British team now studying Louis's samples, is to confirm whether the cells truly lack
DNA. So far, one preliminary DNA test has come back positive. "Life as we know it must contain DNA,
or it's not life," he says. "But even if this organism proves to be an anomaly, the absence of DNA
wouldn't necessarily mean it's extraterrestrial."


Louis and Wickramasinghe are planning further experiments to test the
cells for specific carbon isotopes. If the results fall outside the
norms for life on Earth, it would be powerful new evidence for Louis's
idea, of which even Louis himself remains skeptical.

Right smack in the middle of summer the Earth is actually at its furthest distance from the Sun for the
whole year. On July 4, at precisely 00:08 the Earth will be at a distance of ninety-four and-a- half
million miles from the Sun (aphelion).

On July 20, the Sun moves from the star constellation of Gemini and crosses the border into Cancer.
Throughout July, from around midnight, the star constellations of Sagittarius, Aquila, Lyra and Cygnus
are visible in the southern sky.

Mercury will not be visible during July, as it lies too close to the Sun. However, the planet Venus as a
'morning star' becomes more favourable during July as the angle of the ecliptic with the horizon
increases, so for most of the .month it will be rising about two hours before the Sun. On the morning of
July 23, a thin waning crescent Moon joins Venus in the morning sky producing a mystical conjunction
in the twilight of dawn.

The ecliptic is the apparent path of the Sun's annual motion among the fixed stars when viewed from
Earth. The zodiac is an imaginary band nine degrees either side of the ecliptic which form's the
background to the motions of the Moon and planets.

Mars is still an 'evening star' travelling eastwards through the constellation of Cancer into Leo.
Unfortunately, the low angle of the ecliptic in the western sky conspires with evening twilight and the
faintness of Mars, to render observation of the planet very difficult.

Jupiter is the only other visible planet in the evening sky. Jupiter's retrograde motion against the stars on
the Libra I Virgo border ends on July 6, after which Jupiter begins to move eastwards again. Saturn is
lost in twilight during July and will not be seen.

Full Moon occurs on the morning of July 11, very low down in the constellation of Sagittarius. The
minor meteor shower Delta Aquarids takes place during this month peaking on July 26, when around 10
shooting stars per hour may be seen.

Even though we do not have many hours of darkness at this time of year we can observe the sky at any
time. There is always something interesting to see: changing cloud formations, colourful twilights, the
Sun rising and setting. The sky offers us one of the greatest shows on Earth, is free, available all of the
time, can lift our spirits and makes us forget all about our Earthly troubles.

At this time, all night observation sessions are wonderful after a late evening barbeque. Lie on a sun-bed
overnight and let the magic of the night sky drift by overhead. Remember to use insect repellent unless
you enjoy being eaten alive by midges.

If you have access to a pool, lie in the shallow end intermittently to cool down. Do join me any night
this month and experience the enchantment and Zen-like calmness of the patterns, rhythms, circles and
cycles of the season, nature and the cosmos.
