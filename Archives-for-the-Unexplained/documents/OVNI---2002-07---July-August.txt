                    A UFO INCIDENT NEAR BUXTON 23-6-2002

9.30pm on the 23rd June this year.

Mr Hardwick was driving in his car, leaving Buxton via the Market Place and proceeding towards
Ashbourne. He stopped at the A515 traffic lights junction, crossing the A6.

light appeared to the SW over the village of Harper Hill. It was a brilliant, glittering golden light - the

edges. The light was not white, but it was a brilliant glittering gold in colour. The whole thing glowed! It
was stationary for a while and then it moved a little way to the S.E."

Mr Hardwick continued "The clouds were thick, black and low. The object had stopped briefly, then
ascended vertical at great speed into the black clouds. The incident must have lasted 30 seconds."

When asked about his opinion as to why the object did not have a natural explanation or was not thought
to be man made, Mr Hardwick replied: "The size and the glittering mass. The main feature was the great
light of glittering golden colour. The whole mass of the object was illuminated. It was spectacular for a
brief time."

On the7th June this year, we received a phone call from a farmer who told us that he had seen a 200 metre
trail of cut-off corn plants (in his field). He also told us that there were no footprints whatsoever.

Once we arrived at the field, we saw that the farmer was right, there were absolutely no footprints
whatever, except for the footprints of the farmer himself He had walked some distance away from the trail
as he didn't want to damage the (cut-off) trail for our research.

One strange detail was the fact that it looked as though the (cut-off) trail had stayed wetter than the rest of
the field. it had rained the previous night, but the sun was .shining now and the field was starting to dry, but
the (cut-oft) trail was still wet.

Most parts (cut-off tops) of the plants were next to the plants (in the ground), but in some cases we
couldn't find the cut-off pieces. There were also some cut-off tops that were standing in the so il next to the
cut-off plants (in the ground). It looked as if someone had put them into the ground.

Another strange fact was that some of the cut-off parts were almost dried up. It looks as if these parts were
cut off some days before, because the fanner told us that during the weather that week, these plants would
have dried out in approx. 3 - 4 days. Therefore we formed the opinion that the trail had been formed at two
different times.

                                     VANISillNG ESKIMOS
                                   From Tom Slemen's Mysterious World

In November 1930, Joe Labelle, a Canadian fur trapper, snow shoed into a thriving Eskimo fishing
village situated on the shores of lake Anjikuni in Canada.

Labelle was greeted with an eerie silence. He though this was very strange because the fishing village
was a noisy settlement with 2,000 Eskimos milling back and forth to their kayaks. But there wasn't a soul
about.

Labelle visited each of the Eskimo huts and fish storehouses but none of the villagers was anywhere to
be seen. Labelle saw a flickering fire in the distance and approached it gingerly, sensing something evil
was afoot on this moonlight night. Upon the fire was a smouldering pot of blackened stew. To make
matters more mysterious, Labelle saw that not a single human track had left the settlement.

Labelle knew that something bizarre had happened to the 2,000 people and so he ran non-stop to the
nearest telegraph office and sent a message about his findings the the Royal Canadian Mounted Police.
The 'mounties' turned up hours later and they too were baftled by the mass vanishing act.

An enormous search party was sent out to look for the missing villagers, but they were never found and
the search party unearthed some strange findings. All the sleigh dogs that had belonged to the Eskimos
were found buried 12 feet under a snowdrift at the perimeter of the camp. All of them had starved to
death.

The search party also established that the Eskimo's provisions and food had been left in their huts, which
didn't make any sense at all. Then came the most chilling surprise of all; the search party discovered that
all of the Eskimos' ancestral graves were empty. Whoever or whatever had taken all the living villagers
had also dug up the dead as well, even though the icy ground around the graves was as hard as iron.

Later on that unearthly silent night, the mounties watched in awe as a strange blue glow lit up the
horizon. The eerie radiance was not the northern-lights, but seemed steady and artificial. As the mounties
watched, the light pulsated then faded.

All the newspapers in the World reported the baffling disappearance of the 2,000 Eskimos, although
many believed that a rational explanation would eventually come to light, but the Anjikuni mass
disappearance is still unsolved.
