      We were going to taper off on the infamous Linda Napolitano UFO ab­
duction case until we received the astonishing March/April l99J issue of
the "International UFO Reporter " (IUR), organ of the J. Allen Hynek Center
for UFO Studies (CUFOS), with Jerome Clark (sadly!) as its editor. Let us
start right off by observing that the late Dr. Hynek, whom we knew and lik­
ed, must be spinning in his grave these days, if he has any inkling of what
Clark and associates are doing to the scientific integrity of the organiza-
�ion  e uunded!
      First, we are annoyed that Hopkins & associates persist in calling
the central figure in this fiasco Linda Cortile, when her real name, Na­
politano, has already been published widely since it first appeared in "Sau­
cer Smear ". And the name of the United Nations dignitary allegedly in the
Linda case is still kept secret by Hopkins and his followers, when we all
know it is Javier Perez de Cuellar, former Secretary General of the U.N.
      Secondly, as the title of this article implies, scientific researcher
Budd Hopkins doesn't seem to know much about the location of New York City's
Brooklyn Bridge - near which this alleged UFO abduction took place.    Hop­
kins has apparently lived in that part of the City much of his life, as has
Linda; and yet, on the very cover of IUR, there appears a photograph of the
Bridge, taken by Hopkins, with the caption: "Stereo view of the Brooklyn
Bridge from approximate location of 'Dan's' car at the time of the abduct­
ion ". (This location is in Manhattan.) The caption continues: "The New
Jersey side is across the river on the left, and Manhattan is out of sight
to the right. "
      Yes, Manhattan is out of sight, behind the camera; but unfortunately
for Budd, the Brooklyn Bridge runs from lower Manhattan to (yes!) Brooklyn,
                                   (2)
 not
 -- New Jersey, which is also out of sight several miles behind the camera!
       This is not just an innocent "typo ", folks; it is typical of the "re­
 search " done by Hopkins, who, according to the treatise by Hansen/Stefula/
 Butler, has not even made a thorough search for back-up witnesses among the
 many tenants in the large apartment complex where Linda lives, nor has he
 interrogated security guards there - to find out if anyone other than Linda
 and her family noticed a very brightly-lit UFO hovering outside her win­
 dow at about 3 a.m. on November 30th, 1989 - three and a half years ago!
 As we understand it, there simply are no such back-up witnesses, or      at
 least none have come forward. But apparently Hopkins' vision of "reality "
 does not need such mundane reinforcement.
       IUR, a supposedly scientific journal, devotes about 19 of its 24
 pages to the Linda case. Of the 19 pages, a total of 11 are devoted to
 long diatribes by Clark and Hopkins, in which for the most part they attack
 the character and personality of their sworn enemies, Hansen, Stefula, and
 Butler, while ignoring the factual evidence brought out by these three men.
 Supporting articles by David Jacobs and John Mack occupy another 3 pages,
 and a total of about 4 pages are given to Donald Johnson and Willy Smith
 to argue against the validity of the case.
       It is interesting to note that Jerry Clark and Willy Smith despise
 each other, so perhaps it was kind of Clark to allow Smith's article to ap­
 pear. But never mind; Hopkins was obviously forewarned of Smith's line of
 argument, and successfully devastates it in a rebuttal in this very same
 issue, leaving Smith to look quite bad! (Ain't Science Grand!)
       Your humble "Smear " editor is mentioned only in passing. Clark ti­
 tles his editorial "Saucer Smearers ", for which we duly thank him; Hopkins
 calls us "a dubious personage " (along with Phil Klass); and further on he
 refers to "Smear " as a "scandal sheet " and calls its editor "a cynical and
 parasitic journalist (who) immediately accepts the Linda-as-diabolical­
 hoaxer theory, perhaps because he himself once perpetrated a successful
 UFO hoax. Maybe, in this case, it takes one to accuse another of being
 one, too. " No Budd, it's better than that: IT TAKES ONE TO KNOW ONE!
 Frame that motto and put it on your wall!
        Of George Hansen, who impresses us as a rather mild-mannered sort
 with a good sense of humor, Hopkins writes: "Nor can we let our solemn work
 squeeze out the natural humor of life. Lighten up, George. Wait till you
 read the whole Cortile (sic) story, and then we'll talk about the case.
 Meanwhile, take a deep breath, smile, and have a little fun. "
        But in an earlier paragraph, Hopkins compares Hansen/Stefula/Butler
 to the Muslim extremists who blew up the World Trade Center recently, and
 states: "...Killing American office workers can no more accomplish (their)
 goal than Hansen's character trashing can accomplish whatever it is     he
 thinks is his goal. The more broadly he indulges his arm-flailing and re­
 putation-bashing, the more the cruelty of his own brand of fanaticism re­
 veals itself. "
       It is Hopkins and Clark who are humorless in regard to their     own
 agenda and fanatical in their efforts to force the Linda case     down the
 throats of the members of organized ufology. And it is indeed "Smear " that
 stands as a beacon of sanity, self-criticism, humor, and moderation.     Or
 at least this is what we at "Smear " modestly believe (or half-believe).
