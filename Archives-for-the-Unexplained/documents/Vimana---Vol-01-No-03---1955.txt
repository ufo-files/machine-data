       IATEST SAUCER NEWS FROM THE WEST COAST
             by George Hunt Williamson

I just returned from Arizona and California where I
spent the month of December and part of January. As
usual, there is much activity saucer-wise in that part
of the country.
First of all, the 200-inch telescope on Mt. Palomar is
going to be increased to the equivalent of a 2000-inch
telescope by the addition of an electronic device to the
original 200-inch model. That should prove most inter-
esting.
It is reported reliably that a spaceman named "Jepp" has
landed somewhere in Nevada or Utah and he and his craft
are contained within a force field which does not permit
officials to get near them .
I ran into Harry Myers, mentioned in FLYING SAUCERS HAVE
I.ANDED, and he told me about a new documentary film
called: "WE'VE SEEN THE FLYING SAUCERS" It should prove
to be startling, indeed.
A new element called Telonium has been discovered on the
Giant Rock Airport property belonging to George W. Van
Tassel . It's strange quality lies in the fact that
pieces of it makes the ordinary background count of ra-
diation drop! No other element has such a quality. They
may absorb radiation, but they cannot reduce the back-
ground count! It tests out to 350,000 lbs. p , s.i. Aqua
Regia has absolutely no effect on it and a spectographic
analysis shows a new, unknown band on the spectrum . It
has been called Telonium because it is believed that it
is the same element used by the ancients for their
"imperishable record tablets." More will be revealed
about this startling discovery as time goes on. It has
been verified by reliable mining engineers and others.
I have visited with several electronic firms and univer-
sities. Many of them are now engaged in light-beam-
communication work. The head of a midwestern electronic
company in Indiana, has successfully contacted the discs
by this method and it is believed that shortly absolute
proof will be offered the world through this medium.
Several research centers are now in full-scale experimen-
tation. Those in colleges and schools are operating in
secrecy for the time being.

          FR 0 M    G U E S S I N G TO     THINKING
                        By Baird Wallace
       (EDITOR'S NOTE : The accompanying guest Editorial
        is written by one of the active members of the
        DRSC whose letter in the previous VI.MANA evoked
        considerable comment. Mr. Wallace is a graduate
        electrical engineer from the University of
        Pittsburgh and today serves in an important capa-
        city with a major Detroit manufacturer.)
The technical side of a subject of such universal implica-
tion as interterrestrial spacecraft and unidentified fly-
ing objects is, by its very nature, broad, and for our
small insight, quite general . The scope of the thoughts
and inquiry which the realization of the presence of
spacecraft from other worlds, brings to our minds extends
far beyond the limits of our imagination in all di-
re ctions . There is not one area of human interest un-
affected by this news for it is certainly an inspiration
to all of us who dare to look out from our small little
world, to try and fathom the endless unknown.
These phenomena are causing men and women in every walk
of life to look more closely at the world about them.
The resulting re-evaluation of our science and philosophy
is bound to be a most productive thing for so many of our
concepts have become dogmatic and our theories have so
often taken on the intonation of law.
The great gains of science in our century are a tribute
to the productiveness of minds that strive to look ob-
jectively at nature but in our effort to fill in the gaps
in our understanding of the principles that seem to con-
trol all things, we have spent an entire lifetime in
guessing. These guesses which we have given the more de-
ceptive name of t heories, have accumulated and grown to
such proportions that they threaten to choke the future
expansion of the very science that spawned them!
In the fields of electronics, physics, thermodynamics,
astronomy archeology, geology, paneontology and many
                                                             '
others, guesses have become the main basis for teaching
these sciences to the young men and women in our schools
and universities .
The known facts are mixed with conjecture and the result
is indeed a tragic one . The clear cut dividing line be-
tween truth and fiction becomes fuzzy and confused, and
vast areas of uncharted phenomena a~d natural history lie
covered in this confusion . (See WALLACE - Page 11)

     The coming of craft from outer space in appreciable
     numbers during these last several years is sharpening
     the eye and quickening the tempo of thinking people
     all over the world .
     Anyone who has looked to the sky and seen one of these
     glowing discs gliding soundlessly through our atmosphere,
     scintillating with beautiful color, maneuvering with pre-
     cision, and even, on occasion, stopping to hang motionless
     in space, has certainly felt the surge of cosmic wonder
r.
 l   flow into his mind.
     For those who will but consider it, the mere physical ex-
     istence of such craft means that many of our most respected
     crutches of scientific thought have gone flitting out the
     window.
     There are many who would have us believe that the thousands
     who have seen these ships in the air, and the many who have
     had much closer contact with them have either been victims
     of hallucinations or that they have seen phenomena beyond
     the realms of natural law in some vague category which for
     want of any other name, they call supernatural .
     I vehemently contest these points of view for in the first
     instance the correlation of sighting data is conclusive to
     anyone who looks at it with a searching intellect . And as
     for the second hypothesis, it is my contention that every
     facet of the universe in all its glorious ramifications is
     functioning in accordance with Natural Law. With this
     basic premise of the scientific method as a guidepost, and
     with the reality of the flying saucer as an inspiration, we
     can get down again to the hard business of learning, in-
     stead of the easier life of guessing .
     Everyday, landmarks of the unknown are emerging from the
     fog which we have generated about them . Nature is beckon-
     ing to man to look afresh and understand her Eternal
     Principles .
     In contrast to the vistas which are opening ahead of us,
     our present grasp of science, natural history and philo-
     sophy is very meager .
     We must get our minds into gear for t he principles of the
     universe are behind all things . Knowledge of our lack of
     knowledge is a precious possession. I f we hold the deep
     awareness of this lack close within us, our thirst for
     Universal Truth will drive our very being to sear ch anew
     wi thout the misconception of our monument al guesses and
     ageless mistakes .

   "ABOARD A FLYING SAUCER" by Truman Bethurum, California
mechanic and engineer, chronicles an eleven visit sequence
on the Mormon mesa desert with space people in a large
"apple-pie" type of Saucer. A crew of 32 men, with ruddy
olive complexion, with a beautiful lady Captain, Aura
Rhaines, were its occupants. We know Mr . Bethurum very
well, and believe his story implicitly . He was a speaker
in Detroit late in June, 1954.

   TWO NEW BOOKS: "SPACE, GRAVITY AND FLYING SAUCERS" by
Leonard Cramp, details in excellent order the probable
engineering detail of the Adamski type of Saucer , the
author drawing upon rich electronic and engineering back-
ground, to justify the possibility of such a craft. He
compares Rocket power with Space travel, clearly delineat-
ing the outmoded aspect of the former, in view of the
latter discovery yet to come. This book has a wonderful
foreward in it by Desmond Leslie, who relates the Canister
incident in England as one of the most valid substantia-
tions of George Adamski ' s experience, in the latter part
of "Flying Saucers Have Landed . "

   "FLYING SAUCER FROM MARS" by Cedric Allingham . This
British naturalist writes very sincerely about his study
of astronomic values, and before his sighting of a
"Martian" was well acquainted with space travel thru the
Keyhoe and Adamski experiences. He supplies pictures -
both of Saucers and of a Martian which appear convincing,
and which bear the affidavit of a witness to prove their
accuracy.
