                                    NEWEST   QUASAR


     Discovery of a star-like object which one cosmic yardstick
indicates may be the brightest, fastest and farthest yet found was
disclosed yesterday by the California Institute of rechnology.

     The object, called 0237-23, is one of the mysterious new class
of bright, distant things known as quasars, which is short for
quasi-stellar, or starlike. Little is known about the 200 so far
                                          - 6-
    discovered, but they appear to be much smaller than galaxies and
    up to 100 times brighter.

         Quasar 0237-23 has a "red shift" greater than that of any
    other ob ject yet sighted.  As generally interpreted, a large red
    shift means an ob ject is far out in the universe and traveling
    away at high speed.

         The new quasar's red shift indicates it is receding from earth
    at more than 150, 000 miles a second, or 82.4% of the velocity of
    light, compared with 81.4% for the fastest previously discovered
    quasar.

    AP/Pasadena, Calif.
