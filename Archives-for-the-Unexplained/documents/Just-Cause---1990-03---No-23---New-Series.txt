                                            Another "Smoking Gun"?


                                                             by


                                                      Robert G.    Todd


       Another smoking gun has surfaced which furnishes physical evidence that seems
       to implicate Bill Moore as the author of the alleged Eisenhower briefing,                            as
       well as the Truman-Forrestal memo appended to the briefing.


       Anybody who has ever been the recipient of mail from Bill Moore will recognize
       the rather distinctive return address on his envelopes                     ( see   Figures 2,   3,   and
       4).     Because of the large size of the type used in Moore's rubber stamp,                          his
       return address appears sideways on the envelopes.                      Moreover,   as evident in
       Figures 2 through       4,   it appears that Bill Moore uses a rubber stamp assembled
       from a kit that uses movable,             reusable type that allows him to make up any
       stamp he desires.        This is indicated by several characteristics:


               1.   As Figures 2,     3,   and   4 show,     Moore used the same size and style of
       type for his Arizona address as he now uses in his California address.


               2.   In all occurrences of the letter "I" in "WILLIAM" and "OLIVE"                      ( see
       Figures 3 and     4),   the letter is slightly raised,               a characteristic common to
       rubber stamps assembled from such kits.


               3.   Moore's return address has a rather sloppy,                 unprofessional look to
       it,    again suggesting that he assembled the stamps from a kit.                      Either that,         or
       he had custom rubber stamps made up for him,                    for both his Arizona and
       California addresses,         by a firm that does consistently shoddy work.


               4.   A comparison of Figure            3,   which is Moore's return address from the
       1985    time period, with Figure          4,    his return address from     1990, will show that
       the ZIP code portion of the address in the                    1990 example is placed closer to
       the state abbreviation for California.                     Again,   this suggests that the stamp
       was assembled from a kit of movable,                  reusable type.
   Even more interesting -- and incriminating -- is the fact that the type style
   Bill Moore uses for his return address stamp is identical to the type style
   used for the classification markings          ( "TOP SECRET / MAJIC" and "EYES ONLY" ) that
   appear on the Eisenhower briefing document,                although the size of the type is
   larger for     the Eisenhower briefing.       As evident from Moor-;;g-return addresses,
   however,     he obviously has more than one type style,             so there is every reason
   to believe that he may indeed have more than one size of the same type style.


   In addition,      the classification markings        ( see Figure 1) that appear on the
   Eisenhower briefing paper also appear to have been applied using a rubber
   stamp assembled from a kit employing movable,                reusable type.        Again,    note the
   telltale raised letter "I" in the word "MAJIC".


   No examples were found of Moore's return address that show type of the same
   size as that used for the classification markings on the Eisenhower briefing
   paper.     This shouldn't come as any surprise because type that large has
   limited uses.        For example,    if Moore were to use a type size comparable to
   that used for the classification markings on the briefing document,                         his return
   address wouldn't fit on the envelope at all.                 As noted above,       the type size he
   already uses apparently forces him to apply his address sideways.                          If it were
   any larger,       he wouldn't be able to use the rubber stamp for his return
   address.


   Even more interesting is the fact that the type used by Bill Moore in his
   return address is identical in both style and size to the type used in the
   "EYES ONLY" portion of the classification markings appearing on the Truman­
   Forrestal memo.        Phil Klass has already proven that the signature on the
   Truman-Forrestal memo is a photocopy forgery.


   While it must be acknowledged that there are perhaps many such rubber stamp
   kits in existence,       it must also be considered a highly significant coincidence
   that the same person who is distributing the document also uses                         j ust such a
   kit that employs type identical in style to that used on the classification
   markings appearing on the Eisenhower briefing paper,                    and identical in both
   style and size to the "EYES ONLY" portion of the classification markings
   appearing on the Truman-Forrestal memo.              Now it would appear we have evidence
   suggesting that Bill Moore,          specifically,     may be the author.

       On Timothy Good's Source of the MJ-12 Briefing Paper

      :n the 1987 book ABOVE TOP SECRET, author Timothy Good reprints
seven pages of the famous "Operation Majestic 12" briefing paper for
President Eisenhower. The document explains in great detail the alleged
circumstances surrounding the Roswell UFO crash of 1947, and it's sub­
sequent investigation by an elite panel of government experts.
      Good said in ATS that the paper was made available to him un­
officially in early 1987, before their release to the U.S. press. And
recently in his new book THE UFO REPORT-1990, Good elaborates, saying
that it was "obtained from a CIA source."
      Now if the briefing paper was obtained from a CIA source, then
a significant argument could be made for the MJ-12 story being official
disinformation. CAUS has concluded without doubt that the various doc­
uments are not genuine government releases, so the only question re­
garding the origin of the documents is whether they are official or
unofficial hoaxes.
      According to Moore, his copy of the briefing paper came on a
roll of 3 5mm film sent anonymously to a colleague, Jaime Shandera,
in December 1984. Moore released his copies, made from the film, in
his Focus newsletter of April 3 0, 1987.
      If it is true that Moore's and Good's copies of the briefing
paper were received independently from government sources, then a
comparison of each person's copies might yield interesting clues as
to the perpetrator of this false information.
      For purposes of comparison I have chosen pages 1-3 of Moore's
copy of the Eisenhower briefing as reproduced from Focus for April 3 0,
1987, Moore's first public release of this document. Also chosen are
pages 1-3 of Good's copy of the briefing from his CIA source as re­
produced in ABOVE TOP SECRET.

      1) The "Magic Marker" Deletions - A comparison of the ink de­
letions of the security markings on the Moore and Good versions re­
veals that while the deletions are not identical there is a marked
similarity. They are slash-type deletions, probably done with one
stroke in most cases. In neither case do the deletions completely
obliterate the security markings, allowing one to read what the
classification had been. Early in the MJ-12 debate Moore had already
admitted self-censoring his copy to protect himself in case a security
question was raised as to his possession of the document. One sees
no significant difference between the Moore and Good versions in the
way the markings were applied.

      2) The "Vertical Ridges" - If one compares pages from both
versions of the document you will notice a pattern of photocopier
"noise," i.e., a general dirtiness of the photocopy paper caused by
an uncleaned machine. This noise takes the form of vertical parallel
ridges across portions of the copies. As in the case of the magic
marker deletions, the pattern is not identical but is very similar.
The density and clarity of the noise pattern can vary from copy to
copy, depending upon the maintenance of the machine, but the same
general pattern is there, as can be clearly seen in both copies. And
the pattern can be seen in the same general area of particular pages
(Note areas between paragraphs on pages 2&3). Suggested here is that
the two versions were copied at different times but on the same machine.

      3 ) The "Fingerprint" - If one notes the circled area of Moore's
page 2, you will see two lines at roughly 3 0 degree angles to the

                                   4
horizontal bottom of the page. The lines also appear on Moore's page 3 ,
between paragraphs 1 and 2 (circled). These lines also appear on Good's
page 1 (under EYES ONLY) and on page 3 , between paragraphs 1 and 2. It
                                         "                   "
appears as well on Good's page 4 and his list of attachments page to
the briefing (not reproduced here but found in ATS). The mark "floats"
on the various copies on which it appears, apparently a flaw in the
photocopier roller that becomes imprinted onto various areas of the
papers as they pass through the machine. Since the marking appears on
both copies, I am forced to believe that both came from the same copy
machine at different times.

                           Conclusions

       It is important that the various anomalies cited above are not
absolutely identical in every way, as far as clarity, position and
density are concerned. If they were it could be argued that the original
document, photographed and sent to Shandera, and allegedly copied and
sent to Good by a CIA source, possessed these anomalies. As such each
person's version could still be attributed to a government source. This
is not the case here.
       The Moore/Shandera copy was received in 1984. Hardcopy had to be
made from the photographic film, resulting in copies possessing the
anomalies particular to the photocopy machine used by them. Timothy
Good.'s copies contain the same kinds of anomalies, meaning that the
machine used by Moore/Shandera was the source of Good's copies.
       Press accounts in Britain quoted Good as saying that the documents
were from a "reliable American source who has close connections with
the intelligence community.'' Moore has claimed this all along. So the
remark seems to be a good description of Moore. Then how does one ex­
plain Good's statement in his THE UFO REPORT-1990,that his source was
CIA?
       Either:
         1) Good is lying to protect Moore.
         2) Moore is a CIA source, which he vehemently denies.
         3 ) Good honestly believes Moore to be CIA, which in fact he isn't.
             Lee Graham has already testified in the past that Moore
             flashed an official-looking ID badge which Graham believed
             to be a Defense Investigative Service (DIS) badge (see Graham
             letter to DIS, 5-8-86). Moore at the time gave MJ-12 papers
             to Graham. He later called the badge incident a "joke"
             played on Graham.

      In a 5-11-88 letter to Good from Lee Graham, Graham said, "Bill
Moore indicated to me that you were (like he) a 'controlled informant'
and that it was your function to release the MJ-12 document in England
at the same time that he did in the U.S . 11
                                         .


      Graham added that Good prematurely released the papers and that
Moore had to release them in this country. Graham continued:
     "�says that he doesn't know where you got the document. He also
tells me that some agency of my (the U.S.) government specifically
told him to approach me to leak the MJ-12 documents to the public and
that even when I became fearful cf circulating the thing 'they' were
circulating it in my name."
      It is vitally important that Timothy Good explain this situation.
I like him as an individual and respect his hard work in UFO research,
and I do not wish to see him caught in an awful mess.
