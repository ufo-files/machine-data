SG1A
                     This section of building was occuppied by a military
                 unit.   The entire unit was under the control of an
                 unidentified ministry.  The unit did not belong to that
                 ministry but had a mission of providing security to that
                 ministry.   The unit had both uniformed military and
                 plain-clothed elements. This unit appeared out of place
                 in this environment.   This unit would not normally be
                 associated with this area.
                     The interior of this section of the building was a
                 vast open space which extended up several floors but did
                 not go to the top of the bulding, stopping at the fifth or
                 sixth floor.    On both sides of this open space were
                 winding type stairways. On the ground floor on the right
                 side (facing the street) in the entrance area was a
                 control point or check point controlling access to various
                 areas within the building.
                     In the front of this section of the building there
                 were very long corridors which went to a number of
                 different types of rooms. On the upper floors these were 1
                 "dorm type" rooms for housing military personnel On the
                 middle floors, right side (facing the street) were rooms
                 for military personnel On the middle floors, right side
                 (facing the street) were rooms for military officers. On
                 the middle floors, left side were rooms for civilians or
                 plain-clothes prsonnel.    These roor.1s were quieter, with
                 better living conditions. There were also some offices in
                 this area. On the ground floor right side there were also
                 spacious rooms but these were conference rooms,
                 interrogation rooms and technical oriented rooms.      These
                 rooms v.'ere kept secluded and were dark and dreary as
                 though they were not used most of the time.
                     No further amplifying information concerning subject
                 was obtained from Source during this interview.
               Attached hereto is EXHIBIT I, drawings provided by
           Source concerning the areas described above.
