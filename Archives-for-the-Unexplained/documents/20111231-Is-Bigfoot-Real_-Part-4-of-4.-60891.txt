   If you thought the cost of travel insurance was out of this world,
   prepare for the next frontier, where a policy for a space trip
   cancellation might set you back 700 euros.
   Travel insurance provider Allianz Global Assistance has teamed up with
   the International Space Transport Association (ISTA) to look at
   providing 'bespoke' travel insurance for space tourists as flights and
   holidays outside of our planet become more reality than fantasy.
   'At Allianz Global Assistance, we are looking into space tourism as a
   new market for the next generation of travellers,' said Erick Morazin,
   Director of Global Accounts.
   'Space is decidedly our new frontier and we are now ready to take this
   giant leap forward to ensure peace of mind and well-being of all our
   future space-travelling customers.'
   Mr Morazin told TravelMail that they were hoping to offer insurance
   products from January 2012 but could only make predictions based 'on the
   current knowledge we have.'
   He added that they were working out the particulars as operators other
   than Sir Richard Branson's Virgin Galactic - which plans to offer
   commercial space flights from October 2012 - potentially entered the market.
   Mr Morazin said there were currently 20 different elements that needed
   to be taken into account in regards to insurance and space tourism.
   Products would include cover for aspects such as space training, the
   duration of the trip and trip cancellation.
   Mr Morazin used the example of Virgin Galactic where a ticket costs
   $200,000 (£126,726) and the deposit starts from $20,000 (£12,672).
   A cancellation will mean a refund of some of the deposit and the travel
   insurance - priced around 700 Euros - would cover the shortfall, he said.
   Lost luggage, meanwhile, might be another story.
   'We don't expect luggage to be lost in space. If the luggage is lost in
   space, we won't be able to recover it,' he said.
   It is unclear what might happen if space tourists are taken ill or
   indeed if your passport is stolen by intergalactic pickpockets.
   A statement from Allianz Global Assistance this week reassured tourists:
   'The two partners are committed to accompanying travellers before,
   during and after their space flight to ensure that their unique galactic
   experience, as well as the experience of their families back on earth,
   will be as safe, serene and comfortable as possible.'
