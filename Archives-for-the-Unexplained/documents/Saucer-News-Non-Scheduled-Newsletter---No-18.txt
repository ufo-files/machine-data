Neither of the two would talk much about themselves, and the woman in particular was
a rather unusual person, dressed in old-fashioned clothes.

            Alexander was a middle-aged man of fairly heavy build and medium height.
He had a dark olive complexion.  The only strange thing about him was his foreign ac­
cent, which was impossible to place.   He refused to state where he was born - saying
only that it was a country that my wife and I had never heard of.    He never quite said
that he was from another planet, bu.t he did claim. to have traveled all over the world
without a passport, to have met ever,yone in saucer research, and to have read absolute­
ly everything available on the subject.   He was also interested in psychic phenomena,
and claimed to have extrasensory powers, - although he refused to demonstrate them.

            Alexander's message to us was that he had unlimited funds from some unknown
source, and wanted to finance your Editor on a series of saucer lectures around the
world.  The following evening I met him again, bu.t he never got down to details as to
when these lectures would take place, and my original skepticism about the offer was
thereby increased.  This time he was eating dinner with a rather attractive young
woman whom he introduced as his secretary. He told me that he had to leave the next
day for a very brief trip to Washington, D.C., and that he would contact me again imme­
diately upon his return to New Yorko  But that was the last time I saw or heard from
Alexander!
