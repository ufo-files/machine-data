  know there was a moderate breeze (by the angle of the smoke leaving the towers) which would only moderately "stoke"
  the fire but at the same time move the heat out of the building keeping the temperature rise to a minimum.

      It is quite clear from the impact and initial flames that thousands of pounds of kerosene moving at hundreds of miles
  an hour exitted the buildings in a most manner that even Hollywood could appreciate. The only good record is of the
  second hit which shows the kerosene part of the fire ending quickly and reverting to a smoky oxygen starved fire
  (indicated by the black smoke) very quickly. Those are low temperature fires as the fire department tape indicated that
  they found just before the collapse. Much can be debated but the firemen were on the floors where it hit so whatever
  fires there were were not outrageously hot.

      But as I said originally, all of this is likely answered some place. At least one formal study was commissioned and I
  have no idea if it is finished and so where it has been published. If it has been, it is in some journal for professional
  architects and the next time it will be in the news is when it comes to approving the design of a skyscraper of equivalent
  target value.

      Building commissioners, the builders themselves to attract lessees, th lessees, insurance companies are all going to be
  interested in the next building being able to survive a 9/11 style attack. The group of interested parties will be much to
  large for even the most inveterate conspiracy theorist to explain away if he does not hear of the formal study of the
  collapse.

      The insurance carriers on all the existing skyscrapers and all of the above will be interested in the results to improve
