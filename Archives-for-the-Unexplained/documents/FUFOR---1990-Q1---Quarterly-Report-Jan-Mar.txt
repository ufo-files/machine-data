                                    QUARTERLY REPORT
                                  January - March 1990

     The First Quarter of 1990 was marked by two events which are likely to have
     lasting repercussions within the UFO community: the publication of The Gulf
     Breeze Sightings by Ed and Frances Walters, and the convening of a second
     conference .ao_the abduction phenomenon involving mental health practitioners
     and UFO i nve·st igators.
     The book on the Gulf Breeze sightings, which details the experiences of Ed
     Walters (formerly known only as "Mr . Ed") and his family in Gulf Breeze,
     Florida, from 1987 through 1989. Theirs are only the most detailed of the
     many sighting reports involving more than 100 witnesses in the area during
     that period. The book, published by William Morrow, contains 70 color photos
     taken by Ed, Frances and other witnesses, including pictures taken with a
     specially-designed "stereo camera" and videotape footage shot by Ed in the
     presence of witnesses.
     The photos and videotape were analyzed by Bruce Maccabee, Ph.D., Chairman of
     the Fund and a recognized expert in the analysis of UFO photographs. Dr.
     Maccabee had already published two papers on the Gulf Breeze case (see the
     enclosed Reply Form), and he contributed a chapter to the book on his analysis
     of the photos . Dr. Maccabee's conclusion was that there was no evidence of a
     hoax and that the photographs are of a "True UFO."
     The Executive Committee of the Fund hosted a special reception for Mr. Walters
     in the Washington, D.C., area on March 18, the eve of a two-week "book tour"
     sponsored by the publisher in major cities across the country. The Fund's
     supporters in the District of Columbia, Maryland and Virginia were invited to
     attend and meet the author, who discussed his experiences at length.
     Autographed copies of the book were offered for sale, and a limited number of
     copies are still available (see the Reply Form for details).
     The other significant event was the second conference on "Treatment Research
     on Experienced Anomalous Trauma" (TREAT), sponsored by Rima Laibow, M.D., a
     New York psychiatrist. The conference, held at the Virginia Polytechnic
     Institute in Blacksburg, VA, involved psychiatrists, psychologists and UFO
     investigators in a wide-ranging discussion of various aspects of the
     "abduction" phenomenon and its impact on the emotional health of "abductees."
     The conference was significant becaus e of its- increasing involvement of
     leaders in the mental health community in this aspect of UFO research. Two
     members of the Fund's Board of Directors, Robert Hall, Ph.D. and Dr. Maccabee,
     made presentations at the conference. The Fund is providing $1,000 to support
     publication of the conference proceedings.
     The First Quarter of 1990 was also marked by the first effort to recognize
     outstanding journalism in the UFO field. Last year, the Fund announced the
     establishment of a UFO Journalism Award in the name of the late Donald E.
     Keyhoe, former director of the National Investigations Committee on Aerial
     Phenomena (NICAP) and author of five books and a number of magazine articles
     on UFOs. The award was for the best news article or story published or
     broadcast in 1989 on the UFO phenomenon.
     Print and electronic journalists from across the country submitted entries,
.~   which were screened by a committee consisting of three members of the Fund's
     Board of Directors: Don Berliner, aviation writer; Richard Hall, editor; and
     Fred Whiting, public relations executive and former broadcast journalist.

The Executive Committee also decided during this period to once again
recognize o.ut.standing work in the field of UFO research by providing a $500
check to the recipient of the Mutual UFO Network's award for the most
outstanding contribution to UFO research during the year, which will be
presented at the MUFON symposium in Pensacola in July. The Fund's cash prize
is made annually in the name of Isabel Davis, one of the founders of the Fund
for UFO Research.
During this period, the Executive Committee met with nuclear physicist Stanton
Friedman in the Washington, D.C., area and recei·ved a progress report on his
investigation into the alleged "MJ-12" documents and events surrounding the
alleged crash of a UFO and retrieval of apparent alien bodies outside Roswell,
New Mexico, in June 1947. Mr. Friedman, who is working under a $16,000 grant
made possible by the Fund's supporters, has already begun writing his report
and notes that he has made substantial progress in identifying new--and
potentially significant--sources of information. Contributors of $50 or more
to the project will receive a copy of Mr. Friedman's report before it is
released publicly. We gratefully acknowledge the support of the Mutual UFO
Network and its members for making this vitally important effort possible.

will receive a cash award of $1,000.
        Second place went to Kim Opatka, Staff Writer for the
Latrobe (Pennsylvania} Bulletin, for her six-part series on
UFOs published May 1-4, 1989.    "The series was aimed at
fostering a better public understanding of the UFO
phenomenon and at abolishing misconceptions associated with
the field and persons who have claimed sightings," said
Opatka.
        Another local television news series, "UFOhio,"
produced by WBNS-TV, Columbus, OH, was ranked third by the
competition judges. The entry, which dealt with a week-long
series of reports dealing with UFO activity in Ohio, was
submitted by reporter Tom Berman.    The station alsu
conducted a telephone poll on UFOs, which resulted in more
than 16,000 responses.

        The UFO Journalism Award is named after Donald E.
Keyhoe, author of five books and a number of newspaper and
magazine articles on the UFO phenomenon.     A major in the
U.S. Marine Corps and former aide to Charles Lindbergh,
Keyhoe was former director of the National Investigations
Committee on Aerial Phenomena and one of the first prominent
individuals to call attention to the U.S. Government's
apparent cover-up of information on UFOs.     He passed away in
November 1988.
