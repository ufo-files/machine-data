    This article recounts the overall history of the UFO sightings that occurred during
 southward and then northward flights of an Argosy freighter aircraft during the early
 morning hours of December 31, 1978. As indicated by the maps, the plane was flying
 along the east coast of the South Island of New Zealand.  Also indicated on the maps
 are forty discrete events which occurred during the trip.  Some of these will be referred
 to in this article.

   The information presented here is based upon extensive interviews with the witnesses
and upon research into the radar and photographic aspects of this case.  It is also based
upon information contained on the Wellington Air Traffic Control Tower recording tape,
information contained on the tape that was made in the airplane during the flight, and
upon information contained within immediate newspaper and television releases�

    The history of this case can be divided into two parts which I call the "trip south'
and the "trip north," since the UFO incidents are somewhat different.   During the trip
south the witnesses on the aircraft reported unusual lights which would appear and dis­
appear apparently at random in the general area along the coastline between the Clarence
River and the Kaikoura Peninsual.   During the trip north the witnesses reported a very
bright, steay light that would have been about 35 to 45 nautical miles (65-84 km) north­
east of Christchurch, New Zealand.   They also reported bright lights that appeared to be
just west of Cap Campbell and over the land near blenheim, New Zealand.   During both
the south and north trips, when the airpland was within the range of the Wellington radar
(i. e. within 150 nautical miles - 280 km- of Wellington), Wellington told the airplane
about radar targets in its vicinity.   Occasionally very unusual visual objects were seen
in the specific directions referred to by Wellington.   Analyses of the characteristics
of the Wellington radar and of the weather conditions during the sighting have ruled out
anomalous propagation effects as causes for the radar sightings.   According to the radar
technicians there were also no problems with the radar equipment.   Christchurch radar
did report one anomalous target to the pilot and copilot, but the Air Traffic Controllor
at Christchurch was apparently not very impressed with what he saw.   When interviewed
nearly six weeks after the sighting he would neither confirm nor deny that he saw a tar­
get.   The Christchurch recording tape was erased accoridng to a standard thirty day rule
for re-use of the tape, even though the pilot had asked for it to be saved so that he
could make a copy.

   Analyses of the witness reports, the recorded information, the photographic data, and
of the weather data have ruled out the following published explanations for the visual
and photographic sightings: Venus (not above the horizon until the end of the north
flight); Jupiter (not bright enough, wrong direction); mating mutton birds (not sources
of light, not fast enough); meteors (duration too long to be meteors); balloons (none
known in the area, not bright sources of light, not fast enqugh); stars (not bright
enough); other aircraft (none in the area); ground lights (not bright enough, wrong di­
rections in central cases, identified by crew); secret military maneuvers (ruled out by
the Royal New Zealand Air Force officer in charge of the official investigation); squid
fishing boats (too far away, wrong directions, identified by crew); reflections of in­
ternal lights in the aircraft window (no bright lights in cabin of aircraft - all had
been dimmed by the captian); glowing bugs (not fast or bright enough); and hoax (most
of the witnesses did not know each other before December 30, 1978).

   This radar-visual-photographic case really begins during the early morning hours
of December 21, 1978, when the crews of two Argosy freighter aircraft spotted bright lights
in the vicinity of the mouth of the Clarence River during the time that the Wellington
Air Traffic Control Center (WATC) was reporting targets. One of the aircraft was paced
by a U FO according to WATC. At another time that same aircraft picked up a high speed
target on its own radar and the crew saw a rapidly moving flashing light in the direction
indicated by the airplane radar. The radar-visual sightings took place overa period of
about four hours starting about midnight. They made front page headlines in New Zealand
and Australia on the following day and they created a considerable renewed interest on
the part of the news media in the U FO phenomenon.

   During the week following Christmas, a M elbourne, Australia, TV station, Channel 0 ,
decided to create two five minute news programs o n the December 21 sightings. On December
26, the channel called Quentin Fogarty, a senior reporter who was vacationing in New
Zealand, and asked him to interview the Air Traffic Controllers and one of the pilots who
had been involved. He therefore made the necessary arrangements for the interviews and
also was fortunate to be able to arrange a trip on a similar early morning aircraft
flight. Through his acquaintances in New Zealand, Fogarty was able to contact a film
crew consisting of free-lance photographer David Crockett, and sound recordist Ngaire
Crockett, David's wife. The Crocketts wre unknown to Fogarty before he met them to do
the interviews on December 30. After interviewing the controllers and the pilot, Fogarty
and the Crocketts went aboard the Argosy freighter aircraft which would fly along a track
that would be nearly identical to the one taken by the December 21 flights. The flight
crew consisted of Captain William Startup and First Officer Robert Guard. Startup and
Guard knew each other, but they were complete strangers to Fogarty and the Crocketts.
After the camera equipment was loaded aboard the aircraft the plane departed for Well­
ington at about 9:30 p.m. to pick up newspapers for delivery at Christchurch. Fogarty's
plan was to get some film footage of background scenery (pictures inside the plane,
landing lights, etc.} for use in ·the news stori·es and to then get off at Christchurch
where he would join a friend, Dennis Grant, and continue his vacation. As a result of
"certain anomalous incidents" on the flight south, his plans changed cpnsiderably.
