     Dear Sir:

     I contacted your office, but was unable to flesh out my reasons for contacting you over the
     phone. I hope this letter clarifies things a bit.

     My name is Donna Rice-Bassett. I am currently involved in Dr. John Mack's research into
     "abductions" as a research subject, and treasurer of his non-profit organization, GRAA. I
     will also be taking over the publication of the newsletter to the "contactees" and putting
     together a quarterly magazine for this group in the very near future.

      Your name has come up in a very odd way in the course of my contact with John. This
      seems to indicate some interest on your part which John and others in MUFON wish held at
      arms length due to your very obvious government connections.

      A majority of us who have become John's principle subjects have similar government
      connections, either past or present. This sub-group feels that what is going on here is
      questionable, especially since our various positions have included contact with secured
      materials and/ or activities.

      John is a very nice fellow, but he refuses to see the significance of what is surfacing in some
      of these hypnotic regressions: i.e., that several of us have been used as tools to cause key
      figures to act or think in very specific ways.

      We have termed this ability "overlay". It seems to be some sort of survival characteristic
      which causes another person to essentially think he is in the company of a friendly "twin".
      This situation allows us to extract specific information or to manipulate the individual
      emotionally by generating feelings that the person cannot keep out, because, in essence, they
      are his own.

       My discussions with persons involved in the Naval DNA testing studies has shown that at
       least 1 % of the American population is genetically similar in ways that communities in other
       countries who have been inbreeding for centuries cannot duplicate. This is a puzzle.

       In 1973, I had a small, metal object taken out of a sinus cavity by a doctor involved with a
       government research facility. This object was quite an enigma. I allowed genetic testing to be
       done as a follow up and was told that I "was a study in recessive genetics". This seemed to
       explain the presence of this object to some degree, although the specifics were rather vague
       at the time.

             plan was that the hostages would have been equally effected by the

             nor any other rescue plan was required.

                                                 Non-lethal Technologies

          lnfrasound: Very low frequency sound generators can be tuned to incapacitate humans, causing
               disorientation, nausea, vomiting, or bowel movements. By adjusting the signal, infrasound
               can also be used to crumble masonry.
          Laser Weapons: Low-energy laser rifles with power packs can flash-blind people and disable optical
              and Infrared systems used for target acquisition, tracking, night vision and range-finding.
          Isotropic Radiators: Modified conventional weapons can fire omnidirectional laser-bright rounds
              what will dazzle people or optics.
          Non-Nuclear Electromagnetic Pulse (EMP): Developmental beam generators producing one
              gigawatt of power could be used to explode ammunition dumps within line of sight or paralyze
               unprotected in-use electronic systems, including systems with antennas or systems connected to
              batteries. Vulnerable systems include all engines with electronic ignition systems, gasoline
              pumps, radars, communications, navigation, and unshielded electronic triggers of explosive and
              nuclear devices.
          High Power Microwave (HPM): A national program exists for the development of HPM systems
              designed to disrupt electronics. HPM pulses can either be fired repetitively or HPM bombs can
              be made to disable electronic devices.
          Liquid Metal Embrittlement (LME) : LME agents work by changing the molecular structure of base
              metals or alloys to which it is applied. _ LMEs are clear and have little or no perceptible residue,
              whether sprayed on or applied with felt-type markers. Used selectively on critical metal
              structures-aircraft, ships, trucks, elevators, metal treads, bridge supports--LMEs can cause a
              significant disruption and psychological distress.
          Light Detection and Ranging (LIDAR): LIDAA can be used for plum identification and sensor
              blinding. UDAA systems could be used to Identify the plume of covert drug manufacture.
          Supercaustics: Based on superacid technology, supercaustics can be millions of times more
              caustic than hydrofluoric acid. A round that delivers jellied superacids could destroy optical
              systems, glass blocks, or vision blocks.
          Calmative Agents: Calmatives, or sleep agents, mixed with DMSO (quickly delivers chemicals
              through the skin into the bloodstream) could be used in riot control, counterinsurgency, or even
              in selected hostage situations.
          Anti-Traction Technology: Anti-traction lubricants delivered by aircraft or human agents can render
              railroads, ramps, runways and stairs ineffective for limited periods of time.
          Polymer Agents: Polymers in burst munitions could foul air-breathing engines. Polymer adhesives
              can also effectively glues equipment in place or keep it from operating.
          Combustion Alteration: Internal combustion engines can be disrupted through special munitions.
              These munitions would temporarily contaminate fuel or change its viscosity to degrade engine
              performance.

           ARMY EXPLORING LESS THAN LETHAL TECHNOLOGIES

               Challenged by the Defense Department, the Army's Armament Research,
           Development & Engineering Center (ARDEC) has launched a series of research
           programs that can effectively render the enemy incapable while minimizing
           collateral damage.
               Through the Low Collateral Damage Munitions (LCDM) Programs, the Army hopes
           to develop technologies that can disable, dazzle or incapacitate aircraft,
           missiles, armored vehicles, personnel and other equipment without causing
           extensive collateral damage. In general, the Army hopes the program payoffs will
           include:
               • Increased tactical flexibility in future conflicts;
               • Dramatic performance enhancements beyond those obtainable from
                 conventional bullets and bursting munitions;
               • Low collateral damage capability/minimization of loss of life;
               • Increased hit/incapacitation probability through line-of-sight projection,
                 eliminating current ballistic trajectory; and
!At.;HCAL TECHNOLOGY, February 3, 1993                                                             Page 5


    • Improved logistics/soldier load with bullet-less systems .
    The LCDM program will explore non-conventional means of incapacitating
unarmored/armored materiel and protected/unprotected personnel . ARDEC, located
at Picatinny Arsenal, N.J., is managing the program using RDT&E 6.2 funds. Some
of the current programs are listed below:


                              Low Collateral Damage Munitions Programs

 Acoustic Beams and Bullets: Through Broad Agency Announcement DAAA21-91-BM 1, Scientific
    Applications and Research Associates of Huntington Beach, Calif., was contracted to develop a
     high power infrasonic (very low frequency) acoustic beam weapon and investigate methods of
    projecting non-diffracting, high frequency, acoustic bullets.
         The beam weapon will be a piston or detonation driven pulser which forces compressed air into
    an array of impedanced matched tubes to generate an infrasonic wavefront. Infrasonic waves have
    shown a coupling effect in small enclosed volumes which presents an interesting potential offensive
     capability against bunkers and vehicles.
         Acoustic bullets could be given increasing powers (e.g., capable of inflicting a discomfort,
    incapacitation or lethal effect). The bullet would be emitted from a 1-2 meter antenna dish.
 Optical Munitions: The Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) is performing exploratory development
    of optical munitions designed to disrupt enemy sensors. Both isotropic and directed radiator
    optical munitions have been demoastrated by the New Mexico research facility. The isotropic
    radiator is a multi-directional broad-band visible light source generated by high explosive shock
    heating of an Inert gas. The directed radiator is a similar unidirectional technology. In FY-92 funds
    were provided to conduct a design trade-off study and provide a conceptual baseline configuration
    for an electrically pumped laser module.
 High Power Microwave (HPM) Projectiles: An investigative effort through LANL exploring the
    feasibility of a high power microwave projectile with primary emphasis on determining the optimal
    microwave generator.
 Pulsed Chemical Lasers: In this approach, energy from a pulsed laser will generate a hot, high
    pressure plasma in the air in front of the target surface, creating a blast wave which will result in
    variable, but controlled effects on materiel and personnel. Initial tests achieved the low energy
    levels sought and support continued investigations.
 Target Effects Analyses: In support of both the broadband radiator and laser munitions programs,
    ARDEC has funded the Armstrong Laboratory at Brooks Air Force Base, San Antonio, Texas, to
    assess the blo-effects of these devices (i.e., equating levels of bio-effects to mission defeat
    capability In various combat scenarios).
 Chemical Compounds: In conjunction with the Chemical Research, Development and Engineering
    Center (CRDEC), ARDEC is pursuing programs to evaluate chemical compounds defeat criteria for
    anti-materiel/anti-transport field applications. It is evaluating no collateral damage vehicular and
    personnel envelopment techniques. Advanced riot control agents are being evaluated for rapid
    Immobilization, limited duration with no permanent side-effects.
 Modular Warheads: Harry Diamond Laboratories are exploring modular warheads that can be used to
    deliver a family of non-lethal, low collateral damage, or conventional warheads.

    JCS CONTINUES EVALUATING ROLES-MISSIONS

        In a reversal of an earlier recommendation that Air Force Combat Search and
    Rescue (CSAR) assets be transferred to the Army (Tactical Technology, Jan. 20,
    p. 1), the Joint Chiefs of Staff (JCS) are now recommending each of the services
    retain responsibility for CSAR operations.
        According to a draft copy of a JCS report, "Roles, Missions, Functions of
    the Armed Forces of the United States," the chiefs are continuing to refine the
    future of the military in a post cold war environment. A draft copy of Chapter 3
    was obtained by sister publication, Defense Daily. Highlights of the ·new draft
    include:
        • Merge the Continental United States (CONUS)-based forces of Forces Command
    (FORSCOM), Atlantic Fleet (LANTFLT), Air Combat Command (ACC) and Marine Forces
    Atlantic (MARFORLANT) into one combatant command. LANTCOM asswnes
    responsibility for training and preparing CONUS-based troops for deployment.
        • All Navy EP-3E and Air Force RC-135 signals intelligence (SIGINT) aircraft
    are fully committed and should be retained. This stops previous efforts to do
    away with the Navy's EP-3E program.
        • The contingency and expeditionary forces of the Army and Marine Corps are
    complementary, provide decision makers with valuable alternatives, and should be
    retained. Continue to review the possibility of further reductions in the Army's
    light infantry forces.
        • The Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) should complete its study of
    intelligence organizations and production centers and implement those measures
    that increase responsiveness to operational commanders and reduce costs.
