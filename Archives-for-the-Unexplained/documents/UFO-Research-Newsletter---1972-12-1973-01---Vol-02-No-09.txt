                                    THE DELPHOS CASE

                                By Dr. Robert F. Creegan
[Dr. Creegan is a philosophy professor at the State University of New York at Albany
   and a member of the National Enquirer's UFO reward panel.  In this exaZusive arti­
   aZe, he disausses the aontinuing investigation of the DeZphos aase (see UFO Re­
   search Newsletter, Vol. I, No. ZZ, p. Z, and Vol. II, No. 2, p. 6, No. 3, p. 8,
   No. 4, p. 8, No. 6, p. 4, No. 7, p. 4, and No. 8, p. 6)]

     The broad outlines of the initial reports from Delphos, Kansas, are well known,
having been reviewed in this newsletter and in many other journals. A teen-aged farm­
er in Kansas reported seeing a luminous object approach the farm early·on the evening
of November 2, 1971, either land or hover within feet of the ground, and then move
rapidly away to the south, leaving a glowing circular area on the soil. When alerted,
the parents of the young man supposedly saw the object receding in the southern sky
and they also observed the glowing ring.  It later became apparent that the ring soil
was hydrophobic, that is, extremely resistant to water,·and incapable of supporting
plant growth. Many witnesses observed these phenomena, and there were a few who
claimed to have seen a bright object approach the general vicinity of the farm on the
evening of the sighting. Persons who touched the ring soil reported a numbness or
partial anaesthesia of body areas which had come in contact with the soil.
     Samples of the soil have been studied in one or more laboratories at the Univer­
sity of Arizona, Northwestern University, Utah State University, the State University
of New York at Albany, the University of Kansas, and the University of Missouri, and
also at various private laboratories in California and Illinois. In many cases, how­
ever, the names of the labs -- or of the scientists concerned -- are not for publica­
tion. All parties agree that the ring soil does indeed differ from surrounding soil
in a large number of non-random ways. The excess of calcium in the ring is especial­
ly great, but, besides some small and random differences, there are perhaps half a
dozen other, statistically significant differences. The difficulty is that no rea­
sonable relation has been established between these and any theory of the operation
of an ET vehicle -- or any other type of craft.
     The presence of fungus in the ring might account for the glow (which lasted only
a few days) since some fungi are bio-luminescent. The unwettability of the soil might
be caused by a waxy substance, seen under magnification. This may be basically a hy­
dro-carbon of some sort, although apparently not a well known wax or heavy machine
oil.
     There was no special radioactivity of the ring, nor any sign of ionizing radia­
tion. There is no evidence that any type of intense heating occurred, nor that the
ring area was subjected to unusual pressure.
     Those who have carefully investigated the circumstances of the sighting -- and
the records of the people involved -- are strongly inclined to deny that a hoax was
perpetrated. On the other hand, the laboratory results do not help to clarify the
character of any luminous object that may have visited the area. Unless evidence be­
yond any thus far obtained is introduced, all available theories concerning the inci­
dent at Delphos remain inconclusive.
