                    OUR   NEWS   AND   VIEWS

      Since the issue of the first edition of our magazine
early in December, the 'rains came' and almost forgot to
stop - Natal was soaked to the roots. Our vegetable crop
suffered and we had our losses, but through it we learned
that at the peak of the rainy season it was wise to plant in
small plastic bags under cover and plant out when the danger
of floods was over. So at this time of the year in future
our press-pot system will be in operation .

      The peach trees, although only 20 months old,didn1t at
all mind the continued dowsing they received and gave us a
plentiful supply of the most gorgeous rosy-red, juicy
peaches. Next summer, with more mature trees, we can un­
doubtedly look forward to a magnificent crop of peaches and
as peach trees seem to be so happy in our valley, Terry -
he's our 'tree man' - intends planting lots more and will
also experiment with nectarines and apricots.

      To nurse the soil back to health, to the degree that
it can respond once more to the life-giving radiations of a
cosmic nature, we have commenced a massive compost-making
programme. Guy plans to have approximately 50 tons of
compost available each month, and as 70% of our compost
consists of grass and weeds, Kees put on his thinking cap
and welded together a two cubic metre grass catcher which,
when full, releases the grass in a tight roll which is
picked up by a 'home made' front end attachment to a tractor.
In this facet of the work Dan will be Guy's right hand.

      Recently our members have swelled to include Dan and
Eve from the Cape, Victor who will be with us until October
when he commences his studies at the Agricultural College,
Errol who is a Yoga teacher from Johannesburg and Gareth
and Sue who also hail from the golden city. With this
increase in our ranks and the growing weekly and weekend
courses, we are needing more accommodation; with the approach
of winter tents could afford a little too much. fresh air
and so plans are afoot for the building of thatched rondavels.

      Some interesting Group seminars have been taking place

As we now have an attractive and efficient Housekeeper in

         FROM THE AGE OF LEO TO THE AGE OF AQUARIUS
           App roximately 10 000 B. C. to 2 000 A.D.
       (Compil ed from 1 T oward s Aquarius• by ·Vera Reid)

      The twelve glyphs of the Zodiac may be considered as the
formula of a sequential life process applicable to every
manifestation. As one Age is followed by another with the
outpouring of its particular energies, so man, the microcosm,
reflects the cosmic macrocosm.
      From approx. 10 000 B. C. to 8 000 B. C. the Sun, by pre­
cession of the Equinoxes, was in the Sign Leo, the Lion.
Leo is the Sign of vital power and spiritual kingship; it is
ruled by the Sun, the source of light, the giver of life,
the symbol of the Divine Father, the heart of the Solar
System.
      It is a Fire Sign and the Divine Flame stirred the heart
and spirit, stimulating the human mind by the upward surge
of spiritual energy. During this Age the spirit of the Sun
was worshipped, and the Father principle (Spirit) of
divinity dominated.
      The Sun moved into Cancer, the Crab, approx. 8 000 B. C.
and for the next 2 000 years influenced the evolution and
growth of man.
      Cancer is the Sign of the Mother, family and home; it
is also associated with the inner soul life and the emotional
and psychic faculties. Its outstanding characteristics are
of a nourishing, sustaining, reproductive and assimilative
nature. The Mother principle of Divinity therefore held sway
during this Age. Cancer is ruled by the Moon, and the Great
Mother Goddess, symbolised by the Moon, was worshipped.
Being a Water Sign, the Great Mother had a tremendous emot­
ional impact on her •children•, who, close to her breast
felt safe and sustained. Ancient rock shrines, e. g. Mount
Vaticanus and the Ka-aba were places of assembly from ancient
times (rocks are the haunts of crabs) . Today we refer to this
Mother Principle (matter) as the forces of nature, or simply
Mother Nature (the kingdom of the Devas and Nature Spirits) .
      When the Sun moved into the Sign Gemini (6 000 B. C. to
4 000 B. C.) the psychic and emotional qualities of Cancer

were replaced by those of an entirely different character.
Gemini ( The Twins ) is an Air Sign, which is mental, and so
reasoning power, ingenuity and invention came to the fore,
further stimulated by Mercury, planet of the intellect and
ruler of Gemini.
      Duality was the outstanding feature of this Age, as
both Gemini and its polar opposite, Sagittarius, are dual
Signs. Man, child of the Earth, is a creature of dual
nature ( consciousness ) born in the union of spirit and
mattero Traces of dual organisation in both social and
religious life existed in ancient civilisations and appear
to have formed the background for a set of religious ideas
founded on the worship of dual entities, e. g. the ancient
Assyrian gods Nebo and Tasmut his wife. Pairs of gods,
identified with sacred pillars, are frequently found at the
gates of Babylonian, Assyrian and Egyptian temples. Man,
composed of an immortal Soul, functioning in a mortal body,
is constantly torn in opposite directions - the higher is
ever at variance with the lower; unity between the two is
the ideal to be achieved.
      In each month ( Age ) of the Great Year, spiritual evolu­
tion is quickened and redirected by the teaching of the great
Initiates. The life and words of Rama Chundra is contained
in the epic 'Ramayana•, and although it was only written
down in its present form in the fourth century B. C. , there is
no doubt that it was taught and transmitted orally for many
thousands of years. Rama, an incarnation of Vishnu, the all­
pervading supreme creator, is said to have lived and ruled as
king on earth. Duality is the theme of the 'Ramayana• and
describes the struggle for victory between the forces of
good and evil.
      The age of Taurus the Bull, 4 000 B. C. to 2 000 B. C.
corresponds to that stage in individual development when
the child seeks security in possessions and the collecting
of whatever seems precious to him. During this Age, Egypt
rose to the height of her glory and power, and gives the most
complete picture of contemporary life and thought and is an
example of the racial stage when the accent was on material­
ity. The imprint of Taurus can be seen in the size and
magnificence of the Egyptian buildings, and Scorpio, its
polar opposite, in the preservation of the body after death.

                 THE QUEST FOR DIVINE LIGHT

      There was a time in the history of the race when the
gods stole from man his divinity, and, meeting in high con­
clave, sought to decide where to hide that which they had
stolen. One god suggested that they hide it on another
planet, for there man could not find it, but another god
arose and said that man was innately a great traveller and
they had no guarantee that, eventually, he might not find
his way there. 11 Let us11, he said, 11hide it in the depths
of the sea, at the bottom of the ocean, for there it will
be safe11• But again a dissenting voice was heard, and it
was pointed out that man was a great natural investigator
and that he might some day succeed in penetrating to the
deepest depths as well as the greatest heights. So the
discussion went back and forth until at last one bright god
arose and said: 11Let us hide the stolen jewel of man's
divinity within himself, for there he will never look for
it11• Whereupon the conclave broke up happily as the gods
realised that a truly inaccessible place had been indicated,
and for aeons it seemed as if the light hidden in man was
lost for ever.
