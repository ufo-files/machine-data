      THE PRA INTERVIEW WITH DR. JOHN E. MACK (Exclusive)

At the personal invitation of Dr. John E. Mack, y our editor
attended a meeting at Claridges Hotel, London on August Bank
Holiday Monday 28th August between Or Mack and the Directors df
the magazine "Flying Saucer Review ".

(Or Mack was due to return to the USA the following day , as he
was on the final leg of a tour which had included Africa, Italy
and Switzerland) .

Or John E. Mack is Professor of Psychiatry, Harvard Medical
School at the Cambridge Hospital and Founding Director of the
Center for Psychology and Social Change. He is a Pulitzer Prize
winner and will be best know in UFO circles for his recent book
"Abduction: Human Encounters With Aliens ".

It was this book and his television appearances that landed him
in 11hot water " with the authorities at Harvard University.
This matter which has now been concluded was referred to during
the discussion with y our editor.  An abbreviated transcription
is given below.

Or John E. Mack:-

I think I should make it clear how I came into this. I do not
consider myself a 'ufologist', I don't consider myself an
expert in this field. I started doing this abduction work on
UFO's and I'm still not up to what is going on.

My own involvement with this is quite recent,         I  became
interested in the abduction phenomenon as a clinician, because
it seemed to me that if we were dealing with some sort of human
experience.  As far as I could tell after working with a number
of these cases, all the indications were that there was some
inexplicable relationship with another intelligence.

In the course of my work it is interesting that I would see the
type of delusions that people have and they do not have alien
or UFO delusions, it is just not the type of thing that people
get delusions about.
I've been happy to discover that in many        British groups
interests are much broader than American groups in terms of
religious and spiritual dimensions.  I think the technology and
what they are (UFO's) , this information is very important, but
what is it all about?

I managed to antagonise just about everybody in the United
States!   UFO groups hoped that I would give this sub ject
scientific respectability by demonstrating once and for all
that the evidence and facts showed that these people were
physically removed in each and every occasion, which is not
true, nor is it the main point.

Of course I antagonise all my medical colleagues because I
can't use their controlled study methodology to prove anything
either, so I managed to get the worst of both worlds.

I think it is a mistake for Budd Hopkins and David Jacobs to
claim that in each and every instance there is what Budd calls
a "out of the house experience", but I don't think the evidence
supports that.

Sometime the people are actually observed to be missing and
sometimes the family observe that they (the 'abductee') is in a
trance and still in their bed.    But on the other hand I have
cases where everyone is searching around, where the person has
disappeared for several hours.

I have a case, not in my book, where a woman, she and her room­
mate were abducted at the same time.         The room-mate was

burden of the question should be on the asker, not on me!

The only reason that anyone pays any attention to me, when you
get right down to it, is the fact that I have quite impeccable
credentials as a psychiatric clinician, I can talk to a person
in depth and say "I believe it's this and not this " and that's
