          A Retrospective Review of the UFO Habeas Corpus Petition

                                     By
                              tarry W. Bryant *                                           NOVEMBER   1984


      On July 7, 1983, a UFOlogical and legal milestone, of sorts, occurred in Federal District Court for
the District of Columbia.

       My "Petition for writ of Habeas Corpus Extraterrestrial," by which I sought to have the bodies of
downed "saucer" crew members turned over to the Court for-appropriate disposition, produced a challenge
t hat to this day echoes around the world.

      The case resulted in widespread radio and TV news coverage, including talk-show sessions and live
interviews -- plus all manner of print-media treatment. And it set the stage for what's to come in the
next several months: a freedom-of-information suit to compel the Government to release all its informa­
tion on the various alleged incidents of UFO crash-landings/retrievals. Here, we're talking about such
records as autopsy reports, metallurgical analyses, photographs of the wreckage and of the recovery
site, and intelligence estimates of the craft's origin and mission.

DOOMED AT THE OUTSET

      Of course, no-one, least of all I, expected the petition to survive any longer than it takes to
scan its three pages of text and three exhibits. That the Court chose even to accept it for filing
would have been enough to enter it into the record books as the first-ever legal show-cause challenge to
the Government's paternalistic news management in the Politics of UFOlogy. The ramifications of that
challenge still are multiplying, and are discussed later on in this review.

      What only remotely could have become the •suit of the centurY" were it to have had the services of
a bonafide attorney soon became the shot-in-the-dark muffled 'round the world by the Government's s�eedy
motion for dismissal and the Judge's almost equally swift granting of that motion. Thus died my faint
hope of keeping the case alive so that it could feed on its own publicity and thereby extend its poten­
tial for mobilizing public sentiment against this aspect of the "Cosmic Watergate.•

      Acting as my own counsel, I was just as surprised as the U. s. Attorney to find Judge Oliver Gasch-
's solicitous guidance exuding from the bench for the 20-minute status hearing on July 28. When I ex­
plained to him that I had received the Government's 8-page res�onse to the petition only five minutes be­
fore the convening of the hearing (2:00p.m.), he graciously allowed me about 5 more minutes to finish
reading it before I was to plead my case in that little courtroom nearly filled with news-media people
and friends /associates/onlookers.

genesis of the suit. lhy did tarry w. Bryant, long-time UFO activist, fierce civil libertarian, and
critic-polemicist in the realm of governmental abuse and repression, embark on such an admittedly quix­
otic quest?

      Well, I took as my first cue the response to a public-opinion survey conducted a few years ago by
the Washington, D.C.-based Fund for UFO Research (which, incidentally, had been instrumental in fUnding
the Appeals-Court-appealed FOIA case of Ground Saucer Watch versus U. s. Central Intelligence Agency).
lhen asked to weigh the .prior ity of various research �rojects proposed for the Fund's support, most re­

THE FIDS TAKE THE EASY WAY OUT

      Unfortunately, the Government's arguments on legal technicalities precluded my intended motion for
discovery -- whereby the Government would have been directed by the Court to answer a list of interroga­
tories about the F.B.I. memo as well as about the Army Intelligence's secret requirement, back in Decem­
ber 1950, to operate under an "immediate high alert for any data whatsoever concerning flying saucers"
(a requirement, tersely documented in another F.B.I. memo, that figures heavily in the pet crashed-sau­
cer case of UFO researcher w. Todd Zechel).

      And, of course, Zechel's work, along with that of Leonard H. Stringfield and William L. Moore, fig­
ures as the third major factor in my decision to develop the petition.

      Each of these independent researchers is working from his own focus, via his own methodology, and
with separately cultivated resources and contacts. But they all are helping advance a common cause: the
�rinciple that the public has a right-to-know and the Government has the duty to tell. In the process,
they're laying the trail toward the smoking gun of the Government's coverun of UFO crash-retrievals. As
they chop through the undergrowth of rumors, wade through the quicksand of government/non-government dis­
information, and dodge the predators of public apathy, Congressional inaction, and personal constraints,
they expect to emerge from the jungle, either singly or collectively, with the final answers to these
near-eternal questions: what did the Government know about UFO crash-retrievals, ani when did it know
it?

TOWARD   THE FUTUiE

      What is the timing of that emergence? This question forms the crux of my decision to move forward
vith the petition. It's foot-in-the-door now has been translated into the forthcoming FOIA litigation
for release of the Government's crashed-saucer records•

     ·The strategy: (1) acquire at least one affidavit from a participant in or wit ness to at least one
UFO crash-retrieval incident; (2) file suit with a motion for discovery keyed to that case and to such
available documentary evidence as the March 22, 1950, F.B.I. memo; (3) keep the case alive through any
necessary appeals so as to serve as an incentive for other coverup principals/witnesses to come forward
with their accounts/evidence/testimony.

      Besides that approach, this suit would surely have a technical advantage over the ill-fated peti­
tion, for it would show the plaintiff's legal standing to sue and the Court's jurisdiction to hear the
case -- two elements that Judge Gasch found lacking in Round No. 1 of this David Vs. Goliath contest.
The strategy should reverse that "Catch-22" which required me to (1) show that J as petitioner was act­
ing on behalf of the retrieved UFO occupants and (2) specify the current location of the occupants so
that the Court could determine proper jurisdiction.

      Now, with the impending FOIA suit, the onus will be on the Government to exnlain the whereabouts
and status of the relevant records -- regardless of who files the suit or in which Federal District
Court it's filed.

       What's more, the precis of Stringfield's top six crash-retrieval cases, which I had hoped Judge

      Does this mean that all the effort spent on the netition case was for naught? Hardly. As J said

the decisionmaking process on such a vital public issue as UFO crash-retrieval information.
