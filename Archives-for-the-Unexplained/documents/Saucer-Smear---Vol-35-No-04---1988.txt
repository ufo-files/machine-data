 MISC. RAVINGS:
       We take typewriter in hand to say a few words, mainly of praise,
 about the latest UFO encyclopedia, called "International Who's Who in Ufol­
 ogy Directory", edited by UFO researcher Robert Boyd of Alabama. TWD pre­
 vious encyclopedias, by Margaret Sachs and Ronald Story, failed to catch
 our attention to the same degree (though we are listed in both), mainly
 because they included many entries about the UFO phenomenon itself, ra�her
 than sticking to personalities.
       Boyd has assembled 284 entries from all over the world, and includ­
 ing many people we have not heard of previously - especially the foreign­
 ers. Modestly we must admit that your editor is indeed one of these mere
 284 entries.
       Boyd explains that over 500 people were contacted, mainly on the rec­
 ommendation of a small group helping him with the task. Those who did not
 reply did not get into the book. Leading American ufologists who did not
 make it include Jerry Clark (ha! ), Major Donald Keyhoe, Leonard Stringfield,
 Budd Hopkins, Dennis Stacy of MUFON, George Earley, Jim Oberg, August C.
 Roberts, and many others.
       Boyd tells us that the book is primarily a tool for the people in the
 field to get to know each other. Addresses and phone numbers are provided
 when possible.
       Although in general the dead are not included, there are tributes to
 the late Jim Lorenzen (Coral's husband); Jim McDonald (though he died back
 in 1971); and Dr. J. Allen Hynek. On the touching side, part of the trib­
 ute to Jim Lorenzen is written by his only daughter, who has since died.
 On the absurd side, there is a photo of Dr. Hynek in a silly-looking arctic
 outfit, holding up a copy of one of his own books to the camera, and look­
 ing like a raving crackpot!
       A "statement of position" is included with most entries.   We   were
 surprised at the relatively few who stick by the extraterrestrial hypoth­
 esis. According to Boyd's count, 18 of the 284 are "opposed", i.e., skep­
 tics or debunkers; 89 are "positive"; 59 express no position; and 118 are
 "questionable", i.e., ambiguous, contradictory, etc., in their    remarks.
 Your editor lists himself as a ufological agnostic.
       This book is a worthy effort, and definitely worth the modest price
 of $8.95, which includes postage and handling. Boyd can be reached at P.O.
 Box 66404, Mobile, Alabama )6606, U.S.A., Earth! ....
