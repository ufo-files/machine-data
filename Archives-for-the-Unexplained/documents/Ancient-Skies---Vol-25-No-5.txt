   A few weeks ago, I received a telephone call from
Dr. Eenboom with exciting news. Captain Belting has
built a model of the wooden "bird" which is on dis-
play in the Cairo Museum in Egypt. (See Figure
5).While Egyptologists have insisted that the model
is only a child's toy, representing a bird, many
others have consistently questioned the interpreta-         Figure 2.
tion. Now, Peter Belting has proved that the "bird"
is an airplane'. Whoever made the museum model had
a thorough knowledge of modern aerodynamics. Peter
Belting simply copied the model in every aspect,
including the wing shapes, which have a perfect
aerodynamic profile. The wing span of his model is
1 meter 20. Belting's model made a perfect flight'.
   The unusual configuration of the museum model's
wings and wing end tips show them going down slight-
ly, rather than up as now used in modern jumbo jets.
But during the latest model plane competition in
Germany, an American model builder flew a model
with the wings bowed down, not up, and it performed
perfectly. Everyone is very excited about this de-
sign, because the Egyptian wooden "bird" is estima-
ted by the Egyptologists to be 2,100 years old,
which antedates the designs of Leonardo da Vinci by
some 1,600 years'.
   Dr. Eenboom contacted the Egyptian Ambassador in
Bonn, Germany who has invited him and Captain Bel-
ting to give him a demonstration flight of the
model. Naturally, the Egyptians are very interested
in this experiment.
   Dr. Eenboom explained that the model built by
