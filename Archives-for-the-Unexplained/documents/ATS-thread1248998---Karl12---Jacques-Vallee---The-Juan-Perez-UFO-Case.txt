    
    Within a three-week period in September 1978 there were
    four dramatic UFO cases in Venado Tuerto, a small town
    three hours southwest of Rosario, Argentina. In the first case,
    a young carpenter named Alberto saw seven objects and two
    beings on the ground. In the second case, a twelve-year-old
    boy named Oscar saw three objects and went inside one of
    them, interacting with a very tall man and a small robot. In the
    third case, a sixteen-year-old baker named Francisco felt
    intense heat from a luminous object during a blackout of the
    local transformer. And in the fourth incident, a twenty-three-
    year-old man who was driving at night was “teleported” over
    four miles and had to be admitted to a local clinic for
    chest pains.” – Jacques Vallee, Confrontations – A Scientists
    Search for Alien Contact, p. 121 (Ballantine Books, 1990) (6)

I rented it on Amazon Prime; no sign of any 'free' availability at the moment.

I was in the mood for this film despite not knowing much about the case; Vallee's inclusion
probably swayed it for me, and overall it's a beautifully constructed, gorgeously scored 78
minutes, even though there is no way of proving Perez's experience was physically real or an
early onset of the vivid dreams he has been having since 1978.

Whatever happened in that field, I have rarely seen someone so profoundly affected by a such
an alleged encounter. He withdrew from socialising and found communicating with animals
more fulfilling, but this filmmaker at least cured his loneliness and paranoia that he was alone in
experiencing the phenomenon. Indeed, hearing the wise words of his ancestors seemed to
cleanse the fear of being declared insane - they had accepted these events for generations as
natural contact between humans and the 'creator', at least in their interpretation.

I was oddly reminded of Officer Zamora's experience at Socorro in 1964. Although Zamora
never entered a craft, he saw white-clad 'occupants' who, with hindsight, arguably resembled
lab technicians. Could Perez have stumbled across an experimental project and seen US lab
men inside?

The glove aspect if his story sounds dubious, more so than Perez claiming he saw an
occupant cutting up animal flesh like a butcher - a variation on the cattle mutilation genre, for
sure! The old footage of Perez getting the date wrong by two years was also a bit
disconcerting. And when Perez was touching the craft's interior, the director was unwise to
include a jokey (I assume this wasn't actually heard!) refrain of the five infamous musical notes
from 'Close Encounters Of The Third Kind' (1977) which was still doing big business at the
time of this incident.

A beautifully made film with plenty of philosophical angles to explore, aided by Vallee's
contributions. The lack of firm evidence will aggravate some viewers but, as the OP in the
locked duplicate thread said, this is much more about People and the effects of UFO
experiences on their psyche, whatever the explanation behind them may be. Each case of this
kind must be considered on its own merits, and Latin cowboy Juan's case is more interesting
than most.
