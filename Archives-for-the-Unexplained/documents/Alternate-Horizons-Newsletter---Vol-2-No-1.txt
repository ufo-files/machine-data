 It only hits home occasionally.   We go through life, in saucers as in other
fields,  with a fee-ling bf remoteness most of the time.  But sometimes it hits
you right in the guts.

 Sometimes (I have made reference to it in the past as the "sense of weirdness"
that goes hand-in-hand with the saucer subject) it comes at twilight, looking
at the sky and out at the world generally in this brief, surrealistic time of
day.  It is there, but difficult to put into coherent terms.  Yet it is real;
real in objective terms.

 What is all this?  This fantastic subject we are dealing with.  How much of it
is true, and what does it mean?  Will we ever solve it?  There are places where
I have felt closer to a solution than I am sitting here at this typewriter.

 But just what is this all about?  We hear tales of the Hill family, of UFO all­
ey in South America, of Mothman in West Virginia, of terror on Long Island.
Some, no doubt, are bunk to one extent or another.  But the others?  What of
the others?

 Yet for the most part this doorway to the unknown remains unexplored.  Few
people, I venture to say, are even aware of more than a tiny fraction of all
this.  Of these, how many are really going out and working for a solution?   It
seems that there are damned few.  The few that there are seem so isolated that
what they are involved in is more of a personal search for truth, rather than
an organized study.  This, in itself, is severely hampered by the lack of org-
