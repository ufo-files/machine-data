  JERRY CLARK MISSES THE POINT, AGAIN:
        In his latest editorial for the International UFO Reporter (IUR), call­
  ed "Into the Mystic", Clark laments the fact that some ufologists who con­
  sider themselves "adventurous, forward-looking thinkers" have abandoned the
  extraterrestrial theory in favor of more complex ufological solutions which
  he mistakenly traces directly back to Meade Layne and his Borderland Sciences
  Research Associates (BSRA) of Californiao
        Meade Layne is long deceased, but BSRA, founded back in 1945 or 1946,
 ·still exists under a new director. It can claim to be the oldest UFO organ­
  ization in existence, though it deals not only with 4-D (or "etheric") fly­
  ing saucers but also many other "borderland" (or borderline?) concepts. The
  trouble is that it is doubtful that contemporary 4-D thinkers, such as John
  Keel, Jacques Vallee, Allen Greenfield, etc. have been influenced by BSRA
  in any way at all, if indeed they have even heard of it�
        In going beyond present-day science, no one is saying that UFOs are in
  any way miraculous entities not subject to the laws of nature� They     are
  merely saying that UFOs, like other frustrating mysteries (ESP, ghosts, etc.)
  seem to be unsolvable in the straitjacket confinement of our present scien­
  tific structure. And yes indeed, all these mysteries may (or may not)     be
  linked to each othero Why not?
        Clark himself used to believe in or at least take a great interest in
  mysteries other than UFOs (fairies, for instance:); but now that he has ma­
  tured (?) and become as respectable as he is capable of becoming, he sneers
  in his supercilious way at those who theorize beyond the accepted   norm�
  What a pity:
