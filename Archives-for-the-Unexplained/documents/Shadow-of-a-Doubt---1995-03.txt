          WHEN SCIENTISTS FOOL THEMSELVES

                     Robert L. Park
                   Department of Physics
                   University of Maryland

  We are fascinated with science precisely because of its power
  to surprise us. Indeed, when first discovered,
  superconductivity and radioactivity appeared to violate
  immutable laws of science. Most incredible new scientific
  findings, however, are simply wrong. Often they are the result
  of subtle flaws in instruments or interpretation. Moreover,
  because even scientists tend to see what they expect to see, a
  false report by a respected colleague often carries other
  scientists along on the road to ignominy. This will be
  illustrated with examples ranging from Joe Newman's "energy
  machine" to "cold fusion." Current scientific controversies,
  such as the biological hazards of power line fields, will be
  examined for similar characteristics.

  ROBERT L. PARK is Professor of Physics at the University of
  Maryland and Director of the Washington Office of the American
  Physical Society. He writes a weekly electronic commentary on
  science issues that is widely read by scientists, science
  journalists and government officials, and is a frequent
  contributor on the Op-Ed pages of major newspapers and on
  television and radio news programs. A former chairman of the
  Department of Physics at the University of Maryland, he is
  author of more than a hundred scientific papers in experimental
  surface physics and is the founding editor of the international
  journal Applications of Surface Science.
