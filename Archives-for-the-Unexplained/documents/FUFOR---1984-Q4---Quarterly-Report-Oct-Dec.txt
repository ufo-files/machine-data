         Goudie told Dr. Maccabee that he had attempted to locate
         divers who woul d go down, but hadn ' t been able to do so last
       _ summer. It became apparent that this retrieval attempt was
         being organized by the divers themselves, and apparently
         they were doing it for publicity. Furthermore, they intended
         to carry out the dive whether or not they received support,
         but they . ~~uld definitely need money for a retrieval effort
         if they found something.
         ~o make a long story short, they took a large boat to the
         area on Sunday, December Jl. Their sonar picked up a rather
         large object on the bottom, so they dived ... AND FOUND ... IT (?).
         The object was described somewhat differently by the two divers
         who actually saw it, but the most cogent description is of a
         sort of upside down teacup with a saucer-like rim extending
         away from the edge of the teacup portion. It was buried at
         an angle in the mud, and the portion protruding was large
         enough to give the impression of an object ten or more feet
         in diameter. ·r his disc every was reported in the local press.
         The Executive Committee of the Fund agreed to appropriate $1,000,
         as allowed by the by-laws, for immediate UFO investigation,
         and--in this case, we hoped--retrieval. They went to the area
         the next day and found the object on sonar, but they didn't
         have the capability to pull it out of the water. Fearing that
         someone else would attempt to pull it out, they "committed"
         what to the experienced ufologist is a "mortal sin " : they
         left the object unattended for three days while they acquired
         a better boat! They didn't dare leave a marker on it for fear
         someone else would find it (remember, they had publicized the
         discovery), and they--and the Fund--couldn't afford to hire a
         boat to keep watch on it.
         On Friday, January 4, they dived again and ... IT WAS GONE!    (Not
         on sonar, either.)
         We are left with the recollections of the divers, the recollec-
         tions of those who saw the sonar device readouts (not paper
         readouts!), and some reddish substance that was rubbed off
         the object. Nothing more.
         A complete report of the Puget Sound Retrieval Effort will be
         made available sometime early in 1985. However, we can already
         state that this failed effort has shown us that we need a
         sizeable special fund for immediate UFO case investigation.
         If the Fund had been able to supply sufficient funds in this
         particular case (e.g., about $7,000), we could have converted
         this "treasure hunt" into a scientific expedition, and we
         would have, at the present time, underwater photographs of
         the object, numerous sonargrams of the object and surroundings ,
         physical measurements, etc., instead of what little information
         we actually do have. Furthermore, we would have arranged to

         example) or left "on its own"!
