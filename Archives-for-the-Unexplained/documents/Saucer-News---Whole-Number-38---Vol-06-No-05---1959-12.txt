SECRET PLACES OF THE LION - by George Hunt Williamson (Amherst Press, $4.50)
               - Reviewed by Y. N. ibn A1h�ron, B.D., S.T.M. -

            This is surely the most interes�ing book one could ever read, and
among the most informative. The number of cheap novels the author read as re­
search background for it would astound even the trigonometric Mayans.
             It is unfortunate that we cannot limit this review to a discussion
of the book's entertainment value. Williamson's books are always good for a
laugh or two, especially when we first pick them up. After completing the
first hundred pages, however, we invariably confront a host of hidden horrors
which the publishers have buried deeply within the text, hoping that the read­
er will not notice them until after he has paid the purchase price.
             In Mr. Williamson's last book, the "gaff" was anti-Semitic Fas­
cism. Elaborate stories of an International Bankers' conspiracy were concoct­
ed, in which it was said that certain prominent Jewish families were secretly
plotting to rule the world. In short, all the worst filth of the Hitler era
was revived.
             "Secret Places of the Lion", however, represents a new kind of re­
vival: All the hallucinated and unsubstantiated treasure stories of the past
ten thousand years are revived in a conglomerate history of the world, which
rests for its authority on records deposited at the non-existent Abbey of the
Seven Rays on Lake Titicaca, Peru. In the introduction, Mr. Williamson implies
that he was initiated into these ancient mysteries by a certain Brother Philip
of the Amethystine Order of the Seven Rays. Even those who would accept such
credentials, however, will per force withdraw their support of Mr.Williamson's
efforts when they learn that he has been disowned by the very Order of which
Brother Philip is said to have been a member. An affadavit, signed by the pub­
lic relations director of the real Order, has been printed in the Journal of
Correlative Philosophy for December, 1958.
             From this starting point, Mr. Williamson swings a broad arch into
the realms of fantasy, with only occasional references to his 11mystic records,
golden tablets, and Abbey Scrolls." His system of commentary is an ancient and
dishonorable one. He passes it off with the savoir faire and sleight of hand
common to the profession of prestidigitators. As soon as he has been able to
convince the unwary reader that any �aspect of an historical situation is
open to question, he uses the alleged discrepancy as an excuse for pulling
down the whole framework of historical fact upon which our understanding of
Man's past is built.
             As an added attraction, each of history's great names is made to
shoulder more than the burden of his own name and reputation. An indispensable
feature of the book is the Appendix entitled "Reincarnation Patterns of Iden­
tities� The author believes, for instance, that Pharoah Ankhen-Aton, the great
ruler who has received recent homage as the world's first monotheist, later
reincarnated as Socrates, Epictetus, Simon Peter, and William Lloyd Garrison.
Mr. Williamson makes no attempt to reconcile the disparate views of these sev­
eral personalities, nor even to justify the sequence he has established. Many
unanswered questions present themselves, each calling louder for solution than
the one before. In the end,. there can remain only a good-humored chuckle at
the ludicrous farce which would result from Ankhen-Aton occupying the bodies
of a devout athiest, a fatalist, a polytheist, and a freethinker, each in
their turn.

 up toward the end of his stay in Peru. The most graphic and putrescently rac­

             Drawing only from outdated and unreliable works of reference (in­

            This chronology is based solely on the alleged absence of monument­

             The facts are:
             (1-A) While Williamson has been able to upset the history of Is­

             (1-B) Williamson admits the historical authenticity of Hiram of

             (1-C) Hiram is prominently mentioned in the North Semitic annals,
 especially in those of Assyria, and his dates as given there are consistent
 with the Biblical account!
             (2-A) In the days following World War II, many jealously guarded

 tion in the Jordan Valley. One of the most ·important finds during this era was
 a signet ring from the year 1, 000 B.C. This date was unanimously agreed upon
 by scholars throughout the world because of the peculiar slant of the hand­
