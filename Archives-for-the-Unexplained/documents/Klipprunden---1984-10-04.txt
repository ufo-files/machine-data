    PROJECT HESSDALEN


•   We suppose you have had notice of the UFD-flap wich has been taking place in Norway, in a valley
    called Hessdalen. The valley is situated not far from Trondheim, and has a population of a couple
    of hundred people.

    The UFO-flap started at the end of 1981, when the Hessdalen population began seeing strange lights
    appearing in this mountains area. Same observations have been made rather close to the phenomenons
    (as close as 50 yards). Many observations have been made with a mountain as reference.

    One could see the phenomenons almost every night during the autumn and winter of 1982. Since
    then observations have been made during the same seasons of the year. A smaller amount of
    observations have been made in daylight, and the objects have been described as metallic or black.

    We suspect that same of the observations can be explained as piezoelectrical phenomenons. That
    theory was in a way confirmed during the first part of "Project Hessdalen", when a small earth-
    quake occurred at the end of February.
    But we do not believe that a 1 1 observations can be explained as piezoelectrical phenomenons.
    Many objects have for instance shown an appearantly intelligent pattern of movement, and at

    several occasions the phenomenons seemed to react to the environment •

    One object which has been seen and photographed on several occasions has a cigar shape with a
    big light at each end. A picture of this UFO can be found on the cover of UFO-SWEDENs magasine
    No.1 1984. When an airplane flies over the Hessdalen valley, the two end lights of the UFO
    disappear and a smal light appears in the middle of the object. When the airplane has passed, the
    two lights appear again; the object looks the same as it did befare the plane arrived.
    This has happened not only once but on several occasions, and we find it very hard to believe
    that a piezoelectrical phenomenon can behave like this.

    In June 1983, UFO-Norway and UFO- Sweden decided to start a project to document this flap, and try
    to find the explanation to the phenomenons. The project is devided into four parts:

    1. Measurements and photographing by advanced equipment in Hessdalen.

    2. Documentations of observations and photographs.
    3. Cataloguing, analyses and comparisons with other flap-areas.
    4. Conferences, seminares and hearings with scientists on an international level.
    An advisory cornmittee has been formed, consisting among others of Dr. J.Allen Hynek (USA),
    Dr. Harley Rutledge (USA), Mr. Paul Deverreux (UK) ,     Prof. Jens Tellefsen (Sweden) and
    Prof.Dr. Finn Ravndal (Norway)

In the autumn of 1983 information was sent out to the population in Hessdalen and the nearby area.
We asked the people to help us on this project, and we got a good response. They were anxious to
find explanations for these phenomenons. They also were disappointed that no real inerest was
paid from any governmental officials.

The first part of the project "in the field" started in January/February 1984. For ane month we
had an almost complete around-the-clock watch in Hessdalen. We also had advanced equipment stationed
in the valley, including for instance radar, seismograph, magnetometer, radiation detectors,
IR-viewer and a radio-frequency spectrum analyser. We also had cameras with spectrum-filters, but
unfotunately we were not able to take any photographs of the phenomenons at close distance with
this filter, but we did get a couple on lang distance. These are now being analysed.

One of the most interesting observations during this month, was a radar reading of an object
travelling 18.000 miles per hour. This was also observed by human eye.

We think this first part of the project (Final Technical Report, Part 1, will be available soon),
was a step in the right direction, but because of limited economical resources (among other things)
it was far from perfect. We were granted help from two universities concerning advanced equipment,
but the financial contributions were made by UFO- Norway and UFO-Sweden in addition to individual
donations, and were far from sufficient. We need to find other means to finance the next part of
the project; the"field part" and the other parts described above. This is why we now turn to the
Fund for UFO-Research.

The first part of the project is to be continued this winter. The other three parts are of course
continiously taken care of.
The estimated need for the project during the winter 1984-85 is approximately $ 12.000.-.
