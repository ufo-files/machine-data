       DAVE SCHROTH writes the following letter, which we had to delay for
several issues because of lack of Space:
       "In his letter in 'Saucer Leer', Jerry Clark argues that Phil Klass
cannot be taken seriously. Coming from someone as credulous as Clark,
that suggestion is a real knee-slapper. Apparently Jerry sees no rea­
sons why he cannot be taken seriously. I will therefore name a few:
       "(l �Clark has written that the 1953 disappearance of an Air Force
jet over Lake Superior 'is undoubtedly as authenticated an act of hostil­
ity (by malevolent saucer aliens) as can be found anywhere'. ('Flying Sau­
cer Review', July-Aug. 1968, p. 21). How Clark knows this is a mystery.
In fact, Story's 'Encyclopedia of UFOs' (which Clark has praised) states
(pp. 197-198) that it is impossible to determine the cause of the jet's
disappearance because crucial information is not available in official re­
cords on the incident. Clark's con jecture about hostile UFOnauts is thus
about as logical as the claim of Bermuda Triangle mythmakers that Flight
19 vanished into another dimension. Clark failed to consider that the
jet's disappearance may have had a simple, non-Ufological cause, such as
a collision with birds.
       "(2) Regarding the Merkel, Texas, case in which a little man was
seen descending a rope suspended from a mysterious airship, Clark wrote
in 1975 that 'its occurrence amid the 1897 airship flap should prove con­
clusively that we are dealing with phenomena whose full implications bog­
gle the mind'. (Clark and Coleman, 'The Unidentified', p. 159). Unfortu­
nately, what it did prove is that we are dealing with Ufologists whose
gullibility knows no limits: Two years later Clark announced that the Mer­
kel case had been determined to be a hoax. ('Proceedings' of the    1977
FATE UFO Congress, p. 340).                                  .
       "(3) Besides promoting UFO cases like these that are as 'solid' as
Jello, Clark has endorsed the reality of such far-out wonders as (a) UFOs
landing in Americans' back yards; (b) paraphysical UFOnauts who seem both
to exist and not exist; (c) close encounters with naked UFOnauts; (d)
'fairies' cavorting throughout the British Isles; and ( e) a UFO 'Phenom­
enon' much too devious and perverse to be comprehended by mere human be­
ings. The only problem is that no one has produced a shred of evidence
for the existence of any of these things.
       "(4) Clark has written that 'it is impossible' for a person under
hypnosis to lie when 'in the deepest trance state'. ('UFO Report', Aug.
1976, p. 52). Such a proposition is deep nonsense. In fact, even Dr. Leo
Sprinkle - a full-fledged UFO proponent who once taught Clark how to hyp­
notize a person - has repudiated the myth of the impossibility of lying
while under hypnosis. (See Allan Hendry's 'The UFO Handbook', pp. 172-
1'(3).
       "Such are a few of the inanities propounded by sophisticated Ufolo­
gist Jerry Clark.
       "And we're supposed to take this guy seriously??? "

Dear Jim:

 -I've decided to come out of the closet at last. The National Enquirer
  editors frowned on any outside activities and suspected me of being a
  nut anyway for taking UFOs seriously. A few years back I wrote a letter
  to the editor of Editor and Publisher, the newspaperman's journal, de­
  fending UFO coverage--and a long time later one of my editors mentioned
  it while listing some of the dumber things I had done as a reporter for
_the Enquirer.. After that I wouldn't even let the Enquirer's employe news­
  paper use my name or picture even though I had some fairly exciting ad­
  ventures while chasing UFO stories.

All of this came to an end late in June when the Enquirer invited me to
seek my destiny elsewhere, after 8 years, and I accepted as graciously
as possible. My only real regrets (other than losing a nice steady in­
come) was loss of the use of the telephone (I kept in touch with people
literally around the world) and the Xerox machine.

So, now I am free to say what I please. And it pleases me to say that
probably the brightest spot in all of UFO research, etc., today is your
Saucer zine. It not only helps me keep up with what is going on out
there but it also helps me keep from taking myself too seriously. It
really makes me think twice about opening my mouth about anything in
the UFO business, but it won't stop me. I know that when the day comes
that I put my own stuff into print in one form or another, I had better
be prepared to defend myself. Phil Klass and his frieRs are the least
of my concern because I'm sure there will be plenty o�people on the
pro-UFO side who will be ready to tear something apart. Which is no doubt
the way it should be.

For the time being, instead of trying to go back to newspaper work, which
I did before joining the Enquirer, I am going to try to stay in UFO re­
search full time. At the moment I am writing a book (who isn't), and
have at least two more m·mind. I also have several other projects I am
working on, and there is a strong possibility of a new national UFO maga­
zine's starting up before the end of the year that I would be associated
wi�h in some capacity.

I just returned from· a two-week trip to Brazil, where I did research for
my book, including newt UFO investigations as well as follow ups on cases
I studied several years back. Most of this will appear in my book. I can
say that UFO activity is almost epidemic in certain regions of the Amazon
right now. I spent four days there and would like to go back for an ex­
tended stay. I had more adventures than I care for--three near crashes
on landings or takeoffs in our Cessna 172 and our engine fortunately
blowing apart just before we took off for a 300-mile flight over the
jungle. Twenty minutes later   and they would have been searching for us
yet.

Anyway, please keep me on your list of non-subscribers at this address:
4623 Holly Lake Drive, Lake Worth, FL. 33463. I really enjoy getting
your newsletter and you are free to use any part of this letter that
you wish.

Cheers,
