The Winnipeg Sun - Manitoba, Canada…
20th May 2006…
Planetarium's UFO show worth a close encounter…
Our All-Time Favourite Aliens…
By Lindsay Ward…

   It had been a while since we'd sat back and mulled over our chances of encountering extraterrestrials. We think it was whenever they last re-ran that Simpsons Treehouse of Horror episode where those two green ET-octopus dudes abduct Homer and soak him in rum.
   So, seeing as how The X-Files have been closed for years and sneaking into Area 51 is hardly worth getting shot at, the new Planetarium show, Is Anybody Out There? seemed like a viable source for an otherworldly fix.
   While most Planetarium shows are exclusively made and played in Winnipeg, this joint production between Vancouver's HR McMillan Space Centre, Calgary's Telus World of Science, Planetarium de Montreal and our own space hub is opening around the country this weekend.
   In Bill Nye fashion, the documentary avoids science-related snores by injecting corny humour like cute cartoon aliens singing Purple People Eater - which, like Spongebob, draws in the stoners and kiddies in one shot.
   And to keep the rest of us interested, a plenitude of pop culture references (Alf, Marvin, campy '50s alien movies) and soundtrack artists (David Bowie, Pink Floyd, OLP) snazz up the graphically aided explanations and scientist testimonies - even  though some of the testimonies are pretty snazzy themselves.
   "We might in fact all be Martians," declares one expert after narrator Dave Kelly states life on Mars might have transferred to Earth back in the day; another pro makes the bold prediction we will find E.T. by 2025.
   The show's sweetest eye candy is the Haughton Crater footage Manitoba Museum audiovisual specialist Hans Thater travelled to Nunavut to capture and, of course, the Planetarium's signature animated starry sky.
   Yep, lots to see - but what about that title question? Sure, we learn extremophiles (organisms that can form and live in extreme temperatures) do exist and alien-government treaties don't but when the show's 36 minutes are up, we're still left wondering. But, really, isn't that the best part? Cue the X-Files theme song.
   Is Anybody Out There? shows Saturdays and Sundays at 1 p.m. and 3 p.m. Admission is $5.50 or $20 for the family (up to four kids and two adults).
Lindsey Ward
