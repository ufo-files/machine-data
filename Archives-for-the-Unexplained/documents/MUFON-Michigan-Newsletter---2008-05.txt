                THE CIA BREAKS ITS SILENCE ON UFOs
                                                   Don Berliner, © 2007

          In the summer of 1997, the private UFO community was trying to come to grips with two huge, poorly thought out reports
issued by the U.S. Air Force. They constituted its desperate attempt to deal with the growing public interest in the Roswell crash
which resulted from stories of unusual wreckage and even alien bodies said to have been discovered and quickly spirited away.
          The Central Intelligence Agency chose this time to end decades of silence on the subject with its own poorly reasoned re-
port. This one did little more than challenge the Air Force’s unwanted leadership in the race to unintentionally raise the stature of
UFOs. While clearly intended to do away with the subject, in reality it did quite the opposite.
          For several decades, the CIA had insisted that its only official involvement with UFOs was sponsorship of the three-day
“Robertson Panel” meeting of scientists in January, 1953. Even when faced with declassified copies of its own documents showing
it had been involved in the UFO mystery from 1949 through at least the mid-1970's, the CIA refused to budge from its public posi-
tion.
          It was only after a nervy flying saucer fan publicly challenged CIA Director James Woolsey to look into his agency's UFO
activities that it began to admit to its long-term interest. The first 1997 semiannual unclassified edition of the CIA's "Studies in In-
telligence", published in June of that year, includes an article by Gerald Haines, historian of the National Reconnaissance Office, an
even more secretive organization which controls spy satellites. Entitled, "CIA's Role in the Study of UFOs, 1947-90", it covered
only slightly more than what was already known through long-declassified documents.
          Then it stumbled badly, adding a highly imaginative tangle of the alleged connection between sightings of U.S. Air Force
spy planes (Lockheed U-2s and SR-71s) and UFOs. According to the CIA, over half of all officially-recorded reports of UFO
sightings from the mid-1950's through the end of Project Blue Book in late 1969 were merely innocent misidentifications of these
spy planes. It then "admitted" that it had conspired with the staff of the Air Force's Project Blue Book UFO investigation to cover
up all these sightings of secret spy planes by dishonestly labeling several thousand of them "mirages", "ice crystals" and other at-
mospheric phenomena.
          Much of historian Haines' information apparently came from a censored and only recently declassified CIA study of U-2
operations (The Central Intelligence Agency and Overhead Reconnaissance: The U-2 and OXCART Programs, 1954-1974, by
Gregory W. Pedlow and Donald E. Welzenbach), and was repeated without question or qualification. While he is forced to shoul-
der part of the blame for some egregious errors, one must have sympathy for any historian forced to work within the constraints of
such secrecy-obsessed organizations.

          Shortcomings of the “Spy Plane” Explanation
          1. There is the flatly incorrect statement that immediately after the first test flight of the prototype Lockheed U-2 spy plane
on August 4, 1955, there was "a large increase in UFO sightings", which the CIA presumably felt supported its claim of a direct,
cause-and-effect relationship. Since the CIA later admitted it used the data from files of the USAF’s Project Blue Book UFO inves-
tigation, it has no excuse for its atrocious arithmetic.

number of sightings reported during the month immediately following the first U-2 flight, as compared with the month before: down
from 89 to 67. And there even was a small decrease in sightings when six-month periods are compared: down from 326 to 316.
           2. Both the subsonic U-2 and the later Mach=3+ SR-71 Blackbird were meant for just one type of operational flying:
straight-and-level at altitudes well over 70,000 feet, for many hours at a stretch. Under such conditions, they are invisible to the
naked eye from the ground. Far larger jumbo airliners flying at half that altitude are noticeable mainly when they are leaving con-
densation trails. Even if the spy planes could be seen, they would appear not as discs or ovals or spheres, as witnesses reported, but
as long, slender airplanes (U-2), and long, oddly-shaped airplanes (SR-71).
           About the only times such airplanes can be seen in flight are (1) during take-offs and landings at air bases which are as far
removed from prying eyes as possible; and (2) at air shows when there is no question about what is happening.
           3. Among the distinguishing and especially baffling characteristics reported for UFOs are their oft-described violent ma-
neuvers, such as right-angle turns at high speed, along with their ability to achieve shocking acceleration after prolonged and silent
hovering or slow flight. For a U-2 pilot at high altitude, even level flight is a real challenge, and so any maneuvering must be done
very cautiously. For an SR-71 at high speed and high altitude, a simple 180° turn requires close to 200 miles to complete. The en-
tire turn, therefore, cannot be seen from any single location on earth.
           As for great acceleration, no known type of airplane can increase or decrease its speed in flight so rapidly that it is more
than just barely apparent to the outside observer. This is true even when the maneuvers create g-forces that are great enough to
cause considerable discomfort for the crew and any passengers.
           4. The CIA doesn't even attempt to explain the 3,000+ reports filed with the Air Force of UFOs that were seen before the
first 500mph U-2 flew in 1955. Nor those and many hundreds more seen to fly at great speed before the first predecessor of the
SR-71 flew in 1962. Nor, for that matter, all those UFOs that have been radar-tracked at more than the SR-71's presumed top speed
of 2,500-2,700 mph. Or all the reports of solitary or formations of UFOs flying faster than Mach=1 over the continental USA,
which is permitted only under special circumstances.
           5. The CIA claims that it conspired with the staff of Project Blue Book to hide the spy-plane nature of half the 9,000 UFO
reports made between 1955 and 1969 by calling them obscure atmospheric phenomena. Had that been done as described, some
four-and-a-half thousand reports now in the permanent Project Blue Book files in the National Archives would be so labeled. In
fact, such explanations (justified or not) can be found on fewer than 100 reports for that period.
           Obviously, anything that appears to be a highly maneuverable, disc-shaped or oval object which displays striking maneu-
verability and/or acceleration cannot possibly be a misidentified U-2 or SR-71, despite the extravagant claims of the CIA. Nor, ex-
cept in extremely rare instances, can it be ice crystals or images shifted by mirages, neither of which display high speed, sharp ma-
neuvers, distinct shapes or well-kept formations. Nor can any image shifted by a mirage be seen more than 2/3 of one degree above
or below the observer’s horizon; hence there can be no such thing as a “high-altitude” or “overhead” mirage.

was able to distinguish the object as a delta shape about the size of a jet fighter, reported it as having much higher performance, and

                        BROOKINGS INSTITUTION REPORT
          The December 15, 1960 issue of The New York Times claimed that a Brookings Institution report said Earth's civilization
might topple if faced by a race of superior beings. Perhaps that report helped to shape to government policy of denial and cover-up
of sightings of unconventional flying objects.
          The following is a direct quotation from The New York Times. “Discovery of life on other worlds could cause the earth's
civilization to collapse,” a Federal report said today (December 14, 1960). This warning was contained in a research report given
to the National Aeronautics and Space Administration with the recommendation that the world prepare itself mentally for the even-
tuality. The report, prepared by the Brookings Institution, said “while the discovery of intelligent life in other parts of the universe
is not likely in the immediate future, it could nevertheless happen in time.” Discovery of intelligent beings on other planets could
lead to an all-out effort by earth to contact them, or it could lead to sweeping changes or even the downfall of civilization, the report
said. Even on earth, it added, “societies sure of their own place have disintegrated when confronted by a superior society, and oth-
ers have survived even though changed.”
          NASA paid Brookings $96,000 to prepare the report. The authors, headed by Donald N. Michael, also recommended fur-
ther study of other space activities including the diplomatic and propaganda effects. The study went on to say that if contact was
made in the next twenty years it would probably be through radio communications.
          The cover-up has gone on so long and has involved so many politicians that any immediate release is highly unlikely. The
most likely scenario of disclosure, other than an outright alien invasion, will be some leaks made through the groups trying to use
radio telescopes to pick up signals, because an announcement that a signal had been received from a body located at a safe distance
from earth, would have very little effect on the peoples of earth. Then the process of releasing more and more information over a
period of years would ensure that no one would get in trouble for their past misdeeds.

           Alpha Centauri Should Harbor Detectable, Earth-like Planets
                           ScienceDaily (Mar. 7, 2008)

          A rocky planet similar to Earth may be orbiting one of our nearest stellar neighbors and could be detected using existing
techniques, according to a new study led by astronomers at the University of California, Santa Cruz.
          The closest stars to our Sun are in the three-star system called Alpha Centauri, a popular destination for interstellar travel
in works of science fiction. UCSC graduate student Javiera Guedes used computer simulations of planet formation to show that
terrestrial planets are likely to have formed around the star Alpha Centauri B and to be orbiting in the "habitable zone" where liq-
uid water can exist on the planet's surface. The researchers then showed that such planets could be observed using a dedicated
telescope.
          "If they exist, we can observe them," said Guedes, who is the first author of a paper describing the new findings. The pa-
per has been accepted for publication by the Astrophysical Journal.
          Coauthor Gregory Laughlin, professor of astronomy and astrophysics at UCSC, said a number of factors converge to
make Alpha Centauri B an excellent candidate for finding terrestrial planets. The Doppler detection method, which has revealed
the majority of the 228 known extrasolar planets, measures shifts in the light from a star to detect the tiny wobble induced by the
gravitational tug of an orbiting planet. Factors that favor the use of this technique for Alpha Centauri B include the brightness of
the star and its position in the sky, which gives it a long period of observability each year from the Southern Hemisphere, Laugh-
lin said.
          Detecting small, rocky planets the size of Earth is challenging, however, because they induce a relatively small wobble in
their host stars. According to Laughlin, five years of observations using a dedicated telescope would be needed to detect an Earth-
like planet around Alpha Centauri B.
          Coauthor Debra Fischer of San Francisco State University is leading an observational program to intensively monitor
Alpha Centauri A and B using the 1.5-meter telescope at the Cerro Tololo Inter-American Observatory in Chile. The researchers
hope to detect real planets similar to the ones that emerged in the computer simulations.
          "I think the planets are there, and it's worth a try to have a look," Laughlin said.
          To study planet formation around Alpha Centauri B, the team ran repeated computer simulations, evolving the system for
the equivalent of 200 million years each time. Because of variations in the initial conditions, each simulation led to the formation
of a different planetary system. In every case, however, a system of multiple planets evolved with at least one planet about the size
of Earth. In many cases, the simulated planets had orbits lying within the habitable zone of the star.
          In addition to Guedes, Laughlin, and Fischer, the authors of the paper include UCSC postdoctoral researcher Eugenio
Rivera and graduate student Erica Davis, and Elisa Quintana of the SETI Institute. This research was supported by NASA and the
National Science Foundation.
