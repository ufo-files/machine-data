                  CATTLE MUTILATIONS AND UFOS-~
                           by Jerome Eden

Introduction--
     In the July 1980 issue of The SBI Report, the editors suggest
that I offer a 'rebuttal of David A. DeWitt''S lengthy article, "Sol-
ving the Mutilation Mystery." I accept this challenge for several
reasons. First, DeWitt 's article is another example of the in-
correct approach to the investigation of ~ scientific phenomena.
All scientific . ~owledge is based upon facts, not upon unsubstanti-
ated theories. , The true scientist first goes · about the painstaking
task of uncovering, examining and correlating all known facts within
the purview of his research, without bias or preconceptions. Then,
after examining his factual data, the true researcher develops a
theory, a hypothesis to explain all the known facts. If his hypoth-
esis is correct, the researcher· will be able to develop his work
with new facts. In ., short, his research will advance.
     The solidity of any new theory has a direct bearing upon the
weight of evidence supporting it. This is the inductive method
(Fig. 1) used by every responsible scientific researcher and every
law-enforcement officer. The more evidence brought to support the
theory, the stronger the theory--a relationship analogous to what
the base of a pyramid has to its peak.
