  N otes concern i n g su rveys
1. The circular areas of swirled crop are rarely circular. D ue to the time
which it takes to draw elipses to a high standard , most of the formations
have been drawn with averaged radii .


2. The centre of the swirl rarely coincides with the centre of the "circle" in
a genuine formation . Swirl circles are generally depicted by a small dot.


3 . Unfortunately some of the compass readings made i n the field were
found to be out by an .error of up to five degrees due to the lack of proper
equipment. It was possible to iron out much of this by triangulation but if
extremely accurate surveys are required it would be best to refer to the
higher-quality surveys executed by John Langrish .


4. It is quite possible that some of these formations were man-made with
garden rollers, string and planks of wood . It is equally possible that some
of them arrived by means currently quite unknownn to science. There is
however a position which states that no matter how these shapes arrived in
our fields, they constitute quite one of the most remarkable bodies of art
that has ever been witnessed by modem man .
