OPINION•   The finding has every appearance of being the nesting bed or lair of a
           .   .



female bear of the species.Ursus americanus, i.e., the common American Black Bear.
The small claw marks in the lower part of the fir appear to be those of newly born
cubs. The deep scratches in the madrone are most probably those of a mature bear;
they are typical of the territorial "declaration" of the Black Bear. The hair
ap.pears to be bear hair. The faeces is very bearlike in shape, size and contents.
The teeth markings on the stems found in the "bed" indicate the impressions normal­
ly made by the incisors of bear. ('In breaking these stems the bear uses only
the incisors and the stems are broken off with a single upward or vertical snap­
ping movement. )

APOLOGIES TO GEORGE F,      HAAS:    In the first issue of Manimals Newsletter
I stated concerning Haas' discontinued Bigfoot Bulletin: " Haas felt the
Bulletin was too much trouble for the information he was receiving in return,
and that much of what he was receiving should not be published in the Bulletin
because people who wished to kill a Bigfoot/Sasquatch might use the information
to accomplish that end. The resultant lack of pertinent news in the Bulletin
probably caused many recipients to lose interest in submitting news. "

George F. Haas has written 1 Sept. 1972 stating these statements are erroneous.
He says he greatly appreciated the information he was receiving, which enabled
him to build up one of the largest collections of Bigfoot material extant, but
felt the time, energy and money going into the Bulletin could be better spent
on personal and field research. George Haas recently returned after 2 months,
8 days in northern California searching for evidence (finding none) and plans   ·



many more such jaunts. ( Had we been able ·to carry on normal communications
during this summer project my misconceptions would likely have been corrected
before going to press. ) Haas also says my implication the Bigfoot Bulletin was
censored is wrong; information not printed was either c·onfidential or excluded
for lack of space. ( The same problem will likely confront this editor. )

My sincere and humble apologies to George F. Haas.
