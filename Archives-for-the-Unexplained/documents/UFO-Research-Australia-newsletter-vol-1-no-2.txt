                                                                 By Holly I. Goriss


     In this time of changes within the UFO research field in Australia, {this News­
letter being one of many}, I am going to take the opportunity to recap on a few
points for all of you active in this field.  Do I hear the cry - "We have heard it
all before".  True, you may have, but I think that there are some things we must keep
reminding ourselves of if we don't want to get careless and sloppy.

     Let us start with the fact that there are still a large number of people in this
country, {not to mention the rest of the world}, who regard the UFO phenomenon as a
flosinocity.  Some of these people may change their way of thinking if confronted with
irrafragable data, while others would not believe such things exist even if a UFO
allowed itself to be laboratory tested in front of them,             {suggesting, of course, that
you could coax one into a laboratory, and if indeed you could fit one in same}. This
is the type of narrow-minded thinking that has been the stumbling block of scientific
research for centuries so how can we expect to be free of it. People of this type will,
unfortunately, never change their thinking no matter how much data                 you produce to the
contrary.

     Do not waste your time on them, but instead use that time to further the cause.

     Then, a secondary role of UFO Research groups must be to educate the public on
"Unidentified Flying Objects" and their related subjects such as Astronomy, Aviation,
Meteorology, Photography etc.  After all, the public are people who are going to see
UFOs, and the more they know about such things the better the quality of their UFO
reports will be,        { and greater shall be the reduction in the number of misidentified
satellites, aircraft, stars, meteors, planets, etc. our investigators will receive -
much to their relief }.

     Let us move on.           Einstein once wrote:     "If A = success, then A = X � Y + Z,
when X   =   work, Y    =   play,     Z = keep your mouth shut", to which I add - until you have
all your data.         Never, ever, make a statement on any particular subject, {especially
UFO investigation},           until you have all your data.     This is not only unprofessional,
but can get you into trouble if and when you are proven wrong.              When it comes to UFOs,
the Media, certain Governmental bodies, and some "so called" UFO investigators are
guilty of this.  Do not allow UFO Research {Australia} to follow suit.

     Kahlil Gibran once said: " Say not - I have found the Truth - , but rather,
I have found a Truth".   Any decision we may come to, either personally or as a group,
as to the origin, propulsion, mechanics, occupants etc., of a UFO or the phenomenon as
a whole,     may change with further data.          What seems to be the answer today, may not be
the answer tomorrow.           Keep an open and non-biased mind on the subject.

     Then again, one of the pitfalls to the communication lies in that little phrase,
"It's obvious". What is obvious to A, alas, is by no means obvious to B, and is down­
right ridiculous to c.              Or, in other words, just because you may have what you think
is conclusive data, do not expect everyone to automatically see your point of view.
Because every single person thinks differently from every single other person, others
may draw different conclusion from the same data.              Keep an open mind    to this. Others
may have noticed something you missed, after all, none of us is infallible.                Of course,
if you do consider your conclusion to be the 'right' one, then make sure your argument
is logical, detailed, free from 'holes' and described in such a way that others can
understand your thinking.

     Now that you have all been reminded of the little things that make life difficult,
let us all get together and work at it.             The key word in this field being CO-OPERATION.
So get on your toes, get those minds open, co-operate and get researching.

      After the details of this sighting appeared in the local press, the general
thought was that the witness had seen the two planets - Jupiter and Mars - prominent
in the western sky in the early hours of the morning.

      This is definitely not the case since the objects were clearly different in
position, elevation, size, brightness and movement.

      The witness, whom we will call Mr A., is an engineer and spent 15 years in the
RAAF. It was from his home at Holloways Beach ( 7km north of Cairns) he observed the
two objects. Mr A's house is high-set with a verandah at the rear facing West and
giving a clear view of the mountains, approximately 8km distant.

THE SIGHTING

      Mr A. had risen early on the Wednesday morning, as he usually does , at 5.35 a.m.
He stepped onto the verandah to empty the kettle and noticed two large lights in the
sky to the west. The two objects, approximately 4 - 5 km from Mr A., were stationary
at about 20° elevation. They were about lOrnm at 'arms lenght ' in size and were intensly
bright, so much that Mr A. said he had to hold his hand up to look at them for any
lenght of time.




      The objects were similar in appearance, oval shaped , silver white lights surroun­
ded by an orange-red aura which was noticeably thicker at the bottom. After first
seeing the objects Mr A. become aware of a 'dull humming' sound apparently coming
from the UFOs. Mr A. said the object on the left seemed farther away (i.e. it was
smaller and at a lower elevation) and after 2-3 minutes both objects began to move in
a westerly direction, the object on the left travelled in a zig-zag trajectory and the
object on the right a direct course. The objects moved at a rather slow rate of speed.
Mr A. went back inside for a period of about 20 seconds and when he came out again onto
the verandah the UFOs were still travelling west slowly diminishing in size with the
distance. Mr A. watched until they were at the point some 4° elevation above the mount­
ain range and at this point he went inside to get his wife to see the objects. Returning
after 2 minutes he found the objects had disappeared. The trajectory of the object on
the right had taken it directly above the Cairns airport radar installation on Saddle
Mountain. No observations were made from the airport since the staff did not enter the
control tower till after Mr A's observation had ended (another coincidence ?)•

In the last 12 months UFO Research (NSW) has had very few cases of merit brought to its
attention. Fewer still were worthy of detailed investigation and most of these turned
out to be possibly attributable to mundane factors. This situation has been largely
reflected throughout Australia and overseas except for the odd isolated event of signifi­
cance. There appears to be a low general level of activity.
Quite a number of times, reports have come to our notice referring to events that seem
strange and inexplicable at face value. Upon detailed investigation, these cases are
seen to have been based on strained or honest interpretation, imagination or media
distortion.
One example came our way early this year. An account of recurring UFO activity and un­
usual phenomena centred around a railway camp at Dombarton, just to the west of Woolon­
gong, was reported in a Sydney paper. Prior to the account appearing, the j�urnalist
concerned rang me with details on the affair and warned me that I should not rely on the
version that was to appear in the paper !   Initial enquiries were not encouraging, however,
a UFOR (NSW) team went to the area to investigate. Sporadic nocturnal 1 ight activity
and a few close encounters were alleged, but the details were so sparse, that the events
defied close scrutiny. One event, with 1 ittle supportive evidence was connected with
a landslide on a nearby escarpment, a claim which further enquiries indicated to be
spurious.   Our team was left with the impression that the main percipient may have seen
something, however, more was made of the events than they deserved. Reasonable explana­
tions were apparent for most events and those few that seemed somewhat interesting,
remained aloof due to the lack of detail. lt was easy to see why the location could
conjure up such a situation - an isolated locality - however, while it appears that this
was "much ado about noth ing••, we wi11 be keeping an eye on the area, just in case some­
thing of more substance turns up.
