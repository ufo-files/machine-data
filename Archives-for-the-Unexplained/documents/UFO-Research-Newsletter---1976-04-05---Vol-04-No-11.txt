                              PROBLEMS OF THE BLUE RIBBON PANEL

                                   by Dr. Robert F. Creegan

     [In this exclusive article for UFOR� Dr. Creegan� philosophy department� State Univer­
sity of New York at Albany� and formeP member of the National Enquirer•s Blue Ribbon UFO
Panel� tells of the paper's trouble in maintaining the panel of experts� responsible for
scrutinizing UFO reports and making cash CMards of up to $ZOO�OOO for evidence o:r proof of
extraterrestrial visitation].

     The Blue Ribbon Panel on the UFO problem, sponsored by the National Enquirer, no long­
er exists in its original form, but efforts are being made to assure its survival with a
somewhat new pattern and some changes in personnel. There were repeated changes in respect
to which reporters or editors were assigned to panel affairs. William Dick was the journa­
list most involved for a year or two, and he maintained good working relations with the aca­
demic component. Later, several of the Enquirer staff served for shorter periods.
     Following a meeting in Los Angeles in March, 1 975, came the first academic resigna­
tion -- my own. I was increasingly burdened with my own teaching and research tasks. As a
result of appeals from academic colleagues, however, I indicated I might rejoin the panel,
provided the next meeting could contain a scrupulous review of methodology and procedures.
At no time did the demand receive strongly favorable consideration, so the resignation is
still in effect.                                                                             ...._,\
     Professor J. Allen Hynek resigned from the panel on Noverrber 10, 1975. His letter
said, in part: the aims of the panel 11have not proved to be in harmony with the scientific
objectives of the Center [for UFO Studies] . .. Hynek, however, later offered to continue as
an outside consultant to the panel, though not to be publicly listed.
     In February, 1976, Professors Hynek and [R. Leo] Sprinkle, of the University of Wyo­
ming, met with Enquirer personnel in Florida, but no agreement was reached. Essentially,
Hynek asked for the funding of more serious UFO research. Apparently, Dr. Sprinkle indi­
cated that his own attitude is now flexible, and he suggested a tightening-up of proce­
dures as a pre-condition of favorable decisions. As of this writing, Dr. Hynek is not a
member of the panel; nor am I. The Enquirer has been advised by various parties that per­
haps new members could be found on the staffs of the National Investigations Committee on
Aerial Phenomena (NICAP) or the Mutual UFO Network (MUFON). Apparently, the Enquirer in­
tends to continue with a modified type of panel, but, as of now, the entire outcome re­
mains uncertain.
     My interpretation of all this is that a working arrangement between the academic world
and journalism is difficult to maintain. I am assured the panel will continue to exist.
If it does, one may predict continued problems if the t\'10 components -- journalistic and aca­
demic -- are both continued. If the academic component is lost or weakened, however, the
panel will be the same only in name.
