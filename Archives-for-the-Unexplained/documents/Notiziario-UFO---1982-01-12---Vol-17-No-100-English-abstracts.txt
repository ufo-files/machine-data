- A VISIT TO GEPAN: SOME REFLECTIONS
by Roberto Pinotti
It's well known that some UFO groups are not appreciating the French official
UFO cornmission, GEPAN. C.U.N. has anyhow had good relations with GEPAN, at first
through the French Embassy in Rome, then in London and in Italy.
   In late August, 1981, we had occasion to personally visit GEPAN in Toulouse
with its chairman, Dr Alain Esterle. It is to be noted that GEPAN is not a priv­
ate amateurs association like C.U.N., with all its limits and troubles. GEPAN's
a branch of CNES (the Frenche NASA) specifically charged for studying UFOs since
1977, using�the best financial and technical means within scientific research
and following a logical and operating framework which is not affected by enthu­
siasm or difficulties typical of "traditional ufologists". THese scientists have
no preconceived opinions, nor are they inclined to accept intrusions in their
own field. That's why GEPAN has kept a great discretion upon its activities in
face of a sensationalist press. This "closed" attitude has taken some French
ufologists to hysterically cry that GEPAN is only working towards debunking the
UFO "truth".
   Of course GEPAN (which cannot put itself at the same level of a plethora of
interlocutors showing themselves unable even to speak the same language as GE­
PAN) ignored these rumors and kept on a silent research and analysis work, as
shown by the several "Technical Notes" and "Information Notes" published as
yet.
   We remind those people charging GEPAN for being too much "sceptic" that we
need a sense of reality. WHat is wanted from GEPAN? An antiscientific declarat­
ion to the world that ET spaceships are coming? For years ufologists have tried
to involve science: now it has happened. Does someone have something to object?
Good, but then the rules must be the scientific ones as for language, attitude
and mentality. The others are out of play. And we need a conscience of our lim­
its, as well as an often predicated "professionalism".
   The role of private ufologists will have a decreasing importance as scientists
will offer the first answers to the UFO enigma. That will probably be un-grati­
fying for the UFO buffs, but not for the UFO problem. The scientifically trained
private ufologist will always be able to contribute, like amateur astronomers do.
The others are due to disappear.
   At GEPAN they are working and not losing their time. Let us wish them our
thanks and moral support.
