May 23-24: Birth of the wave?

May 23-24 may be regarded as the birth date of the ghost rocket phenomenon. All previous sightings, during the winter and early spring, were readily identified as natural phenomena. The few interesting incidents, with unusual parameters, occuring before May 23 (such as cases xxx, xxx and xxx), were only reported at a later date, some of them as late as in the 1970s. 

Then, during the night between May 23 and May 24 two phenomena occurred over mid- and south-Sweden, respectively, and these phenomena ”got the ball rolling”. 

Media pressed defence authorities for answers. ”Who doesn´t remember the ghost fliers in Norrland, that never got an explanation, in spite of a thousand and one theories. Shall we now experience a new, inexplicable mystery in the air?”, wrote Aftontidningen (AT). 

It is evident from media articles and documents that a couple of bright meteors probably were the factors that started a false debate about possible foreign intrusions over Sweden. This is not to say that real, unexplained  incidents never occurred, but these seem to have happened at a later date. In our own view this first mistake has its roots in a rather glaring incompetence at the offices of the Defence Staff´s Air Defence department [Fst/L]. But first let´s have a look at the two meteoric (?) sightings:

Case xxx.
The mid-Swedish midnight meteor

Time: Thursday, May 23, shortly before midnight
Place: Västerås, Stockholm, Gävle and Sundsvall areas
   
The sightings that started ”the ghost rocket wave”, at least in the media, and with the Defence Staff, were the two phenomena reported after the night between May 23 and May 24.  

The first of these phenomena was, by all rational judgement, a brilliant meteor. Sighting times differed between 23.45 on May 23 and 00.05 on May 24. Much of the media attention concentrated on ”the Landskrona cigarr” (see next case), which explains the word ”also” in the following press accounts:

xxx-1: FIREBALL ALSO SEEN IN GÄVLE. Gävle, the 26th (TT). A phenomenon like the one observed in Landskrona [see next case] the night on Friday, is now also reported by a trustworthy person from Gävle. He says he observed, at 23.48 on Thursday evening, a fireball with tail, leaving sparks, moving in a southeasterly direction parallel to the horizon and at an altitude of about 35 degrees over this [the horizon]. The fireball was in sight for about three seconds. (Hälsingborgs Dagblad [TT newswire], May 27).

xxx-2. ”FIREBALL ALSO SEEN OVER MEDELPAD. Sundsvall. Even Medelpad has a phenomenon similar to the one observed  in Landskrona. The fireball was observed in Sundsvall and in Borgsjö-Ede in the western part of Medelpad. A night-watch in the harbour of Sundsvall says he saw a fireball, at 23.48 hours on Thursday, moving in a southeasterly direction, throwing sparks and easily viewable. In Borgsjö-Ede, a trustworthy person made a similar sighting at about the same time. Even here the fireball moved in a southeasterly direction.” (Sundsvalls Tidning and TT newswire, May 28). 

xxx-3 ”...a couple of persons in Stockholm has told Dagens Nyheter that they have seen a fireball shortly before midnight the night on Friday. It was a large fireball with a tail. One of the witnesses thinks it had an easterly course and another that it moved towards the SE... At the Stockholm observatory no observation of a similar phenomenon has been made at the time in question, but they don´t deny that it could have been a meteor, since the time estimates are so close to each other.” (Dagens Nyheter, May 28)  [Comment: this sighting was never noted by the Defence Staff. Reports from Dagens Nyheter are generally absent in the Staff files.]

xxx-4. ”...many persons in the Stockholm area stated to the MT that they saw the same phenomenon in Huddinge and Hagalund. No one knows what is going on. One guess is of experiments with secret weapons, but aviation experts that in such a case it is curious that no sound is reported in connection with the observations - long-range bombing practice gives off noise.” (Morgon-Tidningen, May 28 [from Thomas Bullard: The Airship File]). 

”...a driver from Huddinge reports that at midnight, the night on Saturday he saw a luminous projectile which he considers not to be neither an airplane nor a meteor.” (Dagens Nyheter, May 26).

xxx-5. Another report of this meteor, noted by the Defence Staff, seems to have been reported from Untran, a place 30 kilometres to the SSE of Gävle. Here a fireball with tail was observed in the northeast by the town planning director of Gävle. This report may have gone directly to the Staff, although there is no note or memo on the case in the files, only an entry in the table of  

xxx-6. A private person from Västerås reported another sighting which might have been the same phenomenon. It seems this report, too, was made directly to defence authorities, because it was never mentioned in the newspapers. The sighting occured at five minutes past midnight (00.05). (In our experience a time discrepancy of a quarter of an hour is not unusual for high-altitude sightings of meteors reported from many places).  According to the Defence Staff´s report tabulation the fireball with tail, large as a full-moon, fell at ”a maximum of 500 meters from the observer”. A whining or whistling sound was heard but no bang or splash [presumably the sighting occurred close to a lake]. As with the sightings from Gävle the phenomenon was seen in the southeast. 

Status: Meteor
Sources: see above.

Case xxx.
The Landskrona ”cigar” - meteor or...?

Time: May 24, at about 02.20-02.30
Places: Landskrona (Skåne), Karlskrona (Blekinge) and other places in south Sweden and Denmark.

The second series of sightings from the night between May 23 and 24 centered on south-Sweden and Denmark. The prime sighting, which may be regarded as having started the whole ghost rocket wave, occured in Landskrona. 

Although, in their texts, most newspapers were skeptical, media headlines now started to use descriptions like ”rocket bombs” (Landskrona Posten and Aftonbladet), ”remote-directed bombs” (Morgon Tidningen and Aftontidningen), ”projectile” (Expressen) and ”the V-bomb” (Göteborgs-Tidningen).

xxx-1. ”NIGHT WORKERS TOOK SHELTER FROM ”THE ROCKET BOMB” IN LANDSKRONA. Was the sky phenomenon over Landskrona the night on Friday a rocket bomb or a fireball with tail, or what was it? Quite a number of persons seem to have observed the phenomenon... Among the trustworthy observers are two night-watchmen and a repairman at the sugar factory. The later person says that he, at the time in question [2.20 in the night], was on the 5th floor of the sugar factory when the body, which he descibes as like a log, at the most 1,5 meters long and 30 cm in diameter, appeared in his field of vision. 

”He didn´t have the time for more detailed observations because, when the object started to throw sparks, he became scared of an explosion and saught shelter. He had the time, however, to see that first it fired off three ”rockets” and after some second yet another one [decomposition of a meteor?]. The trajectory was definitely horizontal and went almost straight for Copenhagen [in a southwesterly direction from Landskrona]. Earlier in the night red lights had been observed in the northeastern sky [this may have been the midnight meteor, see previous case].” (Aftonbladet, May 25 [Defence Staff files]).

More details are found in Dagens Nyheter: ”..A repairman, who was at an altitude of 35 meters, saw it [the object] against the sky as a wingless, cigar-shaped body, the size of a small airplane, intermittently emitting bunches of sparks from the stern. All observers report that the body flew at an altitude of about 100 meters at the speed of an ordinary airplane and with a southwesterly direction. The DN correspondent managed, late tonight, to contact the repairman who did the observation. He had been on the 5th floor of the sugar factory... [continues with the same info as in Aftonbladet]. (Dagens Nyheter, May 25; also a TT newswire)  

Aftontidningen (and also Expressen) hinted that the witnesses were not in agreement on what was really seen: ”...two night-watchmen wanted it [the phenomenon] to be a fireball with tail, a repairman saw the the phenomenon as a wingless, cigar-shaped body...” (Aftontidningen, May 27; Expressen, May 25).

Expressen interviewed the police superintendent at Landskrona, Gösta Ahlbeck, who stated that he had not seen the cigar-shaped fire-thrower himself. Noone in the police force had, yet one [the police] does not think it is a mistake, and that it probably was some kind of a projectile. (Expressen, May 25).

Dagens Nyheter speculated that the 240 km action radius of V1 bombs had been considerably improved by the Americans: ”Although one should take the information about the wingless, fire-spitting flight over Landskrona with caution, it is quite possible that what people see are V1-bombs, which the Russians shoot off from some test station on the Baltic coast. This is what an air expert...says... The whole of Germany has, says the expert, been combed by the occupation powers, for robot bomb materials and eager tests are going on... What the Landskrona witness saw, intermittent sparks from the stern, fits with the way the V1 works. The witness has estimated the length of the observed projectile at much less than the 6-7 meters of the V1-bomb, but this is easy to mistake yourself on.” (Dagens Nyheter, May 26)

But astronomers were skeptics: ”- It is quite conceivable that it was a phenomenon of meteoric character, says one of the Saltsjöbaden [observatory] astronomers. That one [somebody] says that it was observed at 100 meters altitude means nothing, since  estimates of distance are nearly always wrong when it comes to meteors. It once happened that a person called us to report a meteor crashing behind the Östra Station [the Eastern railway station in Stockholm]. It appeared that the meteor had been seen all the way between Gävle and Kalmar. As a rule meteors are visible as ”fireballs” at 50 to 60 km altitude, but this does not prevent almost everyone to see them sweep over the house-roofs. You always estimate the altitude falsely.” (Expressen, May 25). 

Göteborgs-Tidningen provided other details: ”The light phenomenon over Landskrona...has not yet received any explanation. The three persons, who have seen [it]...were all employed by the same company. It was only one of them, a repairman...who could describe the phenomenon in detail after looking out of a window...neither has any of the night-watchmen employed in the town by Förenade Vakt seen the phenomenon. Many in Landskrona and surrounding areas think it was a [piece of] firework.... Some believe that it was an airplane, on a much greater altitude than that stated, and that it was heading for Copenhagen...” (Göteborgs-Tidningen, May 25).
     
With the intention of trying to find the original witnesses at Landskrona, particularly the repairman at the sugar factory, Anders Liljegren traced the original Landskrona-Posten article with the help of a local library. It is apparent, from the article in Landskrona-Posten that the local newspaper made a direct interview with the repairman, but never published his name. Their version is essentially the same as that published by Aftonbladet.  

Landskrona wasn´t the only sighting place. Pieced together with other reports, the phenomenon looks more and more like a meteor that traversed south-Sweden on a southwesterly course, aiming for the skies of southern Denmark and northern Germany:

xxx-2. LIGHT PHENOMENON ALSO IN EKSJÖ. The mysterious light phenomenon which has been sighted in Landskrona the night on Friday, was also to be seen over Eksjö, according to what a credible person has told this editorial office. The witness had at about half past two in the night seen a large ”star” moving across the heavens with the formation of sparks after it. To the observer it looked as if one of ”the sparks” fell to the ground. ”The star” and the bunch of sparks continued its aerial trajectory, however.” (Smålands Dagblad, May 27). 

xxx-3. A NEW MYSTERIOUS ”FIREBALL” OVER A NAVAL YARD. Karlskrona, Monday (Expressen). As noted before, a person at the naval yard of Karlskrona observed, the night on Friday [at about 2.00 hours, according to the Sydöstra Sveriges Dagblad], a flying object which could be a meteor. The witness thought that the ”heavenly body” went very low. It came from the north and disappeared in [over] Hasslölandet [in the southwest]. It was viewed for about half-a-minute and a powerful rain of sparks was noted from ”the tail”...” (Expressen, May 27; also in Morgon Tidningen, May 27 [Thomas Bullard: The Airship File]).   

The main (social democratic) newspaper in the naval town of Karlskrona took a strong side in the debate: ”On Sunday several persons, who, during the war years, observed the flying bombs over Blekinge [German V-1:s and V-2:s crossed over and even crashed in Blekinge], conveyed the hypothesis that it now evidently is the question of new remotely controlled bombs. The hypothesis does not lack substance and it will be interesting to see if the phenomenon continues and what the sightings, in that case, will lead to. Evidently it is experiments in the Baltic with some new secret weapon.” (Sydöstra Sveriges Dagblad, [morning paper] May 27; same text also appeared in the afternoon newspaper Expressen, the same day)

xxx-4. B. Kinnman, chief of staff at the Kungl. Skånska Trängkåren (T4) regiment at Hässleholm wrote the following report, on May 25, to the Defence Staff´s Air Defence department: 

”In connection with the enclosed article from Sydsvenska Dagbladet on May 25 [on the Landskrona case] I herewith have the honour of reporting the following concerning observations made by a person living here in Hässleholm. That person is [railway] engine driver Malte Särlöv, Kyrkgatan 18 b, Hässleholm.

”On May 25 [probably May 24] at 02.10, S. observed a cigar-shaped object, moving at about 300 m altitude in a southwesterly direction of flight. The speed was incredibly high, and S. could only watch the object for about 10 seconds. He estimated the speed at 600 km/h. Quite a distance behind the object´s stern a powerful burst of sparks was thrown out at uneven intervals. The duration of each burst of sparks was very short, about 1/10 of a second. Between the bursts a thick smoke developed. 

”The object passed Hässleholm at a distance of 2-3 kilometres in direction towards the northern shore of the [lake] Finjasjön. S. is prepared to [personally] verify this information.  On order. B. Kinnman, chief of staff.” (Defence Staff files).

When Anders Liljegren attempted to trace Mr. Särlöv (born January 10, 1907) in the autumn of 1986 he was then deleted from the file of Swedish citizens. Probably he was dead.

And the Landskrona object continued into Denmark...

xxx-6. Yet another sighting in Denmark was reported, via AP, in Morgon Tidningen (MT), May 29 [from Thomas Bullard, The Airship File]: ”The same night that people in Landskrona saw a flying object which flew towards the southwest at 100 m altitude, a border guard in Denmarks southern-lying parish, at Tönder [close to the German border, near the North Sea], made a similar discovery. At 3 o´clock at night the guard saw a bright light, followed by a tail, which very rapidly sailed on a path from east to west, reports the Berlingske Aftenavis.” [This case was published by Dagens Nyheter, May 28, as having occured at 23.00 hours on Monday, May 27. From our sources it is obvious that it is the same sighting and that the MT version is to be prefered before the shorter DN item.]

Conclusion: A fireball trajectory with Eksjö, Karlskrona, Hässleholm, Landskrona, Ringsted, Gjessing and Tönder as observation points is most likely. Witnesses in five out of six places report a spark-throwing object on (generally) a southwesterly course. All six reports mention this (general) direction of flight. 

In retrospect, the case for a ”cigar-shaped”, structured object over Landskrona rests only on the words of one single witness, the [anonymous] repairman at the sugar factory. As we shall see from the coming months of the ghost rocket wave, many other people made the same kind of mistake. The two other witnesses at Landskrona experienced a meteor, as did observers at Eksjö, Karlskrona, Ringsted and Tönder. The observer at Ängelholm was influenced by the wording of an article on the Landskrona sighting to report a cigar-shaped object, The Danish witnesses at Gjessing were probably influenced by the intense media coverage of the incidents when they reported their sighting, a few months later. 

Status: Probable meteor

A curious Defence Staff memo

A curious document, found in the ghost rocket files in Stockholm is an early memo [”PM över inkomna rapporter av observerade eldfenomen”; ”Memo of received reports on observed fire phenomena”] where  the two previous meteoric overflights, on the night between May 23 and 24, are analyzed as if they were airplane or V-weapon overflights. What is probably this very document, was entered in the diaries [logging of incoming letters] of the Research Officer of the Defence Staff on June 3, 1946. The ”analysis”, itself, is found in the ”space projectile” file main file. 

In this ”analysis”, time estimates for the sightings are taken literaly, without regard for human mistakes in timing, and used, combined with the geographical positions of observers, to calculate trajectories and to estimate the speed of observed ”objects”. 

The Staff may have been influenced by the Stockholm newspaper Aftontidningen which made the same kind of ”analysis”: ”If the times are correct it would mean that the speed was something between 350-400 km/h. In Landskrona the opinion was that the speed was about the same as an airplane.” (Aftontidningen, May 27).

The Defence Staff analysis rests on only five of the reports detailed above (see cases xxx-x, xxx-x, xxx-x, xxx-x and xxx-x). The author of the document is, seemingly, ignorant of the difference between ”course” (direction of flight) and ”bearing” (in what direction of the sky the phenomenon was observed by the witness). It is impossible to say what is meant by the word ”direction” [Sw. riktning]  in some of the short  newspaper reports, especially the ”midnight meteor”.  This problem is ignored, but is a constant confusing factor in much of the analysises performed by the Defence Staff in the summer of 1946. There is no evidence that the reports of the May 23-24 meteors were ever analyzed by professional astronomers.    

Another confusing mistake, in this document, is that the dates mentioned in the report are obviously one month wrong. 23/4 (April 23) should read 23/5 (Mary 23), etc. 

Evidently, the document was written in all haste and by someone who was ordered to do it without having the proper qualifications.

During WWII, the Swedish air defence handled more than 16.000 reports of aerial violations of the territory. The bureaucracy surrounding this reporting, which had ended in May 1945, was now seemingly resumed, one year later. Procedures for recording and analyzing overflights of German Messerschmidts or British bombers were, however, neither applicable nor appropriate for handling light phenomena, such as meteors, or (hypothetical) extremely fast-moving rockets, like the German V-2:s of WWII.    

We will now cite the entire document, which is quite revealing:

”Memo of received reports on observed fiery phenomena. 

”I. Reports received have conflicting data on the performance of the fiery phenomena. Speed has been estimated at values between about 100 -- 1.000 km/h, and more. Estimates of altitude has varied between about 0,1 -- 50 km. 

”II. By projecting the trajectories the following kan be certified: 
     1) 23/4   23.45  Untran	northeasterly direction
                   23.48  Gävle      	southeasterly direction
      The distance Untran - Gävle is 30 km. If it is the question of the same phenomenon the distance has been covered with a speed of 600 km/h. If so, the course, however, must have been changed over Untran from northeasterly to northwesterly and over Gävle from northwesterly to southeasterly. 

         24/4   00.05  Västerås	southeasterly direction
     The distance Untran resp. Gävle - Västerås is 100 resp. 125 km. The distances have been covered in 20 resp. 17 min, which means 300 km/h resp. 450 km/h. In this case, too, changes of course must have been made over the previously mentioned places. 

     2) 24/4  ca 02.00  Karlskrona   southerly direction
                  ca 02.20  Landskrona  southerly direction
     The distance Karlskrona - Landskrona 175 km. If it is the question of the same phenomena, which is probable in view of relatively similar course speed will be 525 km/h. 

      3) If the same phenomenon was [should have been] observed in Västerås and Karlskrona it would have covered the 400 km distance in 2 hours, i.e. a speed of 200 km/h.”

(end of document)

In the margins of the typewritten paper someone at the staff penned the comments ”could be rocket” (close to the 600 km/h estimate) and ”low speeds, should be rocket” (close to the 300 and 450 km/h calculation). The calculated speeds have been underlined in red. A ”rocket” that travels at 600 km/h?

Source: Memo found in Defence Staff files. Also: Fst/Forskn.off. [Research Officer] diary: Ank.nr 84/1946: 3/6 1946 from Fst/L [Air Defence department]. War Archives, Stockholm.

Case xxx.
Motorist on ”rocket hunt” in Roslagen

Time: May 25, about 18.30 hours
Place: Road between Uppsala and Rasbo, county of Uppsala 

”GHOST ROCKET  HUNTED  BY CAR THROUGH ROSLAGEN”. The wing- and noiseless ghost flier continues to bewilder [people] here and there in the country. This morning a painter told Aftonbladet that he, Saturday evening, hunted a mysterious fireball for five kilometres in his car. The fireball was faster and disappeared - but during seven minutes the painter, and his company could view the phenomenon. 

”-- Mysterious it was, indeed, he says. We came from Uppsala and were going to Rasbo [about 15-20 km to the NE of Uppsala] and it was almost 18.30 when someone in the car suddenly pointed to the sky and said: - Look at those flashes from the airplane! We looked in that direction. A few hundred meters to the right of the road, and at an altitude of only one hundred meters, a strange thing flew which could neither be meteor nor airplane. The closest thing  would be to describe it as a drop-shaped sphere made of some kind of polished metal. No wings were visible. 

”I drove at a speed of 70 km/h and since the rocket, or whatever it was, had the same direction [as us] we could follow it for five kilometers or about seven minutes. The object´s speed wasn´t great, we estimated it at about a hundred km/h. Finally it disappeared behind the horizon. Only yesterday [Monday] evening did I read in the newspapers that people had seen fireballs here and there as early as on Thursday night. Thus, before I met the strange sight myself I had no knowledge of the phenomenon, ends the painter.”

This case is noted in the Defence Staff´s report tabulations, with Aftonbladet (AB) as the source. There is no evidence, from military files, of any attempt to make a follow-up investigation. The name of the painter is thus unknown. 

Status: Insufficient information, possibly airplane 
Source: Aftonbladet, May 28 [Defence Staff files]

Six Russians caught ”sight-seeing” 
in the archipelago

”A certain sensation was caused in Karlshamn yesterday afternoon when a large sailing-boat with six Russians on board came running in the bay where many Karlshamn inhabitants were enjoying the first real summer warm Sunday this May. One person called for the police and a couple of constables went out. The Russians had, by then, already left the Kollevik pier but returned on being urged back by the police. 

”They explained that they belonged to the Russian steamer ”Kronstadt” which, on Sunday, anchored for degaussing on Monday. They had started out for a sailing tour, but some problem with the boat had occasioned them to go to the Kollevik pier. The police, however, found no problem with the boat. It seems remarkable that seamen that belong to foreign nations are allowed to navigate freely in the Karlshamn archipelago, according to comments made at Karlshamn, and further investigation is now ordered.” (Expressen, May 27)


May: The same kind of phenomena over Russia?

A book on UFO phenomena behind the Iron Curtain cites Felix Zigel, the first serious Russian collector on UFO reports:

”In May 1946 the first accounts were heard of strange, saucer-shaped flying objects appearing at night, the reports coming both from ”experienced” observers like pilots who saw these saucers in the form of ”fire-balls” [the same kind of meteors as seen over Sweden?] during their flights, and also from ”inexperienced people such as farmers and townsfolk who saw them from the ground. Both types of observer mentioned the enormous velocities and accelerations [again: meteors?] of these objects, which were quite unlike anything human beings could simulate in their aircraft built on traditional lines. 

”It is remarkable that Dr. Zigel thus begins the modern UFO period a year or more earlier than the customarily accepted date of 4th June 1947 when the American Kenneth Arnold first noted them [the Arnold sighting took place on June 24]. We think this is correct since in both East and West ”modern” UFOs had been seen already in 1945 and 1946.” 

A figure on the number of reports from Russia, in the Hobana-Weverbergh book, refers to the total number of known cases [by 1972], not the number of reports in 1946. The fifty-one Russian reports listed and detailed in the documentation section of the Hobana-Weverbergh book are from the years 1842-1972. No details are given on 1946 cases, except for the following: 

”...May 1946, [dates] not determined. Zigel...places the beginning of the ”modern” period of UFOlogy in Russia in May 1946: saucer-shaped objects were seen by Soviet citizens and Soviet pilots. The features reported are the same as the typical ones described today.”

Source: Ion Hobana & Julien Weverbergh: UFO´s from behind the Iron Curtain, pages 29 and 279.


May 26: Air show at Eskilstuna

1946, the first post-war summer, saw thousands of Swedes visit air shows all across Sweden. The new wonders of air technology development, accelerated during the was years, were an obvious area of public interest. 

Probably one of the first air shows this year was hosted by the Eskilstuna Air Club on Sunday, May 26. This was just a few days before the first series of daytime ghost rocket sightings, May 28-May 31, and in very close proximity to the area of these sightings. The Stockholm Technical Museum lent the only Swedish copy of ”the hell dog”, the V-1, for display at the show. [The British government had donated the V-1 to the Swedish museum]. This was the first time it was on display outside of Stockholm.

Source: Nerikes Allehanda, May 21; Dagbladet, July 19 [donation of V-1]

Case xxx.
Ship among the clouds

Time: May 27, afternoon

Case xxx.
A crash in the waters off Kvicksund?

Time: May 28, between 16.30 and 17.00
Place: Kvicksund, a sound in the lake Mälaren

This case was found on a few pages of handwritten notes in the Defence Staff files. It was never listed on the tables of cases regularly produced by the Defence Staff, nor is there any documents of any investigation. Maybe the case was investigated and immediately explained, we don´t know. The notes seem intriguing, however. 

From the notes it seems that on May 28, between 16.30 and 17.00 an unknown witness had observed a flying, metallic object come in over Kvicksund at low altitude. The witness heard engine sounds from the object, which then crashed into the water. 

This is what can be rendered from the few words on a slip of paper. In the summer of 1987 Anders Liljegren and Stig Aggestad attempted to trace the witness via an article on the ghost rockets in Folket, a local newspaper. No result.

Kvicksund is only about 50 kilometres to the NE of Stora Mellösa, the scene of the next case, possibly the first of all cases investigated by the Defence Staff which had any quality.

Cases xxx and xxx.
Stora Mellösa - sailflyer or zeppelin?

Time: May 28, evening, probably two different sightings: about 18.15 (the Holmdahl family) and 20.10 (Edstram)
Place: Stora Mellösa, county of Örebro

The first indication of this case was reported in Nerikes Allehanda, published from Örebro: 

”MYSTERIOUS THING GLIDED OVER STORA MELLÖSA”. A mysterious object looking almost like a rocket, or an airplane without wings, was sighted Tuesday evening at Stora Mellösa. The object glided at about 300-500 meters altitude and moved without a sound. 

”The witness estimated its size as about half of an ordinary trainer without wings. The object was round like a giant cigarr in shape and the metallic sides glistened in the sun. The rocket, or whatever you should call this strange thing, had about the same speed with which an ordinary airplane moves. We have questioned the FO-staff [Defence area staff] which, however, has no report from any other source about the strange rocket. It is evidently some kind of an experiment - but what?” (Nerikes Allehanda, May 29).

If Nerikes Allehanda made contact with the regional FO-staff, isn´t it strange that the journalist was not questioned for details on the incident..? It would be a reasonable routine that the regional staff should report the sighting to Stockholm. Instead, it seems the Stockholm Defence Staff first discovered the Stora Mellösa case when it was re-published by the Stockholm paper Aftonbladet (and as a TT newswire in many other papers). 

The Aftonbladet article prompted a reaction from Stockholm. The local police was ordered to make an investigation. On May 31 the chief constable [landsfiskal] E. Nyberg, at the Glanshammar police office [landsfiskalskontor], asked the local policeman [fjärdingsman] at Stora Mellösa to ”urgently perform an investigation into the matter and to question the people who have observed the aerial body. Report is expected at the earliest.” 

Carl Bergh, the local policeman, wrote the following on the same day [May 31]: ”The undersigned has been asked by the chief constable in the Glanshammar district to try to trace people in Stora Mellösa, who possibly observed...the phenomenon. At 19.30 today, a parish meeting and vestry [kyrkofullmäktige] was held at the local government office, with some thirty persons, from different parts of the community, present. The undersigned asked these people whether they had seen or heard about the phenomenon in question, but with negative replies. Only after the newspaper had reported the incident had it become known...”  

It seems that the search for the reporting witnesses took three weeks. The police at Örebro finally located them, and wrote a report. It seems that within a timespan of about two hours, two different sightings were made in the Stora Mellösa area:

”The Police, Örebro, N:r 579. REPORT, Thursday, June 20, 1946. Since the chief constable of the Glanshammar district has ordered questioning of the following persons living in this town, the following persons were questioned on June 19 by first policeman Helge Holmgren, whereupon they stated:

”District superintendent John Sören Edstam, born March 5, 1909, registered and living Toringsgatan 4, Örebro: Probably on Tuesday, May 28, 1946, he was, in the evening, going by car from Stora Mellösa towards Örebro. The objects speed was about the same, like the speed of a sailplane. No engine noise or other sound was heard. Due to the position of the sun [sundown at the Stockholm horizon was at 20.38 this day] the object was lit up diagonally from the back and from the left. Edstam distinctly discerned that the object had two wings, but due to the rather powerful sunlight, the size of the wings or their shape could not be observed.”

”Edstam, who stopped his car as he caught sight of the object, sat watching it for about one minute before continuing his journey. At the time in question it was a beautiful weather and clear visibility. Edstam is, since many years, interested in flying and so he thinks he is trained at recognizing different types of aircraft. His opinion is that the object in question with certainty must have been a sailflyer. 

”Clerk Bror Ragnar Holmdahl, born May 14, 1894, registered and living Oskarsplatsen 1, Örebro: On Tuesday, May 21, 1946 [note discrepancy on the date] was going by car from Örebro to Stora Mellösa, where he and his family has a summer cottage at Åsby [near lake Hjälmaren]. The journey started at about 17.45 and went straight to Stora Mellösa. In the car was Holmdahls wife Elna Holmdahl and their 18 year old son Sven Ragnar Holmdahl. The wife was sitting in the back and the son to the right of Holmdahl. 

”After the car had passed the church at Stora Mellösa and had come about 500 meters to the east thereof, where the terrain is flat and open, his wife said that she saw a flying object in the sky, wherefore the car ought to be stopped so that the object could be closely viewed. Holmdahl stopped the car whereupon the right door was opened. By bending out of the door Holmdahl, as well as his wife and son, could view an object which flew in the air at about 60 degrees angle [over the horizon] and on a level with the car. The object came from the east and moved in a westerly direction. How high up in the air it was, Holmdahl could not decide, but it seemed as if the object went somewhat lower than the regular planes that now pass over this area every day. The speed seemed a little slower than the regular plane.

”The object´s length seemed to be about that of the body of a regular sailplane. In shape the object looked like a zeppelin or a fat cigar. It lay horisontally in the air and moved at an even and steady speed forward in its own longitudal direction. The end which was the nose or stem was thicker than the other [stern] end, which was more tapering. The object seemed to be build like a polygonal [Sw. mångsiding] which could easily be seen when the sunlight was reflected on its edges. The colour was aluminum grey. It seemed as if the object, sometimes, could rock a little on its longitudal axis, but otherwise the object moved at an even and speady speed in a horisontal direction.

No sound or engine noise could be heard. Weather was beautiful with a clear sky and warm air. Due to this the object could be observed very clearly. Holmdahl added, however, that the stern part of the object was like in a shadow because the sunshine hit the object from the forward [part on the] right. 

”Direction of flight was west-south-westerly. Holmdahl estimated that on this course the object would approximately pass over the area of Mosås between Örebro and Kumla. 

”The Holmdahl family watched the object for about 5 minutes when it passed and went away, more and more, on its direction of course. They were at their summer cottage until about 22.00 hours in the evening when they returned for Örebro. During the visit to the summer cottage Holmdahl met no local inhabitant, therefor he couldn´t hear [ask] if onyone else in Stora Mellösa had seen the object. On the following day - May 22 - Holmdahl phoned [the newspaper] Nerikes Allehanda and reported the matter. The next day - May 23 - there was a note of the matter as far as Holmdahl could remember. ”Holmdahl sketched the enclosed drawing of the flying object. Mrs Elna Greta Sofia Holmdahl, born June 20, 1896, and their son, clerk trainee Sven Ragnar Holmdahl, born September 29, 1928: 

”Both corroborated in all parts Holmdahls story concerning the object in question. They also corroborated the drawn sketch of the object. None of them have seen any similar object in the air before. 

”Örebro, the criminal police department as above: /Elis Olsson/, acting detective superintendent, /Helge Holmgren.”

Suspecting a sailplane (from Edstams testimony), E. Nyberg (chief constable at Glanshammar) on June 21 contacted ”...the secretary of [the] Örebro Bil & Flygklubb [Car- and Plane Club], engineer Nils Bergenstråhle from Örebro, who reported that any plane owned by the club had not been used for flying over St. Mellösa on May 21, 1946.”  

Strangely, the police questioned the club about planes in the air on May 21 (from Holmdahls story) while Edstam had stated May 28, one week later, as the probable date. May 28 must also be the correct date for the Holmdahl sighting since Holmdahl phoned the newspaper and the story was published the following day, May 29 [AFU has the article on file]. Thus this that part of the police investigation has no value at all. A sailplane might have been in the air on May 28 (as Edstam believe) while the Holmdahl sighting is more of a mystery. As far as we know zeppelins were not operating in Sweden in 1946. 

Judging from the points of time stated (18.15 and 20.10) this is two sightings from the same geographical area. The two object seem to have been on opposite courses of direction.

John Sören Edstam died about 1982-83. In August 1986, Anders Liljegren spoke to his ex-wife Eva (they were divorced in about 1950-51). Eva could not remember that his husband had talked about the 1946 incident. It is not probable that Edstam viewed the incident as anything exceptional since he thought it was a sailplane. 

The two elderly in the Holmdahl family were dead when we tried to investigate the case in 1986. Their son Sven, now living at Hedemora, has not answered our letters and phone calls.

Status: Unidentified (Edstams sighting might be a sailplane)

June 4: A new phenomenon in Swedish skies!

At least, it couldn´t have been a Vampire jet that was seen, that afternoon (June 4), in the Trehörningen area. At 21.00 hours on that day the first three imported Vampire jets arrived and landed at the F13 air force wing in Norrköping. 

The jets were flown, in sunshine weather, from Hatfield in the UK, through Holland and Germany and through the south of Sweden. 18.000 spectators, on their way home from the soccer game between IFK Norrköping and the British Charlton team, got an extra free performance as the three British-built jets came whistling over their new home town. 

The new planes were the main attractions at several  air shows this summer. It is conceivable that the new jets caused more sightings of ”strange high altitude objects”, ”whistling sounds” and ”smoketrails” than what we know of. 

The first of these airshows were at the Barkarby air force base [the F8 wing] just three days later, June 6. At exactly 11.00 hours, one of the Vampires crossed over the awed audience at only 50 meters altitude and at almost 900 km/hour speed. Wrote the Stockholm Morgon Tidningen: ”Several times the monster flashed by with a sound that resembled a hard-working blowlamp... The pilot put in the air brakes and with an angry, whistling sound the monster stopped in front of one of the hangars.”  

Making another appearance, Sunday, June 16, at his homebase in Norrkoping, lieutenant Jan Oterdahl, head of the new Vampire division, was admired by 24.000 spectators. The same day another 10.000 spectators saw the Vampire - and many other Air Force planes - at the Nykoping air show, hosted by the F11 air force wing. 

Sources: Morgon Tidningen, June 4, 7 and 17.

Case xxx.
Rocket-bomb with intermittent bursts?

Time: Saturday, June 8, at 23.00 hours
Place: Eskilstuna, county of Södermanland

This case was first reported in Eskilstuna-Kuriren on June 11: 

”V-BOMB SEEN OVER HUGELSTA ON WHITSUN EVE. A number of reports of silvery objects, which brings to mind the German V-bombs, have recently been seen in the press, and late on Whitsun Eve a similar phenomenon was seen over Eskilstuna. The sighting was reported to Eskilstuna-Kuriren by an engineer who is an ex-pilot and must be regarded as a skilled and credible observer. 

”-- What I saw can´t be described in any other way than as a rocket with intermittent exhausts, he says. I had just put out the lights and stood by my window, when I, in the semi-darkness, saw something coming out of the clouds. I had the time to see three or four clear ”outbursts” or explosions. The bomb, if it was one, passed to the west of the city and in northerly direction direction [of flight]. It seemed to be going down at an angle of almost 30 degrees - on this I can´t, of course, be definitely sure - and in that case it ought to have crashed somewhere in the neighbourhood. Possiby it went to the north, over Hugelsta. 

”It could not, says the observer, have been any of our new jets, which have a invariable exhaust and therefore shine like an ordinary rocket. The same did the later V-weapons [V-2] while the first [V-1] had intermittent power.”
  
This article was also published by the Dagens Nyheter, the same day (June 11), and, in a much shorter version, by the Svenska Dagbladet on June 12. It is obvious, from cases we have studied, that the Defence Staff didn´t read [the liberal] Dagens Nyheter, while stories published by [the conservative] Aftonbladet and Svenska Dagbladet were duely noted. Thus it wasn´t until the 12th, when someone at the Staff read Svenska Dagbladet, that this case was noted.

Someone (at the Staff) managed to trace the witness and, seemingly, spoke to him on the phone. The witness was an engineer Enhörning, employed by the Bolinder-Munktell company [a tractor factory]. Seemingly, Mr. Enhörning told the Staff he had seen the phenomenon ”at eleven” and the interviewer wrote 11.00 on his piece of paper. 11.00 was also recorded, in the Defence Staff analysis table, while the correct time of the sighting was in the evening, 23.00, after sundown.  

The witness didn´t believe it was a meteorite he had seen, the exhaust burst had been clear and apparent, ”like from several succesive rockets”. ”Didn´t see the object, just a light phenomenon”, ”distance 800-1.000 meters”, ”faster than an airplane, like an airplane diving”, ”no sound”, ”probable crash close to the Hugelsta farm near Stockholmsvägen [the road to Stockholm]”.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       
The case was registered, in the analysis table, as a daytime sighting (11.00 hours) of ”a silvery rocket”, while, actually, it was only a nighttime - sundown was at about 9 o´clock - occurence. The Staff was impressed with Mr. Enhörnings credentials as a technician and as a previous pilot (a colleague!) and with his statement of seeing ”intermittent bursts” (everyone at the Staff knew about the V-1:s mode of appearance), and so the Staff attributed a high value to the sighting. It is very probable, however, that a meteorite frictioning in the atmosphere caused the three or four ”intermittent bursts” observed. The only mystery is, maybe, that noone else reported the meteor that evening. The V-1 tag for what Mr. Enhörning saw may have been inspired by the Eskilstuna air show, two weeks before, where a V-1 was on display for the first time outside of Stockholm.   
  
Status: Probable meteor.
Sources: Eskilstuna-Kuriren (credit: Kjell Jonsson) and Dagens Nyheter, June 11; Svenska Dagbladet, June 12 [Defence Staff files]; Notes in Defence Staff files.

June 19: Commission to England to study rocket weapons 

A seven-headed Swedish defence commission visited England (June 19 -- July 10) to study the problems and progress in rocket weapon technology. The first five days were used for a detailed study of the ”Exhibition of German aeronautical developments” at the Science Museum in London, where V-weapons and other German rocket materials were on display. 

The commission, headed by Lieutenant Colonel Jentzen, was appointed by the Supreme Commander (ÖB) on May 31, just one week after the first flap of publicized ghost rocket reports. It is most doubtful, however, that there was ever any connection at all with the new flaming phenomena in the skies. 

Swedish defence had a natural, long-time interest in new technologies and the overall reason for going to Britain was an interest in learning and seeing for the sake of our own weapon constructions. In February 1945 a cross-service organisation ”the Central command for coordination of reaction and rocket power” (abbreviated: CLR) had been formed to coordinate army, navy and air force development in this area. Members of the commission were also members of the CLR. 

Commission members travelled alone or in small groups, each one with a specialized area to cover, such as ”solid propellant rockets”, ”ballistic measurement techniques” or ”guidande problems”.

July 3: A reminder to keep quiet

1946 was a year of growing tensions. Most countries wanted to know what their adversaries were up to, and Sweden - although declared neutral - was not passive in this game. Several spies were caught and their stories were published in the media. In the war years there was a popular saying: ”en svensk tiger”, meaning ”a Swede keeps quite”, but which could also be translated as ”a Swedish tiger”. 

In early July the following reminder was sent to all units of the Swedish Air Force by the Head of the Air Staff:

”To F1-F18, F20, F21, FCS, FKHS, E1-E4, Flybo S, W, O, N, FBS, SCFF, CVM, CVV, CVA:

”The following is a reminder of the professional secrecy, högkvo [order from the Supreme Commander] on July 1, 1946, nr H 7:5, [and should] be parted to all subordinate personnel according to this:

”On June 8, 1945, ÖB [the Supreme Commander] appealed to the personnel of the armed forces to observe the required secretiveness about Swedish military conditions. Although there has been an armistice in Europe, it could be counted on that the military intelligence organizations of foreign powers still would be working intensely and still would try to find out, in different ways, information about the Swedish armed forces personnel, material and organization, etc. 

”In recent times there have been incidents which confirm our misgivings in this area. From certain foreign parts tangible attempts have been made to collect military information of a secret nature. As an example could be mentioned that a military attaché, at an embassy of a great power represented here, recently tried to bribe a Swedish officer in order to acquire such information. The military attaché in question has now left the kingdom.

”In view of this, the Supreme Commander would, once again, inculcate the importance that the required secretiveness about Swedish military circumstances [should] be followed and that report to the closest superior immediately be made, when suspicions of illegal intelligence activities from foreign powers arise. In order to be able to take required countermeasures, such report by the unit commander (or similar), except for through regular channels, also immediately be transfered directly to the Head of the Defence Staff´s home department. 

”This order should be given to all active or on duty officers and non-commissioned officers (similar). Further, all personnel on duty should be reminded of the orders in högkv:fst nr 7:43, June 8, 1945 (fo nr B 72/1945) concering secretiveness (TLB nr 62/1945) in those parts where they are still applicable. Stockholm, July 3, 1946. On order. A. Ljungdahl, head of the Air Staff.”

One or two years later a Colonel from the Swedish Air Force, Stig Wennerström, received, in secrecy, 5.000 kronor from the Russian air attaché in Stockholm to report details on a secret air field in Uppland suspected by the Russians to be a secret NATO base. This started one of the greatest spy cases in Swedish history, but that is quite another story...

On July 6, six people met at the offices of the Royal Air Administration [Kungl. Flygförvaltningen] to discuss the situation created by the reports about ”flying bombs”. A written record was kept of main points in the discussions. First of all, let us review the backgrounds of the people who took part in the meeting:

Division head Karl-Arvid Norlin (1901-1989), employed by the Royal Air Administration, the materials division. Norlin was partly responsible for the aquisition of planes and materials for the Swedish Air Force. Norlin was called upon as an expert for several defence commissions. Norlin was a quiet man who also was responsible for intelligence matters at the administration, keeping contacts with, for instance, the US air materials division at Wright Patterson AFB.  Left the administration in 1947 to become a director with Stockholms Enskilda Bank (the Swedish Wallenberg group) and a member of the board of the SAAB aircraft company.  

Air director Tore Edlén, another employee of the Air Adminstration since 1945. 

Captain Sjölin, unknown[???], probably from the Defence Staff.

Air engineer K-G. Berg, a radar expert at the Royal Air Administration. 

Doctor C.A.Moberg, an radio communications and intelligence expert from the FRA (Defence Radio Institute). 

Civil engineer Hugo Larsson (1906-1986), associate professor [laborator] at FOA (Research Institute of National Defence) since 1945. An expert in radio, radio navigation, radar and tele communications. Became head of FOA 1952-1957, then a director at Swedish Philips Company 1957-1964. 

The proceedings of the July 6 meeting read like this:

”Flying bombs”

”Most observations give a relatively coherent desciption of the phenomenon. They describe a cigar-shaped object without wings. Size about = a small trainer. Moderate speed and relatively low height. The object is accompanied by an even or intermittent light of fire. A whistling or droning sound. 

”If the sightings on May 23-24 concern the same object speed can be calculated at between 200 and 600 km/h [??? cross-reference to memo]. The object has made great changes of course. 

”The Finnish sightings of June 9 are of a completely different character. The phenomenon is described as a fireball at great altitude and great speed. The ball exploded in the air. 

”2. An object with such a low speed and such a long range, as the Swedish sightings evidence, should be a jet-propelled body with carrier areas [Sw. bärytor]. Rocket or hydrogen peroxide [Sw. vätesuperoxid] power is hardly likely. Possibly it is a manned aircraft.
