           Efforts to investigate the Roswell UFO crash case continued during the first quarter of 1993. The
           Fund received -- and eventually approved -- a proposal by Karl Pflock, a former deputy assistant
           secretary of defense and currently a full-time researcher/writer, to conduct further research into
           the case. The grant will support general investigation costs and a field investigation trip to New
           Mexico to interview additional witnesses and to obtain documentary evidence.

           During this period, the Fund also approved a proposal to support research expenses of Leonard
           Stringfield into reported UFO crash/retrieval cases. The amount of support will be up to $1,200
          in_1993.

           On February 6, the Fund sponsored the first of what may be a series of conferences on the UFO
           abduction phenomenon. Fifty-five persons attended the invitation-only suburban Washington,
           DC, conference, including abduction researcher Budd Hopkins and 20 "experiencers" --a dozen
           of whom participated in a lengthy panel discussion on the phenomenon. The conference was
           coordinated by board member Richard Hall, who has been conducting a local abduction
           investigation effort. (Hall's report on the five-year investigation, Testing Reality: A Research
           Guide for the UFO Abduction Experience, is now available from the Fund. See the enclosed
           Reply Form for details.)

          Also during this period, the Executive Committee approved a proposal submitted by John Kasher,
          Ph.D., professor of physics and astronomy at the University of Nebraska at Omaha, to analyze
          a videotape shot on board the space shuttle Discovery in October 1991, which he believes shows
          truly anomalous objects instead of ice particles (as maintained by NASA). Dr. Kasher proposes
          to use two graduate students and a $17,000 work station to conduct the analysis. The committee
          agreed that the proposal was a worthwhile project and voted unanimously in favor of providing
          Dr. Kasher with up to $400.
