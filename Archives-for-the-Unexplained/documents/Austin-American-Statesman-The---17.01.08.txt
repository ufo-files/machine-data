The Austin American Statesman – Texas…
17th January 2008…

Look, Up In The Sky - I Have No Idea What It Is…

   There's something risky about admitting you've seen a UFO. It's got trailer park written all over it. It's kinda like saying, "OK, so I shave Dale Earnhardt's number into my back. What's it  to you?"
   My little UFO wasn't anything like the one being reported this month out of the Stephenville area, where locals say they saw something large, silent and metallic with bright lights in the  sky. I'm skeptical of UFO reports. Back in the '80s I did a news story on a family in Dayton, Texas who were suing the federal government because they said they'd been nuked by a UFO. They were claiming the military was doing some sort of experimental airborn vehicle testing in the area, near Houston. Something big came out of the sky over the highway and heated up their car. That was their story and they were sticking to it. One woman in the family even had a doctor in Alabama who said she had suffered from radiation damage, although he couldn't pinpoint from where.
   The case finally got thrown out of federal court. I'd suspected that one of the women involved had simply stuck her head in the microwave at the local truck stop.

So here's my UFO story…

   It was November, 2004, and I was headed west on Interstate 10, about an hour east of Fort Stockton in West Texas. The sun had been down for about an hour. There were no alcohol of drugs involved. I had been under a lot of stress from a family matter. But I was as sober as the front row at Hyde Park Baptist.
   Way up on the top of the skyline, as far off in the distance as you could see, I saw a tiny, bright green oval-shaped light, about the size of an M&M. It was tiny, but the color - about  the shade of a green LifeSaver candy - was distinct, eye-catching. All of a sudden, in a second, this green dot dropped from the very top of the sky, down to the bottom of it, then  sped off in a wink and disappeared in the distance. Zip. It was gone. The drop down and the speed off probably took a grand total of two seconds. If that.
   This was out on the flat part of the desert where lights play tricks on you. No aliens took me away to another planet for probes. But I have no idea what it was I saw.
   About an hour later as I was pulling into Fort Stockton I saw some blue and red lights flashing low to the horizon. But that was a different story. The nice DPS trooper gave me a warning for speeding.
   The next day I coincidentally visited the UFO museum in Roswell, N.M. and told the people there about the green light that had zoomed off in an instant. They asked me to fill out a report, but I lost the forms in the back seat of my car somehow and never did. What I really wonder about is what happened to the forms.
