PRIMEBASE;
                           It's Scope And Parameters
                                   (Robert Moore; I.U.N)

                                        Aim of Primebase:
Primebase is an attempt by myself to computer catalogue all possible "true UFO" reports
originating from England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, Ireland & the U.K's offshore
islands. This project was instigated in mid-1998 and has now reached a stage, in January 1999,
where it can be shared with other members of the British UFO community.

The case-data comes from a variety of sources; from group journals, magazines and books. The
emphasis is on reports which are seeming inexplicable. Ideally, one would hope for realms of
well-investigated and highly detailed case reports; however this is sadly not the case in many
instances! Because of this, Primebase shows the quality of the "UFO" report in question, from
probably explicable to possibly "exotic". This allows such marginal cases to be included (to give
the broadest picture possible, and to err a little on the side of caution) without any loss of
scientific objectivity.

Primebase is different from most sighting catalogues in that it is open about the shortcomings of
it's data-sample. It is also unique in that it tries to present a filtered sample of UK sighting
reports, focusing on it does on possible unknowns only.

However, the term "unknowns" cover a wide area! All forms of "true UFOs" are given equal
status in this database; with no "slanting" of the sample towards sightings of "exotics" over
"earthlights" (or sightings of "earthlights" over "exotics", even!). It also notes ball lightning
observations and other similar exotic atmospheric effects, as these fall within the scope of such
a database. Known Ball Lighting reports are clearly shown as such, as are those events
suspected of being UAPs (i.e. earthlights?) or experimental military devices. When a possible
origin is not clear it is simply noted as a "UFO"!

With a greater public airing of this data, it is hoped that Britain's best UFO reports can be
identified. This will can only aid British UFO research. Too little research is conducted on these
events; all too often British Ufology seems to focus on the concerns of the USA , neglecting
instances of the phenomenon as it manifests in this country.

This database has lots of aims; in fact it's aims can only be limited by the imagination! Firstly, it's
most important goal is to be a list of UK unknowns; no small aim by any means. Using records
going back many decades it can clearly show the ways in which the phenomena has changed
over time. It can tell us how many of a specific type of UFO report (i.e. car shops) have
occurred. It can spot possible geological "clustering". It can tell us which year actually was a
"wave" year (rather than a wave of mainly IFOs, plus a few exotics), or even if "true UFOs" occur
in "waves" to begin with!

To my mind, the most important aspect of Primebase is to show us how widespread UFO events
are over a period of time. Do UFO events occur in one place, die out, then crop up again in
another? Are totally different UFOs seen during a specific period of time? Or are same
shaped-UFOs seen within a specific region over a few days (or weeks)? Furthermore, it can tell
us (or at least suggest) whether the same type of UFO has been seen over a period of years, or
even decades!
As this database becomes more widely disseminated, it is certain that new instances of "true
UFOs" will be uncovered. Furthermore, it is hoped that some of the more sketchy report-
summaries can be improved as further data is uncovered by other researchers. It is also
inevitable that some (even many) of the PBASE cases will be accounted for in mundane terms.

Whatever the case, PRIMEBASE can only be of benefit to British UFO research.

It should be noted that PRIMEBASE is based on classification systems developed by Jenny
Randles, Mike Wootten, Paul Fuller and Mike Lewis in the 1980's but altered (often drastically)
by myself for PBASE. Anyone with a knowledge of these systems should be able to understand
PBASE case-descriptions with little difficulty. Many of the innovations used in PBASE (i.e. using
a date-format field for sorting cases into basic chronological order, and a text based field for
showing the actual case's date, which can often be quite "fuzzy") are attempts to resolve the
problems encountered by earlier UFO database classification systems. The original systems
(and my amendments) have all been used in bulk-data research projects and are hence capable
of denoting large number of reports of all conceivable types.

1: PBASE REFERENCE: The unique PBASE reference for the case in question, shown in the
following format; 19770010 (i.e. 4-figure year, followed by PBASE number of case.
In some instances the term "X" is used to donate a probably explicable case; ie 19770011X
"IFOBASE" (IBASE) references are written back-to-front, 00101970, and do not use the "X"
character (as they are *all* explicable events!)

2: Level: Investigation level of case.

3: TYPE: This shows the basic attributes of the UFO reported. It is based on the system
presented in Jenny Randles book "UFO Reality" (Robert Hale 1983), with some amendments.

4: WIT: (Very) basic description of witness details.

5: TEXTDATE: The date (as written in text format) for the sighting concerned;
   following this format; 21 Jun 1998. Also uses some abbreviations (explained
   in greater detail below).

6: TEXTTIME: The time of sighting, using 24-hour clock, written in text format,
   some abbreviations also used.

7: TIMETYPE: Shows whether stated time is either GMT or BST (if blank, GMT/BST
    status of stated time is uncertain).

8: LOCATION: Notes where sighting occurred. This system has two other ways of
   showing location, for ease of use and greater precision.

9: MAAN: Motion and Appearrance Anomalies
   Codified selected aspects of UFO's motion and and appearrance.

10: Shape: Basic description of UFOs form (based on witnesses actual description
  of it's form).

11: Colour: Colour of UFO.

          In fields "10" & "11" a "/" is used to show the hues of different section(s) of a UFO
i.e. a silvery disc shaped UFO with white lights would be shown as [Shape]:Disc/Lights.
[Colour]Silver/White. A "+" symbol shows features of interest in a given UFO. For example a
disc-shaped UFO with a dome and an "aerial" is shown as a [Shape]: Disc+dome+"aerials".
Lastly, an asterisk symbol ("*") shows changes in a UFOs colour/shape. For example a UFO
which changed colour from White to Yellow would be shown as [Colour]:White*Yellow.

12: Duration: Basic duration of sighting, shown as a 1-figure attribute.

13: Size: Size of UFO, shown as a 1-figure attribute. It has three ways to show size;
angular/object sizes, actual/estimated and angular/moondisc.

14: Details: Basic description of report. Obviously a little limited with a 70-character field!
However, using a "memo" field would drastically increase the size of this database, a problem
for a system aiming for a manageable "bulk storage"!

However, this does system has two ways to help you find more data on a particular sighting!
And, furthermore, this field does a fairly good job, anyway, of describing the basic aspects of the
report in question (coupled with the other fields of this database). Where this (and other) fields
are blank, this shows a sighting said to be unidentified, but no further information is known!
Hopefully in such instances further research will uncover the facts concerned.

15: SIGHTING EVALUATION:
In this system shows the grade of UFO concerned (as explained below).

16: INVESTIGATOR:
Shows the person(s)/Group who investigated this report.

17: GREF:
The group reference of the sighting concerned; either an actual group reference or
the magazine in which the report was taken from

18: ARLOC (ARea LOcation Classification)
Shows the basic geographical part of the UK from which the report originated from, plus the
report's Hynek classification system type!
For example the ARLOC rating for a Daylight Disc (DD) report from the West Country (EY) is
EYDD

19: CORD:
Shows the general Latitude and Longitude of the report's sighting location, in hours and minutes
of arc. Written in it's usual manner; however bearing letter is given before hours of arc; i.e.
N51.33 W02.22, as opposed to the usual 51.33N 02.22W. This is to aid searching's, in order to
allow all reports from a given latitude and/or longitude to be easily pulled from the database.

20: SOURCE: A source reference from the report in question, usually a book or magazine.
Sometimes it is not always possible to give one, as the case has not been published in any
publically-available manner.

21: CHRN: Chronological date-format field. Mainly used to sort reports into approximate date
order. In UK full-year format 12/12/1988, in order that century of report can be clearly
determined by computer.
