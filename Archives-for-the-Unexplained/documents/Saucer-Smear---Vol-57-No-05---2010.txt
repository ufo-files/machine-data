      One organization that seems blissfully unworried about the personalities and mo­
tives of possible aliens is SETI - Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence. These
scientists, led by Frank Drake and others, have now been at their mission for exactly
fifty years, according to a recent article in Discover Magazine.
      In all that time, SET! has had no success at all in getting a response from space
beings, if any. There were, however, at least a few exciting moments, most notably in
1977, when astronomer Jerry Ehman announced having received what is famously remembered
as the "Wow Signal" - so named because it looked so much like an alien message that he
wrote "Wow!" on the printout from his radio telescope. For some reason left unexplain­
ed in the Discover article, this signal was never received again, and is now considered
to be unimportant after all.
      SET! scientists are currently more optimistic than ever, in spite of fifty years
of continual failure. The reason for this is that technology has advanced so greatly
during that period of time that the odds for success are a great deal higher than be­
fore. Many planets in other star systems have already been discovered in very recent
years, and. it is now almost certain that we will soon discover smaller, earth-like plan­
ets where the possibility of some kind of life forms is extremely high.
      What we find strange is that these SETI scientists, including also the late Carl
Sagan, don't ever seriously consider the strong {but unproven) possibility that intelli­
gent beings from other planets may be visiting us �· Flying saucers, wherever       they
may be from, are simply taboo for complex reasons. SETl and UFO researchers are on en­
tirely different wavelengths, and it is likely to remain that way. Pity: • • •
