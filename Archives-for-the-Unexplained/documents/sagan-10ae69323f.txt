I would not necessarily call myself a *supporter* of Carl Sagan.
He has definitely influenced my methods of thinking and analysis,
in that he does not think that paranormal phenomena are matters
of belief, but rather are matters of evidence.  (No "I believe in
UFOs" stuff.)  I also feel that way, but where we differ
tremendously is that  1) he argues vehemently that paranormal
phenomena *do not exist*, and 2) he does not consider eyewitness
accounts of credible witnesses to be evidence at best, or even
valuable at worst.  I *do* consider eyewitness accounts from
credible witnesses to be both valuable and evidence, in the
absence of any physical evidence.  So when I say he has
influenced me, it's more that he's spurred me to prove him wrong.
<g>  But I do absolutely believe that his book ["The Demon
Haunted World"] is a must-read for those of us who look into
these phenomena, as you also say in your review of the book.
So no, I'm not a *supporter* of his major statements
and theories regarding paranormal phenomena.  But an admirer of
him as a thinker and scientist, sure.

Sue Kovach
