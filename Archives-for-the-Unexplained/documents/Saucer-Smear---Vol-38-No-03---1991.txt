THE ROSWELL CASE STAGGERS ON, EVER ONWARD AND UPWARD!

      Again regarding the infamous Roswell Incident, a matter on which we
do not claim expertise, it is interesting to note that at least three of
the more important witnesses are currently in trouble:
      Gerald Anderson, the newly-proclaimed witness discussed on Page One
of our last issue, is apparently a fake, if we are to believe the second
paragraph of Kevin Randle's letter in this issue. Randle, who is co-author
(with Don Schmitt) of a forthcoming book about Roswell, states that     "a
diary Anderson supplied is on pre- 1947 paper but used post- 1970 ink". This
is rather astounding even to the cynical staff here at "Smear"!
      Then, from the Jan. 1991 issue of the MUFON UFO Journal (Page 17),
in an article by Jaime Shandera of the rival Roswell writing team of Moore
& Shandera, we learn that another key witness, ex-Warrant Officer     Irving
Newton, has changed his story in recent years, for reasons that the author
does not understand (nor do we).
      Finally, from a very long, detailed Schmitt/Randle article in     the
Nov.-Dec. 1990 issue of the International UFO Reporter, we learn that J.
Bond Johnson, still another key witness, "has consciously changed his ac­
count for unknown reasons". (Page 1.5).
      Is it any wonder that people like Christopher Allan of England throw
up their hands in disgust; or maybe they just throw up. (See, however,
Schmitt's negative letter herein regarding Allan!)
      Incidentally, Stanton Friedman and former NICAPer Don Berliner are
also working on a new Roswell book. That means there are now three cur­
rent teams: Friedman/Berliner; Schmitt/Randle, and Moore/Shandera. The
latter two teams are publicly at each other's throats, with lawsuits pos­
sible, etc. We await further developments with Baited Breath.
      Meanwhile, the search goes on for a (mere) terrestrial explanation
of Roswell. We still don't know just what Peter Gersten has in mind;     and
we admit that John Keel's Fugo Balloon Theory has been shot down. But now
comes a new terrestrial theory from one Joe Kirk Thomas. Writing in the
Jan. 1991 MUFON UFO Journal, he speculates that the, strange amount of se­
curity and confusion at Roswell after the crash, was caused by the Mili­
tary's belief that the ob ject might be a Soviet attempt at balloon recon­
naissance. Thomas is not saying that it was a Russian balloon, but he def­
initely does not think the ob ject was extraterrestrial,- either.   Neither
the "real" nor the "fake" ob ject seen in the famous Roswell photos    looks
anything like an extraterrestrial craft, he insists. We agree!
