                                                  Official State.ent of Policy

        On the basis of~se than 40 years of consistent, close-range observations of objects having radical appearance
        and performance, ii i~ reasonable to conclude that we are faced with a coherent phenomenon for which there is
        no conventional explanation. It is the purpose of the Fund for UFO Research to support all reasonable
        scientific efforts to learn the nature of this phenomenon. The patterns of UFO appearance and behavior strongly
        suggest that they are not merely misidentified natural phenomena. In recent years, it has become apparent that
        this phenomenon involves multiple aspects:

        Extraterrestrial Hypothesis--While physical proof of the extraterrestrial origin of UFOs is not in the hands
        of civilian UFO investigators, there is a growing mass of evidence that points to the distinct possibility that
        some UFOs represent the presence in the vicinity _of the Earth of a non-human intelligence.
        11 Abductions"--While recognizing the bizarre content of so-called "abduction" reports, their unusual degree of

        consistency and supporting evidence suggests a physical, rather than a psychological, basis. Psychological
        studies of "abductees" so far tend to rule out psychopathology as the cause of the reports.

        UFO Crashes--In view of the impressive first-hand testimony to the July 1947 events in New Mexico, it appears
        increasingly likely that the remains of at least one crashed UFO were recovered by the U.S. Government.

        Government Secrecy- -The release of thousands of pages of previously classified official military and
        intelligence documents (whose very existence was long denied) makes it clear that the U.S. Government concealed
        UFO information for several decades. There is no reason to assume that the policy of secrecy has ended.

                                                     1990: The "UFO Year In Review"


                    The UFO mystery continues to grow in complexity and general appeal, even though there has been no noticeable
                    increase in sightings in the USA. After many years in which UFO activity was limited to high-altitude fly-overs,
                    there is growing evidence of their much more direct approaches to Earth.

                    In the absence of U.S. Government willingness to take responsibility for dealing with this long-term mystery,
                    private groups have shouldered the load, even though they have severely limited fmancial and technical resources.
                    Pressure continues on the government to release its considerable store of UFO information, so that the American
                    people will be better able to deal with this matter from a position of knowledge, rather than forced ignorance.

                    Over the past 40 + years, as many as 90% of all UFO sightings have gone unreported (according to informal
                    surveys) bec ause the wifnesses fearec! rioicule from tneir governmen , the press and even their friends. Of
                    those sightings that have been reported--an estimated 100,000--about 90% have turned out to be normal things
                    seen under unusual conditions: aircraft, stars and planets, meteors, clouds, etc. However, five-to-ten percent of
                    UFO cases, (or between 5,000 and 10,000 sighting reports) usually remain unexplained following investigation.
                    It is the clearly unexplainable sightings that form the heart of the UFO mystery.

                    A Gallup poll conducted in June 1990 indicated that one person in seven (14% of the adult population, or the
                    equivalent of 25.9 million people) has seen something they would call a UFO. Nearly half of those surveyed
                    (47%) said they think UFOs are something real, as opposed to people's imagination. Previous surveys have
                    indicated that acceptance of UFO reality rises with one's education and income level.

                    The Fund for UFO Research, a nonprofit scientific organization based in Washington, D.C., has as its primary
                    function the support of individual projects in scientific research and public education. But the normal course of
                    events has expanded its scope to include receiving UFO sighting reports, dealing with "abduction" reports and
                    providing information for the media.


                    UFO Sightings

                    During 1990, hundreds of UFOs were reported from all parts of the USA and around the world. But as most
                    of them were seen at night, few details could be described, and so there was little information on which to base
                    scientific analysis. The main areas of intense activity were Russia and Belgium, where hundreds of reports were
                    well publicized and given considerable credence by government officials and the press. In the Moscow area,
                    scores of reports were made by Air Force pilots who were directed toward unidentified radar targets and picked
                    up the UFOs visually and electronically. Their descriptions of huge, lighted craft having amazing performance
                    were supported by top generals in the air defense system. In Belgium, hundreds of reports were made by--and
                    to--the Air Force, and highly frustrating attempts to intercept were freely confirmed by the government.


                    Government Activity

                    While the U.S. Government continues to stonewall on the subject of UFOs, claiming--despite considerable
                    evidence to the contrary--that it no longer has any interest, other governments have adopted more open policies.
                    The Soviets cooperated with the publication of detailed accounts of military sightings, while the Belgian Air Force
                    even took private UFO investigators aloft in military aircraft during a period of great UFO activity. The Soviet
                    Academy of Sciences now has a full-time specialist--former submarine commander Vladimir Azhazha--
                    coordinating the activities of more than 100 local and regional investigative groups.
Crashes and Retrievals

There are now three highly credible reports of a UFO having crashed and its remains being recovered by the
government that have been testified to by many witnesses: two in New Mexico in 1947, and one in Pennsylvania
in 1965. In each instance, civilians got to the scene before the military arrived and were able to get a good look
at the wreckage before being chased away with stern warnings. The Fund for UFO Research is supporting
efforts to locate more witnesses and to record their testimony, and in 1990 the organization held a closed meeting
of ten such persons. (A fact sheet on the New Mexico UFO crash cases is available upon request.)


"Operation Majestic Twelve"

An 18-month study of the controversial 1952 "Eisenhower briefmg paper" (on its face, a report prepared for
President-elect Dwight D. Eisenhower on the recovery of UFO wreckage and bodies from New Mexico in 1947,
which led to the establishment by President Harry S Truman of a TOP SECRET government operation called
Operation Majestic Twelve) was completed by nuclear physicist Stanton Friedman and published by the Fund
for UFO Research. It concludes that while there is no evidence to support the charge that the document is a
hoax, neither is there any way to prove that it is genuine. But in view of the strong evidence for the event that
triggered the document, it seems reasonable to assume the briefmg paper is probably genuine.


Abductions

This is by far the most bizarre aspect of the UFO mystery, involving as it does the alleged involuntary, temporary
removal of innocent persons by presumed aliens for what is described as "unconventional medical procedures."
Well over 1,000 accounts have now been recorded, and several hundred have been investigated in detail by teams
of UFO specialists and mental health professionals. The consistency of the accounts, extending even to details
which have been withheld from publication as an internal check, points to the growing likelihood of these strange
events being real. In 1990, there was one private conference on the abduction phenomenon for psychologists and
psychiatrists, and two more have already been scheduled for 1991.


Crop Formations

While there is no clear link to UFOs, many people think the hundreds of precisely geometric formations which
have appeared are highly susceptible to conventional scientific study, yet no reasonable explanation for them has
yet been offered. The increasing frequency and complexity which marked the 1990 "season" has prompted
suggestions that they could be a form of communication from an unnamed intelligence, although no messages
have been deduced from them.


Mystery Airplanes

At any one time, some UFO sightings almost certainly can be blamed on secret airplanes, though airplanes that
were secret 30 or 40 years ago are all now public. There is a growing number of reports and rumors of very
high-performance American airplanes of triangular shape which resemble UFOs reported in the eastern USA
and as far away as Belgium by publications such as Aviation Week and Space Technology and The Wall Street
Journal. Their lack of jet-like noise, often startling speed and maneuverability, and presence thousands of miles
from known test bases, suggests either an amazing technological breakthrough, or a coincidental similarity of
shapes and behavior.


The Fund for UFO Research, now in its 12th year, continues to raise money to support research into the above
matters and others, with the goal being the solution of the UFO mystery. During 1990, total income to the Fund
exceeded $70,000; since it was organized in 1979, the organization has raised more than $200,000 to support
scientific research and education projects relating to Unidentified Flying Objects.
