      First, our apologies to all of you for the fact that this is sue is reach­
ing you more than a month behind our usual schedule . The vast amount of pre­
paration which went into the 1967 Congress of Scientific Ufologists in New
York , plus the necessity of moving our office to larger quarters, are mainly
responsible for this delay. Another factor was a roberry which occurred in the
new office over Labor Day weekend . Our postage meter and other important items
were stolen, including valuable material from our files .
       This leads u s to the main topic of this editorial. It i s very clear , as
of this writing , that the long-standing UFO mystery has quite suddenly entered
a new and apparently more dangerous phase . We use the word "dangerous" advis­
edly, not wanting to sound like alarmists .
      However , information reaching us during the past few months , from several
reliable sources , indicates that leading researchers as well as ordinary sau­
cer sighters throughout the country are being subjected to an unbelievably
complex and extensive series of frightening events . Among thema Attempted as
well as successful ''hush-ups" by mysterious strangers usually dressed in dark
clothing; Weird or threatening phone calls and letters; The appearance of cars
with meaningless license plates or no plates at all, which have followed cer­
tain UFO investigators in the course of their work; and other events which are
so utterly fantastic that we hesitate to put them into print.
       Please do not think that we have lost our equilibrium or are merely try­
ing to •• jazz up11 the UFO investigation. We are merely reporting the evidence
which we have uncovered .
       It is difficult to pinpoint just when and where these events began. Our

civilian investigators, but who ( as we later learned by checking with the man­
ager) , had no connection whatsoever with the Commodore . These men were appar­
ently working out of a room near the hotel's coffee shop , to which there is
acces s both from the lower-level hall and from nearby Grand Central Station.
There is even reason to suppose they had wire-tapping equipment set up tempor­
arily in this room - though by the time anyone thought to check this out , it
was too late . The Convention was over and the men, whoever they were , had de­
parted .

                                - by John A. Keel -

        Arter almost twenty years of total confusion and meandering journeys
down blind alleys , hard-core Ufologists around the world are finally closing
in on the real " secret" of the flying saucer puzzleo The extensive world-wide
flaps of 1964-67 have provided a wealth of new clues to the mystery , and now
all the pieces are being fitted together.
        Already thousands of people in the United States know the full " secret , "
believe it or not , and thousands more will have the answer before this year
ends . These people comprise a select group which we now call '' silent contact­
ees," and they have been playing a small but important role in the numerous
flaps of the past three yearso In my travels throughout the country in the
last year , I have uncovered and interviewed dozens of these " silent contact­
ees . " They all have the same story to tell , and more important , most of them
have been given identical artifacts as proof of their experience .
        The " silent contactees" differ from the over-publicized "contactees" of
the past in several important ways . First of all , they are afraid to speak up
and reveal what they know. They find themselves in the midst of a complex con­
spiracy which poses personal threats to them , and they have been involuntarily
enlisted to participate in a series of experiments and adventures which go far
beyond anything ever dreamed up by hack science-fiction writers .
        Extensive " contacts" have actually been going on since 1939, bu t many
have been handled in such a manner that the " contactee" often did not realize
what was happening to him, and frequently rejected the whole situation as some
kind of hoax , or , in some cases , a hallucination or experience with " ghosts . ••
In many instances , once the ''contactee 1 s" role in the phenomenon was finished ,
they were somehow "brainwashed" and all conscious memory of their experiences
was wiped away. I have learned of other cases in which the " silent contactees"
either went insane or committed suicide . Still others changed their names and
fled to new areas , or even new countries , in an effort to escape their strange
fate .
       A rmmber of very well-known UFO researchers have been " silent contactees"
for years and have cautiously sprinkled genuine clues in their books and arti­
cles , but have never dared to reveal the whole story. Other prominent Ufolo­
gists have been contacted briefly and given specific information to be delib­
erately passed on to their readers . Ironically, the real "truth" is so bizzare
that sensible researchers usually reject it outright until they themselves
have first-hand experience . Many of them find it impossible to cope with emo­
tionally and they quietly abandon UFO research in the interests of self-pre­
servation.
        Nearly all of the speculations and wandering " scientific" conclusions of
the last two decades are totally false . The celebrated " scientific method" can
not , in fact , be successfully applied to this situation. Astronomers and sci­
entists are the people least equipped to understand the phenomenon , thanks to
their rigid disciplines and preconceived notions of the Universe . Metaphysi­
cians , on the other hand , are too gullible and too quick to apply any pseudo­
scientific explanation.
        There is no simple answer to the mystery. All of the current theories
ranging from ''extraterrestrial'' to ''fourth dimensional" can not actually be
applied. Such erroneous concepts mislead researchers and blind them to the
actual truth buried in the available data . The only effective way to cope with

         ANOTHER "MAN IN BLACK'' CASE: ( Special to SAUCER N&IS from John Keel , via
Gray Barker and James Moseley.) A barking dog was responsible for transporting
a modest young man on Long Island into the eerie world of flying saucers ,
strange men in black garmets , and threatening phone calls , - the dark world of
fear and frustration that is swallowing up hundreds of innocent people ever,y
week , while governments look the other way and pretend that nothing is happen­
ing.
         The dog ' s howling wakened Joseph Henslik of Greenlawn , Long Island , N .Y .
on a morning in October , 1966 ( he does not remember the exact date . ) He looked
outside and was excited to see a strange circular object hovering directly
over the post office building not far from his home . He grabbed his camera and
took a whole roll of film, shooting a sequence of pictures of a luminous          me­
tallic disk with a windowed dome on top. Since the object was very low, he was
certain that he had obtained some sensational pictures; and when he received
the finished prints back from a local commercial processer a week later, he
saw that he was right. At least , this is what he claims, for no one else ever
saw those pictures'
         Two days after the negatives and prints were returned to him, Henslik
was visited by a pair of strange men. He was returning home late, about 3
a .m. , when he found them waiting for him. They were both of medium height with
deeply tanned complexions and dark hair. Both were dressed in baggy black
trousers, with black turtleneck sweaters and loose black jackets which he de­
scribed as looking like "black smoking jackets or pajama tops . "
         ''We want to talk to you about your sighting," one of the men said, in a
voice with a slight accent which sounded Scandinavian. "We ' re from the Govern­
ment . "
        Mr. Henslik asked them for credentials, he says, but they told him that
they were from a secret agency which they were not allowed to name . They went
on to tell him certain facts about himself wh�h astounded him - facts that
not even his closest friends knew.
         ''We know that you have taken some photographs," the men contirmed, "and
we want you to turn them over to us, for the sake of yourself, your family,
am your world, and as a good American. "
         The flabbergasted young man decided to stall, and told them he had not
gotten the pictures back yet. He was told that they would be back in exactly
twenty-four hours, and that he had better have the pictures available for them
at that time .
         Henslik remembered that one man had a slight scar on his left cheek ,
while the other had an identical scar on his right cheek.
         The next day Henslik studied the pictures carefully with a lens . He says
that there was definitely some markings on the object, but they were too fuzzy
to read. "If I could have blown the pictures up, I probably could have made
out what they said," he claims . He also says that he noticed some kind of
large shadow moving up and across the windows of the craft in the final frames
of film.
        At 3 a .m. the following morning, three men appeared, all dressed iden­
tically. Again they threatened him vaguely when he appeared reluctant to turn
over the pictures . He finally gave them both the negatives arxl the prints .
They checked the negatives carefully with a flashlight and then warned him
sternly to keep quiet about his sighting and the photos "for the sake of your
family and your world . " When they left, he did not see any vehicle, although
he live s on the outskirts of town and a vehicle would be necessary to visit
him.
         In the months that followed, Henslik learned of Gray Barker , and claims
that he wrote Gray six letters . Barker never received any of them. Finally,
after coming across Barker ' s book, "They Knew too Much about Flying Saucers,"
in June of this year, Henslik decided to call Barker long distance .
