A curious Defence Staff memo

A curious document, found in the ghost rocket files in Stockholm is an early memo [”PM över inkomna rapporter av observerade eldfenomen”; ”Memo of received reports on observed fire phenomena”] where  the two previous meteoric overflights, on the night between May 23 and 24, are analyzed as if they were airplane or V-weapon overflights. What is probably this very document, was entered in the diaries [logging of incoming letters] of the Research Officer of the Defence Staff on June 3, 1946. The ”analysis”, itself, is found in the ”space projectile” file main file. 

In this ”analysis”, time estimates for the sightings are taken literaly, without regard for human mistakes in timing, and used, combined with the geographical positions of observers, to calculate trajectories and to estimate the speed of observed ”objects”. 

The Staff may have been influenced by the Stockholm newspaper Aftontidningen which made the same kind of ”analysis”: ”If the times are correct it would mean that the speed was something between 350-400 km/h. In Landskrona the opinion was that the speed was about the same as an airplane.” (Aftontidningen, May 27).

The Defence Staff analysis rests on only five of the reports detailed above (see cases xxx-x, xxx-x, xxx-x, xxx-x and xxx-x). The author of the document is, seemingly, ignorant of the difference between ”course” (direction of flight) and ”bearing” (in what direction of the sky the phenomenon was observed by the witness). It is impossible to say what is meant by the word ”direction” [Sw. riktning]  in some of the short  newspaper reports, especially the ”midnight meteor”.  This problem is ignored, but is a constant confusing factor in much of the analysises performed by the Defence Staff in the summer of 1946. There is no evidence that the reports of the May 23-24 meteors were ever analyzed by professional astronomers.    

Another confusing mistake, in this document, is that the dates mentioned in the report are obviously one month wrong. 23/4 (April 23) should read 23/5 (Mary 23), etc. 

Evidently, the document was written in all haste and by someone who was ordered to do it without having the proper qualifications.

During WWII, the Swedish air defence handled more than 16.000 reports of aerial violations of the territory. The bureaucracy surrounding this reporting, which had ended in May 1945, was now seemingly resumed, one year later. Procedures for recording and analyzing overflights of German Messerschmidts or British bombers were, however, neither applicable nor appropriate for handling light phenomena, such as meteors, or (hypothetical) extremely fast-moving rockets, like the German V-2:s of WWII.    

We will now cite the entire document, which is quite revealing:

”Memo of received reports on observed fiery phenomena. 

”I. Reports received have conflicting data on the performance of the fiery phenomena. Speed has been estimated at values between about 100 -- 1.000 km/h, and more. Estimates of altitude has varied between about 0,1 -- 50 km. 

”II. By projecting the trajectories the following kan be certified: 
     1) 23/4   23.45  Untrannortheasterly direction
                   23.48  Gävle      southeasterly direction
      The distance Untran - Gävle is 30 km. If it is the question of the same phenomenon the distance has been covered with a speed of 600 km/h. If so, the course, however, must have been changed over Untran from northeasterly to northwesterly and over Gävle from northwesterly to southeasterly. 

         24/4   00.05  Västeråssoutheasterly direction
     The distance Untran resp. Gävle - Västerås is 100 resp. 125 km. The distances have been covered in 20 resp. 17 min, which means 300 km/h resp. 450 km/h. In this case, too, changes of course must have been made over the previously mentioned places. 

     2) 24/4  ca 02.00  Karlskrona   southerly direction
                  ca 02.20  Landskrona  southerly direction
     The distance Karlskrona - Landskrona 175 km. If it is the question of the same phenomena, which is probable in view of relatively similar course speed will be 525 km/h. 

      3) If the same phenomenon was [should have been] observed in Västerås and Karlskrona it would have covered the 400 km distance in 2 hours, i.e. a speed of 200 km/h.”

(end of document)

In the margins of the typewritten paper someone at the staff penned the comments ”could be rocket” (close to the 600 km/h estimate) and ”low speeds, should be rocket” (close to the 300 and 450 km/h calculation). The calculated speeds have been underlined in red. A ”rocket” that travels at 600 km/h?

Source: Memo found in Defence Staff files. Also: Fst/Forskn.off. [Research Officer] diary: Ank.nr 84/1946: 3/6 1946 from Fst/L [Air Defence department]. War Archives, Stockholm.
