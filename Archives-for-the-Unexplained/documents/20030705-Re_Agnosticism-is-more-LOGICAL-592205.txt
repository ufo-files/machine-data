  In talk.atheism Big Bird <condor@biosys.net> wrote:

         wen-king@myri.com (Wen-King Su) wrote in message news:<bdt6ek$s78@neptune.myri-
         local.com>...


                 Both "god does exist" and
                "there is no god" are claims that stand in need of proof.


         They sure are.


         As you folks like to talk about "probabilities":
         the negation of "the probability of X is zero" is
           "the probability of X is more than zero".
            It is NOT "the probability of X is one".


         I.e. the negative of "there is no X" (<- this is the exact same as saying
         that the probability for its existence is zero) is "there may be an X".


         The negation of "there is no god" is "there may actually BE a god".


         Thus the claim "there may be an X" is just as much in need of a proof as the
         claim "there is no X".


  Wrong, as usual. To make a negative of a statement, you put a 'not' or a
  'no' into it. So if the statement is "there is not god" you get "there is
  not no god", which is semantically equal to "there is a god."
  Now were you also screw up is in equating "there is no X" with "the
  probability for it's existence is zero." The chance that an X CAN occur can
  be greater than zero without the X actually occuring. Thus the odds of rain
  on any given day can be very high but yet it doesn't rain. Even saying that
  "X is impossible" isn't the same as saying "X does not exist." All that
  saying "there may be an X" means is that "the possibility of X has not yet
  been shown." And that isn't really a statement of fact that's true for all
  but actually as an implied "[shown] to me" in it. So there's no need of any
  more proof that "the impossibility of X has not been shown to me" than there
  is for "ice cream tastes good to me." They both are facts but they both only
  apply to me.
