ISIS: • Bali:For the past 1o centuries, the inhabitants of the island of
Bali have believed that their water godess Dewi Danu is living in the cra­
ter lake   of the Batur vulcano. All irrigation work had to be in keeping
with Agama Tirtha, "the religion of holy water" . The whole system of con­
sultations between the farmers, the priests and thewater godess was aban­
doned years ago for a more western and scientific approach. The result was
a desertation of the once fertile agriculture of the island. Now two re­
searchers of the University of Southern California, anthropologist Stephen
Lansing and ecologist James Kramer were able to show that the ancient ri­
tes were ecologically almost perfect, and they have introduced a computer
programme based on these rites that will re-establish the old system.
 (Geoffrey Cowley: The Electric Godess, Newsweek, March 13, p. 44) - This
shows that not all mythical water creatures have their biological counter­
part, and the attempts by cryptozoologist to find the " real" animal behind
the folklore may sometimes resemble von Danikens search for the robots
behind ancient mythological drawin gs.
