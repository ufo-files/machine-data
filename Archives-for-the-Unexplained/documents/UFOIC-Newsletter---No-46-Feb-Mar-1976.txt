                                                   EDITORIAL


      Firstly we must apologise for not issuing a newsletter in November/December
      1975.     This was due to the fact that most of our editorial and clerical
      staff were on holidays and we had no one to do their work.


      Apologies are also in order for the late issue of the first 1976 newsletter.
      This has been due to several factors:


      As you are all no doubt aware,            UFOIC is a non-profit organisation receiving
      no financial       support other than membership fees and proceeds from the sale
      of UFO literature.          At the same time we are having to face increased running
      costs for items such as printing,             stationery,      postage and rents.       The
      situation has been financially grim for some months with members of the committee
      subsidising the organisation.             We have decided that this state of affairs
      cannot continue and have therefore had to examine very closely the future of
(·
      UFOIC.     We had a number of alternatives open to               us:-


               close the organisation altogether to public subscription and retain
               a small research unit personally funded by the investigators themselves

               Try to reduce costs by limiting the number of meetings held during
               the year

               Possibly moving out of the city area into one of the suburbs where
               rents would be cheaper and therefore costs. reduced


      We decided on alternative two,            as we felt we had a moral obligation to members
      to continue on a public footing and also felt that a city location is more
      central to most suburbs arid provides reasonable transport links.


      We have therefore decided no longer to hold members meetings and to limit
      ourselves to 3 large public/members meetings per year.                   1976 meetings are:-

proof in themselves.   The McMinnville May 11 1950 photos, the June 1976 San Jose

                                        A BOOK REVIEW

              "UFOs Over the Southern Hemisphere" by Michael Hervey

 The 1975 hardcover book, "UFOs Over the Southern Hemisphere" published by Robert
 Hale (London} professes to be the result of 27 years research by "one of the
 world's leading authorities on the subject" .. namely Michael Hervey.

 The book, in fact, is an uncredited and slightly expanded version of a 1969 Horwitz
 paperback. At that time, it enjoyed considerable popularity, mainly because
 it represented an attempt to compile "all" Australian and New Zealand reports.

 To people unacquainted with the now generally acknowledged extent of the UFO
 phenomena, the book was both entertaining and informative. But, serious research­
 ers in retrospect have made valid criticisms of the paperback. It could at best,
 only be regarded as a superficial compilation, lacking both indepth research and
 original scholarship.


 To describe Hervey as "one of the world's leading authorities" would be totally
 erroneous.   The book has no original research and I can say, with some authority
 that I seriously doubt whether Mr. Hervey has ever personally investigated a
 UFO sighting, the results of which have become available for examination by serious
 researchers.   Indeed the book is obviously based heavily on newspaper reports
 (often documentation in the book proceeds no further) and numerous publications
 of UFO research groups throughout Australia. It is only in the case of relying on
 the latter source that documentation sometimes raises itself to a somewhat more
 acceptable level. The "first hand" sections of the book consisting of letters
 by witnesses of UFO phenomena could have provided a basis for some original
 research, but Hervey even fails to do this.

Apart from the presence of a large number of low weight sightings, of obvious
limited relevance, the book has many errors in details, and in a significant
number of cases fails to document recognised explanations. An excellent example
involves an undated report occuring sometime between 1955 and 1958. Hervey
describes an unknown object seen in a paddock near Millicient, S.A. by a Mr. R.
Banks. The sighting actually took place on January 8th 1956 and was subsequently
revealed that the witness most probably had observed a meteorological balloon.

The new hardback makes no attempt to remedy this position. Indeed, the situation
is somewhat aggravated. In the "Recent Sightings" Chapter, one glaring mistake
must be pointed out.  Hervey alludes to the "UFO sightings" over Taree during
September 1972 but fails to indic·ate that these reports were conclusively
ascribed to observations of the planet Venus.

The "Sundry Sightings" sections are a new addition, but many of the sightings
listed are merely repetitions of cases included in the main text. Another
valid criticism is that Hervey has kept the same (1969} "Forward" detailing the
"current" status of UFO research in Australia. No attempt has been made to
detail progress of research since 1969.

In conclusion, this rather shallow book presents an unfortunate contradiction.
It is an entertaining compilation of sorts of the UFO phenomenon "down under"
on one hand, but on the other it falls far short of providing an accurate, well
researched look at the UFO problem.
