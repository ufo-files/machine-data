                    "SAUCER SMEAR" EDITOR INVESTIGATES

                    �NEW.   UNPUBLISHED CONTACTEE CASE!

      Recently we were informed through former researcher Gray Barker con­
cerning an alleged UFO landing and contact which occured near Romney, West
Virginia about 4 a.m. this past March 22nd.  Although investigation is not
our prime motivation here at "Saucer Smear", we nevertheless eagerly seized
the opportunity to meet Barker in Romney and interview the lucky  contactee
first-hand.  We were the first and so far the only UFO researchers   to   in­
vestigate this case at the site!
      Donn Shallcross is a 36-year-old construction worker who lives    with
his family in a large trailer located next to an isolated mountain   road.
Across the road from him are rolling hills and two abandoned farm   houses
some distance apart.  It was from this direction that Shallcross saw a bright
light come noiselessly to Earth.  He assumed that a small plane had crashed,
and being a member of the local volunteer rescue squad, he woke his   wife,·
told her to phone the state police, and took off by himself in his •68 Olds­
mobile along an unpaved road to try to reach the area of the supposed crash.
      After Shallcross had driven just a little ways, the headlights     and
motor of his car died out, and he proceeded on foot, with a flashlight. Be­
fore he reached the open field where the bright light had landed,   he   was
startled to see a normal-looking man standing before him, wearing   a   one­
piece silvery body suit.  Shallcross recalls that the suit covered   the en­
tity's entire body from head to feet, like a diver's "wet suit". There was,
however, an opening for the eyes, covered with what looked like glass     or
plastic.  In his hands the visitor held a rod about three feet long,    which
did not seem to be a weapon, and did not unduly frighten Shallcross.

              There followed a conversation through mental telepathy which lasted
      something like a half hour, though Shallcross can only remember parts of it.
      (It's obvious to us that if he is telling the truth, hypnotic regression is
      needed to bring back the missing memories: ) As soon as the contactee real­
      ized that he was dealing with some sort of alien rather than a normal pilot,
      he started to ask appropriate questions. Among other things, the visitor
      told him that he is a "watch guard"; that he is "not from here" but      has
      been here many times, as have other visitors; that God and Jesus Christ are
      valid; and that we of Earth should use atomic power for energy rather than
      for war. He also talked telepathically about pyramids ( shades of Art Good­
      stone! ) and mentioned the name "ITZAMMA", who turns out to be, according t·o
      Shallcross, a Myan Indian Sky-God deity.
              Suddenly, somewhere in the course of this thought transference ses­
      sion, both Shallcross and the entity moved about 25 yards without his know­
      ing how or why! Thereafter Shallcross was close enough to see details of
      the craft for the first time. The UFO was about twenty feet in diameter,
      and shaped like the oft-heard saucer placed upside down on top of another
      saucer. There were at that time two white lights visible at opposite en9s
      of the craft, and a door was open, from which poured out heat and a purple
      haze of some sort. There was also a dome on top and a circular window near
      the top, which appeared to go around the whole circumference of the object.
      A purple glow also radiated from this window.
               The visitor seemed like a two-dimensional projection to Shallcross,
       as his mouth never moved when he "talked", and especially because, at the
       end of their strange conversation, he simply disappeared from one second
       to the next: At that point the door of the craft was suddenly closed, pre­
       sumably with the occupant inside. The central part of the object lit up
       with alternate red and yellowish-orange lights and started spinning around
       faster & faster, making a humming sound. The UFO then took off      slowly
       from the ground. When it reached the height of a nearby telephone pole
       it became ever more noisy, and left the area so rapidly that it was out of
       sight, past a hilltop on the horizon, in about three seconds. Shallcross
       recalls that the further away it flew, the brighter it was, for some rea-
       son. �
               When the contactee returned to his car, the lights and engine worked
       normally. When he reached his trailer he was still so agitated from the
       encounter that he almost threw up. He was also sunburned, apparently from
       the purple radiation that came from within the craft (as in the famous
       Cash case in Texas). He states that all the exposed portions of his face,

noticed the burns; but unfortunately Shallcross did not go to a doctor.
Burkhart, whom we interviewed separately, stated that in his opinion the

       Officer Burkhart confirms, however, that the state police received
two anonymous calls that night about a light plane apparently going down
near the Shallcross property. These phone calls were received just a min­
ute or two before the call from Shallcross's wife. Burkhart therefore went
to the UFO landing location for the sole purpose of investigating a plane
crash.; and, finding no sign of any such crash, he considered his job done,
and went back to the station house after questioning Shallcross. Thereafter,
he states, he did an informal background check-up on Shallcross, and found
nothing to either strongly confirm or deny the UFO story as told by the
contactee.
       Perhaps the most interesting aspect of this story is the effect the
incident has had on Donn Shallcross, who is a religious though uneducated
man. Since the flying saucer encounter, he has devoted himself diligent­
ly to reading up as much as possible about pyramids, UFO flight patterns,
and other such things. He has decided ( or possibly he has subconsciously
learned from the encounter) that the UFOs have a base in· a certain sparcely
populated section of the U. S. northeast. He will not reveal the exact lo­
cation for fear that hostile Earthmen would get there ahead of him.     His
hope is to go to this secret location this coming · summer with his family.
Strangely, he does not expect the UFO to land again in his area, nor does
he feel he has been specially chosen for any mission . But he does believe
that the Visitors have advanced knowledge that he might be able to persuade
them to share with Mankind, for the benefit of all.
       That, gentle reader, is the story: Like all such stories, it cannot
be proven true or false. Apparently something landed near Shallcross's
trailer at about 4 a. m. on March 22nd. Only he knows what happened during
his alleged private encounter with the UFO occupant; and quite obviously,
even he does not know, nor can he remember, the full implications of what
transpired. If "Saucer Smear" receives further details on this interesting
case, we will duly inform our readers.

           WILHELM PUBLISHES DEFINITIVE EXPOSE OF "TOMATO MAN "
     We have received from Charles Wilhelm of OUFOIL ( Cincinnati, Ohio) a
long-awaited report on the alleged space entity we sarcastically called
"Tomato Man" when we discussed the case in our Feb. lOth 1981 issue    and
elsewhere. We also ran a deliberately unintelligible photo of "Tomato Man"
in our Oct. 1st, 1980 issue.
      In brief, "Tomato Man" supposedly crashed and died in a flying saucer
in July of 1948, just over the Mexican border from southern Texas. This
event was described in detail by a mysterious "source" known only to "Dr. "
Williard Mcintyre of MARCEN - a controversial Maryland-based UFO club. Two
photographs were supplied with the story, making this the first time in
ufological history that pictures were offered to the public in an effort to
prove a "crashed disc" case. If true, the story could have been the most
sensational and important news item of modern times!
      A detailed report on "Tomato Man" was eventually brought out by "Dr."
Mcintyre together with Wilhelm and one Dennis Pilichis of Rome, Ohio. As
a group, these three called themselves the Coalition of Concerned Ufolo­
gists. One of the Coalition's purposes at the time was to express their
viewpoint in regard to complicated feuds they were having with    Leonard
Stringfield of Cincinnati on the one hand, and your "Saucer Smear" editor
on the other - both of whom had expressed skepticism about the authenticity
of the case. All the complex details of these feuds have been explained in
past issues of our zine.
                                      (5 )
        Now, the Coalition has broken up. Pilichis has gone his own way, and
is, with Mcintyre, involved in a long investigation of a bizarre Bigfoot
case near Rome (Ohio) . Wilhelm has "buried the hatchet" with us, as we
noted in ·a recent issue, and is now feuding with Mcintyre. Again to make
a long story short, Wilhelm now believes (as we do) that he was duped by
Mcintyre into supporting "Tomato Man" as being a dead alien.
        Wilhelm's current report, prepared with the help of other members of
OUFOIL, is called "An Investigative Report into the Alleged Alien      Body
Photos". It runs more than fifty full-sized pages, and is an excellent job
of research! It would be hard to exaggerate the amount of trouble these
investigators have gone to, in order to show that the crash probably involv­
ed a human pilot in a light plane, rather than an alien in a spacecraft, or
even (as has been suggested) a dead monkey in an early V-2      experiment.
Point by point, the authors show that the Mcintyre-supplied story does not
check- out in any way!
        Our only criticism would be that the booklet suffers from far too
many misspellings and similar minor errors•   .But to us, the important thing
here is that a pro-UFO team of investigators has made a thorough, rational,
and sincere attempt to show that a sensational saucer case is not authentic.
        This, then, is the definitive work on "Tomato Man", just as your edi­
tor's expose of George Adamski's "desert contact", many years ago, was the
definitive work an that subject. Questions will always remain, as Wilhelm
admits in his tome; but no one in or near his right mind will continue be­
lieving "Tomato Man" to be from Space after reading this booklet. Available
for $6. 00 from OUFOIL, P.O. Box 436, Fairfield, Ohio 45014.
        Footnote: Belated credit to Bob Easley of Cleveland, who coined the
now-classic term, "Tomato Man".
        Further footnote: In fairness, we phoned "Dr." Mcintyre for his re­
buttal and/or comment on the above. He states that his comments an Charles
Wilhelm are unprintable, but that he was present when Ron Schaffner of OU­
FOIL recorded a whale sound from a phonograph record which he (Schaffner)
later used on an Ohio radio program as the purported sound of Bigfoot in a
cave.
        Mcintyre also states that he will soon be publicly showing a sensa­
tional Bigfoot movie, filmed in Washington State in the early 1970's, which
shows four different Bigfoot-type creatures in clearly-visible sequences
(i. e. , even better than the famed Patterson Film) . Finally, Mcintyre tells
us that his own "Tomato Man" update will be in the next issue of the long­
awaited MARCEN Bulletin. He agrees, however, that "Tomato Man" is clearly
not from Outer Space.

            NICAP's DEMISE CHEERED BY SO�, MOURNED BY NONE
      NICAP - once the most powerful pro-UFO group in all Saucerdom, has
merged with CUFOS, according to an article by Richard Hall in the February
1982 MUFON Journal. In the article, Hall traces NICAP's history, from its
heyday under the direction of Major Donald Keyhoe (long retired) , to its
decline & demise in later years.
      Old-time UFO fans will recall the running feud between SAUCER NEWS
and NICAP, culminating in "A Resolution for a Better NICAP" published in
our March 1966 issue, and signed by about two dozen of the top ufologists
of that era.  Keyhoe got even by illegally refusing your editor access to
the NICAP bulletins, even though these were passed out free to members of
Congress and whoever else was thought to be friendly to the Cause.
      Another high point in the feud was the day your editor phoned Richard
Hall, then #2 man in the NICAP office, and was accused by Hall of recording
the conversation.  (Your editor, to this day, has never owned a tape re­
corder! ) Your editor screamed "PARANOID! " at Hall, and hung up. In   the
intervening years, we have exchanged a maximum of about half a dozen words,
mostly mutual "hellos" when we meet at saucer conventions.
                                    ( 6)
       We should mention here the little-known fact that, following the 1966
"Marsh Gas" flap, your editor gradually replaced Major Keyhoe on the lucra­
tive college lecture circuit, and spoke at well over one hundred colleges
throughout the U. S. during the next eight years.    (We in turn were gradual­
ly replaced by the genial Stanton Friedman, whose authoritative beard and
"-NUCLEAR PHYSI CI ST" lapel button out-classed us completely. But we love
you, Stan:)
       What was our long-running feud with NI CAP all about? Frankly, it was
based on .old-fashioned unfriendly rivalry,' but we did have at least    one
serious point: I n an effort to downplay the apparently ludicrous claims of
the contactees (Adamski, Betherum, Fry, etc. ) , NI CAP made arbitrary rules·
as to what kind of UFO cases it would "allow". At first a saucer could
come close to the ground, as long as it didn't land; then the unwritten
rules were eased so that it could land as long as no one got out; and fin­
ally., under continuing pressure from us and from the Phenomenon itself, NI ­
CAP allowed the spacemen to get out of their craft as long as they didn't
say anything philosophical: I n other words, NI CAP was forced to accept the
"little men" stories, though they still rejected the contactees.
       We were by no means pro-contactee, but merely insisted on fairness.
Where would the NI CAP of old fit into today's Saucerdom, where abductions
have become commonplace, not to mention contacts of all kinds, interplanet­
ary sex, murder by UFO, and a host of other horrors! ? Many of the most
serious & highly ·educated of ufologists now accept at le.ast the possibility
of hypnotic regression, Jt-D or 4-D entities, alternate universes, and the
like. The artificial line NI CAP tried to draw between "crackpot" and "be­
lievable" has become so blurred as to be non-existent. The "spiritual lead­
er" of the Field is no longer Donald Keyhoe but Dr. J. Allen Hynek, a truly
outstanding scientist who will accept the possibility of just about       any­
thing. I t is most fitting indeed that Hynek's CUFOS was the organization
that took over the dying NI CAP, and we wish Hynek well in this & all his
other endeavors.
       Of course, it can be argued that NI CAP's "public relations" approach
was correct, in that it presented the UFO mystery to the public in a rela­
tively simple, 3-D, "nuts and bolts" form. NI CAP insisted that UFOs had to
be physical interplanetary craft, and nothing else. The general public of
that era agreed that if saucers existed at all, they must be interplanetary.
Now that the facts as well as the theories have become much more complicat­
ed, the public is confused and relatively "turned off", not having the time
or interest to explore the subject deeply. For many people a National En-
quirer article has the same weight as a scientific treatise. But what can
we do? The mystery is complex and confusing, and to present it any oth�r
way is a fraud, in our opinion.
       One final note before we leave NI CAP to its eternal rest: Most peo­
ple believe NICAP was founded by Major Keyhoe, whereas in reality it was
founded in 1956 by a relatively unknown ufologist named Townsend Brown. We
remember Brown's original glossy brochure, in which ambitious, costly plans
                                     ·

       When Keyhoe took over about a year later, he scaled the plans down
to reality, and led the organization off into its almost paranoiac anti-Air­
Force stand, rigid in the belief that we would all learn the Truth if only
 the Air Force would open up its files. Again, your editor disagreed, and
 often ridiculed Keyhoe in print for this obsession. Here was a retired Ma­
rine major, with a board of directors consisting largely of other retired
 officers (including a former head of the CI A! ) screaming endlessly about
 military secrecy: We still think there was something strange and       even
 "fishy" about this, and we still insist that the Military does not have
 "The Answer". Documents released in recent years under the Freedom of I n­
 formation Act have proven interesting, but have not solved anything. Only
 the Phenomenon itself can provide a solution, in our opinion - --if it cares
 to and is able to.
