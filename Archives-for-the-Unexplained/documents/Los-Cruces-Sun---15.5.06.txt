White Sands Missile Range Facility Looks Deep Into Outer Space…

This column is provided by White Sands Missile Range's Public Affairs Office. For more information, call the office at (505) 678-1134.

   At the northernmost edge of White Sands Missile Range sits the Ground-Based Electro-Optical Deep Space Surveillance facility. Its mission is to detect, track and identify all tasked space objects within its area of coverage. The system is capable of tracking deep-space objects as small as a soccer ball in orbits from 3,000 to 20,000 miles with a limited near-earth detection capability.
   Much like its sister Air Force sites on Diego Garcia in the Indian Ocean; on Maui, Hawaii; and at Moron, Spain; the White Sands detachment is far from its operations group and wing headquarters. It has the distinction of being the first operational site in the Ground-based Electro-Optical Deep Space Surveillance system. Observation data is provided to the Joint Space Operations Center at Cheyenne Mountain Air Force Station, Col.
   The location at White Sands was chosen for it's near perfect weather year-round and almost absolute darkness on moonless nights. Because the site uses an optical sensor, mission operations are limited to relatively clear sky conditions at night.
   The site employs three powerful telescopes and can send an average of 11,000 observations per night on as many as 2,500 different satellites to include payloads, rocket bodies, and debris.
   Also, the unit is the sounding board for new innovations in optical surveillance. New equipment such as the infra-red cloud imaging system and the Deep STARE upgrade from ebsicon tubes to Charge Coupled Devices have been proven at White Sands prior to installation at the other optical detachments. These multi-million dollar upgrades have improved optical surveillance seven-fold and put GEODSS in the forefront of USSTRATCOMs space control mission.
   The White Sands site has been around since before Air Force Space Command and began as Detachment 1, 1st Strategic Aerospace Division, Strategic Air Command in April 1981. Since then, the unit has changed hands several times and finally became part of the 1st Space Wing, Air Force Space Command on May 1, 1983. Both the detachment and its parent squadron were reassigned to the wing from the 73d Space Group on May 15, 1992, when the 21st Space Wing assumed responsibility for all space surveillance units.
   Upon closure of the 18th SPCS in June 2004, the optical dets were brought directly under the 21st Operations Group.
   The site is operated and maintained by 19 contract workers. One Air Force Captain assigned is responsible for ensuring the mission is accomplished and the contractor's work performance is satisfactory.
   The current contractor is Northrop Grumman Technical Services, which has held the contract since October 1, 1983.
