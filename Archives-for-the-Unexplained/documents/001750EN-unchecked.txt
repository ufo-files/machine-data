Memo
Regarding  observations of  “ghost projectile” in the neighbourhood  of  Svenstavik railway station.

At 13/8 1946 between 1100 _ 1130 o´clock was observed a so-called “ghost projectile” in the neighbourhood  of  Svenstavik  railway station.  The observation was done from three different observation places,  on the attached  sketch  from south  numbered 1 – 3. 
The projectile, which  caused a strong  whining sound, is described as about 60 centimetre long and provided with short wings somewhat before the middle.
The course of events at the observation was as follows.  At observation place 1 was a man called Åström occupied with cross-cutting of wood, when he heard a strong whining and howling sound. When he looked up at that direction from where the sound came, he saw  a projectile with aforementioned appearance. The projectile flew  with “high” speed at approximately 100 metre altitude and with an angle of dive of about 30 degrees on straight northerly heading. Åström was convinced that the projectile would end in the lake, but a birch curtain at the shore stopped him from observing the crash. The sound ceased immediately after the projectile was obscured by  the birches.
Approximately 25 metres farther north (observation place2) was another man standing, Andersson, who did similar observations regarding sound and appearance of the projectile. Andersson saw the trajectory more at cross-course and in a gap in the birch curtain. He thought he saw  how the projectile was losing speed and “wobbled with its wings”. He is convinced that the projectile hit  the water but could not see the crash because of the curtain of birches.
At observation place 3 was  a man, Edström, occupied with digging a foundation. He also heard the strong sound and did the same observations regarding the appearance of the projectile as the others. 
The course of the projectile was from Edströms place approximately + 45 ((vet inte vad han avser??)).
Also here the trajectory of the projectile was obscured by the curtain of birches, when the projectile was in a approximate heading of 5700. No observations from the opposite shore was made.
The depth of water in the bay is in the deepest place about 2 metre, but  is the larger part of the bay no more than 1 metre. The bay is in the spring drained. The bottom constitutes of hardpacked clayey sand and is near the shore rather stony. The water is muddy, why the bottom cannot be observed at larger depths than 3 to 4 decimetre.
