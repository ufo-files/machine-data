    Our Review                                                                                             Opinion of the webmaster, subject to debate

  This is the "sequel" to Rhodes' previous work, The Making of the Atomic Bomb. The book picks up shortly
  after WWII and follows the development of the "Super", as the hydrogen bomb was so quaintly
  nicknamed. Almost the first third of the book covers the Soviets' extensive espionage program, which
  enabled them to drastically reduce the development time of their own weapon. The book goes into detail of
  exactly how hydrogen bombs operate (for those do-it-yourselfers out there), with material that had been
  classified until recent years. An excellent companion to Rhodes' first book. -- tm
