                                                           THE TIME PARADOX
                                                                   By G. Heard.

                 If the earth was moving through the ether, as observations had shown it
was, then the double journey of lightbeams, first from east to west, and then
from west to east, should take slightly longer than one of equal length from north
to south and south to north.                              Logically, the time lag between the two beams would
reveal the earth 1s speed through the ether.

                 But the results were against all "common sense"!                                It proved conclusively
that, despite what happens in the case of oarsmen (one rowing a certain distance
upstream and downstream and the other rowing an equal distance across stream
to arrive at different times, the difference in time between the two boats reveal­
ing the speed of the current) light beams have equal transit times.

                 To check for inaccuracies, the experiment was repeated, with the appar­
atus turned through 90 degrees so that the "downstream" leg became the cross ..
stream 11 leg, and vice versa.                             Yet, still the lightbeam s arrived at precisely the
same instant I                The over-riding truth of the matter is that the speed of light is
constant, regardless of the earth's motion.
