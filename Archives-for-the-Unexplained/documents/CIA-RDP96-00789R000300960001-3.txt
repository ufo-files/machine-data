Project 8802 Interim Report:


Note:   In each case the large building in the center of the photo
was assumed to be the target structure.   However, descriptions
may include activities associated with the site in general.   Data
refers to present time unless otherwise noted.


Source #018:  Inside the target structure a machine oil smell was
present.  Also, dimly-lit rooms with long tables along the walls.
There was the overall impression of an "assembly" area where
things were "added to" an already assembled thing.   Descriptions
of the "thing" include tall, slanted, slender, thin, straight,
and a "funny green color".   People were working on this from
underneath it and had to assume awkward positions to do so.

Source #079:   Described the interior of the target structure as
being much like a "laboratory".   The area was sterile; a large,
odd-shaped piece of equipment was sitting on a table.   Workers
here examine and probe.   The work is tedious and frequent breaks                          '
are taken.   Associated ideas include "optical, illusionary,
binary, movement, flow of data" and "charting, mapping,
examining".   Concepts connected with the end product
were "upgrading, updating, new".

Source #003:   The description of the target structure includes
perceptions of spacious, bright, white rooms and {perhaps)
chrome/stainless steel pipes or tubing.   People are engaged in
ongoing activities; its like a hospital, but more intricate.
Concepts associated with this idea are: "micro-segments,
microscale, elaborate, detailed, complex, and assemblage."
Scheduled to occur sometime in the near future something like a
trial run of technical equipment--like the first operation of a
prototype.   This idea seems to be associated with an
object/machine that is horizontal, well made, metallic,
mechanical, electrical and electronic.   "Test-bed" is also a
related concept.   The object is precise and complex; {perhaps it
might be compared with an arming/functioning mechanism for a
device).   Presently it is being "assembled"; but this task is
complicated.   People must place components into proper relative
positions requiring very tight tolerances and where direct
observation of the placement is often not possible--almost like
they have to "feel" as they put it togeth~r.
