    Now, nearly five decades after the     inception of its modern
period, the so-called UFO phenomenon       remains as a critically
challenging element for the formation of an intellectual1y defen­
sible world-view.  The appreciation of this philosophical predica­
ment has b een adversely    influenced by sensationalized media
treatment of the   UFO   subject;  by deliberate state programs
designed to forestall  objective consideration of the situation
generally; and probably due, also, to the radically unexpected
nature of the phenomenon; and to subconscious anticipation of an
attendant, enormous threat potential.

least a highly significant problem exists because a large fraction

hypotheses are to be reasonably fitted to the full spectrum of

broader framework of a coherent world-view.    Thus, largely on
the basis of the first two of these requirements, it can be seen
