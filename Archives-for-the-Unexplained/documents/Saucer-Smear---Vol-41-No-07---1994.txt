A BLAST FROM THE PAST: THE STRANGE STORY OF ROBERT SPENCER CARR:
-        -------           --                   -                       --




      On April 28th of this year, Robert Spencer Carr died of an apparent
stroke at a nursing home in Clearwater, Florida.   Carr (not to be confused
with pseudo-inventor Otis T. Carr) was in his early 80s.
      According to Curtis Peebles' excellent book "Watch the Skies! ",    it
was in October of 1974 that Carr, a retired teacher of mass communications,
held a sensational press conference, in which he claimed that for the past
25 years the Air Force had twelve alien bodies in deep freeze at Wright-Pat­
terson Air Force Base. Also that two flying saucers, both captured in 1948,
were hidden at the base. He is also quoted as saying, "Five weeks ago,     I
heard from the highest authority in Washington that before Christmas    (1974)
the whole UFO cover-up will be ended. There will be a public admission that
UFOs are real and are piloted by human-like beings. " (One more example of
how "end of the cover-up " stories never turn out to be true! )
      Somehow your "Smear " editor managed to get in touch with Carr    years
later - possibly through Gray Barker. In March of 1984, your editor togeth­
er with two other people interviewed Carr at his luxurious home on       the
ocean front of Clearwater. At that time Carr had a defective artery at the
back of his head (or so he said) , and was a semi-invalid, being unable to
stand up for long or leave the house easily. He said little about earlier
events, but told us in some detail about a series of contacts he himself had
had in 1983 with Ji-foot-tall humanoids who visited his property in a space­
ship about ll months in a row, on nights when there was no moon.
      These creatures came in a 19-foot-diameter craft. There were three
occupants, two of whom were able to "shield their minds " from Carr's tele­
pathic powers, whereas the third could not. Thus Carr was able to read the
mind of one spaceman, even when the creature was not communicating     tele­
pathically with him. Their craft always landed in the small garden under
the deck of Carr's home, being extremely careful not to be seen by anyone
other than Carr, who seemed to share their paranoia.   Carr gave them    pure
water and did all sorts of favors for them, hoping they would eventually let
him go with them to their mother ship or beyond; but they never did.
      Although Carr told us he never gave interviews any more, he was very
kind to us for a whole afternoon - apparently because he seriously believed
your editor to be an Intelligence agent of some sort. He therefore felt ob­
liged to te�l us all he knew!
      Carr asked us not to print the interview in "Smear " until after    his
death, so as not to embarrass him. We made him this promise, and kept him
on our mailing list for ten years after that, so that we would know of his
death when it cameo  Carr rarely communicated with us by mail until about a
year ago, after he finally sold his house and moved to the nursing home. He
then started writing long letters on various UFO-related sub jects, and we re­
sponded as best we could. Finally we wrote reminding him of the 1984 inter­
view, and asking him if he still felt the same way about it. He never ans­
wered that question, but in going through Robert Carr's papers, Carr's son
Timothy found an empty envelope addressed to your editor. On that      basis
Timothy Carr got in touch with us.
                                             (4)
      One of the people with us at the 1984 interview was a highly-trained
nurse, and it was her opinion that Robert Carr believed what he was telling
us about the extraterrestrial visits to his home, and that his experiences
were hallucinations caused by his physical condition. On the other hand,
Timothy Carr probably has the real answerc  He informed us on the telephone
recently that his father told these stories and several other similar ones,
just to make himself seem more interesting. And thus we close the file on
Robert Spencer Carr....

and negatively-oriented person, beset by one bout of bad luck after another.

be a magnet for bad luck. Whether this was his acquired life-script or his
karma, I don't know, although I suspect he himself would presume the latter.
