Stora Mellösa precinct
Örebro
Written by the district police superintendent in Glanshammar district
Submitted on July 19, 1946

Report on sightings of ghost lights in the town of Usta, Stora Mellösa, Sweden, on Friday 12 July, 1946.

On Monday, July 15, an article in the newspaper Nerikes Allehanda made me aware that a light phenomenon had been observed in the town of Usta, Stora Mellösa. By reason thereof, I tried to reach the author by telephone in order to obtain information regarding the person who had observed the phenomenon in question. Thus, I got in contact with a woman by the name of Inga Eriksson.
Mrs. Inga Eriksson, born on 23 December 1912, resident of Odensbacken, stated the following information.
On Friday, July 12, 1946, at 10 o'clock PM, she had stood in the garden next to her house, which is located 125 metres East of Usta school. Suddenly, she heard a sharp sizzling noise in the air and as she directed her gaze upwards, she saw three silvery triangle-shaped objects, which travelled East to West. The observation lasted about two seconds. Mrs. Eriksson couldn't remember noticing a trail of smoke, nor was she able to provide information on the approximate distance between herself and the objects.
Mrs. Eriksson is unsure of the course of the objects, but she has stated that they were moving horizontally and not in a zig-zag formation. While the phenomenon didn't cause any sharp noises, Mrs. Eriksson has stated that it sounded remarkably similar to a certain rocket plane that she had seen in the city of Örebro.
At about the same time as Mrs. Eriksson observed this strange phenomenon, her mother appeared beside her and said that there had been a power failure. Mrs. Eriksson's husband is an electrician and owns a device  that indicates the time of a blackout. Within moments, lights were back on. It could be theorized that the phenomenon itself had caused some electrical interference.
Major assembler Ragnar Barret, also a resident of Odensbacken, lives about 1 kilometer from the observer and owns a similar device, but has nevertheless stated that it didn't register any kind of disruption during this time. Whether or not there had been any problems regarding electrical interference in the adjacent buildings remains an unanswered question, since they weren't illuminated during this late time.
Mrs. Eriksson describes these objects as three triangles, each moving in a sequential pattern. These objects were about 80 cm in length, 50 cm in width and the space between each triangle was about 20 cm.
Thus far, we haven't made contact with any additional observers.


Wednesday, July 17, 1946.

Stora Mellösa

Carl Berg
District police superintendent
