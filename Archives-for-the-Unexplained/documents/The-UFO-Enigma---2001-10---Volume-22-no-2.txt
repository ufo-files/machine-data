                                    BIG TRIANGLE SEEN


In mid June 2001 the efforts of a small group of skywatchers was rewarded with a
sighting of a very large triangle. Four people, including myself, were spending the evening
at a Missouri farm visiting and watching the sky for whatever came along - planes,
meteors, aerial lights, satellites, anything. Three of us were in a position to see a large
portion of the sky, unfortunately the fourth person was not and didn't see the craft.


At 10:30 PM after an uneventful evening of watching a perfectly clear dark sky two of saw
something almost directly overhead and called the attention of the third person io it.
What we saw was a triangular shaped object that was perceived as being at a very high
altitude and of enoumous size. We had nothing to use for comparison for size or altitude
but our impressions were in agreement. The object was very high and very large. It's
width was equal to slightly more that the span of three fingers at arm's length - that's
huge! It was an equilateral triangle and solid. It occulted stars as it moved across the
sky in a NW to SE direction. Each of the three points had a bluish-white light and the
"bodY' of the craft was covered with many smaller dimmer lights of the same color. In
the center of the triangle was one brighter light that was on for about three seconds then
went out, reappearing about one minute later for another three seconds. This light was on
four times before the object went out of sight. It was the only light that changed, no
others flashed, strobed, changed color or altered position. The brightest lights, the three
on the points were about 3.0 magnitude, determined by comparison to the brightness of
known stars. It was completely silent. If there would have been any sound had it been
closer is open to speculation.


It did in fact look like a "moving starfield" or starfield camoflage. We probably wouldn't
have noticed it against the starry sky if we hadn't happened to look directly at it and
realized that 1) there was no constellation of that configuration in that part of the sky
and 2) it was moving. It moved in a straight line at a steady speed with no variations in
course or speed from the time we watched it from almost directly overhead to the time it
went out of sight about 5 deg. above the horizon. It didn't "tum off" or disappear or
speed up it simply got so far away we couldn't see it. It was in sight for 5 minutes, timed,
not estimated.


An odd thing about the motion/direction of the craft was that while it moved in a straight
line it didn't move with the apex, the point of the triangle straight ahead. The triangle
was slightly skewed with the leading point slightly to the left of the line of motion. Our
perception was that the leading edge was not the point but a flat side, not completely
turned flat edge first but perhaps 15 degrees from point first. The distance was such
that all we could see was lights and shape, we couldn't see anything metallic or otherwise
or color but it was definitely solid as shown by the fact that it blotted out stars as it
passed.


From many other skywatching sessions we have organized what to watch, etc. So we
observed as carefully as possible direction, speed, lights, light or pattern changes, sound,
shape, whether or not it was solid, time in view, and our individual impressions. After the
object was gone we compared our perceptions and impressions of what we had seen and all
                                                                                 ·




three of agreed in every detail. Our only comment was and is- mind-boggling!


We didn't attempt to take any pictures. The camera and film kept at hand requires a
light/star of about magnitude 2 to register a useable image and this object was no more
than magnitude 3 so we decided to watch as closely as possible rather than waste time
trying to center a faint image, focus and make an exposure that we knew from experience
would almost certainly fail.


We were witness to an incredible sighting that certainly made a lot of uneventful
skywatching well worth the effort. This was a case of being in the right place at the right
time so keep looking up!


J.D. Scarpellini, Chief Field Investigator, UFO Study Group of Greater St. Loouis
