  Don't be fooled by this story. It's a red herring. Whether it is
  true or not this is not the first time the US has accused these people
  of spying for Iran. What you now describe as a nail in the neocon
  coffin was used as a nail in the Clinton Administration's coffin five
  years ago.
  Aras Karim was named on the search warrant for the Chalabi raid. Aras
  is no stranger to US intelligence officials. His uncle, Dr. Ali
  Karim, is part of the "Iraqi Six" who were imprisoned by the FBI (via
  the INS) because of their connections to Aras Karim. This happened in
  the late 90's.
  Now the Iraqi Six's case against the INS was represented by none other
  than former CIA Director James Woolsey. Woolsey successfully
  negotiated their release, although Dr. Ali Karim still sits behind
  bars waiting for a "security office" to review his case. At the trial
  the FBI testified that the CIA believed Aras, who was on the CIA
  payroll with Chalabi as part of an operation to stir up Kurds in
  Northern Iraq, was also on the Iranian payroll.
  Not only that, but in 1995 one of Chalabi's men, Khidhir Hamza,
  released a set of documents about Iran's nuclear program to US
  officials. The documents were later found to be fraudulent and
  written by an Iranian man originally in Farsi.
  So, basically, the charge of spying for Iran has been an
  ace-in-the-sleeve of the US all along. They could have played it at
  any time.
  (Also, someone in this thread suggested Chalabi was a convenient
  scapegoat because he is a foreigner and Muslim. Not quite true. He
  is a graduate of MIT and the University of Chicago. He is a
  Washington insider and a British citizen with residency in London.)
