FROM ME TO THEE,
(Vikki T.)
Nov 30, 1998
Hi Gang,
Saturday, Nov 21 started out like any
other Saturday. The editor and I were working
at CAC HQ and since I'd been working on a
project that had worn me out, I talked him into
taking a break for a restaurant dinner after we
dropped off the day's mail.
It was after dark and rain was coming
down in torrents, so visibility was bad when the
idiot light went on for the battery. So while I
was unloading mail, he found that the alternator
had stopped turning. Our stress levels were
building and after some near-panic driving to
find one thing and another, Avaton went into
what I thought was hyperventilation, and had
me drive.
Our original plan was to find a car part
or repair place, and the restaurant was still
waiting, but as I heard him gasping for breath, I
realized we were driving to the hospital-
despite his request to just drive him home. I got
him there in about 5 minutes, ran into the
emergency room explaining he couldn't breathe
and needed care immediately-then waited for
what seemed to be an eternity (probably 3
minutes) before medics actually became visible.
Once they got their hands on him, things
went quickly and efficiently. While I gave
paper work information, they went to work and
had him breathing better very quickly. That
was the beginning of the long road back to
normalcy.
In short, we came within 10 minutes of
not having our editor. He had that long to live.
What I had thought was hyperventilating was
congestive heart failure and his lungs were as
full of fluid as they could be and him still be
here to tell the tale.
We were lucky that we were so close to
the hospital when it hit and lucky I could make
the drive without an accident or without the car
battery giving up the ghost. If the car had died,
so would have Avaton. The cardiologist told us
he was lucky the admitting doctor was patient,
as most would have jammed a breathing tube
down his throat-which sometimes doesn't
work out, but did for Avaton.
Avaton says if you guys are interested,
he'll tell the tale from his perspective sometime.
He doubted it would be of interest, but I've
never known anyone that close to death
before-
He was stabilized, then transported up to
ICU, where he spent Saturday-Wednesday. All
the local family were allowed in Sunday, which
I heard later may be have been because it might
be their last chance to see him (usually only 3
visitors at a time are allowed, I guess).
Sunday was the angiogram, which really
surprised the cardiologist, because it showed no
blockages: in fact, the artery system of a 35 year
old! He told Avaton's kids they all had the little
spots of plaque that did show up and while
doctor said he didn't believe in chelation, he
had no other explanation for the clean test. So
you folks considering chelation, this is a
dramatic demonstration of how well it works.
What was the problem, was the heart
muscle, which is pumping 35 percent at present.
The average heart is putting about 70-80 out, so
he has a ways to go, but Dr. George has told
him to take Cardioguard (which contains
CoQ10, carnitine, and hawthorn berry) and
germanium to increase the oxygen in his blood;
so we expect to see a full recovery over time.
So, while I finally get to write you guys
myself, it's for an occasion I would never want
to repeat. But while I have your attention-this
is when we send our annual holiday gifts-to-
CAC suggestion letter, so I hope you remember
CAC in your holiday generosity. It's been
suggested we raise rates and stop asking for
donations above them, but we get so many
letters from folks saying they're just barely able
to meet them that I'd hate to do that. So I'm
hoping those of you who can, will help at
whatever level you feel our work is worth.
Also, some may feel like sending
flowers or some such for Avaton, and he
requests any monies that might be spent that
way be put where it would really do CAC some
good, sent to us to meet CAC's expenses.
