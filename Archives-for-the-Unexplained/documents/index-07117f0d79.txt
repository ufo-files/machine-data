   Credibility = Very High: In general, this catagory is a rating of the author's intellectual discipline and the strength of his argument (which are

  This is the story of Nikola Tesla, the brilliant, obsessive and slightly crazed inventor (as are the good ones)
  of alternating current, Tesla coils, wireless power transmission and who laid much of the ground work for
  the development of radio. The book also covers Tesla's bitter feud with Edison, his chief rival at the time.
  The work of Tesla is beloved by conspiracy buffs, who credit technology descendant from Tesla's original
  work with UFO propulsion, mind control, death rays and earthquake generation. This book sticks to the
  facts, though, and is considered the definitive Tesla biography. You ought to read this one before delving
  into the wild stuff. -- Glenn Campbell

  Publisher's Synopsis

  Portrays the trailblazing nineteenth-century inventor, the man who introduced the fundamentals of robotry and computer
  and missile science and who harnessed the alternating electrical current used today.


  From the Book Cover

  A visionary genius,his discoveries radically changed-and continue to change-the world we live in. "A dramatic and
  poignant portrait. "-Discover

  "A visionary genius as fertile as any in the modern history of science..."

  Flamboyant, eccentric, almost suppernaturally gifted, had he been born today he would still be ahead of his time. Called
  a madman by some, a genius by others, and an enigma by nearly everyone, Nikola Tesla was perhaps the greatest
  inventor the world has ever known.

  He was a trailblazer who created astonishing, world-transforming devices, often without theoretical precedent. It was
  Tesla who harnessed the alternating electrical current we use today...Tesla who actually invented radio... Tesla who
  invented fluorescent lighting and the incredible bladeless turbine. He introduced us to the fundamentals of robotry and
  computer and missile science, which continue to create and transform the future.

  This fascinating biography, acclaimed by American Scientist as "excellent...a significant contribution to the recent
  history of science," is a riveting journey into the mind of the nineteenth-century wizard who was Edison's enemy, Mark
  Twain's friend, J.P.Morgan's client, and mentor to many of the twentieth-centuey's most famous scientists.

  Margaret Cheney is a science writer and author of two other nonfiction books. She lives in California.
