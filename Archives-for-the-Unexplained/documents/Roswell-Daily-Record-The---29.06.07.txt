The Roswell Daily Record - Roswell, New Mexico…
29th June 2007…   No Vacancy: Roswell Booked For UFO Fest…

   The UFO Festival is attracting visitors from all over the universe, filling hotels and RV parks with reservations weeks in advance of the event.
   Roswell's population is expected to double during the festival and some members of the community are taking extra measures to provide as many visitors as possible with adequate lodging.
   Pat Seminatore, communications coordinator for the Chamber of Commerce, said she has received various calls from outsiders seeking to lodge in Roswell. Seminatore has suggested they visit the Chamber's website to investigate local lodging. She has also been giving them contact numbers for the Chambers of Commerce in Artesia, Ruidoso and as far as Carlsbad so they can seek lodging there.
   Seminatore mentioned she has been contacted by several Roswell locals who have extra bedrooms they are willing to rent during festival days so that she can refer anyone needing lodging to them.
   At this point, Seminatore said she has no way of judging if there will be more visitors this year to the UFO Festival because it is the 60th anniversary of the supposed Roswell  Incident. She said many of this year's visitors may have come to Roswell for previous festivals.
   "I think the people who came last year, or during the 50th anniversary, have already made their reservations way in advance," Seminatore said.
   Marv Davis, owner of the Western Inn, said his hotel has been booked from July 4-8 for the past two weeks.
   "I've turned away between 30 to 40 people," Davis said. He said he has witnessed a gradual increase in the amount of visitors to Roswell for the UFO Festival over the years.
   "It's getting more and more people every year," Davis said of the festival. "There are more people calling in that are being turned away. I don't know what they're going to do," he said in  a sad tone.
   Davis said it would be illogical to build more hotels in Roswell because they would be unable to sustain themselves during other times of the year. He said advertising of the festival and the news of a possible UFO theme park may have brought more people into town this year.
   When the theme park is completed, Davis said, "this place will go crazy. It will be like Disneyland."
   "Because [the UFO theme park] is different, we will get people from all over the world," Davis added.
   Rebecca Jordan, front desk clerk at the Holiday Inn, has been working at the hotel for two years. She said there seems to be more people interested in coming to Roswell for this UFO  Festival.
   "This is a lot busier than it has been," Jordan said. She said the hotel has had to turn away nearly 50 people. She said during any other time of the year Holiday Inn staff refer people to  other hotels in town, but now, there are no vacancies in Roswell.
   "Recently, we've been referring people to Artesia, Portales and Ruidoso," Jordan said.
   Paul Nunez, front desk clerk at Motel 6, said the hotel is booked during the UFO Festival dates and the staff receives numerous calls daily.
   "There's nothing we can do," Nunez said of the lodging crisis. He has suggested to potential visitors to contact the Motel 6 in Ruidoso.
   Nunez said he is excited about the upcoming festival and that it may compare to the 50th anniversary festival.
   "It's going to be just as big as the 50th," Nunez said. "This year's festival will be bigger than last year's."
   Seminatore investigated lodging accommodations Thursday morning and found only a handful of available rooms and availability for "select" dates during the week of the UFO Festival. Select dates means these hotels and motels have availability on single days  during the festival- not the entire week.
