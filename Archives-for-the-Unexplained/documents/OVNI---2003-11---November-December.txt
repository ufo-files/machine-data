                           The Sudden Death of Graham Birdsall

                              7th April1954--19th Sept.              2003




                 'Big in stature, Big in his generosity and Big in his impact on Ufology'



The whole of the 'Ufo1ogical' world was stunned when the news first came through of Graham
Birdsall's sudden collapse with a brain haemorrhage in the early hours of Thursday Sept. 11th.

In spite of valiant efforts by the hospital surgical team, which included a 7 hour operation, he
succumbed to a final massive heart attack at 5am on the morning of the 19th September. His wife
Christine and his two daughters were by his side.

Messages of condolence were sent from around the world to the grieving family. They came from
Ufologists and many leading scientists in the UFO research field.

Graham was the Editor of 'UFO Magazine', which had grown from strength to strength under his
leadership. It became one of the Worlds leading magazines in the presentation of UFO events and the in
depth reporting of government and military activities both at home and abroad.

Graham travelled widely, attending UFO conferences around the world. One of his most interesting
reports being that of his recent visit to Hong Kong and detailed in a recent issue of UFO Magazine.

There should be no need draw attention to the continued success of the annual 'Leeds UFO Conference'
organised by Graham and his staff. It grew 1n stature year by year and became under his direction, the
biggest event in the European 'UFO Calendar'. Understandably this years '22nd Leeds International
UFO Conference' due to take place from 26th-28th September, was cancelled immediately Graham
became ill.

His vibrant leadership in the field of Ufology will be greatly missed and his passing leaves a void in the
subject that may take many decades to refill.

                       UFO SEEN OVER WARSOP, MANSFIELD
                                         (8th October 2003)
UFO sightings have been few and far between in the last few weeks, but a report from Mr P. Martin is
worthy of a mention.

Mr Martin telephoned your editor recently in an effort to find someone that would listen to his story and
not ridicule him. His verbal report of an event over Warsop near Mansfield, Notts. was followed up with a
UFO Sighting Account Form and the details of the sighting that took place at lam on the 8th October 03,
are given below.

"I was about to get into bed, when through the window I saw what looked like a plane at first. But the
more I looked, I realised that it was not moving."

"There are houses across the road from my house and this object was hovering above the skyline of their

"The sky was clear and the two tiers of lights on the object were very bright. The top lights were large

length. It was not directly over the house, but way back over some fields. Then as I watched, it slowly de­
scended down until it was obscured by the rooftops over the road."

"There was no sound and the object was too big for a plane, also the lights were too bright for it to have
been an aircraft."

disappeared and he also mentioned that he had seen objects over the same area, seven years previous. At

                                  A Further Family Bereavement Experience                          (29-9-03)

Dear Omar,

I had a similar experience to 'Carol' (OVNI Sept/Oct) in 1978.

I had been with my first husband to his Mother's funeral, who had died very unexpectedly with a heart
attack. Late that evening, back at home, the rest of the family had gone to bed.

I was on my own watching television in the lounge, when suddenly an oval shaped sort of light appeared
before me.

It framed a scene, there was a straight road, disappearing into the distance (like a railway line does). On
the left was a row of 1930's semi's (houses), on the other side of the road on the right were playing
fields, I noticed that the land was completely flat.

Walking away from me, with their backs towards me, were two people. One of them turned around, it
was my mother-in-law. She smiled and waived and said to tell everyone (her family, she had 6 children),
that she was well and happy and not to worry about her. The she walked on down the road with her
'companion' (who I didn't recognise or see the face ot).

At this point I was 'gobsmacked' and amazed, so I screwed up my eyes and pinched the top of my leg,
thinking I was dreaming. Then I opened my eyes and the scene was still there!

The colours in the scene were 'out of this world', sort of luminous and pale, but brilliant and bright, not
like any colours I have seen in this world.

As I watched the two walk away, the scene just gradually faded.

I blinked a few times, consciously and found I was still sitting there in the lounge.

I can understand why Carol didn't tell many people about her experience. When I told my husband and
his siblings about it all, thinking it would comfort them, I was accused among other things of being a
'witch'!

Since then, I have become interested in the 'unknown' and 'unseen' and had many psychic experiences.

I had better get back down to earth now!

                                           From the Moon to Mars - The Story of Beagle 2
                                   A Lecture at Derby University by Dr Geraint Morgan, 1
                                                                                        51h October, 2003

For any of you that have been outside after about 7pm, you can hardly have failed to notice the glowing orange "star" in the
southern skies. On August 27th, Mars was closer to us than it has been for 65,000 years. Some people have imbued this fact with
great significance, but whatever you believe, it seems likely that this is the reason why ESA (the European Space Agency) chose
this time to launch its Mars Express mission. The Beagle 2, a British-built lander package, not originally planned to be part of the
mission, and Mars Express were launched aboard a Soyuz rocket, from the Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan on June 2nd
2 003. The Beagle 2 lander is due to touch down on the Martian surface at around 3:00am on Christmas day this year.

The Lecture at Derby University was due to be given by Professor Colin Pillinger                   - the "instigator" and main "driving force"
behind the Beagle 2 project. However, Professor Pillinger was called to a project meeting and so another member of his team, Dr
Geraint Morgan, stepped into the breach. Dr Morgan gave his lavishly illustrated,fact-packed lecture to a people-packed theatre.

                          Beagle 2 was named after Charles Darwin's ship. After Darwin made his voyage on HMS Beagle, he drew
                          up his theory of The Origin of Species - which is now the basis of accepted thinking about how life evolved
                          on Earth. It is hoped that the science carried out using Beagle 2 will shape our understanding of how or
                          whether any life has ever evolved on Mars.

                          The 1st part of the lecture was something of a combined history and chemistry lesson, where Colin
                          Pillinger' s work on analysing both Moon rock and meteorites was explained. Colin Pillinger pioneered a
                          number of techniques for improving the quality of mass spectrometry readings from rock samples, allowing
                         more information to be obtained from smaller samples of material. Various slides explained some of the
chemistry and science behind the study of meteorites that have been discovered over the years. The tale was related of the only
known fatality caused by a meteorite fall - in 1911, a meteorite struck a dog in Nakhla, Egypt. The main reasons for studying
meteorites are to try to find their chemical composition and therefore that of their "parent bodies". This typically gives us more
ideas about the formation of the early solar system and any organic compounds that may have been present.

It was then explained how Colin Pillinger decided that, following the announcement of the Mars Express mission in 1997, a
Lander "module" would be an essential addition - particularly because, according to the mission plan, he had calculated there
would be a "payload space" of 120 kg. Pillinger outlined his lander plan to ESA, but they then advised him that only 60 kg of
payload would be available for a lander and he would have to find the money to fund any such project. Pillinger and his team,
                         showing tremendous skill and tenacity, overcame many technical and logistical difficulties in designing and
                         building Beagle 2. He brought in the expertise of a number of private companies, including McClaren (of
                         Formula 1 fame) to build the shell and structure of Beagle 2, and Astrium (manufacturers of the Sky TV
                         satellites) to design and build the descent and landing system. The design of the
                         lander was revised from the original "box-shape" to the more elegant "pocket
                         watch" or "clam shell" design, which made the mechanics of unfolding the
                         solar panels more simple (and reliable). Professor Pillinger and his team
                         achieved another engineering miracle, by shrinking the components required
for a mass spectrometer down from the size of several fridge-freezers, into a 4 inch cube. The so­

     The evenings have now drawn in and it is dark soon after 6 p.m. A good test of sky transparency is whether
     the Andromeda Galaxy is visible to the naked eye. The great Galaxy should be easily seen from locations free
     of artificial light pollution on a Moonless night. To find Andromeda, venture out into the blackness at midnight,
     look due north, stretch your head right back (gently) and you will see a small fuzzy, hazy image: that is
     Andromeda.


     Our own galaxy (the Milky Way) is actually a giant, its mass being equal to one trillion Suns and its diameter
     about 100,000 light years. The Milky Way belongs to the Local Group of galaxies, a small group of three
     and over thirty small galaxies. The Milky Way is the second largest galaxy after Andromeda, which is around
     two-and-a-half million light years' distance.
•



     A number of faint galaxies lie much closer to us. Several of the dwarf Local Group members are satellite
     galaxies or companions of the Milky Way. The closest is around 80,000 light years distance and is actually
     interacting with our Milky Way galaxy, which is very gradually swallowing it up. Moreover the Milky Way and
     Andromeda are on a collision course and will merge together in the very distant future. The two galaxies are
     inextricably gravitationally bound and their fate is already sealed.


     Mercury and Venus are both low in the evening twilight while Mars dominates the evening sky. Saturn is at a
     good altitude by midnight before Jupiter rises in the morning sky. Do make every effort to observe all five
     naked eye planets this month. Remember to wrap up warm on these cold November nights.


     Full Moon occurs on the 9th. November Moon name$ are Snow Moon, Dark Moon, Fog Moon and Mad Moon.
     November was the ninth month in the oldest Roman ealendar. In the Celtic tradition it was the beginning of a
     new year. The Celtic year ended on the eve of Samhain (Halloween) and a new one began the day after.
     They considered it a Moon (month) of beginnings and endings and many still do.


     The Leonids meteor shower occurs on the 18/19th and promises to be quite spectacular again this year. I
     shall be out all evening lying on my sun bed looking up at the heavens. I shall be thinking of you, watching the
     meteors with me and though we are separated by distance we will all be watching together, connected by our
     collective unconscious and by our wonderment of the cosmos.


     Enjoy Your Evening Sky!
