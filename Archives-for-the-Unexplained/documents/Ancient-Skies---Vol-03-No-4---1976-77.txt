WHO GAVE US OUR IDENTITY-CARD?
By ZDZISLAW LELIGDOWICZ*
      In 1975 a book appeared in Poland by the well-
known traveller, writer and physician, Dr. Waclaw
Korabiewicz, entitled Sladami Amuletu (On Amulets).       FIGURE 1. ANCH        FIGURE 2. PACHOMIUS CROSS
Dr. Korabiewicz gives an account of his long and
arduous studies concerning the cross and its origin.
He presents proof that a cross as the so-called
"anch" (see figure 1) was known and adored in Egypt
in remote ancient times. The word "anch" meant the
"key of life:, "life", "eternal life". The "anch"
sign is prevalent on most E~yptian reliefs and papy-
rus. The stem of the "anch still exists in many
contemporary languages; for examr,le, "ancla" in Span-
ish, "l'ancre" in French, "Anker' in German, and in
English, "anchor", all with the same meaning, anchor.
      Another design of the cross was known in Egypt,
but also in many other regions of the earth, inclu-
ding North America. This r,attern Dr. Korabiewicz
calls the "Pachomius Cross• (see figure 2), because
the sign was introduced in the Fourth Century BC in-
to Christianity by Pachomius, a former soldier of
Constantine the Great, and later the founder of the
first Christian orders in the Middle East. Previous-
ly, the sign of Christianity was a fish.
      Dr. Korabiewicz further shows that all patterns
of the cross in the Middle East regions always had
and still have loops on their arms, like the anch,
and he recalls the "Huzul Cross"(see figure 3), as
a good example of such crosses. Huzuls are people
living in the eastern Carpathian Mountains.
      The Orthodox Church cross is a further modi-
fication of the Huzul Cross, with an additional
slanting arm above the horizontal arm. (see figure
4).
       In the popular Polish science monthly "Problemy"
 (Problems), 1975, Number 7, Professor Waclaw Gajewski
of Warsaw University, in an article entitled "Kod gen-
etyczny a ewolucja" (Genetic Code and Evolution) ac-
cording to the Nobel Prize winner Crick and Orgel,
gives the schematic pattern of tRNA (transcriptional
rybonucleicacid) that guides all protein snytheses
in all living beings.(see figure 5). While the dis-
coverers of tRNA call the design a "clover leaf",
it is easy to see the Huzul Cross, the Orthodox Cross,
and the Anch, which now means more than ever, the "key
of life."
