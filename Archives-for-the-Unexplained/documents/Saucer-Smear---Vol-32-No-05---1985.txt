IN WHICH
YOUR EDITOR MEETS THE SPACE PEOPLE!
      In the course of your editor's recent wanderings in the western USA,
he stopped in Clear Lake City, Texas, to commune with ace non-subscriber
Dr. Thornton Page & charming wife Lou. Although we never saw any clear
lake ( just a bayou full of dangerous animals), we did take a grand tour
of the nearby NASA installation, where Page worked until his recent re­
tirement. This tour was followed by a marvelous dinner party, consisting
of Page, Moseley, super anti�saucerer Jim Oberg (who also works with NASA),
and last but certainly not least, one Karl Henize of NASA - all four of
said persons accompanied by their corresponding wives. Henize is destin­
ed to achieve astronaut status very shortly, when "Spacelab II" goes aloft
circa July 12th.
      Also present at the party was a ninth personage, details about which/
whom we will not yet reveal. Hopefully our next "Smear" will feature pho­
tos of this personage together with some of the party guests. In all, it
was a memorable evening; and our sincere thanks to Thornton Page for mak­
ing it all possible. . . .

      Space does not permit us to do a full critique, but let us       start
with a few words about an article called "Rendlesham Revisited", publish­
ed in the now-defunct British zine, "Probe Report" (April, 1983),        and
written by Probe's editor Ian Mrzyglod together with one Martin Shipp.
The "Probe" team physically revisited the site about two years after the
original incident, and investigated five specific sub jects: The alleged
landing site; A "Two (not Three) Men in Black" incident near the landing
area; Animal disturbances at the time of the sighting; Weird effects that
were supposed to have happened to a car driven near the landing area by
two of the original group of investigators; and TV/electrical interfer­
ence caused by the alleged landing.
       The landing site turned out to be one of several completely similar
clearings in the Forest, caused by the systematic removal of trees by per­
sons authorized to do so.      (No one had bothered to check this out!)    The
"Two Men in Black" turned out to be one of the female UFO investigators
and her boyfriend. The other three sub jects had equally mundane or incon­
clusive explanations. "Probe Report", composed of sensible, conservative
pro-UFO people, concluded that the case did not warrant further investi­
gation!
       And yet, we have "Sky Crash: A Cosmic Conspiracy", written by Bren­
da Butler, Dot Street and Jenny Randles. Truthfully, we have not really
bothered to read all of this book (though it is here on the shelf); but
Dot Street is described in a letter from one of our pro-UFO-but-conserva­
tive British correspondents as "an excitable bird-like person, garrulous,
and with a strong will to believe. " Brenda Butler, on the other hand, is
described by the same correspondent as "a buxom dark-haired country girl,
whose main source of information is the local hostelry, where the local
USAAF Base staff congregate. Having been in the Army myself, it doesn't
take much machination of my nasty suspicious mind to figure out what prob­
ably happened: Doubtless some enterprising soul whose mind was on less
ethereal matters, decided to gain the young lady's interest by fabricating
an interesting tale. I hope his efforts were suitably rewa;rded!" (End of
quote. )

      A lively discussion period followed Korff's talk at the above-men­
tioned skeptics' meeting, consisting largely of a long technical argument
between Sheaffer and ufologist Brad Sparks regarding the famous 1952 Trent
photographs. It seemed to us that Sparks, using detailed material from
Dr. Maccabee, won the technical argument hands down; but we still are not
convinced that the Trent photos are really proven to be genuine.
      Bill Moore, who was also in the audience that night, shared with us
the intriguing "fact" that Phil Klass has threatened to kill him.    When
called on this, he retreated to the position that even if Klass did not
mean the threat seriously, he is nevertheless "sick" . All the Believers
in the audience seemed to agree with this, with the exception of your edi­
tor.
      On the next night (May 24th), Korff hosted a sparsely attended UFO
party near his home, which was most en joyable, and where the food & booze
were most delightfully plentiful.
      However, Korff gets very low marks on the way he planned the conven­
tion, in general. He failed to notify the ufological press about the con­
vention at all, so that the only mention of it beforehand was in "Smear".
He also managed to choose a motel for "delegates" which was utterly ri­
diculous, for complex reasons; and thus absolutely no one at all ended up
staying there!
      Nevertheless, Korff presumably did his best, and saved the NUFOC
from extinction by holding a convention at all. For this we owe him our
gratitude. Next year's NUFOC will be in Phoenix, Arizona, hosted by an
old pro - Hal Star. We have every reason to assume that things will     go
much better there.
      Finally, we should mention perhaps the most important thing about
this year's convention - which was the presentation of our annual "Ufolo­
gist of the Year" award to CUFOS treasurer John Timmerman (in absentia) -
a man who lives in the shadow of Dr. Hynek and thus has never, in     our
opinion, received the recognition he deserves. For several years he has
unselfishly given time and money to the CUFOS cause, and has shown himself
to be a sincere, sensible ufologist. The UFO movement would surely gain
respect and generally prosper if there were more good men like John Timmer­
man around.
      Unfortunately, we must end this convention report on a sour note: For
technical reasons, Korff did not have the award plaque ready in time for
the convention, and for further technical reasons, it still has not arriv­
ed at our Headquarters as of this writing - so that we can send it on to
John.
      Come on, Kal! Give us all a break!
