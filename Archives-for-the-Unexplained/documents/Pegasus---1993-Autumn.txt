                                  THE PITCH HILL LINE
                                     by Jimmy Goddard


On October 7th 1990, Eileen Roche and Gordon Millington were on Pitch Hill in Surrey,
with the idea of organising a visit there on November 5th, the anniversary of a spectacular
UFO sighting there. This never took place, but they were nevertheless to find something
there that was to occupy them and a number of others for the following year, and the
research is still continuing. They tried dowsing for any ley or earth energy lines that might
be there, and they found a very wide, powerful band of energy that has not been equalled
in any other so far found.


At the following Surrey Earth Mysteries Group meeting I decided to try to draw the line
on the map, and noticed that it went through a number of interesting places- South
Holmwood church, Holmbury Hill hillfort, and skirting Anstiebury Camp - as well as
running along some stretches of straight track, and a number of straight edges of woods.


We had a field trip roughly once a month from then onwards, and investigated various
sections of the line. We tried to take note of everything we could, whether it seemed
relevant at the time or not, in case it might prove to be of value later. We picked up a
number of other lines while on these trips, but they were mostly very narrow, usually
about six paces wide. Occasionally we would find wider ones, but none to compare with
the Pitch Hill line, which was consistently about 70 to 100 paces wide.


The first trip we went on was to visit the stretch of coincident track near Shellwood
Manor. We found the edge of the line was near the edge of the path on one side, but
extended far beyond it on the other. It was so powerful that it has always been easy to
distinguish from other lines. Also on this trip we visited South Holmwood church, a very
powerful site entirely taken in by the line. Later I was to do another interesting
experiment there.    The church was built in 1838 and has an interesting "subconscious
siting" story- two local ladies were watching some boys playing on the spot and felt very
strongly that there ought to be a church there. This mysterious phenomenon of
subconscious siting of places of worship has been noticed many times before and was even
more apparent in the modern evangelical church, the Tollgate Chapel, which we found
later on the line. It is not limited to Christian churches either, as Woking Mosque, built in
the nineteenth century, is the centre of at least six leys, and Chris Street's Earthstars axis
ley passes through the Central Synagogue, Hallam Street, Westminster Cathedral, and a
number of churches of other denominations.


Mention of this ley brings to mind a very striking experience on one of the Pitch Hill ley
trips. We had followed the line to the point where it meets the Chris Street axis line, and
we noticed a flock of birds, later found to be Canada geese, flying towards us apparently
along the Street line. As they passed overhead, they changed direction and flew off along
the Pitch Hill line! Possibly coincidence, but it made a great impact on us at the time.
Although I have been interested in earth mysteries since the sixties, I was new to dowsing,
but of course having been bitten by the bug continued to do so on holid�ys and at other
times when possible. I found that the frequency of occurrence of leys seems to be in
inverse proportion to their width. The commonest are about six paces wide; ten-pace
ones are slightly less commo� and twenty to twenty-five pacers quite rare, though I did
find a few. Nothing so far has equalled the Pitch Hill line, however. It is something very
special, but even now we don't know why.


It goes off the east coast, appropriately, near Leysdown on the Isle of Sheppey; in the
other direction it goes through the Devil's Punchbowl at Hindhead and further west
through the "rude giant" hill figure at Ceme Abbas in Dorset. We picked it up, strong and
wide, in both places on different trips.


When on holiday in Derbyshire in 199 1, I made another discovery which I was to find
consistent everywhere. I dowsed the width of a ley that passed through the cottage where
we were staying, and found to my surprise that it doubled in width for about an hour
around sunrise and sunset. I tried this elsewhere and found that whatever the original
width of a ley, it always seems to double at these times. I could hardly believe the Pitch
Hill line could do so, but when I went to try it at South Holmwood Church, I was amazed
to find it increased to a staggering 2 12 paces wide!


Later I was to find another surprising thing about the line, though I realise this must
remain speculative because of the vast distances involved. When trying to plot the line as
a great circle on a globe, I found it seemed to go through the immediate areas of three
very significant places. One is Mount Everest, the highest mountain in the world, and
known to the native people as "Goddess Mother of the World". Another is Ayers Rock,
sacred to the Australian aborigines and which makes a great impression on many white
Australians and other visitors. The third is the area of the mysterious Lines ofNazca in
Peru.


There is still much to find out about this amazing band of energy, and field trips will be
continuing.
