                            Sea Shadow stealth ship seen on TLC
     From: BaDge <baj7d@avery.med.virginia.edu>
     Date: Fri, 1 Aug 1997 19:47:06 -0800


  Some of you who know me from the SkunkWorks list know I'm fond of the
  navy's Sea Shadow.
  Since it's stealthy, and A51 is intimately tied to stealth, I thought I'd
  share this.
  TLC, 'How'd they do That', just played the most in-depth review of this
  boat I've seen yet. (Sat 7/26 1930ish)
  They also showed it in drydock, and we finally see the undepinnings in
  great detail. Basically twin mini-submarines powered by twin engines in
  the bowels of the hull, are driven by bubble-formation resistant props,
  with the only other underwater feature being twin airplane-style rudders
  near the end of each pontoon.
  This ship was used mainly as a test bed for technologies to reduce heat,
  wake, sound, radar, and visible signatures.
  I see no reason why it couldn't almost completely submerge, nor why it
  couldn't make 40-50 kts as a hydrofoil.
  The black skin is exactly that. You can see a skeleton of ribs over which
  the black coating/covering is stretched in one shot.
  Catch it if you can! ;-)
  regards,
