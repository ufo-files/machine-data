Houston is situated at the juncture of the Morice and Bulkley Rivers, with a
population of approximately 2600.

Originally known as Pleasant Valley, Houston was established in the early 1900 's
as the tie-cutting center for the Grand Trunk Pacific Railway. Its first hotel, a
large tent, was pitched in 1913. The 1940's brought the forest industry to Hou-
ston, which has flourished ever since. The town was named the 1999 Forestry
capital of BC, and today you can see one of the world's largest sawmills under
one roof on a free sawmill tour. Two famous fly-fishing rivers, the Bulkley and
Morice, meet at Houston. Their legendary steelhead runs provide much of the
recreation, along with fishing for spring and coho salmon. As a tribute to the
sport, the community built the world's largest fly rod - 18 metres long (60 feet)
and weighing 364 kilograms (800 pounds), which can be seen at Steelhead Park.

In winter, cross-country skiing is popular, and the Morice Mountain Nordic Ski
                                                                                                        I
Club maintains 25 kilometres of cross-country trails. These trails double as
mountain biking, hiking and nature walking trails in the summer. A hike up
China Nose Mountain rewards you with panoramic views and opportunities to
spot many different plant and wildlife species, including lady slippers, fems,
mountain goats, black bears, bald eagles and more.

Other outdoor recreational activities include golfing on two nine-hole courses,
snowmobiling, rock climbing at Owen Hat, camping, hunting, and snowshoeing.

The best recreational activities, accommodations and Houston hospitality are
ready for you. Contact the Houston and District Chamber of Commerce at (250)
845-7640.
