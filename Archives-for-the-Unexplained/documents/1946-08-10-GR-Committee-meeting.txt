August 10: Committe meeting

”Conference at the Air Administration on August 10, 1946 concerning projectiles over Sweden. 

”Attending:
”From FF:Colonel Jacobsson, chairman
Division head Norlin
Air Director Norén
Air Engineer Malmberg
”From Fst:Colonel Bonde
Major Ahlgren
Captain Olin
”From FOA:Director General Björkesson
Professor Ljunggren
Division head Fehrm
Licenciate Eklund
Engineer Rynninger
”From FRA:Division head Kempe
Doctor Moberg

”1. Fst [the Defence Staff] gave a short summary of incidents since August 5. An average of 10 report per day have come in. Some of these could be attributed to airplanes, but the great part concerns unexplained phenomena.

”Two types of observations can be distinguished:
”a) reports speaking of fireballs with great luminousity and no sound, 
”b) reports speaking of spool-shaped objects, leaving behind smoke but insignificant lights; no sound. 

”Reports are distributed over the whole country but have their greatest concentration on a line Stockholm - Örebro - Karlstad and most common over Stockholm.

”Examples of reports: 

”On August 5 a spool-shaped object has been seen near Landskrona, size and appearance like a cigar on 1 meters distance. No rudder and no wings. It first went on an easterly course, then turned to north-northwest. [case xxx] 

”Same day, between 14.00 and 15.00 one has, at another place, seen a dark object pass over the road 15-20 meters and at about 3 m altitude, in front of a car. It was estimated to be about 1 meter long and 25-30 cm in diameter. It moved with a roaring sound and disappeared over a field. No crash observed. [case xxx]

”On measuring service [Sw. mättjänst] at KA 1 [Coast Artillery regiment] on Mälsten, August 5, at 18.35, a conscript Haglund saw through the measuring instrument a spool-shaped object [case xxx]. The instrument was a 4 m, stereo, 20x magnification. On being questioned at the Air Administration on August 9 the following was stated by H:

”The object came into the instrument at bearing 0445 and was lost in bearing 0570 (bearing 0 = south). It had, in relation to the observer a somewhat going course] (approximately westerly) [? meaning of this is unclear). Height elevation was estimated at 3 scores [Sw. streck] and distance at 20.000 m. Since the place of the sighting is on 40 m height, flight altitude should have been about 100 meters. The object was followed for 13 seconds, and this would then correspond to a speed of about 400 m/sec. H. describes the object as spool-shaped, somewhat thicker in the front and tapering backwards. No wings. The diameter was greater in relation to the length than with a regular ship torpedo. At regular intervals it let out black smoke rings from the back. No sound was heard. The other personnel at the measuring station saw nothing. The object seemed to be somewhat longer than a light-house which is 10 m high and situated about 8.000 m from Mälsten. If it went at a distance of 20.000 m, it would thus be 25 m long. 

”To check on these calculations a J9 [Swedish designation for a US Seversky EP-1 fighter] shall be flown on some different trajectories and observed through the instrument by conscript Haglund. 

”Colonel Jacobsson reported that in Finland smoke rings had been observed without any object in sight. FOA 1 investigates if propulsion according to the V2 principle can create smoke rings. 

”Professor Ljunggren pointed out that projectile could get the required carrying capacity [Sw. bärförmåga] through rotation. 

”2. Major Ahlgren reported that Colonel Dahlgren ends the search at Kölmjärv on August 10. The ore detector and the personnel is moved to the lake near Gällivare and work will start there on August 12. Emptying of Kölmjärv is not recommended. No appliances, except for those [already] up there, are required. The two mine detectors FOA has received should remain with FOA to be used at other places, if necessary. 

”4. The ER [radar] reconnaissance has, during the previous week, not given any result. The service time 22-24 is to be changed to 21-24 through the agency of Air Director Norén. Other [service] times remain the same.

”5. Dr. Öhman, Saltsjöbaden, has studied the received reports and points out that the great abundance of reports on July 9, 10 and 11 could possibly be a large meteor which has exploded over Sweden and split over the country as smaller meteors. The reports on the 10th and 11th should then be attributed to wrong datings. This later [theory] shall be investigated by Fst [the Defence Staff].

”6. A lot of very light slag has been sent in from Örebro. This shall be investigated by FOA. 

”7. KMF [Royal Naval Administration] has offered Fst [Defence Staff] to set up an ER [radar] station of 500 kW with 100 km range against airplanes. It would, for instance, be set up on Mälsten. Of the costs some 3-4.000 kr should be covered by Fst.

”Decision: The station should be set up at Mälsten. This is acted upon by Fst, who also will investigate if not KMF could cover all the costs. 

”8. FV [Air Force] mobile ER-units are ready by August 12. One of them is immediately set up on Torön [southern part of Stockholm archipelago] or in the vicinity [of that place] through the agency of Air Director Norén. Service hours will be 14.00-16.00 and 21.00-24.00. 

”9. Professor Ljunggren has submitted a preliminary report of the investigations of 15 finds. Of these, 11 can safely be said to have no origin from any projectile. Of the other 4 possibly 3 can have another explanation than a projectile, while it is very difficult to explain where the fourth find, the yellow, burnable substance [case xxx], comes from. Investigations of this will continue. 

”In view of the sudden disappearance of the observed objects, Colonel Jacobsson asked if one can expect such an advanced auto destruction [mechanism]. Professor Ljunggren though this was possible. On one hand one might think that the objects are heated so much that they disintegrate, on the other hand one might think of a so much advanced mechanical decomposition that the particles become invisible.

”10. FRA intercepted on August 5, at 13.30, from airplane at 2.500 m altitude, near Ölands norra udde [the northern tip of the island of Öland] the same signal as before. By measuring the maximum power the direction of the sender was estimated to be on the line east - west. 

”The ground [signal] interception should be postponed and resumed when the personnel has been trained. 

”Airplane at F11 with registration apparatur [recorder] is ready to be put in service within 3 days.

”11. The question of photo reconnaissance towards Peenemünde is with the Cabinet [the secretary of defence].

”12. The investigation of similar phenomena in Denmark has not given any positive results. 

”13. Next meeting to be held at FF [Air Administration] on August 20, 14.00, without further notice. 

”Stockholm, August 10, 1946, /signed/ Eric Malmberg”
