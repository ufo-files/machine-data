       HIS "VISIT" HERE WAS UNFORGET TABLE:

       Last April 23, 1965, GEORGE ADAMSKI , 74,
 (born April 17, 1891), one of the foremost pioneers in
Flying Saucer research, left this earthly life. Mr.
Adamski was well known to most students and investi­
gators of the Flying Saucer subject. He had made a
name for himself around the world as a lecturer and
teacher about space craft, people from other planets,
and Cosmic Philosophy.
       For several years prior to his historic meeting
with a space ship in the California desert , Mr. Adam­
ski had observed and photographed space ships through
his telescope --- from his home at Mt. Palomar, in
California.
       He first made news, howeve�, after his exper­
ience on Nov. 20, 1952, near Desert Center, Calif.
At that time he met a Venusian Scout Ship and its pilot.
From that time on, his life was completely changed.
With subsequent contacts and with increasing new
knowledge, he felt that his responsibility was to reach
the public with as much information as he could give
them about the Space People and their mission to Earth.
       He became world-famous as the author of several
of the most-read books in the Flying Saucer field , plus
many articles.
       It was while Mr. Adamski was lecturing in the
east that he contracted pneumonia. He died in a
Washington, D. C. hospital. In accordance with his
wishes , there was no funeral. As a veteran of World
War I, his ashes were interred at Arlington National
Cemetary, in Virginia.
       Although he was a man of simple and humble
origin, George Adamski became great in quiet, digni­
fied service to his fellow man. He taught what he
loved and knew best, giving generously of his time and
energies to all who would listen. For he had learned
