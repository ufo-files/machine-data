                         Possible missing link in Art Bell mystery
     From: campbell@ufomind.com (Glenn Campbell, Las Vegas)
     Date: Wed, 21 Oct 1998 15:38:37 -0800


   Here is a possible missing link in the Art Bell "Why Did He Resign"
   mystery...
   In his "sign off" on Oct. 13, Bell said:
      "You may recall about a year ago... I told you that there was an event,
      a threatening terrible event occurred to my family, which I could not
      tell you about. Because of that event, and a succession of other
      events, what you're listening to right now is my final broadcast on
      the air."
   A search for interesting news events in Pahrump about a year ago reveals
   this article...
   http://www.lasvegassun.com/sunbin/stories/archives/1997/jun/06/505971364.html
   As the URL implies, this is a news article from the Las Vegas Sun on
   June 6, 1997. It begins...
           Pahrump substitute teacher arraigned on sex charges
           PAHRUMP, Nev. (AP) - A 32-year-old Pahrump substitute teacher
           accused of befriending boys by giving them alcohol and marijuana
           and then soliciting sex acts from them was arraigned Thursday on
           11 counts.
   The substitute teacher is accused of having homosexual relations with
   several Pahrump teenagers, who are not named in the article. The
   teacher, who was convicted in May 1998, was HIV positive. See also:
   http://www.lasvegassun.com/sunbin/stories/archives/1998/may/07/507185157.html
   Could this be connected to the "threatening terrible event" that
   happened to Bell's family?
                                                    * * *
   Monitoring the Art Bell controversy, I am reminded of two fictional
   parallels.
   One is the 1960s TV series "The Prisoner". Patrick McGoohan is a
   former intelligence operative held captive on a strange island and
   asked over and over "Why did you resign?" The show is allegorical

   and seems to be discussing freedom and free will. It resonates in
   interesting ways when compared to Art Bell's circumstances.
   Art Bell is held prisoner on the Island of Pahrump, or more precisely,
   in his doublewide mobile home there. He is beseiged by the media who
   want to know "Why did you resign?" All he has to do to get off the
   island and regain his privacy is tell his captors what they want to
   know, but he refuses? What is his motivation? Is it concern for his
   family? Personal dignity? Freedom? Is he defiantly declaring "I am
   not a number; I am a human being!" Or is he refusing to answering the
   question because keeping the secret gives him some kind of power over
   his captors? At least it makes them pay attention to him.
   No one would be asking the question now if Bell had not set us up for
   it. He had to know that by discussing a "threatening terrible event"
   and not specifying it he was enticing his conspiracy minded audience
   into a frenzy of speculation. (The Heaven's Gate suicides ought to
   have taught Bell something about the dangers of speculation, but he
   disclaims all responsibility for that terrible event.) In effect, he
   told his listeners, "I've got a secret, but I'm not going to tell." If
   Bell really wanted to retire from the air, he could have done it more
   quietly and handled his family problems in such a way as to not
   attract attention to them.
   One wonders if the prisoner is on the island because that is where he
   chooses to be.
   The other parallel is with Paddy Chayefsky's 1976 movie "Network". In
   it, network news anchor Howard Beal, faced with lagging ratings,
   announces on the air -- in a darkly serious voice similar to Bell's --
   that he will shoot himself during the next live newscast. Ratings
   instantly go up, and Beal is given his own show as the "Mad Prophet of
   the Airwaves."
   Glenn
