Searching for ghost bombs in Storsjön [lake]

Each and every day we receive reports of alleged sightings of "ghost bombs". These observations have indeed become a great mystery. Recently, sightings of these phenomena have been recorded in Jämtland [“county”] and a projectile is believed to have crashed in the area surrounding Storsjön a few weeks ago. During the last ten days an ongoing search has been underway. Even though it has proved possible to localize the area where the impact was made, no searches have yielded any positive results. On the contrary, the mystique thickens.
The air defence department has set up a special department for the compilation of reports on ghost projectiles. Still, captain Lars Falk of the air defence division in thw town of Östersund has received a great deal of extra work with the many space projectiles that have been reported. A couple of weeks ago, a small number of people observed a ghost bomb in an area near Storsjön and it was described as passing just above their heads with a whizzing sound, after which it disappeared down into the lake. The defense staff immediately received a report on the incident and Captain Falk was tasked with making some research. The intense searches for the projectile are still in full swing as of tuesday.
- The work is based on observations of no less than five eyewitnesses, whose veracity is reliable to say the least, particularly as the phenomenon appeared in broad daylight, explains Captain Falk. We have calculated the most probable area, within which the projectile almost certainly must have crashed. Furthermore, we're quite sure of the trajectory of the projectile, so we have a fairly limited area to search within.
A visit to the places where the eyewitnesses claimed to have been standing at the time in question confirms Captain Falk's claim regarding the limited site of impact. Two boats have been used in dragging a 45 meter long chain along the lake bottom. The sea bottom has been thoroughly searched and minesweeped. Though, the minesweeping proved useless since the mine detector wouldn’t work under water, explains corporal Svahn.
- Instead we systematically searched the area in question and took some items with us. So far, most of the results has only been a few sunken logs. We have also been able to look down to the bottom - the water is only a few feet deep at its most - but nothing suspicious has been found. Even so, it is clear that the projectile that came whizzing over the neighborhood wasn't fictional, continues corporal Svahn. I've encouraged all eyewitnesses to draw the space projectile much like they remember seeing it. They all seems to be pretty unanimous that it was a cigar-resembling object with a few small wings on its sides. Though, where did it go?
Yes, that question might prove quite difficult to answer. For our own part we would be willing to accept Captain Falk's theory, that the projectiles are made of some undefined mass, which shatters completely when the fuel runs out. This is also a fully acceptable explanation to why we've never found any remains of these projectiles, despite searching carefully.
Yesterday afternoon, all research surrounding the crash site at Storsjön were cancelled until further notice.
Captain Falk remarks: "We eagerly await the day when we can get absolutely secure reports on the crash. Consequently, it is of great importance that the public notify us as soon as they get hold of any relevant information. Such messages should be sent to the second military command headquarters in Östersund, the air defence division. News of ghost bombs may always reach us sooner or later - if only by the daily press - but it is obviously important that we receive reports of these observations as soon as possible. Explanations to these mysterious phenomena are indeed of interest to the whole country. Despite all theories, we are still quite uncertain about the origin of these mysterious projectiles.”

Source:  Östersunds-Posten,  1946-08-28
