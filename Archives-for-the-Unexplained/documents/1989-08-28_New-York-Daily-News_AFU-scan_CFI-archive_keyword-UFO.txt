WASHINGTON- The first "stealth" test of Northrop's flying-wing B-2 jet
bomber this summer was a remarkable success. Trouble was, four decades
ago somebody else did it and nobody much cared.
  Shortly after World War II, Northrop test pilot Max Skelton flew the YB-
49, the forerunner to the B-2, on a grand tour of air-defense stations in the
western U.S.
  "Nobody found him - untii he was directly overhead/' says Tim Wool­
dridge of the National Air and Space Museum. "The sad part is that they
didn't continue the testing."
  During World War II, Nazi designers Waiter and Reimar Horten also
built a jet-powered flying wing, the HoiX. It actually flew in early 1945, but
later crashed when an engine failed. A copy is stored at the National Air
and Space Museum's warehouse at Silver Hill, Md.
