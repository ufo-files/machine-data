UK UFO Periodicals Directory		
extracted from 		
UFO Periodicals International Directory		
		
Release 2.1		by Edoardo Russo, CISU (Italy)   - February 2008
		
		This is the from the updated (enlarged, corrected) second edition of the directory, issued more than two years after the first one. New data fields (not already in the first edition) are shown in red color, Several fellow researchers sent their amendments and additions, and they should be heartfully thanked: Ole-Jonny Braenne (Norway), Ignacio Cabria (Spain), Gilles Durand (France), Roberto Labanti (Italy), Ferhat Talayhan (Germany), Diego Zuniga (Chile). It goes without saying that Anders Liljegren gave a strong hand with his own update of AFU (Sweden) collection index, Jean-Luc Rivera's inventory of his own periodical archives, and the SCEAU (France) inventory of Sos-Ovni archives were invaluable, too.
		
		
1) General criteria		
		
	1.0	All descriptions (names) of fields are in English. 
	2.0	A double criteria for inclusion was adopted: it has to be an at-least-partially UFO publication; and it has to be a serial publication (periodical or aperiodical). Single special issues (even if entirely devoted to UFOs) are not included, as well as monographic publications even if showing a serial numer, since these are better classified as books.
	3.0	Apparently non-UFO periodicals with occasional articles on UFOs were not listed (should be in the newsclipping part of our files); on the contrary, non-UFO periodicals with regular UFO features were included. I had troubles at understanding which was the case for some periodicals in languages unknown to me.
		
2) Brief descriptions of fields		
		
Inventories		Seven different fields were used simply showing a "1" if a given title is present in the inventory of the six collections we've got, i.e.: 
Afu		Archives for UFO Research, Sweden (Access database, updated February 2004)
Bufora		British UFO Research Association (Library Catalogue, by Adam Bailey, dated June 2001)
Cisu		Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici, Italy (direct inventory by Edoardo Russo, April 2005; updated, October 2007)
Greenwood		Barry Greenwood's Inventory of UFO Publications in his files (SHG website, updated 2001)
Rivera		Jean-Luc Rivera's own collection of UFO periodicals worldwide (October 2007)
SosOvni		Sos Ovni director Perry Petrakis' publication files, as catalogued by SCEAU (April 2006); now incorporated into CISU archives
OSU		Inventory of items donated by Bill Jones to Ohio State University Library (SHG website, undated)
Area		A numeric code for a fast, empirical division by language families (actually: different shelves in CISU files). See "Legenda A" below.
Nation		The existing nation where the periodical is/was published, according to the international standard two-letter codes (ISO 3166-1 alpha 2).
Language		The main language the periodical is/was written in, according to the international standard two-letter codes (ISO 639-1).
Title		The name of the periodical.  It is written in its own language, except if it's not in a Latin alphabet (eg. Cyrillic, Japanese, Greek): in such case, the name is translitterated to Latin characters (whenever possible). If the first word is an article (eg. "the" or equivalent), this is postponed after the rest of the title and a comma. Title is the only univocal key for the whole file (while all other fields are accessory ones): if (and only if) the name changes, a new/different entry is created.
Acronym		The acronym usually known for the periodical, if existing.
Place		This is a secondary information, often useful for distinguishing homonimous periodicals. It is used only for larger or "Ufo-periodicals-richest" countries. Different criteria were used for each country, according to known local habits (see "Legenda B" below).
PublisherAcronym		Since most UFO periodicals are/were published by a UFO organization, and UFO organizations usually love long names and thence short acronyms.
PublisherName		This may be the full name of the UFO organization or, for commercial periodicals, the publisher's.
ManBehind 		It's not always the editor, but most often so. It's not shown for those periodicals who had a long list, but is often useful for recognition purposes as of homonimous titles.
Relevance		Since our archives also  collect periodicals which are only partially ufological, a code is proposed for showing the amount of UFO relevance of each periodical, if known.  (see "Legenda C" below).
Diffusion		Since UFO periodicals range from the full-color, glossy, commercial magazines selling 50,000 copies worldwide to the small, mimeographed bulletin circulating 15 copies within a local group, a code is proposed for showing which is the case (originally used by Ignacio Cabria in his inventory of Spanish UFO periodicals). See "Legenda D" below.
FirstKnownYear		This is not the year of publication of the first owned copy, but the year we know (or we deduce) the first issue with that title was published.
LastKnownYear		The latest year as of known or owned  issues.
LastPublicationYear		The final year of publication as far as we can ascertain.
Published Issues		The total number of published issues.
Becomes		Whenever a periodical changes its name, its next name is shown here for retrieval purposes. The opposite (i.e. the previous name) is not shown, for sake of redundancy.
Updated		Date when entry was added or modified, if after the previous release (1.0).
Owned Issues		The total number of issues owned by CISU.
CISU Owns		Inventory of issues owned at CISU headquarters (items from Sos-Ovni archives are shown in red): if single issues are owned, the issue numbers are separated by commas (eg. "2, 4" means we own Nos. 2 and 4, not 3); if a continuous series is owned , the first and the last one are separated by "-" (eg. 3-11 meaning from 3 through 11); if  numerated by volume/number, they may be shown as either "3:2-3:5" or "3(2-5)".
		
3) Code tables 		
		
Legenda A: 		Area
	1.0	USA
	2.0	Great Britain and the United Kingdom
	3.0	Other countries using English as main language (Australia, Canada, South Africa)
	4.0	France and French-speaking countries
	5.0	Spain, Portugal and South America
	6.0	Other languaged (but Italian): German, Scandinavian/Slavian, not European languages
	7.0	Italian (bear with me...)
		Note A) - for those countries which have got more than one main language (eg. Switzerland has 4, Canada has 2, Belgium has 2, etc.) periodicals are put in the area of the corresponding language.
		Note B) - international editions in English/French of some UFO periodical from other language areas (eg. Argentina, Brazil, Italy, Japan, etc.) have been left in the Area of the main language.
		
Legenda B:		Place
USA		two-letters code for the state
UK		City or county
Canada		Three-letters code for the province
Australia		Three-letters code for the state
France		Two-digits code for the department
Spain		City or province
Italy		Two-letters code for the province
		
Legenda C:		Relevance
0.0		Contactee-oriented 
1.0		Exclusively or mainly UFOlogical 
2.0		UFOs are one of the main subjects
3.0		Main subject is not UFOs but UFOs are a regular (though secondary) feature
4.0		Non-ufological periodicals with occasional UFO articles
		
Legenda D: 		Diffusion
1.0		Internal or small circulation bulletin/newsletter of an association or by an individual
2.0		Magazine sold (also) by subscription or local distribution
3.0		Wide-circulation magazines
4.0		Internet-distributed publications (e-zines, web)
