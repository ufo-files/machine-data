   • Tasmania:         The sensation was COII1Plete - "Body of a serpent with the head of a
   horse" screamed the headline in several German newspapers                  •   .   However, the head
   was that of a sea-horse, so it was a pretty small sea-serpent. It was one of 20
   new species discovered by zoologists in the shallow sea off Tasmania. As oceano­
   logist Peter Last of Tasmania's state scientific organisation CSIRO explained
   to reporters, zoologists had discovered a new species of skate in the Bathurst
   Channel in 1988. Now Hobart zoologists further investigated its habitat, the
   shallow sea          (only 30 ft deep) off the southwest coast of the island. The water
   there is poor in nutrients and has nealy no visibility due to mud washed into
   the sea by rivers from the Southwest National Park. For the last two years, it
   was found this strech of sea harboured up to 20 unknown species, many of which
   could have existed in symbiosis for several thousand years. As biologist Mike
   Driessen pointed out, the majority of the animals had not yet be scientifically
   described. They include a colourful marine slug, species of coral and spongue,
   the s�ate and "other animals that seem like straight out of a science fiction
       movie." None of the article refers further to the "half snake half horse" ani­
   Mal, but           they point out the skate was the only species of vertebrate found,

biology, which is also possible). Biologists pointed out such animals were
normally found in depth of 100 to 300 m, not 10 m, but the dark muddy water had
folled the creatures.          (Frankfurter Rundschau, 7 June, p.34;          Ludwigsburger Kreis­

 • Yeti in China:        I have two conflicting news items on the most recent Chinese yeti
 expedition. First, on May 20, p.2, the taz reported that an expedition for the yeti
 had returned after weeks of search without a trace of the creature.On June 10,. p.7,
 the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung said a Chinese expedition had returned with
 "indirect evidence" for the yeti after an intense search of the mountains of Hubei.
 The scientist Wang Fanchen told Xinhua on June 9 he had filed 114 sightings of
 the yeti spanning the last 70 years.
