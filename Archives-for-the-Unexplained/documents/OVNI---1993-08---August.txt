Members of the PR A attended the CCCS Conference on Saturday the
7th of August, the day when Omar Fowler, Roy Dutton, Chad
Deetken and a number of other well known speakers addressed the
Conference.

Omar Fowler introduced the 1 atest PR A paper on the possible
cause of the single type of Crop Circle, which we consider to
be a natural phenomenon.

The talk, illustrated with slides, covered the period from 1985
to the present time. It showed the PRA's work in the field of
crop and soil analysis, aerial photographic work ( in both
colour and infra-red ) and ended with   slides illustrating the
theory that circles are      the result   of charged particle
interaction in the ionosphere ( see sketch 5 ) .     The 1 atter
theory is due mainly to the work carried out by Paul Foster and
Jeff Whitear, who have spent many hours in both theoretical and
field study of the Crop Circle phenomenon.

The earlier work of identifying 55cm single standing stems in
Crop Circles, often a hundred or more miles apart, was carried
out by Omar Fowler.         This also resulted in the identifying of
11Crease 11 and 11 burn 11 marks, which again has been shown to be a
world-wide phenomenon in Crop Circles.

During the Conference, members of the PR A met Simon Wescott, a
Producer,   ( Granite Films & TV Productions Ltd. ) with his
assistant A 1 ice Keens-Soper ( see photo ) . They had recently
returned from the USA, where they met Dr. W.C. Levengood a well
known biophysicist, who has also been studying the Crop Circle
phenomenon.     It was quite a pleasant surprise to hear from
Alice Keens-Soper, that Or Levengood 11 thought very highly 11 of
the PRA's work!

A number of the the PR A's papers were printed for the occasion
and these attracted a great deal of interest.           Copies were
passed to Or Roger Taylor and the Chairman of CCCS, Michael
Green.    Several American visitors also bought copies and Chad
Deetken ( Canada ) of the North American Institute for Crop
Circ 1 e Rese arch, has pr·omise d to exchange information on his

                        CRO P CIRCLE NEWS

The Centre For Crop Circle Studies ( East Midlands ) newsletter
dated the 29th of July has a cover photo of a prominent
pictogram at Charley Knoll Farm near Loughborough.

The cruciform set which measured some 300ft long by 200ft wide
and also has two "grapeshot" circles, appeared on the 7th of
July this year.

The   PRA   first heard of the pictogram from Mike Newark
( Coventry ) on the 27th of July, far too late to carry out any
effective evaluation of the incident.

We cannot account for the fact that as a prominent Crop Circle
researching group in our own right, or as individual members of
CCCS ( East Midlands ) , we were not informed of the incident as
soon as it came to their notice.

We deplore a "closed shop" attitude to the Crop Circle subject,
which appears to have been taken in this instance.

Our first paper on microwave activity published on the 11th
September 1991, commences with the paragraph   "The information
given in this paper is for the benefit of Crop Circle
researchers and is published as a guide to an area of research
that is worthy of further investigation and study".

The Crop Circle pictogram drawings shown on adjoining pages are
an example of our own "open" attitude to the subject.        Both
drawings, ( which have been sent to us by Jeff Whitear ) arrived
in this mornings post ( 17th August ) . The Surrey set consist of
a circle with a "smile", which appeared on the 27th of July,
only approx. 20m from where a pictogram appeared 1 ast year.
The remaining circles appeared early in August. The exact date
is not known at present, the Cheesefoot Head pictogram drawing
was also undated.

A Crop Circle was spotted by Omar Fowler on the morning of the
6th August.   It was visible in a field near Stanton-by-Dale,
near Sandiacre.

After driving to the area and locating the circle in a field of
Barley, a quick assessment was decided on before calling in
other group members with the necessary equipment.

On approaching the circle,    the crop showed indentations,
typical of someone walking through to the circle area. At a
distance of two metres, it was obvious that the circle was a
hoax.  The floor of the circle was a "mess" with none of the
known characteristics of a genuine event ( photographs were
taken for the record ) .

                   SOMETHIN G TO THINK ABOUT
                        by Pat Delgado

I have thought about the UFO situation for a long time now and
the fact that if we were to collect a good detailed report or a
sensational video recording of UFO sightings daily, would we be
any closer to understanding UFO's?

Forty five years has not produced any "real" information, a
mountain of reports yes and with it another mountain of
speculation.    I know we must keep collecting reports and
details, but I am sure the initiative to obtain the key
breakthrough does not lie with us, I think we would be arrogant
to think it does.

Like other mysteries, they will be dispelled by "others" when
it is intended to be so. Meanwhile we have no option but to be
content with our present level of intellect.



                      AUGUST UFO REPORTS

August 2nd:  A witness reported seeing six red lights flying
independently and at a steady speed SW to NE over Derby at
10pm.

Alan Hitchcock is attempting to trace other witnesses to this
event, but so far, none have been found.

August 5th:  A Mrs Fox of Allestree, Derby, saw an      unusual
glowing "rugby ball" while driving home at 11.30pm.

Mrs Fox was driving on the Kedleston Road in the direction of
Allestree, when she noticed a glowing ball of light hovering in
the sky in the direction of the Derby Rugby Club. The witness
stopped her car and watched the light for approx. five minutes.
She described it as being in the shape of a rugby ball, glowing
with a brilliant light, hazy outline and it appeared to
"wobble".

The light from the object appeared to be spikey, as though it
was emitting rays of light, but this latter observation may
have been due to the fact that Mrs Fox was watching the light
through her car windscreen.   Mrs Fox also stated that she did
not hear any noise associated with the glowing light.

Although Mrs Fox normally carried a camera in her car, on this
occasion she did not have it with her. The night was cool and
clear ( stars were visible ) . She estimated that the light was
only approx. 100 metres away.

After a few minutes Mrs Fox became frightened and drove away
from the area towards her home.
Editors Comments on the Mrs Fox sighting:-

The witness watched the object for several minutes and this
enabled her to report a number of important features, often an
indication of UFO activity:-

     produced the 11 hazy 11 outline
( c) The recognition that this was 11 something unusual 11 ,
