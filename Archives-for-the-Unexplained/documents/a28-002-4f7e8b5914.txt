  From Glenn:
  On the feedback form, LaViolette flagged his message, "Do Not
  Reprint," a request that I usually respect. I choose to reprint the
  message here anyway because (A) He is asserting copyright on a
  document, and I think if you are going to do that you must do it
  openly, and (B) He is requesting "compensation," which really rubs me
  the wrong way.
  For more on our email policies see http://www.ufomind.com/org/email.shtml
  I generally _will_ remove documents from this server when an author
  claims copyright infringement, but I am not going to compensate anyone
  if the mistake was innocent. In this case, there was no copyright
  statement on the document, and it had already been distributed on
  another mailing list. Only a portion of the original article was
  included in our posting.
  I am certainly not going to provide compensation by including any book
  in my catalog. The only criteria for including a book in the catalog
  is that I personally think the book is worth more the the purchase
  price and is genuinely useful. Our motto: "We won't sell rubbish"
  (which unfortunately eliminates 98% of all UFO and paranormal books).
  This philosophy is different from that of rest of the website, where
  rubbish is generally welcomed, if not cherished, so long as it is
  linked to the appropriate index page.
  As for the B-2 thesis, let's say I am unconvinced. There could be
  other reasons for electrically charging the leading edges of the B-2.
  Perhaps in some way it helps reduce the radar profile of the plane --
  which is the whole point of stealth. Perhaps it helps reduce
  turbulance or encourage laminar flow. Maybe, just maybe, the B-2 does
  indeed make use of antigravity technology, but to me books on the
  subject and most other anti-gravity claims haven't yet crossed the
  line into "worth the money."
  LaViolette has sent me another message, an excerpt of the original
  article, which he says I should use to replace the article posted to
  the website. I do not feel that I owe him this courtesy, however. I
  am generally non-judgemental about these claims and would would not
  object to this message if submitted through normal channels, but if I
  am forced to decide how to use my own resources, LaViolette's thesis
  does not strike me as worth the effort.
  The original message has been edited to remove the offending material,
  with the deleted section clearly marked.
