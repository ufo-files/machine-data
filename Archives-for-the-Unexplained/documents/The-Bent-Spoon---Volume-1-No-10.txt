I  n the landscape of paranormal media, there are traditionally two unique, yet sepa-
   rate, brands. One geared toward the believers, and the other more skeptical in
nature. The problem is that the true believers rarely, if ever, embrace a skeptical
attitude or ask tough questions of their community; instead preferring to surround
themselves with like- minded individuals that reinforce their own belief systems.
And the skeptics, likewise, promote science and critical thinking largely to those
already open to it, or who are active participants in the skeptical community. This
results in an echo chamber effect, wherein the same ideas are bounced back and
forth, guru-student relationships are inadvertently created, and neither side ends up
learning much about the other.

Enter: The Bent Spoon.

The Bent Spoon is a skeptical magazine for the true believer. Within its pages you
will find Q&A between those with opposing viewpoints, interviews with leading
investigators and thinkers, as well as articles which will not only provide in-depth
analysis, but also be critical of both believers and skeptics alike. Along with re-
views, comic strips, and other lighter fare, The Bent Spoon hopes to foster an at-
titude of outreach, forming a middle ground where believers and skeptics can come
together and have a conversation about the issues and questions we’ve all given
thought to.

The Bent Spoon. Where extraordinary claims meet ordinary explanations.

Last month, I contacted... a prominent figure in the ghost hunting community after stumbling across
his website. There were a number of claims made in the mission statement that piqued my interest, not
only for their dubious nature, but their perfunctory usage on a large number of similar sites. I emailed
the owner of the site, requesting a brief interview for publication in The Bent Spoon to which he
agreed. “Honored” was the term he used. Seen below are the five questions I sent, intending to spark a
larger dialog.

In your mission statement, your team is referred to as a “science based research group”. I’ve noticed
that a good number of modern ghost hunting groups or paranormal investigators have dubbed them-
selves a scientific group, but when asked to explain, they typically claim that it merely means they don’t
use psychics. Can you describe to us your investigative methods and how they follow the scientific
method?

Your team employs quite the formidable arsenal of equipment, ranging from environmental monitoring
devices, Tesla coils, and home-brew gadgets. What is the purpose of gathering any of this data? More
specifically, if any anomalous data were recorded, what do you do with this information and how can
you determine that it is in any way paranormal or even anomalous?

The well known Sherlock Holmes quote, “When you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains,
however improbable, must be the truth.” seems to be a common method among paranormal investiga-
tors. If you do practice this method, how do you feel that it is possible to overrule ALL possible expla-
nations in what can only be considered an uncontrolled environment? Is this method not truly a thinly
veiled argument from ignorance (i.e. the decided explanation is true because it has not yet been proven
false) ?

In the research articles section of your website, you have a list of PDFs taken from physics text books
and lecture slides covering topics like Faraday’s law, quantum mechanics, and electrodynamics. In
what way do these pertain to your research and how have you managed to connect these natural phe-
nomena to something that has no real documented properties or characteristics? Furthermore, if you
have formed a hypothesis in regard to the relationship between these phenomena, how do you propose
to design falsifiable and repeatable tests to confirm it

Could you define the term “pseudoskeptic” and differentiate it from what you believe is true skepti-
cism?
Also, are you under the impression that the general population of the skeptical community is simply
dismissive of paranormal claims or that they may just hold a higher standard of what is claimed to be
evidence?
Typical, run of the mill “ghost hunter questions” that, possibly, could form an interesting discussion,
if said “best- selling author/ researcher/ president and founder” could support his claims of “scien-
tific- based data collection”. Perhaps I gave him too much credit at the time, because I was very eager
to hear back from him. After two weeks past and no word, I wrote to him, asking if he was having
trouble. He claimed that he hadn’t received the questions (oddly, given that all other emails arrived in
his inbox without a problem.). Co-Founding partner, Bobby Nelson, predicted this situation, however,
and recommended sending the questions from multiple emails. I took his advice and resent them from
two personal accounts in addition to the official Bent Spoon address. I received confirmation this time,
along with a brief explaination that it might take a few days for the response, as he was preparing for a
presentation.

40 days later...

After no word from the interviewee I decided to check up with him on Facebook to see how he was
coming along. I was surprised to receive quite a bit of animosity from him and his co- authoring part-
ner, with claims of unprofessionalism and name- calling. He had also apparently sent the answers quite
some time ago and insinuated that I was having “email issues”.
       To give a bit of context; Bobby Nelson had emailed the partner of the paranormal investigator in
question in the interim of my forty day wait, inquiring about the interview questions. Bobby had some
familiarity with her, unlike my subject, and assumed the he could casually approach her. The result
didn’t go as expected

       Back to facebook. I explained to the two making accusations that Bobby and I are indeed, two
separate people, to which I received an apology from the co-author just before I was blocked from her,
hindering my ability to actually read it. The rest of the conversation was screen captured and is seen
