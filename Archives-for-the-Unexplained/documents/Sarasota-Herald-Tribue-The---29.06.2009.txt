29th June 2009…

Serious Media, Serious Integrity…

   LA Times columnist Tim Rutten raises a good point in attributing the MSM's 24/7 Michael Jackson coverage to the triumph of "consumer sovereignty." At week's end, Basij goons were busting more heads in Tehran, Barak Obama and Kim Jong Il got hissy over missiles, and  historic climate-change legislation was moving in Washington. But none could gain much traction against the Jackson monopoly, thanks to collective editorial decisions driven in large part by a massive Internet stampede toward the fate of The Gloved One.
   Rutten lamented "the fact of simple, brute commerce: popular culture in the form of film, music and TV now provides an outsized share of the financially strapped media's advertising  revenue." And he warned, "surrendering to it ultimately undercuts what's genuinely valuable about serious news media."
   As a member of the serious news media, which is collapsing on an hourly basis, De Void is also having trouble resisting those commercial pressures.
   At one point, in a craven impulse to squeeze out a few extra Web hits, De Void actually reached for Michael Luckman's "Alien Rock" book to shamelessly parrot some of Jackson's more obscure idiosyncrasies. But what would be gained by mentioning how, during his convalescence after the 1984 Pepsi hair-fire incident (from which his addiction to painkillers allegedly began), of all the videos he could've chosen to watch, he picked "Close  Encounters of the Third Kind"?
   And wouldn't it be the worst kind of pandering to try to lure Jackson fans into a UFO blog with his 2001 claim that he came from a planet outside our solar system named "Capricious Anomaly in the Sea of Space"? Or to regurgitate quotes from his 1980 UFO sighting from the cockpit of a plane one night?:
   "(I saw) illusions in the air that man has never seen. It was total dark and you saw these big icebergs that were glowing in the night. And then I looked far out into the sky and saw purple, green, and blue crystals sparkling, and turning in the air. I said, 'What is that?' And the pilot said that he didn't know, they'd only seen that once before. I said, 'My God, I'll never forget this.'"
   Would it add anything to our knowledge of the man - or of UFOs - to mention how MJ edited an "I Was Abducted" tabloid headline into his "Black or White" video? Of course not. It'd be rightly perceived for just what it was - a cheap stunt. And that won't happen here. Now or ever. Because this is serious news media.
