A comparison of the relative positions of the sun, the object, and my
location will indicate that this was probably the explanation of the

On each occasion the brilliant light lasted
only for two to three seconds.
When the brilliant light ceased I was still able to trace the
path of the object.
It seemed to start another broad curve from northwest
to northeast, then disappeared in some thin white clouds that were at high
altitude.
f. After the object disappeared I again turned to look at the

As nearly as I can judge,
it had moved a distance

During that time the object in the sky must
have covered at least forty or fifty miles, but I have no sound basis for
estimating its ground speed.
It was impossible to estimate the size or shape of the object,
as these were no more clear that the appearance of an artillery shell when
watched from Seem the breech of a gun during firing into the sky.
It was impossible to judge the speed of the object, for its
altitude was uncertain.
It did, however, cover a very great distance in
the time that I observed it, and was many times more rapid in flight than
the airplane (See map).
It appeared to make no change in altitude as nearly
as I could judge, and it disappeared above clouds which I believe are of
the type formed from ice crystals at extremely high altitude.
The attached sketch gives an idea of the directions involved,
but I cannot vouch for its accuracy as to scale, due to the fact there
was no way to judge the distance to the object.
For example:
When the
object appeared to me to have been over Long Beach, it might well have been
farther away and hence over Santa Ana.
As a check on the reliability of the observation the following
note is added:
I described what I had seen upon my arrival at my quarters
