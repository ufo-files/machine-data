AUDIO UFO CATALOG


     This is a listing of recordings on the UFO topic in the files of UHR and
other assorted collections. All programs are recorded on standard audio
cassettes and listed chronologically. The key to reading the entries is as
follows: Title/Show, Source, Subject, Date and Cassette Number. The
cassette number indicates the location by a two-letter code and the number
of the cassette in that collection, for example: (BG126). Location codes will
be listed at the end of this introduction. Where information is partial on the
list, I would welcome specifics filling those gaps.
     Radio broadcasts constitute the predominant medium for audio/visual
UFO archiving through the late 1970s when home video recording became
popular. Thousands of programs exist on tape but there is no central
catalog to consult in assessing the true extent of radio coverage of the
UFO phenomena. Private researchers hold most of this bulk with very
limited access available to the public. In many cases, such items are the
only record of a broadcast PERIOD, making those records unique in the
world.
     Other types of audio recordings, such as witness interviews, lectures,
special non-broadcast productions for retail sale, and investigator’s notes,
are as fragile and even less available than the radio recordings. Some of
these are not meant for anything other than the recorder’s own reference.
     It is certain that many of these recordings have been lost to history,
never even having become known to exist to others. We also face the
problem of deterioration of the cassettes. Everything ages. Tape becomes
brittle and eventually is unplayable. It becomes a very costly task to save
such data by transferring to a more permanent medium.
     It is hoped that this will be the beginning of a great master catalog of all
recordings ever done on the UFO topic. The plan is to add data from other
collections with a coded flag to indicate where the recording is held. If
there were no audio records of particular radio broadcasts available, I
would still like to add references to them for the sake of history.
     For any questions, contact Barry Greenwood at:
uhrhistory@comcast.net
