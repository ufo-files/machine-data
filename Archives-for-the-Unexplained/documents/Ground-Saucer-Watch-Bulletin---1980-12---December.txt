                             DIRECTLY SPEAKING
                             by: William H. Spaulding/Director

I have been asked numerous times within the past year, by various news media
reporters, where is UFO research going in the eighties and what is the next step
now that the FOIA (Freedom of Information Act(ion)) has been effectively stopped
by the government? Both of these questions are very good and extremely important
to the UFO movement. There are many who believe that new information will surface
in the form of a major UFO incident or through some "secret" information released
by an ex-military/government official.
There are, of course, many other researchers who strongly believe that collecting
data on UFO cases for another thrity years, will only add to the volume of reports,
photographs, and "stories" and never prove anything over what we already know.
Prior to answering these questions, we need to reqress slightly. The subject of
UFO's is a ~ temporal one, existing with both peaks and depressions of public/media
interest. This cyclic curve is generally attributable to the quantity (not quality)
of media reported UFO incidents and the fad syndrome which surfaces every two or
three years in the form of "outer space" themed movies or television shows. Although
the media is not entirely responsible for the higher interest in UFO's during the
past few years, we had "Close Encounters of the Third Kind", "Star Wars" and Jack
Webb's TV attempt of the Air Forces' involvement with their UFO projects.
People were thinking UFO's, seeing UFO's and generally interested in the subject,
to a higher degree than was shown in the year 1980. Our own files this year,
which are comparable to all UFO organizations, revealed less interest and less
reports of UFO incidents. This writer wants to believe that the cause is attributed
to the recurring patterns of the phenomena and possibly the lack of public interest
in the second generation of UFO movies, therefore, "Close Encounters II" and
"Hangar 1811
              However, what appears to be surfacing is public apathy. Many of us

think that the standard approach to UFO reporting to the body politic can no longer
suffice. The average citizen, who is only half-interested in the subject, is
wanting something new, rather than just another UFO report or a sensationalized
story of an abduction, followed by the customary opposing public disclaimers by
most of the experts in the field.
Some examples recently making the news are; the crashed saucer cases, with or without
the witness name and position in life revealed, the ravings from Switzerland(*l) which
really regressed all of the serious research efforts, and the excellent press
coverage given to the anti-ufologists. Does the anti-ufologist have a better story,
or superior·,· facts? Not necessarily. But they do have their act together and
make an admirable foe to the pro-researcher.
With this background scenario, it appears it is difficult to answer where UFO

But all is not lost. There is hope if some fundamental steps are taken, by all

It seems logical that all of us must start to take some united position on the

propulsion systems for weaponry and space travel? It's a very expensive decoy, if that's
what it is. Also if flying saucers are only a Federal mirage, for what purpose? There
could be some frightening answers. The years ahead promise to be very interesting!

INTRODUCTION
According to the book UFO ... Contact From The Pleiades, a Swiss citizen named
Eduard 11 Billy 11 Meier has had over 130 contacts with aliens, recording over 3,000
pages of quotes from them, and has taken hundreds of clear pictures of their craft.
He has also been given rock and metal samples, which according to his story, defy
conventional explanation. The book goes on to state that an investigation conducted
by Wendelle Stevens, Thomas Welch, Britt-Nilsson-Elders, and Lee Elders, has shown
all of Meier's claims to be genuine.
If the events as described above were truly factual, it would certainly be one of
the most remarkable occurrences the world has ever seen. But as we shall discover,
none of the purported events appears to be able to withstand careful scientific
scrutiny.

                           Why UFO Research Will Fail -
                        A Close Look at Causes and Effects
                           BY: Willi,am H. Spaulding



UFO research, as we know it today, will fail very soon due to the shortcomings
of the involved persons in the field. This is a harsh comment for anyone
to make, especially for someone who has been involved for half of their
li fetirne, as in the case of this writer. Never before have so many persons,
who are involved with the study of a mystifying phenon:enon, accomplished so
little. It has become apparent that UFO researchers . in over thirty years of
investigating these unknown aerial incidents, have obtained so little data
and no unilateral policy regarding the origin of the phenor.e non.
UFO researchers have done more to hurt their own cause than any newsmedia
blasting pro-UFO theories or any high publici ty hoaxer. To the outsider,
the ufologist comes across, in many cases, as a sincere person but without
the right answers, so necessary to appease the inquisitive minds of the curious.
It is one thing to give salient information on a gurported UFO incident and another
to follow these statements with what sounds like (or is at least presented
as) irrefutable facts that UFO's represent visitations from outer space. However,
this situation cannot be avoided, as there is no "official" spokesperson
for UFO researchers. Likewise, there is no central clearing house for data
evaluation and dissemination.
In reality, when one takes the time to review the entire UFO field there exists
numerous problems that require imrrediate attention. The following capsulates
these major areas of concern:
1.   The popularity of UFO's is slipping, to such a point that public and
     media apathy is noticeable. This is not entirely the fault of ufologists.
     UFO's are a temperamental news item that is predicated on the volume
     of quality incidents and/or sensational reports which are generally
     exploited for monetary gain.
2.   UFO researchers and organizations have not provided the media or the
     public with a united front on their goals, objectives or analyse s of a
     typical incident. Generally, the media will interview t wo or more heads
     of VFO organizations or an expert in the field and each person will present
     a different viewpoint on the incident, which tends to confuse the issue
     and bewilder the interested parties.
3.   It appears that each major UFO organization has contrasting goals.
     Sorne organizations claim UFO's are from outer space, others are investigating
     abductions and the paranormal, and some are just maintaining that UFO's are
     a mystery and doing little, if anything, to solve the problem. One common
     factor is the lack of corrmunications, as little data is being disseminated
     between organizations.

4.   There is less than five hundred active ufologists in this country. By
     active, I mean persons who are vigorously pursuing UFO research on a non-
     hobby basis.
5.   Most UFO researchers belong to multiple organizations and this is a difficult
     situation to say the least. Besides having their priorities mixed up, many
     of these individuals get bogged down with the political problems existing
     between organizations and researchers. There is no obvious cure for member-
     ships to multiple organizations, unless all groups adhere to a membership-
     by-invitation only status and that would be difficult for most parties,
     since they depend on membership dues for operating capital.
6.   During the past thirty years, since the conception of modern day sightings,1
     there has been very little in the way of a scientific break-through in
     technology, techniques or new methods of analyses.
7.   There exists an extremely large data base of UFO reports, however, most are
     incomplete or lack the pertinent information necessary to provide any
     realistic evaluation of the incident.
8.   There is very little communication between UFO organizations nor is there any
     appreciable quantity of data being shared. Dissemination of UFO reports
     is a good idea as it spurs additional opinions and generates new interpretations
     possibly overlooked by others.
9.   UFO researchers do not have an exorbitant amount of strong UFO materials, of
     the variety that offers absolute proof. To date, all we can collectively
     prove is that a government cover-up is transpiring, there have been some
     bonafide sightings with supportive photographic, radar or other secondary
     evidence to supplement the observation and that an unknown phenomenon/object
     exists.
10. Most of the major UFO conferences, held in years previous, have not come forth
    with a workable solution on how to attack the UFO situation. In many instances
    well-written papers have been given on the subject but have not offered any
    proposals on the future and what to do with the stacks of unprocessed data.
The above listing capsulates the obvious problems and situations that confront
many UFO researchers. However, there appears to be a more significant problem,
the philosophical differences existing between organizations and individuals.
These differences permeate the entire field and have caused heated debates over
extremely mundane points, in an attempt to prove who is right.
The philosophical differences can be categorized into two major groupings; the
believers and the non-believers. The believer grouping contains some persons who
envision that all research should be concentrated on the contact cases. This
category can be related to a religious philosophy or the much-publicized abduction
syndrome, whereby, earthlings are kidnapped, medically examined and "returned"
to their .normal lifestyle with a portion of their "experience" surrounded in
secrecy. Generally the subjective tools of regressive hypnosis or polygraph
examination are used to provide answers to the missing time of the abduction experience.

In another believer category, the supporter strongly supports the extra-
terrestrial hypothesis. These persons know that seine and/or all of the
'unexplained' sightings are visitations from outer space with a sundry of
reasons for the visit to earth. This category generally researches the
sightings of UFOs but pays little attention to . the reports of "space creatures".
In the category of the non-believer, things are easier to explain. It's
simply a matter of choosing a group that fits their pet theory. This ranges
from the .hoax, misinterpretation, mental problems of the witness(es) and
insufficient data of the incidents. It is so easy to stereo-type UFOs into
these divisions, even the reports that are difficult to explain, due to the
separation of signal from the noise and the past history of so many reports
deteriorating under close scrutiny.
This paper is not going to argue the question of who is right or at least half-
right. Nor is this writer or the organization - GSW - going to elaborate
extensively on the reasons why we believe that UFOs are a real physical source
of the non-extraterrestrial variety. The intent of these \'lritings is to exhibit
the reasons of confusion in the field of research and the important fact of why
UFO research is not solving the continuing unanswered questions related to the
phenomenon.
Since the over-all problem with ufology is the lack of a well-defined list of
goals and objectives and because the field is so large no one person or organization
can effectively research the entire spectrum a 1one, the fo 11 owing recommendati ans
are offered as an immediate solution to the total UFO problem.
1.   All splinter groups of researchers, dissatisfied with the present situation,
     should not be ignored. Their ideas should be heard and evaluated by all
     major organizations. These ideas, if sound, must be implemented into the
     over-all goals of the major groups.
2.   Define the major categories of research and assign a group of persons from
     the various organizations to evaluate these categories for their importance
     to the solution. Example: are abduction cases worthy of additional study,
     what have we learned from UFO photographs, what is a colTTnunity-wide
     acceptable list of hoaxed UFO incidents, how many and what UFO incidents
     could stand up to an outside scientific analysis, etc.
3.   Elect a team of spokepersons from the field to represent UFO research at
     major press conferences. This team would be changed at pre-selected
     intervals and should include a few of the predominant figures in the field
     and the lesser-known field investigators, who generally obtain most of the
     data for the UFO organizations. Of course, UFO researchers, who make a
     living from the subject phenorrenon may find this hard to live with.
4.   Standardize the field investigative techniques with a simple UFO handbook
     which would include a section on field investigations, photo evaluation,
     witness interrogation guidelines, usage of field equipment, etc. Other
     disciplines have exact criteria to work from and UFO research is no different.
     A machinist handbook is a typical example of what is needed.
