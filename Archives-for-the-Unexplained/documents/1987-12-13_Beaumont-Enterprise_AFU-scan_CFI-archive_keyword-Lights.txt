KANSAS CITY, Mo. (AP)       -­


A rocket body burned up as it
entered the Earth's atmosphere
Saturday night, putting on a
spectacular light show over a
broad area of the West and
Midwest, the National Weather
Service said.

  The lights streaked across
the sky about 8:50 p.m. CST,
and callers from Montana to
Arkansas said they saw vividly
colored streaks of blue, red and
other hues.

   "We're getting all kinds of
calls - one of our officers even
saw it," said Cpl. Joe Schmidli
of the Independence, Mo., po­
lice.

  The Weather Service in Wi­
chita, Kan., citing information
from the North American Air
Defense Command, reported
that the lights were from the
rocket's re-entry. It was not
immediately clear what rocket
had re-entered the atmosphere.

   Switchboards at police head­
quarters and radio and news
stations lit up as people called
to report the phenomenon.
