 Killer
 bacteria
 invade
 hospitals
         by Oliver Gillie
      Medical Correspondent

 A NEW variety of bacterium,
 resistant to all the common
 antibiotic drugs, is invading
 hospitals in the British Isles.
 Similar bacteria have caused
 great problems in the United
 States and Australia, where
 some hospitals have found
 them impossible to get rid of.
    The bacterium is a variety of
 Staphylococcus aureus - a germ
 which is commonly pre�ent on
 the skin and causes minor
 infections such as pimples, boils
 or infected cuts. In healthy
 people    such     infections  are
 seldom a problem but in a
 hospital the same bacterium
 may infect wounds and cause
 complications which can kill
 seriously-ill patients.
    An outbreak of the resistant
 bacteria has caused the inten-       •


 sive-care unit at the Royal Free
 Hospital in north London to be
 closed. One patient in the unit
 died partly as a result of the
 infection. The other patients
 were moved to another ward to
 enable the intensive-care unit to
 be closed for cleansing.
    Dr Jean Bradley, infection
 control officer at the Royal
 Free, says: "Potentially it is a
 very serious situation. Once
 introduced into a hospital such
 as this it can be impossible to
 get rid of. In some hospitals in
 the United States they have just
 had to learn to live with it."
    Spread    of   the    invading
 "Staph". as it is called in
 hospitals, has been halted at the
 Royal    Free by      intensifying
 ordinary measures of hygiene
 and by isolating those patients
 who are harbouring it. For the
 past three weeks the Staph has
 not spread and the ward and
 intensive-care unit have been
 reopened after cleansing. Tests
 have established that the bac­
 terium is not being carried by
 staff members.
    Profe or Mary Cook, an
 expert in hospital infections at
 the Public Health Laboratory in
 Colindale, is collecting infor­
 mation about the bacterium.
 She says: "We don't know how
 common it is or how many
 deaths it may have caused
 because there is no established
 system for reporting it. We are
 hoping to nip it in the bud and
 prevent a serious situation from
 developing."
