Glowing cloud a mystery
   BUFFALO, N.Y. ( UPI ) ' A strange luminous cloud spotted by hundreds
of people in eastern states prompted a flood of calls to police, the UFO
reporting center and radio stations but no one knows what the phenomenon
was, a National Weather Service meteorolog ist said today.
   Meteorolog ist David Sage said it was unclear what caused the
unusual cloud, which he described as having a dull glow "like glowing
gas."
   Sage said he never had seen a cloud quite l ike it before and
speculated that it might have been a noctilucent cloud, a luminous cloud
of unknown substance that occurs at great heights above the earth's
surface.
   "Hundreds of people apparently called radio stations and police in
the eastern third of the country," he said.
   Sage said the whitish cloud was not part of a meteor shower that
had been visible in the area in the past two days, nor did he think it
was caused by pollution. He dismissed the possibility of it being
anything supernatural or extraterrestrial.
   Tom Bauerle of WGR radio in Buffalo said that at about 10 p.m.
Tuesday "my show was inundated with calls from people who saw a strange
luminous cloud."
   Bauerle said he called the National UF_O Reporting Center in
Seattle, and the cente.r had gotten more than 100 calls from people in
eastern states asking about the cloud.
    "The UFO Center checked with ( NASA's ) Wallops Islan d launch site
in Virginia to see if it might ·have been a rocket test; however,
officials at Wallops Island told the UFO center that they did not launch
any of the four rockets they were supposed to launch last night,"
B auerle said.
   He said the UFO center agreed with the National Weather Service in
concluding that "it was some sort of natural phenomenon, and the
available information precludes making a definitive judgement on what it
was."
