      FRIEDMAN'S LATEST LITERARY BLAST:
      "Science Was Wrong" is a tome by the inevitable Stanton Friedman, together with co­
author Kathleen Marden, who is a ufologically active niece of famed abductee Betty Hill.
The subtitle of the book .is: "Startling Truths About Cures, Theories, and Inventions 'They'
Declared Impossible" - and we all know who "They" are�
      This is not a saucer book, but a very interesting treatise about instances in which
scientists or other authority figures of that era declared key inventions (such as power­
ed flight, the telephone, radio, TV, etc.) to be either impossible or at least impractical.
We were already aware of some of these situations, but by no means all.
      Of course the point is well taken; but it becomes far more complicated when scientif­
ic opinions are somewhat equally divided as to whether a given invention or procedure is
valid or not. The clearest example of this problem is the chapter on global wa�ing, a
subject about which the controversy still rages on. Our insight is that politically con­
servative people like the evidence that shows the danger is basically not caused by human
activity (co2 in the atmosphere, etc.), whereas environmentalists and political liberals
(Al Gore is an extreme case!) take the opposite point of viewo Which side should a scien­
tifically ignorant person like your editor believe??
      The most interesting section of the book is the last, "Frontiers of Science", with
chapters on Psi Phenomena (apparently pronounced E.I), UFOs, and Alien Abduction. To make
a long story short, Friedman apparently believes in some aspects of the paranormal, but he
still adamently refuses to consider the strong possibility that UFOs are from some sort of
paranormal realm rather than being physical spaceships from another star system. Many deep
ufological thinkers, such as Hynek, Vallee and Moseley, believe otherwise. What if someday

      Sitchin shot to fame in the mid 1970s with his first book, called "The 12th Planet•;

started by the Anunna.hi, a -race of ETs from a "lost" planet in our own solar system call­

the "ancient astronaut" group of modern literary figures. Another was Erich von Daniken,
who still raves on. We don't know the educational background of either of these two men.

      Exopolitics is the movement dedicated to understanding & getting along with ET crea­
tures that have never been proven to existo It also involves the totally unproven notion
that our government knows vastly more about these creatures than we doo Perhaps we earth­
lings have already flown to other planets ourselves; but the public will never know the
Truth unless this small band of pioneers continues to push as loudly as possibleo
      This is a well-intentioned group of idealists, but their movement is doomed to fail­
ure. This is partly because, in spite of public demonstrations involving more than one
million people each, it took us years to get out of Viet Nam. Sadly, the exopolitics
movement is minute indeed compared to this. Not only that, but their unproven claims may
very possibly not be valid:
      So, we of "Smear" have basically stopped writing about the movement. We even ig­
nored their most recent Event {Sept. 27th, 2010) at the prestigious National Press Club
in Washington, D.C., where seven former u.s. military personnel spoke publicly (again) re­
garding their knowledge of UFO incursions at nuclear weapons sites during the Cold    War
era. This above-mentioned gathering was relatively successful in regard to generating pub­
licity.

out that four of the press conference participants were there to discuss UFO activity at

      Another retired officer named Eric Carlson was also involved in one of these inci­
dents, but does not remember any mention of a UFO being present at the timeo This was a
specific incident at Malmstrom's Echo Flight, on March 16th, 1967, when all ten of their
ICBMs supposedly shut down at the same time.
