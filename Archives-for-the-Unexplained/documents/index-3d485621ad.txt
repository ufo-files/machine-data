    Our Review                                                                                            Opinion of the webmaster, subject to debate

  We were not sure that the world needed Phil Klass's take on the Roswell crash, but nonetheless here it is:
  released after most of the Roswell hoopla has died down anyway. Can you guess his conclusion? No alien
  craft crashed near Roswell! Okay, if there is nothing new in Klass's attitude, it is still interesting to see the
  current Roswell debate filtered through his jaundiced eye. Because it was completed recently, the book
  covers some of the later developments not reported in others, including Project Mogul, the GAO report,
  Pflock's criticism of Glenn Dennis, the fall of Don Schmitt and the defection of Kent Jeffrey. Having been
  around ufology longer than most still-living proponents, Klass seems to have plenty of dirt on everyone,

  and he follows up on a lot of previously unresolved issues. We are impressed with the sheer density of data
  here. Klass's strength is keeping track of documents and sequences of events. Although he uses no
  footnotes, he gives precise dates for almost every event he mentions and provides enough clues to lead us
  to the source document. Even if you don't agree with his slant, this is an important reference work on the
  Roswell case and probably Klass's best book yet. -- Glenn Campbell

  Comparison to Other Books

  Although this book is valuable for its facts and arguments, Pflock's Roswell in Perspective better explains how and why
  the "incident" occurred. We recommend reading that book first.
