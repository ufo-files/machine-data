A high-altitude torpedo over Uddevalla

FLYGMOTOR
GG/VO

To the Defence Staff

Re: Observations made concerning a space missile

The undersigned, employed at AB Flygmotor in Trollhättan, made the following observations regarding a space missile on August 22 at 9.38-9.41 pm. I was in my summer house at the estate of Sund at the Byfjorden near Uddevalla.

First of all I wish to point out that this is not a supernatural or a cosmic phenomenon. I am 100 percent sure that this object was manufactured in a mechanical workshop.

I was in my summer house and was busy reading something when I heard the sound of an aeroplane which is not at all unusual there. This time, however, I especially noticed the sound as the volume  
was increasing all the time without seeming to reach a maximum. 

I was listening to the noise for about one minute before I got  up to have a look, and during this period of time the aeroplane should normally have  passed. What mainly caught my attention, however, was the sound of the engine. It sounded like a slow moving combustion engine but the bangs were considerably more powerful.

When I got out on my doorstep I immediately saw the object towards the starry night sky. It was straight up in the sky at about 70 degrees angle from the ground.

Observations:

Shape. The shape of the object was according to the enclosed watercolour. However, I wish to point out that the exact shape was difficult to see as I was disturbed by the sparkling light from the stars in the background. The body of the object was thus torpedo shaped but with other proportions than those of a torpedo or an aeroplane, in so far as the diameter being remarkably large in relation to the length. In the back there was an exhaust blaze with a diameter not quite as large as the largest diameter of the body, but at least as large as the diameter of the rear part. The length of the blaze was about the same as the diameter. The colour of the blaze was yellow, in a shade about the same as the watercolour. I could not notice any movement of the blaze.













(Colour) painting of the object made by the witness.


I had the opportunity of observing the shape of the rear part of the missile for quite some time and that is why I think I have got it right. As regards the shape of the front part, however, I am not quite sure about this. At first I thought I saw something sticking out radially from the front part but I could not with certainty establish whether it was something else than an optical illusion caused by the sparkling starlight which kind of disturbed the profile.       

At first sight I could not notice any wings and that is why I especially looked for them, however without succeeding.

The sound. I had no impression that the sound was coming behind the object. The noise was very strong and it sounded as if it was coming from a combustion engine with a low number of revolutions, but with the difference that the bangs were considerably more powerful. It was quite different than the fast moving, well accumulated and buzzing sound from an ordinary aeroplane engine. The sound cannot either be defined as roaring.

Another kind of sound phenomenon which I noticed was heterodynes which would indicate that there was more than on source of sound. I estimated the "number of revolutions" to be 
1 rev/sec.  

I could not notice any changes in the number of revolutions or in the absolute sound intensity.

No other sounds like whining could be heard.

The trajectory. The trajectory seemed to be linear both sideways and vertically.











Map of the area with flight path and notes by the witness.


The direction. The compass direction was checked on the following day and was determined to be 280 degrees with a deviation of 4 degrees taken into consideration. The direction thus was ONO, the missile came from the sea and proceeded towards Stockholm. See map enclosed.

Period of time. The period of time during which I watched the object was 2 minutes and before that I could hear it for about 1 minute. With reservation taken to these estimations.

The altitude. The altitude was very difficult to determine. According to my opinion however it was about 1000-2000 m, closer to 1000 m. I could see that the altitude was high when the object went away because it disappeared fairly high above the horizon instead of behind it. The reason why I could not see it any longer was that the distance became too long.

The velocity. Of course the velocity was also very difficult to determine. If we  assume that the altitude was about 1000 m I would assess the velocity to be approximately the same as the velocity of  an aeroplane type J 21, probably higher.

The size. With the assumption of the altitude being 1000 m I would say that the missile was somewhat longer than a fighter and the diameter was at least twice the size of the one of a fighter.

Summing up. It could not have been an ordinary aeroplane, partly because

a) the sound could be heard for too long a period of time
b) the sound was too high, absolutely considered, and in relation to the altitude
c) the number of revolutions was too low in relation to the velocity
d) with all certainty there does not exist any combustion engine causing bangs that powerful

It could not have been a jet plane with a constant combustion.

The object was probably equipped with a jet propulsion unit with an intermittent combustion, that is a unit equipped with valves.

Enabling the missile to keep the straight trajectory which it actually did, with all certainty it must have been equipped with wings. It could be possible that the irregularities which I noticed on the front part of the object could be the outer parts of small wings, similar to those of the V1-bomber.

Any superstructure, similar to the one of the V1-bomber could not be observed.   

Trollhättan, August 24, 1946
/signed/
Gustaf Göthberg
Master of mining engineering
