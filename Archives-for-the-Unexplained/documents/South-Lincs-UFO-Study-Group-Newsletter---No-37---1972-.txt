Bright Star. Report by Richard Thompson.
After leaving work on July the first, Mr. Kane, aged 19 yrs. of
Lincoln, journeyed along to Somercotes with the intentions of' an all
night fishing expedition. Being on his own and possibly feeling
somewhat tired through lack of activity, he fell asleep. When he
finally awoke he began to look around the sky for weather signs. It
was then that Mr. Kane's attention was attracted to what appeared
 to resemble a very bright whitish star in a south-westerly direction.
The starlike object was a spheroid shape, and gave the impression
of being stationary at first; after about a second or so it began to
move away at a constant velocity in a south-east direction until it
disappeared behind some cloud. Although Mr. Kane continued to observe
the area around this particular cloud, he could not find any trace
of the starlike object again. The sighting lasted between 7 to 8
seconds, at no time during or after the observation period was any
sound detected at all: Mr. Kane further states that it was impossible
to tell whether the object was metallic or not and neither did it
pulsate, also the elevation was far too high to estimate with any
accuracy.
When asked about an-y psychological effects, Mr. Kane said that in
no way was he aware of any.
The object did not have any extraordinary characteristics. Unfortuna­
tely there was no oth0r witness.
