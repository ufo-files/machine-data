The lookout tower is a unique method of fire detection. Even though they are used in
reduced numbers, lookout towers have specific advantages that make them excellent
detection units.

A: Fire towers exist in remote sections of the country and by plan offer almost unlimited
visibility. Lookouts often see things that employees in town and under roof do not see.

BRIEF DESCRIPTION I DEFINITION OF ANOMALIES

Many Lookouts see strange balls of light, unfamiliar looking structured craft ,
symmetrical objects with running lights, or hear strange sounds from undetermined
sources. These objects may not be "natural phenomena" (as described later in the
addendum: page 39) but may be seen hovering, moving at unusual speeds, merging
into each other, appearing to change shape and volume, and on some occasions,
disappearing from sight while in plain view.

The movements of these anomalous objects can be startling, stimulating, and
frightening. This study shows that when these objects show unexpected and sudden
movement directly to the lookout tower that they can cause great fear and anxiety. In
this study I have found five instances where the lookouts have fled the area or hidden
themselves in the tower in an attempt to shut out such experiences. In two such events
the lookouts suspect that they may have been abducted.

There is a lot of necessary and unavoidable subjectivity in the subject. No one can
"really tell you" what it is like to raise children if you have no done it. No one can
"really tell you" what it is like to be in a combat situation on the battlefield. From my
own experience, and from those with whom I have discussed this subject, there is no
way anyone can "really tell you" about what it is like to come face to face with an
anomaly- a UFO. You have to have been there to understand the surprise and, at
times, the fear.

LIMITATIONS OF THE STUDY

I experienced great difficulty in locating and contacting present day lookouts. The
USFS has basically shown very little to no interest in assisting me in contacting
lookouts. I could not get a formal link with the agency even though I submitted a small
pilot study to regional and national USFS supervisors. (see addendum) Therefore I
had to depend upon the FFLA (Forest Fire Lookout Association), mailing to lookouts
that I knew, finding individuals on the Internet, networking, links with UFO researchers,
and word of mouth. Therefore, I really have inadequate coverage of present day
lookouts. I was unable to contact other allied agencies such as, the BIA, State Forest
agencies and private fire fighting groups. In that respect, this research was very
limited. The summary shows the wide dispersal of dates and regional nature of the
sighting respondents.

Lookout positions are very hard to obtain . The few contemporary lookouts I did contact
were VERY HESITANT to let me use their sightings. Approximately fifteen persons
told me their stories verbally but refused to write them down. Probably 70 % of all the
participants do not want their names used nor their lookouts identified. It is clear that
this subject intimidates both lookouts, workers and supervisors.

Lookouts have a definite problem in reporting such events to the ranger station and to
supervisory personnel. The reports are usually met with humor, disbelief, and in some
cases, derision. Knowing this, many individuals will simply not report any anomaly
they may see.

Lookouts are loyal to their job and do not want to be "taken down" or to be derided by
their colleagues. Consideration should be made for education of what lookouts might
see and appropriate reporting procedures should be available. (See summary : page
37)

This study examines 82 sightings primarily by lookouts. For those of you who have not
served as lookouts, try to empathize with lookouts working in isolated environments.
The lookouts in this study averaged well over 10 summers of experience.

Let me make it clear, most of the lookouts I contacted HAVE NOT seen anomalous
events but many have. (Keep in mind I was unable to conduct a wide survey.)
Lookouts who have seen things they can 't explain, find themselves in the middle of a
very unsettling and unusual event.

 I have withheld names and locations of some of these events as a condition of
securing the reports. The study has a small N because I am unable to systematically
survey all lookouts. Part of my recommendations at the end of this study is to request
permission to contact contemporary lookouts in the Pacific Northwest and to provide
them with a way to record and report such events.

SUMMARY OF MAJOR FINDINGS

I now have 82 sightings in my collection . Finding material for this study has been
difficult because the investigator had no assistance by which I could poll the present
and past lookouts. Here are some of my conclusions:

**Most experienced lookouts that I have been able to contact have never seen UFOs,

* Comets-reguarly occurring mall masses (usually composed of ice and dust)
that burn in outer space. They usually trail a gaseous tail. Normally this tail

less in brightness than Venus, they are called shooting stars. They are usually
only seen for a few seconds and usually travel in a straight line or a wide arc.

BACKGROUND OF LONG'S STUDY - I am particularly indebted to researcher-writer
Greg Long for the use of some of his sightings in this study. For anyone wishing to
seriously pursue the nature of the sightings of this period (1960s-1970s). this is a must
read book .

Some of the data in this research comes from the book Examining the Earth Light
Theorv-The Yakama Microcosm. Mr. Long's data is more elaborate than my own . He
has analyzed some 200 separate sightings gathered by Bill Vogel and David Akers.
Vogel was a fire control officer at the Yakama Indian Reservation. These reports were
gathered from the late 1960's throughout the 1970's. Mr. Long and David Akers
reviewed information from the original reports of lookouts, field workers and from
personal interviews.These examples are included with Mr. Long's permission .
                                                              •
His book is a careful analysis of sightings in this 1.25 million acre reservation . Long
also met and interviewed Mr. Vogel before his death and found him to be a very
creditable individual. Long was particularly interested in studying balls of light (usually
orange or white in color) the phenomena most reported by individuals. He wanted to
see if the theories of Michael Persinger (Prof. of Psychology and Nero-Physiology) and
John Derr (U .S. Geological Survey) could account for the phenomena at Yakama.

The researchers held that almost all activity reported as anomalies can be accounted
for by the stresses and strains of geological land forms. The ideas relate to electrical
energies that can, and do on rare occasions, generate electrical phenomena.

The Earth is 7,918 miles in diameter, but the Earth's crust is only about 25 miles
thick.This crust, floating on a dynamic bed of magma, reacts to the pressures from
below and subsequent grinding of forces causes not only earthquakes but massive
amounts of energy. Some of this power can be released in the form of electrically
charged balls of light. These balls can be seen briefly for periods of time before and
                                            42
after earthquakes of 4.5 magnitude or greater.

Persinger and Derr believe that Earth tensions are responsible for almost all
anomalies reported on land as well as in the skies. In 1981 a researcher at the Bureau
of Mines subjected small amounts of a granite core to pressures of 32,000 PSI and
produced short-lived balls of light. However, most of the sightings in my study don't fall
into these categories. The visual description and duration of UFO movements
certainly show some kind of directed maneuvers.

Long 's study also concluded that many objects in the Yakama area did not follow the
Earth Light Theory. His study was on balls of light but observers also spoke of a variety
of objects including some metallic shapes. These metallic appearing objects were not
the focus of the Long-Akers research.

I also submit that ball lightning probably does not fit many cases. It is generally
observed in connection with lightning storms and , even then the balls of light do not
last more than a few seconds and are only 4 to 16 inches in diameter.
