The Centre’s unexplained percentage in
early years was well above that of the
RAAF. The Centre tended to receive
many older cases which were added
into the existing records, not something
the RAAF probably did. A feature noted
elsewhere in the world is that the
stranger events are reported to a UFO
group rather than a public authority.
Many interesting cases surfaced years
after the event, most usually uncovered
during investigation into some other
mundane event.        The result is that
unexplained cases would be added to
earlier years whilst few if any explained
sightings would be added to those
figures.
The UFO percentage of reports
investigated during the past 38 years is
18.8%. However, this figure has been
boosted during years of intense
sightings in the mid 70s and 1996. The
last few years have been 50% below
the average.
Astronomical explanations are the
largest stimulus for a sighting, about
35% of cases being covered by this
explanation. Aircraft at 13% comes in
second on the list, with satellites
accounting for a further 9%. Insufficient
information for a conclusion covers
about 10% of cases. The remaining
percentage is made up by a spectrum of
events such as flares, balloons, ground
lights, weather, noises, hoaxes etc: just
to name a few.
The most reports in the log book for a
single year is 193 in 1976, this works
out to be a call to the Centre every two
days. A very busy time for those who do
the investigation in their spare time. On
the other hand the lowest number of
calls in one year was 46 in both 1984
and 1990.
The year with the highest number of
UFO sightings was 1974 with 58
unidentifieds. At the other end of the
scale is the year just completed (2002)
with a meagre 4 unexplained events.
Do you have a sighting? Maybe it will
correlate with a record in the Logbook?
