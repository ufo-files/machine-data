I couldn't believe what I was
seeing.
Not wanting to take my eyes
away from the three red lights that
were slowly and silently cutting
their way across the night sky, my
fingers fumbled blindly in the dark-
ness of my car as I searched for
my two-way Nextel.
When my brother responded, I
could tell by his disoriented voice
that he had been in the process
of going to bed
"The red lights are back," I said
Of course, he didn't believe me
at first. Nearly a year before, in
October 2004, we both had stood
awestruck as the same lights,
which looked as if an artist's brush
had dropped three small dabs of
reddish-orange paint on an immac-
ulately black canvas, flew over his
home in Tinley Park in a rough tri-
angular formation.
What were the chances that
such a strange and once-in-a-life-
time experience would happen
again, and so soon?
Forgetting about the hambur
ger and fries that only a moment
before had been foremost on my
mind, I got out of my car and
tried as hard as I could to get a
better view of the anomalies.
As in August 2004, they took the
better part of an hour to fully tra-
verse the sky and never made any
sound one would associate with
an airplane or helicopter.
When I finally arrived at home
that night, the lights still were
faintly visible through a stack of
clouds that had moved in.
Whether it was my imagination,
an optical illusion caused by the
clouds or something else, I
watched as one by one, each of the
three red orbs shot off and disap-
peared into the darkness.
What exactly was it that I and
hundreds of other Tinley residents
saw that night? I'm not sure.
Although I would definitely clas-
sify the lights as unidentified fly-
ing objects, I'm not willing to say
there was anything paranormal or
supernatural about them.
What I can say, however, is that
they weren't hallucinations. They
weren't road flares ties to balloons.
They weren't airplanes, and they
weren't Mars, Jupiter or Mercury.
They were something else
entirely.
