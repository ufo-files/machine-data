Several videos on earth mysteries made some time ago have just been uploaded to
YouTube. I have been an amateur film maker since the late 1980s, but in the early
days they had to be distributed on video tapes. Now with the advent of YouTube they
can at last be broadcast to a world-wide audience. One of the earlier ones was A Walk
on Stanton Moor, on holiday with my wife in Derbyshire. The year was not recorded,
but it was almost certainly late 1980s.

Stanton Moor is on the eastern edge of the
Peak District limestone, an outcrop of sand­
stone which holds in its 150 acres of
heather-clad moorland, one of the finest
collections of Bronze Age remains to be
found in the British Isles. Over seventy
barrows are scattered across the moor, with
stone circles, cairns and standing stones.
The best preserved is the Nine Ladies stone
circle, 33 feet in diameter with its King
Stone close by. This was once enclosed by Dowsing the Nine Ladies stone circle
a stone wall which has since been removed.
27 acres of the moor were gifted to the National Trust in 1934.

Goddard over a period of about twenty years, but having some overlap with discoveries
