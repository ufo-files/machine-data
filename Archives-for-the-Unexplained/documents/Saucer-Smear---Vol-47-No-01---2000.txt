 THIS IS   AMAZING,   RANDI   -   AND ALSO   AMUSING!
       The Jan.-Feb. 2000 issue of CSICOP's "Skeptical Inquirer" (sometimes
 called the "Skeptical Enquirer") contains an article which we find aston­
 ishing, to put it mildly. The Fellows and Scientific Consultants of the
 dreaded CSICOP organization were asked the question - Who are the   out­
 standing skeptics of the twentieth century? No list of names was provided,
 and no suggested criteria. These learned gentlemen were thus free    to
 choose anyone they liked, the only restriction being that the person's ma­
 jor contributions are from the twentieth century - not earlier.
       The winner, as you may have guessed by now, was James Randi!    The
 irony is that, just a few years ago, Randi resigned from CSICOP under a
 cloud. He had caused that august organization to lose its libel insurance
 because of various off-the-cuff remarks he made about Uri Geller and oth­
 ers, for which he was sued. The details of these several lawsuits    are
 long and complex, but the upshot was that Randi left CSICOP and is    now
 closely allied with Michael Shermer's rival skeptical magazine out of Cal­
 ifornia, called simply "Skeptic".
       The first six runners-up after Randi are CSICOP members in   good

BOOKS TO KEEP YOUR BONFIRE BURNING BRIGHTLY:
      For some reason, we have lately been receiving many more (free) re­
view copies of UFO & other offbeat books than we ever did before,    and
there just isn't Space in our 8-page format to review them properly. Yet
we feel morally obliged to say something, following our motto "if you can't
say something bad, don't say anything at all". Thus we will follow    the
lead of Bob Girard of Arcturus Books, who writes in his monthly catalogue
just one short scathing paragraph about each of the books he is trying to
sell.
      First there is "Gardner's Whys & Wherefores", written for Prometheus
Books by Martin Gardner, who is a brilliant scholar and a pillar of    the
dreaded CSICOP organization (Committee for the Scientific Investigation of
the Paranormal)G  This is a collection of essays on various topics of in­
terest to the author, including a 6-page treatise on the mathematical con­
stant "Pi". Flying saucers are not even mentioned, which is a merciful
thing, as Gardner's views are rigidly negative on that particular subject.
      Then there is "The Mystic Healers - A History of Magical Medicine",
by our olde acquaintance Paris Flaw�onde, who is said to be a widely rec­
ognized expert in the fields of modern mythology (such as UFOs! ), the oc­
cult, and paranormal experiences. This book purports to be an expose of
fake cures of many kinds, including those by the notorious Church of Scien­
tology. Published by Scarborough House, this is a thoroughly revised and
updated edition of an earlier work.
      Next we have "Alien Base: The Evidence for Extraterrestrial Coloniza-
tion of Earth".   This is one of a series of high-profile books by    our
friend Timothy Good of England, whose earlier work "Alien Contact" was a
best sellerG   Now Good purports to believe that Earth is actually the base
of operations for several different alien species. The photographs     are
very interesting, but unfortunately they include several by George Adamski,
Howard Meng.er, and Paul Villa. Egads!
      Next - "UFO Odyssey", by prolific offbeat writer Brad Steiger and his
wife Sherry. This book is said to provide profound insights into tantaliz­
ing questions such as: what are UFOs and how will their existence affect
our own� in the new millennium? We can't really knock this book, because
page 275 (which the authors kindly marked for us) contains your "Smear"
editor's quoted opinions about the philosophical aspects of the UFO mystery.
      Finally, we have "Alien Identities" by Richard Thompson, with a fore­
word by famed author Whitley Strieber. Says psychiatrist & psychic    re­
searcher Berthold Schwarz, on the back cover: "Stimulating, well organized,
and an encyclopedic smorgasbord of UFO data and ancient Indian counterparts�
- whatever that means. Schwarz goes on to say that "Alien Identities"
stands to become a classicG  Enough said! ?
