 Gary McKinnon



Lawyers said he could be jailed for up to 70 years –
including the prospect of being sent to Guantanamo
Bay as a terrorist suspect – if convicted in the US of
sabotaging vital defence systems.[>

[>

He has admitted accessing 97 US military and Nasa
computers but has never been charged in Britain and
said he hacked into the Pentagon looking for
information about UFOs.[>

[>




The House of Lords rejected a plea by unemployed
McKinnon, 42, from Enfield, north London, to quash an
extradition request granted to US authorities in 2006.[>

[>

His legal team vowed to carry on the fight with an
appeal to the European Court.[>

From the bedroom of a house belonging to his
girlfriend’s aunt, he used a home computer via the
internet to wander around the networks of a wide
number of US military institutions. [>

[>

But he has maintained he was motivated by curiosity
and that he managed to get into the networks only
because of lax security.[>

[>

US authorities claim he stole 950 passwords and
deleted files at Earle naval station in New Jersey which
is responsible for replenishing arms for the Atlantic
fleet.[>

[>

He was also charged with hacking into 16 Nasa
computers and one defence department system.[>

[>

A statement by solicitors for McKinnon, who was not at
the House of Lords, said: “Gary McKinnon is neither a
terrorist nor a terrorist sympathiser.[>

[>

“His case could have been properly dealt with by our
own prosecuting authorities. Instead, we believe that
the British Government declined to prosecute him to
enable the US government to make an example of him.
