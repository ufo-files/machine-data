 MORE ON DR. JOHN MACK, THE UFO ABDUCTION GURU:

        In our last issue we briefly mentioned an article about John Mack that
 appeared in the April 2 5th issue of Time Magazine. This article,   entitled
 "The Man from Outer Space" is subtitled "Harvard psychiatrist John     Mack
 claims that tales of UFO abductions are real. But experts and former pa­
 tients say his research is shoddy". Together with this heading is a delib­
 erately goofy-looking photograph of Mack.
        The most devastating part of the article is the testimony of    one
 Do�na Bassett, a professional writer who infiltrated Mack's inner circle
 with a fake abduction story and with the deliberate intent of exposing him
 to ridicule. Her most amusing account is of being on board a spaceship with
 John Kennedy and Nikita Khrushchev during the Cuban missile crisis in 1962
 (?) , and of comforting Khrushchev by sitting on his lap. Mack was sitting
 on a bed in a darkened room in his house, and when he heard the above story,
 he "became so excited that he leaned on the bed too heavily, and it col­
 lapsed". Apparently Mack believed the yarn, though come to think     of it,
 Donna Bassett is only 37 years old now, and would have been a child at the
 time of the Cuban missile crisis. Of coursep that's hardly the only flaw
 in the story!
        Our interest in all this heightened when we learned that Donna's hus­
 band E d, another professional writer, had once worked for "Aviation Week"
 in Washington, D.C., and therefore must have known arch-skeptic Phil Klass,
 who was an editor there for many years. We became obsessed with the notion
 that Klass must have put the Bassetts up to their expose of Mack; but sadly,
 it turns out that this was not the case. However, Klass is in touch with
 them now, trying to steer their intended book into CSICOP'S publishing wing,
 Prometheus Press.
        After the Bassetts broke with Mack, they received threats from   the
 "Mackies" (Mack's hard core of abductees) , and recently moved from one ad­
 dress to another, both within North Carolina. "Smear" went to a great deal
 of trouble to locate them, and finally had hour-long phone conversations
 with both E d and Donna. (As it turns out, we could simply have asked Phil
 Klass for their phone numberp now that he is in touch with them.)
        We learned little from the Bassetts that was not already clear from
 the Time article. In essence, the Bassetts have serious legal and moral ob­
 jections to Mack's methods as well as his conclusions. Incidentally,    Mack
 runs a tax-exempt research organization which recently received a $2 00, 000
 grant from the Human Potential Foundation, headed by C.B. Scott Jones,     a
                                    ( 2)
shadowy operative in the UFO scene. Jones' organization is funded at least
in part by Laurence Rockefeller of the famous Rockefeller clan. Laurence
is a man now in his 80s and deeply into New Age interests. Some say that
he is Over the E dge.
      The Bassetts were very cooperative with us, as was Diana DiSimone, head
of MUFON for the state of Massachusetts. Diana does not much like the Bas­
setts, nor does she approve of Mack, either. In a written statement     for
"Smear", Diana states in part: "The sting operation succeeded though it
wasn't necessary. A legitimate piece of investigative journalism surely
could have produced a warranted yet constructive criticism without the use
of deception. E ven though the expose presented some valid argumentsf   it
also revealed more about being mean-spirited than it did about why research­
ers as well as experiencers are caught up in a fantasy world....The hoax­
ter's approach was destructive as well as derisive, linking ufology with the
abduction phenomenon well ahead of any evidence connecting them, and dis­
missing both as a cult..."
      Another s-ource familiar with the Mack story writes: "Certainly, even
for those who believe in alien abductions, Mack is someone to watch out for.
He's not professional, he doesn't do follow-up research, he treats his cli­
ents with indifference, and all of his clients seem to walk into his office
confused and needy, and walk out infused with millennial New Age self-con­
fidence and political and cultural views identical with Mack's. This     is
what I keep trying to tell my abductee friends who worship Mack just because
he has a Harvard professorship. Avoid any UFO investigator who has a po­
litical agenda and who is more interested in New Age religion than in sci­
ence. That means Mack. Jerry Clark is right. It's the Space Brothers all
over again...And why is it that only Mack's abductees have these spontaneous
past-life regressions all the time? And why is it that these past lives are
all of the cliched kind, like E gyptian hieroglyphic painters and American
Indian warriors? I mean, come on! ..."
      We will have more to say about Dr. John Mack in future issues, as Time
and Space permit.. .   .

astounded to find out that there really is a Col. Michael Aquino, who wrote

      The May 1994 issue of the Mutual UFO Network UFO Journal is an un­
usually interesting one.  In fact, for better or worse, ·this esteemed zine
is starting to read more & more like "Saucer Smear"! The lead story in this
issue is an expose of the famed Canadian "Guardian" case, or "Guardian Ca­
per", as the Journal calls it. This complex case involves,    among other
things, a videotaped nightime scene of a landed UFO, complete with      alien.
According to this Journal article ( part one of a two-part series) , the whole
affair is a hoax by one of its principal investigators, Robert Oechsler
who, incidentally, is now threatening to sue. Stay tuned!
      Also in the May issue is an article about UFOs that are ( gasp!) bigger
on the inside than on the outside. This is a mind-boggling problem that has
supposedly shown up in a number of cases.
      Another item concerns the outcome of the voting for the MUFON 1994
Annual Award. Originally, a volunteer postcard vote was taken, resulting
in a return of only 3%. The first vote was then canceled, and a second
vote resulted in a much larger return, with ( ugh!) Budd Hopkins as the win­
ner. Rumor has it that the secret reason the first vote was canceled was
that Pat Marcutillio won it, by high-powered pressure tactics and campaign­
ing. Walt Andrus denies this rumor.

      Armand Laprade, editor of "Would You Believe?", has had a serious
stroke and is not likely to ever be able to continue publishing his zine.
"Would You Believe?" had only a small circulation, and was devoted largely
to reprints of articles, etc., from the ufological past, going all the way
back to the 1950s at times. We never met Laprade, but corresponded    with
him regularly, and sent him much material. We hope he can recover!...
      On the weekend of May 2 1st, your editor attended another of Tim Beck­
ley's New Age oriented UFO conventions in San Diego, California. Beckley
sponsors annual conventions in San Diego and Phoenix, Arizona, and seems un­
willing to try new cities, though his paid attendance generally gets smaller
every yearo  This time there were at least 40 speakers, including your edi­
tor, but not more than about 100 people were present in the lecture hall at
any one time. In fact, for our "workshop" ( in a side room) , discussing our
40 years of ufology, only ONE paid person was present! Lea Boldt, the metal
bending lady discussed in our last "Smear", was in San Diego as a speaker,

and she beat us out by having TWO paid attendees at her "workshop". Others,
like Vance Davis of the famed "Gulf Breeze Six", had as many as fifty. But
over all, this convention scene was just another dreary instance of "preach­
ing to the converted", and even Beckley himself was turned off....
      On the way to California, your editor stopped off in Clarksburg, West
Virginia, where our late friend Gray Barker lived for most of his adult life.
A film maker named Ralph Coon was there to do a documentary on Barker's life,
and we were grateful for the opportunity to take part in it. The document­
ary consisted mainly of interviews with Barker's family and with those few
local people who knew him. In our segment we told of his wonderfully cyn­
ical sense of humor, his sense of "wonderment" ( but not belief) in the fly­
ing saucer subject, and the tragedy of his death in 1984 - which almost sure­
ly was from AIDS. In short, we simply told it as it is - or was....

or possibly a nuclear detection balloon, which can pick up the atmospheric
shock waves from a nuclear explosion thousands of miles away. In conversa­
tion with your editor, Peebles notes the fact that the alien bodies      were
found intact rather than hideously damaged as would be more normal in a
crash. This fact casts suspicion on the account of dead aliens at the crash
site, though it does not prove anything one way or the other.

1947 was lost and never recovered - at least not by White Sands personnel."
