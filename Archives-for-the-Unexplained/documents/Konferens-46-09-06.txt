Meeting protocol, September 6th, 1946
Subject: projectiles intruding on our borders.

Present parties:

From FF:

Colonel Jacobson, Chairman.
Fld Kjellson
Chief of Bureau Norlin
Fld Norén
Fli Malmberg

From Fst:

Maj Ahlgren

From KMF:

KK Oxenstierna

From FOA:

Professor Ljunggren
Ingenjör Rynninger

From FRA:

Bureau Director Kempe

1.

As of August 30th, only 12 reports regarding projectiles have been submitted to Försvarsstaben, proving that public interest in the matter is dying out rapidly. Most reports involved eyewitness data regarding occurrences of light phenomena.

Although Försvarsstaben has examined the alleged impact on Bräcke and the report of Uddevalla in great detail, no new information has been uncovered.

A compilation of four reports from Östersund shows that the object possibly had a westerly course and a speed of about 450 meters per second.

The Supreme Commander has issued a number of highly specific rules in regards to the air surveillance during the exercise.

The military attaché in Helsinki has announced that the use of previous statistics regarding the observations has now been banned.

As of now, there are no new rocket launchings taking place from Porkala.

2.

All quiet at the FOA. At the next meeting, Professor Ljunggren fully intends to submit a final report on the investigations made so far.

3.

A minesweeper has been prepared to FRA's disposal for a week, from September 10th.

4.

Colonel Jacobson announced his recent meeting with famous journalist Mr. Goulding, claiming to have received a lot of interesting information (soon to be communicated to the representative of the Interior Department).

It should be examined whether it is possible to discuss these overflights at UN level. Maj Ahlgren will sort this issue out along with the Supreme Commander.

According to a recent and very persistent rumour, a rocket would have been lost across our borders during August 24th. However, no such events were in fact reported during this date.

5.

The next meeting will be held on Monday, September 23rd, at 4:00 PM without special notice.

Stockholm, September 6th, 1946

E. Malmberg
