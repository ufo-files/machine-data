                               POLITICIANS AND UFOs


        Sometime in the fifties,                 writer Desmond Leslie asked George
Ward, then British Secretary of State for Air, about a UFO they
had both seen; his reply is on record, and refreshingly frank:
"What am I to say? I know it wasn't a balloon. You know it wasn't
a balloon. But until I get a saucer on the ground in Hyde Park
and    can   charge      the   public     sixpence            a   go    to   enter,    it    must    be
balloons,         otherwise     the    government          would       fall and I       would lose
my job".  The government of the United States has a similar
fondness for balloons.  In  1947 there was the famous Roswell
weather balloon, replaced in 1995 by a super spy balloon that is
equally hard to credit.               "If they lied in                 '47," say folks,         "then
why should we believe              them now?"

        Naive      ufologists,        failing        to understand            the institutional
nature    of      government      mendacity,          look        in   vain   for     the    security
leak    that will        enrich    themselves and their publishers                           and find
instead      only       cleverly      presented          disinformation.              They    do    not
understand what Daniel Ellsberg told the US Senate Committees on
the    Judiciary        and   Government Operations in May                      1973 - that the
operation         of government        security positively                   demands that those
with high security clearances lie about their knowledge. We print
extracts from Ellsberg's testimony in this issue.      It repays
careful study,          for it shows that what is to be maintained at any
cost is the exercise of power,                   and public confidence in those who
wield it.         It is arguable that this is necessary and inevitable.


       If UFOs represent an alien power,                          beyond the control of the
rulers       of    terrestrial        nations,           then      to    that    extent        public
confidence         is   undermined      by       their     mere        existence,      so    perhaps
governments should keep the knowledge of that alien power secret?
Maybe they should do a deal with it -perhaps they could do both.
Perhaps  they  have  done  both. It  is  easy  to  de�and  that
governments speak out on this topic - yet difficult to foresee
the possible consequences, so maybe the aliens themselves will
soon resolve the dilemma by landing on the White House lawn in
the traditional science fiction fashion! Some people think such
a revelation may really happen.                      We'll        wait and     see!

                  JACQUES VALLEE ISSUES WARNING TO UFO RESEARCHERS



          As reported in the Nov/Dec issue of UFO Magazine, famed UFO researcher
    Dr. Jacques Vallee pointedly warned of two dangers confronting UFO researchers
    when he spoke at the Omega UFO Conference in North Haven, Connecticut in early
    October. The first danger comes from government abuse of UFO information and
    possible manipulation of public beliefs. The second danger, Vallee said, is
    that UFO research itself seems to be "falling back into its infancy."
          Regarding UFO information in government hands, Vallee said:
    "Clearly the major governments have data. Tons of it. My tentative conclusion
    is that they don't know what to do with that data. If they are covering up
    something, it is primarily their own ignorance. But there is more. They are
    lying about the fact that some of their covert services use the phenomenon
    to manipulate public opinion and to obscure their own craven feats..."
            Public belief in UFOs, Vallee continued, "could be used to disguise
    tests of new weapons platforms and could serve as a shield for mind control
    experiments involving the use of drugs on unsuspecting patients." The
    reference to mind control was apparently          in connection with claimed
    experiences of alien abduction. Vallee is outspokenly critical of researchers
    who regard abduction - especially as reported under hypnosis - as literal
    alien contact, but he does not dismiss all abduction accounts as pure fantasy.
    Instead, he believes that at least some abductions could be the result of
    government-sponsored experimentation.
            Regarding the current state of UFO research, Vallee first called to mind
    a co/QIQent made by the late Dr.J.Allen Hynek, with whom Vallee worked for many
    years. In a private conversation, Vallee says Hynek told him, "Nobody will
    ever give you or me a dime to do serious research on UFOs. But if we were to
    cross the line of professional ethics and went back on stage pretending we had
    the answer with a capital A, and told these people Ufology was the cure to all
    their spiritual anxieties, we could raise a million dollars today."
           Vallee then COJQIQented, "In the last few years that fine ethical line has
    been crossed again and again by many people claiming to provide both spiritual
    and scientific answers...I'm an active researcher, and I've become a sorrowful
    bystander in this field. My impression as I watch this festival of absurdities
    from the sidelines is one of unceasing wonder with a touch of sadness. The
    wonder comes from the realization that even in our supposed age of technology
    there is so little critical examination of the facts ... There is no credible
    ongoing effort scientifically to come to grips with the underlying phenomenon,
    which I continue to believe is real and informed ...All we have left is a few
    groups of believers standing on mountaintops with flashlights, expecting the
    aliens to come down and shake their hands. In that sense, the UFO research is
    falling back into its own infancy, into a folklore that doesn't even have the
    odd charm of the contactees of the 1950s."
          However, Vallee ended his speech on a note of hope: "I continue to hope
    that someday we will be able to sort out the signal from the noise and get to
    work on the real UFO phenomenon. If I do feel one major responsibility, it is
    to transmit the data to posterity so that future scientists will be able to
    make sense of what seems to us to be hopelessly dank. If we can resist the
    temptation to jump to hasty conclusions, we may emerge intellectually better
    and spiritually stronger into the 21st century. "
