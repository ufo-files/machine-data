Case xxx.
A meteor over a navy vessel

Time: Sunday, June 9, at between 00.30 and 01.30.
Place: Sandhamn in the Stockholm archipelago. 

A civil employee at the Stockholm navy yard wrote the following report:

”Report on light phenomenon in on account of Fst Avd L, Nr 7:49, on June 12, 1946. To the Defence Staff, department L.  Herewith I humbly report that on the 9th I observed a light phenomenon under the following circumstances. 

”Place of the observer: on board the [Navy] picket-boat Regulus moored to the club house pier at Sandhamn. Time: Sunday the 9th of June at 00.30 - 01.30. Direction: At sea off Sandhamn in the direction of Revengegrundets light-buoy. Degree of altitude: 2 
-6°.  Character and movements of the light: Steady white light moving in a slight curve fron the right to the left (from south to north) and at the same time descending. Duration about 3 seconds from discovery, wherefore other persons present, who were notified about the light, did not have the time to perceive it. The light had a very great likeness to a ”starfall” but was very powerful and moved at a slower rate of speed. The night was so bright that ordinary falling stars would not be possible to observe. First impression was that someone had signaled with a rocket [distress rocket] at sea, but the horisontal movement and the beautiful weather made this unlikely.  Stockholm, June 20. /signed/ Pontus Melin, civil engineer, employed by the Stockholm naval yard, telephone ”Marindistriktet” 306.”

Status: Probable meteor
Source: Report on June 20 [Defence Staff files]
