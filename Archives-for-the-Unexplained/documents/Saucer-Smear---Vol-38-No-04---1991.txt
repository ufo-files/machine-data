       When one writes a skeptical, highly independent & irreverent zine
 like "Smear ", one inevitably makes enemies - sometimes deliberately, some­
 times unintentionally. There are those who have called us the "National
 Enquirer " of ufology. Then there are those who just don't talk to us at
 all!
       A few weeks back, we had a Phone Problem with famed UFO researcher
 John Keel, a man we continue to respect and admire in many ways. We had
 published his Fugo Balloon theory, and various readers then proceeded to
 shoot it down (notably Bill Moore and Jerry Clark) in ·their own zines. In
 the course of the above-mentioned phone conversation, Keel remarked that
 everyone in the UFO field is crazy. We replied that this must mean he is
 crazy too. This set Keel into a frenzy, and before hanging up he screamed
 that he is not in the UFO field and never has been. This seems odd, since
 he currently is-president of the New York Fortean Society (composed mostly
 of ufologists) and writes a regular column for FATE Magazine!
       The case of Phil Klass is much more complex. Some time in June (?)
 of last year, Klass phoned us - obviously very annoyed - and warned     us
 specifically against libeling him in the next issue of "Smear ". He never
 explained what libel he was anticipating, but our best guess is that some­
 one informed him (quite erroneously) that we intended to name him as     the
 person who "planted" the UFO model in Ed Walters' former home.   To    this
 very day we do not know whether the model was "planted " or not, but if it
 was, Klass would be the last person we would suspect - for various reasons.
       We were annoyed by Klass' threat, and it gave rise to our highly
 negative remarks about him on Page 4 and the top of Page 5 in Vol. 37, No.
 5 of "Smear " - an issue which we somehow neglected to date, but    which
 should have been dated July lOth, 1990.
       Anyhow - we recently phoned Klass, the above subject came up, Klass
 denied his earlier threat, became enraged, and started taping the conver­
 sation - claiming that he had never taped us before (as if we care! ) , but
 will always do so in the future. The other subject bothering Klass was
 our intention to keep after Al Seckel, the leading light of the Southern
 California Skeptics, a supposedly independent branch of CSICOP, with no
 legal connection at all with the parent organization.
       In view of the fact that CSICOP has been named in multi-million-dol­
 lar lawsuits by Uri Geller and Dr. Eldon Byrd against, primarily,     James
 Randi, it is understandable that CSICOP would be anxious to put a legal
 distance between itself as an organization and the actions of individual
 members and/or local affiliates. But Klass, apparently speaking for CSI­
 COP at the same time as claiming he is not an official CSICOP    spokesman,
 made it crystal clear that we may well be sued if we pursue the Seckel mat-

ter written on Nov. 22nd, 1990 by researcher Robert Todd to lawyer Peter Ger­
sten, who has stated publicly (and in "Smear ") that he is on the trail    of
proof that the Roswell Incident did not involve an interplanetary craft. What
we have here is a formerly secret pro ject of the Army Air Force called PRO­
JECT MOGUL, which involved the use of high-altitude balloons for complex re­
search purposes. Todd's letter to Gersten, which contained several enclos­
ures we do not have, was merely a preliminary report on his research,     and
since it was written several months ago, there may well be an update which
we also do not have.
      Two fascinating things emerge, however: There was a launch on July
Jrd, 1947, almost the exact date of the Roswell Incident, involving "a ten­
balloon cluster launched by the New York University team; payload less than
fifty pounds; peak altitude 18, 500 feet ". This launch was made from Hollo­
man Air Force Base, New Mexico.

with whom Bill Moore has interacted in recent years! We have �eglected to
