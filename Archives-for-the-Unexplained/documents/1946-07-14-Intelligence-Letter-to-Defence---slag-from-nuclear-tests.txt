July 14: Slag from the nuclear tests? 

This letter was found in the FOA archive files: 

”Skoghall, July 14, 1946. To the Defence Research Institute. The undersigned would like to call your attention to the so called ”light bombs” which these days have been so commonly observed in our country. I, for my part, would like to call the Institute´s attention to [the possibility] that it could be residue from the ”atom bomb test” at Bikini on July 1. A fact is that, after the bombing of Nagasaki in Japan, last year, a great fall of ashes and slag started several hundred [Swedish] miles [one Swedish mile = 10 km] from there, fourteen (14) days later. Those slag pieces that now fall, about the same time after Bikini, might seem very theoretical. We know that the whole world is investigating the effects of ”the atom bomb”. Imagine if Sweden could be the first country which came [out] with the first report on the same! Sincerely, Ingvar Lindén, Lunden 46 C, Skoghall.”

The letter was taken seriously at FOA. Their head, professor Ljunggren wrote, July 25, this answer: 

”The Defence Institute herewith acknowledge receipt of your letter of July 14 and will report that the questions covered in your letter at this moment is being investigated. Stockholm, July 25, 1946. G.Ljunggren, Professor.”

Source: FOA. Handlingar 1946, Fia-85-8511. Diarium C1, 1946. [War Archives]
