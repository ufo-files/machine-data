Case xxx. 
Did a ghost rocket light the fire at the Svartvik factory?

Time: July 26
Place: Svartvik, about 10 km south of Sundsvall.

On the night of July 26 a huge store of timber belonging to the Svartvik sulphite paper mill caught fire. Pulpwood for two months of production was destroyed.  

”GHOST ROCKET CAUSED THE SVARTVIK FIRE? The cause of the fire investigated by the police. No result.  Was the great Svartvik fire caused by a ghost rocket; that question is asked in Njurunda [place of the slag finds on July 9, case xxx]. The cause of the million fire is being investigated by the police and the ghost bomb theory has been advanced, even from police sources, but so far the great fire, which started violently in the night, has not received a final explanation.

”The many ”aerial phenomena”, rocket bombs or whatever they may be, which have appeared lately, have given rise to wonder and anxiety. The order from the military authorities to the witnesses of the phenomena, not to divulge the places or crash-sites, has not contibuted to calm the anxiety. 

”It is clear that one cannot avoid to put certain strange fires and other things in connection with these mysterious, fireglistening objects. The numerous ”ghost rockets” over certain parts of Norrland do not seem to have gone by unnoticed. Thus it is claimed, according to what Sundsvalls Tidning reported the other day, that a violent fire, which happened in an old unused hen-house somewhere in Norrland, is a work of a ”ghost rocket”. The electrical wiring of the hen-house were dead and no burnable or easily inflammable goods were kept in the house. 

”Having taken part of this report it is difficult to free oneself from the thought that even other fires, in recent times, could have come about in the same way. 

”- We have, so far, not found any reason for the Svartvik fire, says head of the fire-brigade Ebbe Carlsson from Sundsvall. The only thing known today is that the fire started in the machine room. That a ghost rocket would have caused the fire catastrophe I think is less probable, even though such [phenomena] have been seen in our area in recent times. 

”Neither chief constable T. Gedda from Njurunda can, today, give any answer concerning the cause of the fire. Of course, in view of the violent outbreak of the fire, the ghost bomb theory cannot be excluded, he says, but then comes the fact that noone has reported seeing any light phenomenon at the time of the fire. Police investigation continues and we hope that light will be shed on the case.” (Sundsvalls Tidning, July 31). 

But TT, the Swedish newswire agency, was more cautioned in their commentary, the same day (July 31): 

”During the investigation of the fire at the Svartvik sulphite factory pulpwood store, when a million cubic feet of pulpwood was destroyed, the cause of the fire has not been ascertained, but a theory that the fire might have been caused by a ”ghost rocket crash” has no credibility with the police. There is no proof at all for this theory. People interviewed have not seen anything pointing to a ”ghost bomb”. On the contrary one has seen the fire start close to the machine room of one of the hauling tracks out on the timber islet. Chief constable Gedda brings to attention that for a long time, before the fire, it was very hot, and a spark which usually does not ignite a fire can [then] have fatal consequences. We have seen this by the many fires along railway embarkments this summer. The state police is in care of the continued investigation, and, as common with larger fires, the Institute of Criminology Research [Kriminaltekniska anstalten] will, if needed, perform some tests. One must investigate shafts and bearings of the machinery at the place where the fire is believed to have started.” (TT newswire nr 20, July 31). 

By August 16 the investigation was over: 

”OVER-HEATING THOUGHT TO HAVE CAUSED THE SVARTVIK FIRE. The Institute of Criminology Research, which has investigated the great fire at Svartvik on July 26 has, in a now official statement, comes to the conclusion that the fire could have been caused by over-heating of a bearing in the transmission of the engine in the machine-house...but this cannot be established with complete certainty...”
(abbreviated from Sundsvalls Tidning, August 16). 

At about the same time as the Svartvik fire, another paper mill (Katrinefors in Mariestad) was partly burnt to the ground, damages estimated to be in the region of 2-3 million kronor. Noone accused ”the ghost rockets” of that fire. The theory can largely be attributed to the paper Sundsvalls Tidning, which had initially published - and played a great role in - the Njurunda crash [of July 9] story. Njurunda is only about 10 kilometers to the south of Svartvik.
