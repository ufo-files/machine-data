Project 1946:
The Ghost Rocket Wave 
January 1946

Strange luminous clouds in the morning and evening skies of January the 4th started this, the most ”phenomenal” year in Swedish UFO history. The snow-covered landscapes turned red and green. A bright fire-ball passed over the Swedish county of Dalarna on January 17, and there were other strange phenomena in the skies.

Swedish defence completed their technical analysis of the wreckage of a V-2 bomb that had crashed in south Sweden on June 13, 1944. 


Case 1.
Luminous morning clouds frightened horses and dogs

Time: January 4, in the morning
Place: Many places in South Sweden

A newswire from TT (Tidningarnas Telegrambyrå), the Swedish Newspaper Agency. published by many Swedish newspapers on January 8:

”On January 4, at 7.50 and 8.15 hours [in the morning] a number of fully reliable witnesses in Lund and surrounding areas, sighted, according to what professor Knut Lundmark [a wellknown Swedish astronomer, 1889-1958] has told [the newspaper] Arbetet, a light phenomenon in the sky which is difficult to explain. It seemed like a luminous zone from the south to the north and it had the strongest luminousity at the zenith. At Revingehed [a military training field] the army watch-dogs crawled into their kennels when the peculiar luminousity began. One horse, hitched to a wagon, lowered his head towards his legs and remained in that position as long as the light-phenomenon lasted.”

Weather observer Andersson in Hörby noted in the log book: 

”On the 4th between 7.50 and 8.10 a luminous phenomenon was seen over almost the whole sky. It was almost dark-red in colour and strongest at the zenith. The sky was clouded and the clouds were at a low altitude. The animals shied at the sight of the phenomenon, the dogs even crawled into their kennels. All electric lights indoors took on a greenish hue.”

Status: Luminous clouds.
Source: Several Swedish newspapers (such as Sundsvalls Tidning and Falu-Kuriren), January 8, 1946. Notes from the SMHI (Swedish weather bureau) yearbook copied by ufologist Sven Schalin. 

Case 2.
Dark-red morning clouds at Uppsala

Time: January 4, 08.30 in the morning
Place: Uppsala, north of Stockholm

A weather observer at the Meteorological Institution at Uppsala wrote in his diary: ”On the 4 th, at 08.30, a light phenomenon was seen. Air, snow and house-facings turned dark purple, the clouds were dark-red.”

Status: Luminous clouds.
Source: SMHI yearbook, notes from the yearbook copied by ufologist Sven Schalin.

Case 3. 
Luminous evening clouds in the Stockholm area

Time: January 4, evening hours
Place: Stockholm area

On January 9, yet another TT newswire published by many newspapers:

”The strange luminous phenomenon, sighted in Skåne [the southernmost province] a few days ago, was also sighted in Stockholm, however this time in the evening of the same day. Professor [Bertil] Lindblad [astronomer, 1895-1965] of the Stockholm observatory [at Saltsjöbaden] has explained to [the newspaper] Expressen, that there is no other probable explanation than that it was a zone of high-altitude clouds lighted by the sun, shortly before sunrise [this comment refers to the south-Swedish phenomenon in the morning]. Such strongly luminous clouds can sometimes create very distinctive light effects. What happened here in the Stockholm area was something similar, but in the evening, shortly after sunset. Many saw it from Saltsjöbaden and there even the altitude of the clouds was measured.”
  
Status: Luminous clouds.
Source: Several Swedish newspapers (such as Smålands Dagblad), January 9. 


January 4: First remote-controlled plane

The Stockholms-Tidningen, reported, from New York [January 4]  that instruments for remote-control of aircraft had now been constructed by the Bell Aircraft Co. The remote-controlled plane is equipped with a television transmitter whereby the controller, on ground, or in another airplane, can follow what is happening with, and inside, the plane, He can also see ”how the line of the horizon looks from the cockpit”. The new method of control is ”expected to save many lifes of test pilots”.

xxx-F: ”A witness in the area south of Rommehed saw the phenomenon at exactly the same time as the first reporter, that is two minutes past six... These persons are, however, sure of seeing that the meteor died out before reached the ground.  - This report seems rather stange, says teacher H. Frendin. January 18: A meteor over France?

”An American C-54 transport plane was 7.000 feet over the French countryside on its way to Paris. Some 3.000 feet below the plane was a layer of broken clouds, but above that the sky was clear. It was at 11 o´clock at night on January 18, 1946. The flight was uneventful until suddenly the pilot of the C-54 saw a brilliant ”shooting star” appear 35 degrees above the horizon. The ”shooting star” streaked downward without exploding. The brilliant blob dropped below the eastern hoizon, but then, much to the pilot´s surprisem the ”shooting star” quickly reappeared! The ”shooting star” came back into view and: ”...described a tiny hyperbola of perhaps one degree altitude and fell again from sight.” This were the words of the pilot who felt that the ”shooting star” did the impossible by ricocheting or rebounding. This event marked the beginning of numerous sightings of strange phenomena in the skies of Europe.”

Source: Loren Gross: UFOs: a history. 1946: the ghost rockets, page 1.


Case xxx
Meteor or contrail over Småland

Time: January 23, 15.00 hours (afternoon)
Place: Northern parts of the county of Småland

”A mighty light phenomenon was observed at about 15.00 hours, on the Wednesday, over the northern parts of [the county of] Småland, for instance at Eksjö and Mariannelund. At very great altitude a streak of light, almost like a comet, moved in an east-westerly direction. The light, which could be seen for 12 minutes, was reportedly seen, at about the same time, at Rydsnäs. Eyewitnesses guess that what was seen was a meteor. It is not impossible, however, that it was an airplane, exhaust-gases from which transformed into ice-crystals, giving the illusion of a streak of light.”

Note: Seeing a meteor for 12 minutes would not be possible but seeing the (luminous) vapour trail from a meteor for such a time would be possible.

Status: Probable meteor or contrail
Source: Smålands Dagblad, January 24.


January 25: The extradition 
to Russia of the Balts

On January 25 - a much regretted day in Swedish history - the Russian vessel Beloostrov left the harbour of Trelleborg, on the southern tip of Skåne, with 145 Balts (mostly Latvians) and 234 German ex-soldiers on board, bound for extradition to Russia. One Balt committed suicide on the quay. A divided and hesitant Swedish ministry had finally decided, on a majority vote, to follow the word from the great red power in the East. Even the Swedish king, Gustaf V, had asked Stalin to leave the Balts aside but with no luck.

Sketch of a B-18 bomber 











Eight Swedish air force B-18 bombers, plus one B-3 transport plane, were flying on a training mission from Västerås to Kalix, when they got caught in a powerful snowstorm and all nine planes had to force-land within a small area near Härnösand. One of the planes landed in the harbour of Härnösand and sank through the ice, only to be recovered 33 years later, restored and is now part of the historical collection of the Air Force museum at Linköping. Another of the B-18 bombers (air force serial number 18180) was last heard of at 1410 hours reporting on long wave frequency that  they were lost with only 50 litres of petrol remaining. The plane, and crew of three people, was never heard of and has never been found. 

Status: Unidentified

Case xxx.
The February 17 fireball

Time: Sunday, February 17, about 23.30 [February 18, about 00.30 for the sightings in Finland]
Places: mainly county of Västerbotten and the western part of Finland.

A powerful meteor - or several meteoric stones - lit the skies of the coastland of  Norrland on Thursday, February 17. AFU has gathered about twenty reports from newspaper sources, which is probably only a sampling. For reasons of space each report has been shortened to the most essential details: 

xxx-A: Powerful light over the whole coastland of Västerbotten, mostly in the Umeå area. Mrs. Elin Grubbström, wife of the lighthouse-keeper at Bjuröklubb, had gone to bed when the room was completely lit up for about two seconds. Then darkness and another intense light again. (Norra Västerbotten, Febr. 19)

xxx-B: A similar sighting from postman Kalle Ahlevård in Uttersjöbäcken. He had been reading when he noticed the very strong, blue-white light. No sound. (Norra Västerbotten, Febr. 19)

xxx-C: A witness at Hörnsjö got the impression the meteor was at the same altitude as the clouds. The heavenly phenomenon - 
a poem on the meteor

The man in the moon rises his golden head
behind the snowcovered tops of the great wood.
But he, on his nightly passage, does not delay
by those, which he meets on the starsprangled sky.
He’s not tempted away by the brilliant bridesmaids
and does not go astray ‘mong the stars of heaven 
and although he often his back on us turn, 
secure he walks his way what may ever happen

But suddenly in the sky a fireball was glistening, 
obscuring the shine of the moon and the stars.
It went like a Jehu and then it lowered
to earth, where the snow cover hid every stone
The fireball was then heard to explode, 
so that the cabins were shaking. Then it was no more. 
What was it? one wondered. Was it an omen, 
a bomb, a comet, or the final judgement?

But the calm of the man in the moon was not rocked
since much he has seen in his wandering. 
”Say, who are you to run in this way,
not knowing the rules of traffic or having no sense
to show both esteem and regard towards others,
who also shall travel ‘cross the sky?
Yes, you should be ashame, you madman small! 
Go home to your mother, who nurtured you badly!”

In Stöcksjö and Lycksele and Rödåliden
in Flarken and Ulvoberg, Kroksjö and Ström, 
one looked at the watch, noting the time
and wondered slightly, if this was not a dream.
And men were almost blinded by the lustre, 
whenever the meteor was seen, wavering its tail.
But the deck of cards was hidden, smarting the soul,
by judgement, anyone wants to be the honest one.

		Gärdsmygen [The wren]

Source: Västerbottens-Kuriren, February 26, 1946.

March 


March 5: In a classic speach in Fulton, Missouri, Winston Churchill warns ”the free world” of ”the Red tyranny”. Only the anglo-saxon nations can save the world. A psychological war against socialism had started. ”The Cold War” is a new expression coined.   

A proposition to the Swedish parliament asks that Sweden join the newly formed United Nations. The proposition is passed on June 27.  

Russia decides, in March, that their troups shall immediately leave Bornholm, the Baltic island. As peace was declared in Europe, in May 1945, Russian bombs fell on Bornholm, the start of a 10 month long occupation.


March 13: Operation Paperclip

”On 13 March 1946 a new name came into being: Operation Paperclip. It was decided to offer.. German scientists and inventors very favourable long-term contracts which would allow them to bring their families to America and eventually to become citizens... The team of 127 engineers and inventors from Peenemunde, grouped around von Braun, stood out among them. They had been together for a long time, and had experiences and achievements in common. The V-2 rockets were their creation. Now they were to work together on developing and perfecting a missile for outer space.” (From: Josef Garlinski: ”Hitler´s last weapons. The underground war against the V1 and V2”, 1978)


March 18: V-2 rumours from behind the (new) ”Iron Curtain”

A TT newswire, with the [British] Daily Mail as the original source, distributed on March 19 helped set the scene for what was to occur in the spring and summer of 1946:

”SECRET V-2 EXPERIMENTS. London, March 18 (NTB from Reuters). German scientists and technicians who work under Russian supervision will shortly release a number of V2-bombs from a secret research stations on the Baltic, reports the Daily Mirror Berlin correspondent, Edwin Tetlow. The Soviet Union follows the example of the British who fired V2-bombs from Cuxhaven in October last. 

”The bombs that are to be used for the secret experiments on the Baltic coast, were found almost completed by Russian troups in a German factory in Mecklenburg, about 95 kilometres northwest of Berlin. They have later been completed into fullgrown V2-bombs by scientists who worked on them for Hitler during the war.”

”Other messages from the east, the correspondent continues, confirm that the Soviet-Russians systematically seek to find out everything the Germans know about the nuclear bomb, V-weapons and other ultra modern war innovations. Physicists who helped by planning of the German cyclotrones , huge magnetic machines used for nuclear fission are among the experts now working for the Russians.”

Source: NTB newswire published March 18 by Aftenposten and Dagbladet. TT newswire published by Sydöstra Sveriges Dagblad, Umebladet, Norra Västerbotten and other Swedish papers on March 19.


Case xxx.
Aurora borealis & magnetic problems

Time: March 25, March 28, etc.
Places: all over Sweden

April 19: Wright Field review of ”German aerodynamic studies”

A large number of allied documents on German technical developments during WWII were freely offered, in 1946, to Swedish researchers in the defence industry and defence authorities. One sample of these documents, ”Brief review of German aerodynamic studies”, Air Technical Intelligence Review Report Number F-IR-1-RE is dated 4-19-46, authored by ”Personnel of Air Technical Ingelligence under Command of USSTAF” and distributed by ”Headquarters, Air Material Command, Wright Field, Dayton, Ohio”. [US Classification: Restricted].

The document reports on late August 1945 interviews with a number of German aircraft designers by Dr Francis H. Clauser, Exploitation Division, USSTAF, Douglas Aircraft Co. Clauser first interviewed two young German gentlemen, Mr. Heinemann and Mr. Zacher who have worked in Prien with the Flug-Technische-Fachgruppe, ”a group of engineers, pilots and craftsman that started out as an academic flying group, but during the last year of the war was engaged in the design of a series of flying wings”. A German aerodynamic designer, Dr. Lippisch, had been ”their guiding light”. Lippisch had tested elliptical shaped bodies in wind-tunnel at the end of the war. 













Ideas for triangular-shaped flying wing with elliptical wing surfaces. 

Dr. Clauser, in his skeptical report, refutes some of the young German engineers more wild claims, but also recommends that the group should get Air Force support to test their DM-1, a flying wing glider, ”completely designed and about 95% constructed”, out of a Munich airfield. 

The report also includes interviews with aircraft developers such as professors Georgii, Sänger and Dr. Bredt. Sänger and Bredt had published ”calculations that show that rocket-driven aircraft are capable of travelling more than halfway around the earth”. Dr. Clauser met and had technical discussions with Sänger, Bredt and Georgii in Ainring [near Salzburg, Austria] on August 31, 1945. 

Clauser concludes that ”...a rocket similar to the V-2 but equipped with a reasonable set of wings will have astounding performance regardless of the drag, it it is given a large initial velocity so that the great majority of the fuel is not dissipated ineffectively at low speeds.” 

Source: Report found in Air Administration: The missile weapons bureau. Unregistered documents of historical interest. Series Ö1, volume 9. [War Archives, Stockholm].

Detonations in the southern Baltic

Time: April 23, 24, 25, 26
Place: Counties of Blekinge, Skåne and Kalmar, the southern part of the Baltic, Bornholm (Danish island in the Baltic)

”EARTHQUAKE IN BLEKINGE MADE HOUSES RATTLE. Mysterious phenomenon Wednesday evening centered on the Karlshamn area.  On Wednesday evening a series of rather powerful earthquakes, which at some places made houses tremble, and window-panes rattle so much that one thought they would break, were observed at different places off the coast of Blekinge. The earthquake - which it probably was - was followed by a dull rumble. The phenomenon was most prominent in the area of Karlshamn and on Listerlandet [peninsula where the town Sölvesborg is situated]. The shocks were also noted in Karlskrona, although not as powerful there. From Karlshamn the SSD correspondent reports that a series of shocks occurred between 18.45 and 20.10, whereupon a powerful quake was noticed at 21.30.

”...Railway clerk Albert Magnusson was on his way home at 18.45. He heard a rumble of about the same type as when the navy is using their heavy gunnery at sea. When, at 19.35-19.40, he returned to the Karlshamn railway station, he noticed that a door started to tremble. He thought this was curious since the door is heavy and was locked. This tremble of the door he noticed at two times with a short interval...”

The Sydöstra Sveriges Dagblad (SSD) published about ten other witness reports from Blekinge (places such as Mörrum, Hällaryd, Bräkne-Hoby, Åryd, Sölvesborg, and Hörviken), which are not translated in detail, here. In Karlskrona the first weak quakes were observed at about 17.00 hours. The quake seemed to come from out of the sea [the Baltic]. ”As a peculiar thing it could be noted that on the 28th of April last year [1945] - thus almost one year ago - a similar quake was noted on Listerlandet.” (Sydöstra Sveriges Dagblad, April 25).

The trembles were also noted on the southern coast of Skåne, down to Trelleborg. Trembles and rattling windows were reported near Simrishamn, Ystad and Trelleborg between 19.00 and 20.00. The seismograph at Lund did not, however, register anything like a quake. Window panes were rattling at Rönne and other villages on the Danish island of Bornholm [in the southern Baltic], as they did during the war when fighting was most intense in north-Germany and Poland. (Helsingborgs Dagblad, April 25). 

”Even on Thursday trembles were noted at different times along the coast of Blekinge. From reports it seems the trembles this time were more powerful towards the Ronneby area [more to the west]... A man employed by the Karlshamn electricity board tells us...that he had already on Tuesday...noted quakes, which seemed to come from the sea. These trembles were not as powerful as those on Wednesday evening and Thursday. The trembles on Tuesday reminded him of heavy gunnery at sea... 

”...The SSD correspondent at Asarum had, Thursday, a telephone call from a person at Nötabråne, about 4 kilometres to the north, just as a quake was noted. The person at Nötabråne noted the tremble a little while later [than the SSD correspondent]. From this one could conclude that the tremble came from the south... ”NUCLEAR TESTS OR SETTLING ON THE BOTTOM OF THE BALTIC? The rumble has now moved to the north and has been heard at Kalmar. Stockholm the 26th (HD). Are there Russian nuclear bomb tests going on in the southern Baltic? ”Fishermen and others, who have experience from close contact with the war, state with determination, that, as opposed to the denial from naval authorities,  it is the question of heavy gunnery or bombs. A few fishermen, who have been far from the coast, describe the noise as dull, clearly defined reports. 

”New reports does not bring us closer to a solution, on the contrary. Now the rumble has moved northwards and was heard up in the Kalmar area. This time, too, the phenomenon cause doors to shut and paintings to fall from walls. This has happened in Kalmar.  

”Science keeps to the theory that it is quakes at the bottom of the sea, but to them it is yet strange that the centre of activity has moved so far north. This transfer strengthens the view that, after all, it is detonations caused by ships on the move, as the fishermen think.

”....Swedish military authorities - naval as well as army - declared on Friday afternoon, that the detonations do not come from Swedish exercises or minesweeping... It is also denied by military experts that gunnery at sea could close doors and make paintings fall from a wall. One is mostly bent on identifying the strange happenings as geological phenomena, a hypothesis that has support from science, where it is thought that the dull rumblings come from local settlings in the layers of the earth, not unusual, for instance, in the chalk layers near Bornholm. Settlings of such limited proportions that they do not register on seismographs.” (Helsingborgs Dagblad, April 27).

Time: mid-May, 1946
Place: Ramåsen, Stöde, county of Västernorrland

During 1946, the chemical laboratory of Försvarets forsknings-anstalt [The Research Institute of National Defence], FOA 1, analyzed more than 30 finds of materials believed to have a connection with the phenomena in the sky. ”The specimen is made up of several pieces of a light-grey mass, which, in it, includes withered blades of grass. The gras is tightly packed into the mass. I then looked up [into the sky] and I caught sight of this object. For a very short time, two seconds, did I note the object. What confuses me is that I heard the noise before I could see the object. Maybe something passed over me before I looked up and could see the object?

”Before arriving in Falun I met a friend whom I told about what I had seen. I had thoughts of going to the Falu-Kuriren editorial office and tell them about what I had seen, but this I never did. I was in a hurry to go home, since the next day I was to start a [new] work for the railway company. There I also told [my new work-mates] about what I had seen, but noone said anything. Maybe they thought they had gotten a strange new mate. On the next Saturday I got married. This is what the object looked like. 





Oskar Karlssons teckning.





”On my honour.”

From the questionnaire, signed by Oskar Karlsson on August 9, 1979, the following data can be extracted: Mr. Karlsson was alone to see the phenomenon. He saw the object at about 11.00-12.00 hours on Tuesday, May 21, 1946. It was ”not a cloud in sky, sunshine, no wind at all”. The object was darker than the sky. The witness heard a hissing, like an artíllery....[word unreadable]. The object crossed the sky going from the north to the south, very quickly. To the question, ”What is your personal opinion about the phenomenon?”, Karlsson wrote: ”That it was some experiment from Soviet or the US, like a V2-rocket.”  

In view of the time that has passed between sighting and report [33 years] no effort was made, in 1979, to acquire more data. 

Status: Unidentified
Source: Questionnaire and letter, August 9, 1979.

Case xxx.
Luminous ball over Öresund

Time: Tuesday, May 21, 22.10 hours
Place: from Hälsingborg, over the Öresund

This report of a luminous ball was published by Helsingborgs Dagblad after a series of luminous phenomena had been sighted, in the south of Sweden, between May 23 and May 26.

”MYSTERIOUS FIREBALL ALSO OBSERVED IN HÄLSINGBORG. A luminous ball of the same kind as has been sighted at various places in our country, was - according to what HD has learnt - seen in Hälsingborg. It was Miss Anna Teieri, Margareta-platsen 6, who, in the company of a few friends observed the mysterious phenomenon. 

”- It was Tuesday evening last week...as we were sitting a few people on our balcony. We live on the sixth floor and have a good view out over the Sound [Öresund, the sound between Sweden and Denmark]. At about 22.10 hours we sighted the fireball in the direction of Helsingörsgatan [a street in Hälsingborg]. We first thought it was a star, but when we saw that it moved and also that it had a strange yellow colour, we understood that it must be something else. Later, it flamed up och threw sparks now and then. It took 35 seconds before it had passed [over] the Sound and disappeared behind the Viadukten [the viaduct - ?].”

Status: Insufficient information [star, balloon, airplane?]
Source: Helsingborgs Dagblad, May 29.
