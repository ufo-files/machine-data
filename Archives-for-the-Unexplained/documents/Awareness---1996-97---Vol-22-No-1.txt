                                         EDITORIAL

Last time I discussed prediction - Nostradamus style - and the significant bright comet
approaching the Solar System.      Now "Hale-Bopp" has come and gone and we all had some
splendid views of it in March and April.       Unfortunately a certain group of cultists calling
themselves " Heavens Gate" decided there was a spaceship travelling behind the comet and 39
misguided individuals dispatched their souls in ceremonious fashion hoping they would make
their cosmic connection in this way.      Needless to say the group were lead by an ageing
charismatic called Marshall Herff Applewhite who had been touting extreme beliefs for over
20 years.   The reasons for throwing life away seemed utterly senseless - however we have a
different analysis by Michael Soper which may explain why it happened.


It seems when an unexpected cosmic event occurs, the human reaction is to overplay it or
market it as some portent of doom.            About 2 years ago the        first rumblings about
"Hale-Bopp" had it on a collision course with the Earth, and I was told by scared looking
conference delegates who had picked up rumours.            In the event the nearest the comet
approached was 100 million miles or so away - fat chance of a collision!               Yes it might
happen one day, but if you think in 3 dimensions the odds are truly astronomical, literally.
Nothing much has changed since primitive races made sacrifices and doomsday predictions at
each eclipse of the sun!


What looks to be a consequence of the 39 suicides in America was a very odd statement
released by the Pentagon on April 2nd that: "We cannot substantiate the existence of UFOs
and are not harbouring the remains of UFOs."        The great government inspired investigation
of UFOs from 1947 to 1 969 (Project Blue Book) numbering 1 2,6 1 8 cases had shown none
"represented technological developments or principles beyond the range of our scientific
knowledge."    "No UFOs reported, investigated or evaluated posed a threat to America and
there was no evidence that the UFOs were extraterrestrial vehicles."


Any notion that the above statements would stop mass suicides on this or any future
occasion is sadly mistaken.     Logical or rational thought does not enter into it.       From our
point of view, we did at least get an indicator that the US government had ceased their
investigations in 1969 (or thats what they say). They make the arrogant assumption that a 22
year survey excludes there being any arrivals from outer space at any time before or after
this period.   What if there were aliens visiting from 1980 onwards or came every 100 years to
do surveys?    It is quite likely that the 1947-1969 survey could fail to spot them.   The chances
are that extraterrestrial life would far exceed the human span of "three score and ten", and
an assessment covering 2000 years might be more appropriate than 22.
