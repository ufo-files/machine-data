                   SKEPTICS, UFO RESEARCHERS, & OBJECTIVENESS

                           by John L. Black

   What is behind the formation of the greater majority of UFO groups?    The answer
to this question is almost certainly that the group wishes to solve the UFO mystery.
And what is the purpose behind this?    Besides personal satisfaction, there is usu­
ally a great desire to educate the public.    The biggest stalemate in these at tempts
to "preach the truth" about UFOs. excluding the censorship by our government and the
communications problem, has been the hardened core of people who are completely un­
reasonable about the subject.    A classic example of this skeptic group is Dr. Don­
ald Menzel, the now-f�mous astrophysicist who continually claims that "flying sau­
cers are mirages, refractions from ice crystals, water droplet reflections, and
so on. " He is completely unreasonable, for while his claims have been scientific­
ally disproven, and even denied by the Air Force, this scientist (and I use the
term loosely) refuses to this day to submit.    Because of people like this, one of
UFOlogy's major goals, that of public education, has been seriously impaired.
The real reason behind this stubborness is far beyond me.
   First the skep tics wanted photographs to prove that UFOs exist. Well, we gave
them photographs, enough to convince many people, but apparantly not enough for
them.   Nex t they screamed for films. �his wish was also granted. Next they clung
to the newly developed radar•   •   until radar reports came in.
                                    •   •   •   •                 Now they began
clutching for straws, they wanted PHYSICAL EVIDENCE, and we gave it to them.
Even today, they want a "grounded" saucer.
   What does all this mean?    It means that unless UFO researchers concentrate some
of their efforts oa stunting the growth of stubborn, dogmatic people, UFOlogy could
be dras tically set back. I don't mean that we should try to make everybody into
equally stubborn believers, which might be worse, but we should strive for OBJECT­
TIVENESS. Can we ask for more?     Then if there are still skeptics we will at least
know that we had our chance.    Perhaps the best place to start would be our own UFO
organizations!    If the researchers, the persons who study this subject, set an
open-minded example, there will be a greater chance that the rest of the people will
follow suit. But if we jump to conclusions, make snap decisions, and investigate
only the aspects of the UFO mystery which suit our taste or personal whim, then we
will all turn into carbon copies of Dr. Donald Menzel.
   James W. Moseley had a good point in his September, 1962 issue of Saucer News
where he stated "few civilian groups make any a ttempt whatsoever to solve any
UFO sightings."    (page 11, "My Interview at Wright-Patterson Field") . If we take
this atti tude, and yet still claim to have proof, and claim to have looked fairly
at both sides of the UFO enigma, we are nothing but hypocritical time-wasters.
How can the public be expected to look fairly at both sides of the UFO question
if the very investigators of the subject do not do so?
   Much of this problem falls into the hands of the heads of UFO group and their
respective investigating staff.    (If they indeed have one) � But the individual
researchers also have a big responsibility         for it is we who keep the UFO
                                                    •   •   •   •   •   •   •



groups going, by our support. If we drop our support from organizations we think
to be dangerous to the goals of UFOlogy. they can no longer be a danger to our
attempts to solve the mystery of the unidentified flying object.
   It is up to us - - - which will it be?

      The mystery of ball lightning has always been a favorite of mine as I find
it closely related to the mystery of the unidentified flying object.    Perhaps
some persons will say that ball lightning is a phenomenon in itself and not
at all related to the riddle of the flying saucers.    To this I could only say:
How do you know?    If you are not interested in phenomena such as this then you
should not belong to this organ�zation.
      Here is a list of a few ball lightning cases which were supplied to me by
I.N. S, 's Chief of Investigation James C. Kelsy.  Others similar to these have
been published in such magazines as Fate, Search, and Unknown Worlds,

Case #1. A Mr. Richards of Great Meadows, New Jersey, was visiting a friend
in Beaverskill, New York.   The cabin he was in had some telephone wires which
came into the building.   During a terrible thunderstorm a fireball, abou t the
size of a croquet ball, came in on these telephone wires.   It slowly flew across
the room, went under a bed and then back over it, hit a radiator and without
w�rning disappeared into thin air.

Case #2.   Miss Bellows was a young girl playing outside when an electrical storm
began.   A strange object the size of a croquet ball floated towards her; she drew
in her stomach, too frightened to do anything else, as the fireball turned, barely
missing her. She described the fireball as having a solid core surrounded by flames,

          The followi�cases were told to James Kelsey by John Bellows.

Case #3.  Mr. John Bellows, in the year 1930, was walking up a hill with his
father during an electrical storm. At the summit of the hill a lightning ball
came out of the woods, crossed the road, and flew down the hill.  An explosion was
soon heard, but not seen because of the trees.  Apparantly no damage was done,
The lightning ball had a diameter of about a foot and had a number of three foot
flames trailing behind.

Case #4.  Some three and a half years ago during a large thunderstorm a lightning
ball came on the telephone wires into John Bellow's house.  It knocked the phone
to the floor, crossed the room and hit the television.  The tube and the picture
tube exploded.

     These, as most will realize, are merely sample cases.   Our files are large
and so are the number of ball lightning reports over the past 500 years,
Perhaps this phenomenon can be linked with the fireballs which are so o ften re­
ported with flying saucer in formation.  I think this is an important subjeet and
should be investigated along with i ts bigger brother, the unidentified flying
object.
