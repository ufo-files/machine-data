                 FERVOUR AND FAITH IN THE FOREST
                In the benighted days before radio and TV, Americans in remote rural areas, far from
                churches, were in danger of being starved of religion. The solution was to take the
                churches to the people, in the form of camp meetings held in forest clearings or on
                the shores of rivers. Gatherings such as this 1852 meeting drew immense crowds
                who arrived in their wagons, pitched their tents, and gave themselves up to days and
                nights of fervent worship. Prayer turned into howls of ecstasy; worshippers fell by
                the score writhing on the ground, or convulsed in 'jerks' which spread through the
                congregation by contagion.

                 (Engraving in the Leipzig Illustrirte Zeitung)
