      Paraphychologist Stewart Robb of Anaheim, California, died of an ap­
parent heart attack on May 25th at the age of 81. Your editor knew Stew­
art for many years, since the days when we appeared together on many Long
John Nebel all-night talk shows in New York City. When Stewart and his
f�mily moved to California, we visited them frequently, accompanied by our
daughter Betty, who was about the same age as Stewart's daughter Denise.
      Stewart was a brilliant, highly-educated man, but in his many writ­
ings over the years, the Big Money eluded him. Nostradamus was one of his
favorite topics, and he has a current book out on that subject. He was al­
so very interested in classical music, and found the opportunity to com­
bine this interest with parapsychology when he came upon an English lady
named Rosemary Brown, who, supposedly with little musical education, 1s
able to play on the piano her own compositions in the style of several of
the classical composers. The theory is that these famous composers feed
her the notes from the Other Side.
      Stewart was a firm believer in an afterlife, and nothing could dis­
suade him. However, he went through his life proving and re-proving this
same point over & over again, to his own satisfaction. Over a period of
several years he would frequently have a friendly seance in his    living
room, in which the key element was a tape recorder turned up to maximum
volume - which causes distortion. He would have each person in the room,
in turn, ask a question of the Spirits or make a comment. Then a blank
space would be left on the tape before the next person spoke. When the 20-
minute tape was over, it would be played back to see if any Spirit voices
had come through. Because of the distortion and the outside city     sounds
(cats on the roof, airplanes overhead, etc.) , many arguments ensued as to
the meaning, if any, of anomalous sounds that would be present on the play­
back. Your editor attended several of these long evening sessions, and in
our opinion nothing significant was ever heard, EXCEPT one time:
      That night, probably in 1973. we went through the 20-minute tape and
the 20-minute playback several times, with the usual ambiguous results. At
that point Betty, who was about 10, took us aside and said, in effect, "Why
are you grown-ups acting like children? There are no spirits. You're just
wasting your time". I suggested that Betty sayJust that, when her turn
came on the next tape, and she did this. When this tape was played back,
Betty's opening remark was followed by an unmistakable loud whisper saying,
"She's a fake", followed by Betty talking again, followed by another whis­
pered sentence, "It couldn't be true". The whispered words did not overlap
