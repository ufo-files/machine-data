   My remark regarding John Weeks III was not meant as an insult or a
   description of an inferior mode of thought. It is a 'truism' with
   regard to us all. In John Weeks III case, however, he has severly
   restricted input because of unnecessary rigor.
   It is possible to disprove everything. How? All knowledge is 'stored'
   as historical data. Historical data cannot be reporduced no matter how
   hard you try. If you reproduce an identical situation it is still
   different because it will exist in a different time. Not to mention
   that it may or may not be a genuine identical reproduction and that
   this 'new' data will, once again, exist as . . . historical data.
   Too little 'rigor' and errors intrude, which must be painstakingly
   removed.   Or, you can tighten it up and have nothing left. It is also
   possible to use 'rigor' to coverup lack of knowledge.
   When rigor is too tight many usable facts are lost. It is also
   possible, in an attempt to achieve 'perfect' rigor, to grasp onto false
   negatives, debunking and coverup, to cast doubts on a given statement
   or state of affairs. Those 'negatives' have to undergo the same rigor
   of the original statement to be legitimate objections.
   Believe me, there is debunking and coverup with regard to the more
   sophisticated physics and technology items that are often discussed on
   the Usenet. Many subjects touch on covert or 'black' projects. And,
   sometimes, debunking and coverup are used to gain dominance in Usenet
   discussions.
   I do not do this, however. I deal in facts, but not over processed,
   reduced to powder and dust, facts. I operate on the "if it works, use
   it" principle. If it stops working, then use something else that does
   work. Reduce everything to . . . nothing . . . and we will all sit
   around in a daze. Not productive!
