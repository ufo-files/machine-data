                        The "X- Effect ..

     Ufologists have noticed the way in which certain film and
TV  writers  have  begun  to  embody   in  their  work  the  more
speculative details concerning UFO occupants, human abductions,
secret government bases and the like. Similarly, the discrediting
of the Warren report on the Kennedy assassination encouraged the
assertion that he was shot to prevent him exposing a deal done
with the grey aliens by former president Eisenhower.

     The public exploration of such possibilities might seem, on
the face of it, a good thing: obviously the public has a right
to know what is and has been done in their name and at their
expense. There has even been a suggestion that the raising of
these questions in the media has been inspired and encouraged by
the u.s. government, as a preliminary to forthcoming official
admissions that UFOs and their occupants are affecting Earth
culture in ways not necessarily beneficial. Some people even
think that governments will be willing to admit that they have
consistently lied,  though always of course with the best of
intentions.

     Unfortunately for the cause of Truth, however, the effect
produced by the presentation of such ideas in a fictional context
has diminished rather than enhanced the likelihood of their ever
receiving serious public consideration, for it provides a fantasy
framework within which a false frisson derived from daring to
think the unthinkable can be enjoyed in safety. It has provided
an effective way of removing the detonator from potentially
explosive material.
