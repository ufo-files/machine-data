vocalization emitters by which the animal is capable of simultaneous yet divergent,

 165,000 cycles per second.  By such means, a dolphin can project to others of its kind

When together in groups, all cetacea vocalize among themselves, and alter their speech

humans, dolphins will surface, and force themselves to vocalize in the 10,000 to
20,000 cycles per second range, which is abnormal for them, but so happens to be the

It is apparent from the research done with the cetacea that these creatures of the sea

There is a growing body of evidence which suggests that, in dim antiquity, mankind
may have indeed had powers of perceptions and understood forms of communications like
that of the dolphins, but lost to us over many generations.   As one example, we have
always believed that writing was one of the greatest inventions of any civilization,
but the truth is this has not always been considered so.   Writing was looked upon by
the ancients as a step backward for civilization, not a step forward.    Plato, in his
Phaedrus, indicated that writing encourages forgetfulness in the minds of those who
learn it, because they forget to cultivate memory.  He stated:    "Their trust in
writing, produced by external characters which are no part of themselves, will dis­
courage the students' use of their memory within them.   It is an elixir not of
memory, but of reminding:  it offers students the appearance of wisdom, not true
wisdom, for they will read many things without instruction and will therefore seem to
know many things, when they are for the most part ignorant and hard to get along with,
since they are not wise but only appear wise. " The earliest men did not invent
writing, because they had no need of any of the artificial means of preserving know­
ledge.  They possessed what we today call eidetic memory; that is, the ability of total
recall, to grasp knowledge instantaneously and to retain it totally and accurately.
When mankind degenerated to his present levels, that ability was lost, and other
methods--such as writing--had to be invented to aid the failing memory.    We can
sympathize with Gregoire de Tours, who wrote in the sixth century:    "The mind has lost
its cutting edge.  Alas, we can hardly understand the Ancients. "

read by later generations who had forgotten the purely mental states of knowledge, the

The existence of psychic abilities in the past also helps explain the appearance of
some recognizeable cultural traits in one ancient civilization, and their curious
absence in another.   The problem with interpreting the remains of the past is realizing
that there have been not one, but two types of civilizations--what might be termed

powers of the mind, interlinked with the psi-electrical forces of nature and the earth.

Archaeologists and historians have studied only the physical civilizations, because
their remains were purely material, and have thus been easier to find and interpret.
