     During the 1950's the contactee movement reached its peak. The likes of Howard
Menger, Truman Bethurum and others gained massive publicity through the media. Numerous
flying saucer conventions were held where contactees could peddle their par~pnlets, bonks,
pnotograpns, souvenirs, etc. Other noted contactees such as George Van Tassel and
Gabriel Green sponsored their own conventions which drew thousands of spectators and
followers. These conventions were beneficial in gaining-publicity for their general
flying saucer activities. 0ver 150 contactee-oriented c.lubs existed. These clubs
habitually interlocked their benevolent space brother beliefs into the occult, i.e.
astrology, metaphysics, mysticism and related subjects.
     The contatees' success in gaining sensational publicity and subsequent domination
of the UFO scene, confused the general public to the point where few could not
differentiate between the contactee and a prominent Ufologist. The contactees'
priorities were financial gain and notoriety. The result was deterioration of the
respectable study of Ufo'logy and fortified the ridicule fa~tor. The contactee movement
was so influential on the public and media that serious Ufologists spent much time
trying to correct the damage and set the record.straight on their claims . Capt.
Ruppelt was one of the many who. had a high interest in the subject and was driven
off by this lunatic fringe.
     Is this dark history of Ufology repeating itself? There are strong patterns to
suggest it is. Since the publicity received by the JAL sighting in January, hundreds
of articles written on the UFO phenomena have appeared in newspapers and magazines
worldwide. The main focus of these articles was on the Mj-12 controversy, abductees,
the Meir contacts, channeling, and the harmonic convergence. The electronic media
also lends a helping hand in thrusting these topics into the public domain.
       Tltose outside of the UFO community tend to equate prominent Ufologists with the
absurd claims of the cnannelers, the Billy Meir contacts, etc. Ironically, twa people
outside the UFO community -Kinder and Streiber, are creating more problems and fueling
the ridicule factor that could r-ival the contactee movement of the oO's. Gary Kinder
in his promot1onal tour for ''Light Years" claimed he performed a thorough, objective,
investigation into the Meier contacts. "Light Years" only serves to promote the
contactee movement. We now know Kinder knew the Meier contacts were a hoax, as did h1s
pub lisl1er, but "Light Years" was published anyway just for tinancia i gain. Streiber,
the cosmic guru of the 80s with a great following, is now promoting channeling and is
due to publish a book on the topic. A prominent UFO researcher from the Exeter, NH
area reports that area residents refer to Streiber as "Mr. Ufo logy". Jamie Shandera
of the infamous M.S.F. trio, also not of the UFO community, was the recipient of the
strange cylindrical package containing the widely-publicized MJ-12 papers. Moore,
Shandera and Friedman, in my opinion, were puppets in a disinformation campaign and
victims of their own biased research. fhe media, hungry for information, e~pecially
during the summer months, quickly picked this up in their press releases. In a matter
of weeks the MJ-12 documents and parties involved received media attention worldwide.
Because of the media hype, many people in and out of the UFO community believed the
