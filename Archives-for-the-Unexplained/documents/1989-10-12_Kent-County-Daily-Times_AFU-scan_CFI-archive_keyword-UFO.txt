MOSCOW (UPI) - Soviet UFO
specialists Tuesday entered the
fray about a reputed spaceship
landing in Voronezh and said
claims of giant, three-eyed,
silver-costumed visitors don't
prove an interplanetary visit.
  But the Soviet press was
undaunted and printed more
accounts of flying saucers.
  Trud, the official newspaper of
the state-run trade unions, said a
spaceship swept across the even­
ing sky Monday over the island of
Sakhalin in the Soviet Far East.

  In Dalnegorsky, also in the
Soviet Far East, three space
visitors seen near their craft
ducked behind the bushes when
a motorcycle approached, the
newspaper KrasnaJia Znam11a
reported. Giant footprints were
later found, it said.

  In Voronezh,300 miles south of
Moscow, the head of the UFOlogy
Council of Scientific and Engi­
neering Associations probed the
granddaddy      of  the   recent
claimed sightings- the reported
Sept. 27 park landing of a space­
ship that allegedly frightened
children playing soccer.

  A report transmitted Monday
by the official Tass news agency
and picked up by the world press
said a UFO landed in the Soviet
city of Voronezh, and several
creatures similar to humans only
10 to 13 feet tall, with small
heads and three eyes, emerged
from a hatch along with a small
robot

  But V. Azhazh, head of the
local UFO society, told the Izves­
tia newspaper: "Personally it is
erroneous to connect such facts
with astral intelligence or with
visitors from other worlds. "Our
knowledge is as yet too scanty for
a scientific interpretation of the
phenomena."
