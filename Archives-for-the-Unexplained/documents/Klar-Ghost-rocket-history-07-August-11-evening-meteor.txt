Not like the V-2!

”The London correspondent of Dagens Nyheter during the war points out that it was a characteristic for the V-2 that noone noticed anything of them before they exploded. Only in very exceptional cases one could see a glimpse of the projectiles before they hit ground. Their speed was also so great that the sound from a passing bomb could only be heard only a while after the bomb had passed. So, any weapon of the V-2 kind it can´t be, says editor [Daniel] Viklund.” (Dagens Nyheter, Aug. 14, also TT newswire, Aug. 14)



Case xxx.
Swedish scientist saw the fireball

”- If an object is seen in the sky, exactly at the same time from widely separated places in our oblong country, it can´t be the question of a rocket or a ”ghost bomb” of earthly origin, but a projectile from space, thus a meteor. I saw myself the fireball which appeared on Sunday night at 20.50 and I can attest that it looked like and behaved in a way that is typical of brilliant meteors. 

”The one who says this is a prominent Swedish scientist, who for the time being prefers to be anonymous in order not to have his telephone besieged the next few days. I was in a boat on Furusundsfjärden [the Furusund bay], about one [nautical] mile N of Furusund, he tells Svenska Dagbladet, when suddenly I caught sight of an unusually sharp bluewhite shine at 40-60 degrees [relative] altitude over the horizon, in the NNW. The object which was only visible during a few seconds, released several smaller fragments. The fireball probably had a diameter about 1/3 of the full moon , which was visible in the east. The real dimension one could not, of course, assess, since the distance was unknown. 

”As the fireball, according to numerous reports in the newspapers, was visible at exactly the same time from widely separated parts of south Sweden to the upper part of Norrland, the altitude over the earth must have been very great, and one can therefore not hardly doubt the cosmic origin of the fireball. In my view it is very unfortunate to, as has happened, refer to the light phenomenon in support of the view that the mysterious light phenomena all and sundry originate from rockets. In this particular it was, in my conviction, the question of a large fireball from space, a so called bolide.” (Svenska Dagbladet, Aug. 14, also TT newswire on Aug 14)
