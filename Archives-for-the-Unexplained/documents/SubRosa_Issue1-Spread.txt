Spiral    Path
  by Cernig
         I have been a student of the mystics,               I have been a single leaf,
               And a miller by a stream.                     On a mighty rowan tree.
     I have delved in the bones of Mother Earth,        I have been a single stalk of wheat,
            And tended goats on hillsides.              And the scythe which cut it down.
        I have been poor more often than not,      I have known my name more often than not,
                 And happy, by and by.                         And cried, by and by.
            I will ﬂow into the sea one day,              I will ﬂow into the sea one day,
               Yet still I have my source.                   Remembering my source.

            My home is Caledon of old,                      We are a gracious people,
          Beyond the wall of turf and tree,              Who save our insults for battle,
          Raised in folly by a Latin hand.                 Or for true friends over ale.
     What need for temples and icons of gold?                 Cross us at your peril,
       We have the hills and sheltered glens,         As the ghosts of old invaders will tell.
         The crags whose weathered faces,             The day they sent the Legion North,
            Have glowered in the mists,                   Was a time for singing songs,
     Ere your paltry lands rose from the waves.               And whetting blades.

        I have been an otter playing in a burn,       I have been a mist upon the mountains,
               And a kestrel on the wing.                And a winter wind through trees.
        I have been a wolf howling to the gale,    I have been a snowﬂake on the hearthstone,
            And a hermit in a hidden cave.              And the sunlight on a window pane.
      I have sang the song more often than not,          I have loved more often than not,
                  And died, by and by.                        And lost love, by and by.
            I will ﬂow into the sea one day,               I will ﬂow into the sea one day,
               Yet still I sing my source.                A long journey from my source.

       My home is a hut of wood and thatch:          I have been a sword that broke in battle,
          You might sneer at this hovel,                       And a child born a King.
            But you would not dare.                           I have been a single word,
        My wife, of royal blood and true,            And the thief who took your gold away.
            Would slit you ear to ear,                  I have learned more often than not,
              Before you ﬁnished.                                And tried, by and by.
         We value manners highly here.                     I will ﬂow into the sea one day,
       Care not a whit for snide politeness.                  To join, at last, my source.
