    brightly glowing a dull orange or yellow. The object was apparently hovering, or perhaps slow-moving, at an elevation

                                                                                     ·

       At about this stage they observed e second object, similar to the first, but appearing to be about three times the
    apparent size, or much closer. This second object was in roughly the same relative direction from the car as the first
    had originally appeared, but at a higher elevation, estimated at about 60 deg.rees. The second object now descended to the
    same elevation as the first, and they both moved across the road, seeming to keep a constant distance apart, estimated at
    about 5 degrees. When tne two objects had reached a position on the left hand side of the road in a direction at about 45
    degrees to their direction of travel, W, who was sitting in the rear left hand seat, filmed one of the objects for 20 or
    30 seconds through the window of the moving car. Which of the two objects was filmed he was not sure, but later examin­
    ation of the film suggested that it was the second of the two reported. After the filming the objects slowly moved
    together further away ahead of the car, and eventually disappeared into the distance.
       The above account is based on a first interview with the two brothers, followed by a later separate interview with W
    alone. The times and angles quoted should not be taken as accurate estimates, but as mere guesses in response to questions
    asked. The witnesses were not very confident about estimating the objects' angular sizes, but W thought that the first
    object when appearing at its largest might be about equal to the moon's diameter. He agreed that distances and sizes
    were unknown, but he had the impression that the first object was two or three miles away when first sighted, and was
    perhaps two or three hundred feet in length, while the second object appeared to be much larger, possibly six or seven
    hundred feet in length. In short, both witnesses thought that the objects were at a considerable distance, and much
    larger than aeroplanes.
       The third witness, C, verified the main details in a later telephone conversation, although he thought the objects were
    further away, perhaps 10 or 15 miles. He was particularly impressed by the rotation manoeuvre.
       At no time did the car stop. The entire observation took place from the moving vehicle. As no precise times were noted,
    there was some uncertainty about the length of time the objects were in view, but it was agreed that it must have been
    something between 15 and 30 minutes. The two brothers could not recall passing through any town while· watching the objects,
    while C thought the objects were still visible, far in the distance, as they passed through Benalla, although he could not
    be sure of this. The estimated speed of the car during the observation was 55 mph, so it seeme that the observation took
    place somewhere between Violet Town and Benalla.
       The witnesses reported no effects on the functioning of the car, nor did they experience any physiological reactions.

    distance behind were also aware of the objects. However, they were not interested in flagging down this car, and explained
    their failure to stop at any stage as due to their feeling of some apprehension due to the objects' presence.

    phenomena with no apparent means of propulsion. All three seemed greatly impressed by the unusual nature of these objects,
