    wild in the field of Ufology. A Rationalist, in the philosophic sense, is one
    who believes that reason alone, unaided by observation, can provide knowledge

    faculty, but it can only analyze facts and uncover possible explanations un­
    less used in conjunction with empirical observation. The possibilities uncov­
    ered may be very inter esting, but they are not facts.
                   The UFO Rationalists have dreamed up systems which appear to ex­
•   plain UF01s. These appearances, however,are deceptive because the explanations
    are empty. They tell us nothing new, nothing tangible, nothing subject to ver­
    ification in case we want to check up on it. The dangers of Rationalism are
    obvious. Such systems, often offered in all sincerity, are mistakenly present­
    ed as being empirical fact. Actually, unlike scientific explanation (which
    combines empirical data with logical analysis) there is no logical necessity
    for accepting a. rationalistic statement of fact in preference to any one of an

                       YOU TOO CAN SEE A FLYING SAUCER!
                             - by Justin Case -                                   •
                        Would you like to see flying saucers? Do you want to
meet and talk with visitors from other planets, or even take a ride in one of
their space ships? You can do so quite easily. In fact, the less educated and
the more simple-minded you are, the easier it is for you to do all this. But
there is considerable danger involved, as you shall soon see.
                        Most of us are familiar with hypnosis, .in which the
hypnotist gains control over some person1s mind (the person is hereafter called
the subject), and makes him do many things that the subject could not or would
not do when in his right senses. The subject sees, feels, hears, smells, and
tastes whatever the hypnotist tells him to, even though there is nothing there.
To the subject the imaginary becomes the real. He will even harm himself or
commit crimes against others, so completely is he under the hypnotist's con­
trol, and he will be totally una��re of what he did, unless the hypnotist
chooses to tell him afterwards.
                       The dangers of this form of hypnotism are obvious. About
one person in five can be hypnotized. Some people are so susceptible that they
can be hypnotized by a recorded voice, or over the telephone, or even against
their will. To protect such people, some countries have outlawed the practice
of hypnotism except by licensed practitioners, and then only for legitimate
purposes.
                        But there is another and much more dangerous form of
hypnotism that is of special interest to us. It is dangerous because it cannot
be outlawed, for it is beyond any governmental control. This is called auto-
suggestion, or self-hypnosis, in which the subject puts himself into somewhat
the same mental state as if he were hypnotized by someone else. A common name
                                                                                  •
for this state of mind is day-dreaming, and many people indulge in it without
realizing how disastrous it might become.
                        Such people are usually seeking an escape from the
harsh, disagreeable realities of life. They are little people who want to be
great, timid souls who want to be heroes, obscure individuals who want fame,
troubled people who want peace. They start off by creating in their own minds
the kind of world they want. They picture themselves as being what they wish
to be, doing the things they want to do, and getting the things they de­
sire. This is such an easy and pleasant process that the subject indulges in
these day dreams more and more frequently. Gradually he begins to spend more
time in his dream world than in the real world. As time goes by, the imaginary
world becomes more and more real. Eventually the subject lives in it entirely,
and he cannot consciously return to the real world. When that happens he has
lost all contact with the real world,he bas lost all control over his own mind,
and is ready for the lunatic asylum. That is why self-hypnosis, or auto-sug­
gestion, or day-dreaming, is dangerous.
                        Since they live in worlds which they have created to
their own special order, lunatics are usually quite happy people. Often they
are harmless. But sometimes their day-dreaming is of the more frightening type,
full of horror and terror, and such people end up as raving maniacs who are apt
to harm themselves and others. Thus any form of day-dreaming or self-hypnotism,
if carried too far, will lead to disaster.
                        It is only in the last few decades that psychologists
have begun to investigate and understand the widespread practice of self-hypno-
tism, its causes and ef£ects. They have begun to establish "mental health"        •
clinics. It may shock some of us to learn that two out of every three patients
in our hospitals are suffering from some form of mental illness, in which the
