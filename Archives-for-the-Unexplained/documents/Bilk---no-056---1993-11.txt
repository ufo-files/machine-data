• Is the merman a shaman? San rock-art from southern Africa close to Ezeljagdspoort
shows strange beings, a mixture of a human with long, fin or wing-like hands and a
fish tail. Now David Lewis-Williams and Thomas A Dowson and Janette Deacon have
suggested in Antiquity (June 93; reprinted in The Ley Hunter 119,p.4) that these
are not merman, but symbols of the shaman in flight - the fish tails are actually
swallow tails•   • However, on p.l3, Paul Devereux mentions traditions of the "subterra­
                     .


nean and subaquatic journey" of the shaman. Could not the tail symbolize both? And
I wonder if not the earliest of all merman-like figures, the Sumerian Ea (Babylonian
Oannes, of Ancient-Astronauts-fame) has something to do with it? Perhaps he had a
swallow's tail originally, but when he was later introduced into written lore, his
shamanic origins had been forgotten and he was turned into a fish-man?

• Michel Raynal sent me new clippings on the supposed giant octopus of Florida.
The BBC Wildlife (August 1993, p. 33) has a one page report by J Richard Greenweell
on it, recounting the 1897 Florida carcass and the second find, in May 1988, on
a Bermuda Beach, of a "large, grey, shapeless mass of tissue". We learn that samples
of the thing have been tested by several institutes, but no conclusive evidence as
to what the things are has been found. A shark, however, is being ruled out. Michel
also sent me his article, Le Monstre de Floride de 1896, co-authored with Michel
Dethier, from the Bulletin de la Societe neuchateloise (date missing) which summarises
the case.
