                     For many years people have reported seeing strange,        apparently un-      .
          explainable sights in the sky or even on land,        water or underwater.     The popular
          term associated with these sights has evolved from 'flying saucer' to 'UFO' to
          simply 'unidentified phenomenon.'

                   The so-called 'modern' period of sightings commenced June 24, 1947 with
          Kenneth Arnold's repor t of nine strangely hehaving objects near Mount Ranier,
          Washington.  It is ironic that the term 'flyin·g saucer' was used by the media to
                                                                                                              .,.




          refer to what Arnold saw because he described the objects as crescent-shaped, not
          disc-shaped.      The only reference made by Arnold to the word 'saucer' was in des-
        cribing their movement which he likened to "saucers skipping over water."                Discs
        had however been reported before,   and in fact the word 'saucer' was used to
          describe an incident occurring near Denison, Texas on January 24, 1878.  The day
          following the sighting the Denison Daily News reported that John Martin,             a local
          farmer,   reported seeing a dark flying object_in the shape of a disc cruising high
          in the sky "at a wonderful speed, " and used the word 'saucer' to describe it.

                    Following Arnold's report,       hundreds more poured in to the authorities.
          Some were even reported prior to Arnold's,        but did not get the media attention
        his attracted.       Government reaction was swift.         In July 1947 the U.S. Air Force
        was officially charged with the responsibility of investigating 'unidentified
        aerial objects' to determine if they posed a latent threat to U.S. national
        security.     In September,   the chief of the Air Technical Intelligence Centre            (ATIC)
        sent a communique to the Air Force Commanding General stating ATIC's conclusion
        that UFOs were real,      and urging the establishment of a permanent project to analyze
        future reports.

                    Thus,   on January 2,   1948,   Project 'Sign' was established,    with headquarters
        at Wright-Patterson AFB in Dayton, Ohio.           In September a top-secret 'Estimate of
        the Situation' report went from ATIC to the Air Force Chief of Staff,               General Hoyt S.
        Vandenberg.      The report concluded that UFOs were interplanetary.          However,   Vandenberg
        requested more proof,      but at the same time ordered ATIC to publicly take a low-key,
        almost negative attitude to the UfO problem.           Because of internal disagreement among
        Sign's personnel,      and the nagging worry that some sort of Soviet weapon might be
        involved,    Project Sign was re-named Project Grudge,         and reports were then evaluated
        on the premise that UFOs could not exist.

                    This was puzzling in light of the previous conclusions of ATIC,           and many
        people began to sense a cover-up.           Surprisingly,   in spite of continuing announce­
        ments that all UFO reports were delusions caused by hysteria,            and that Project Grudge
        was therefore being disbanded,        the study was continued under a new project leader,              •

        A.F. Captain Edward J. Ruppelt.         In March 1952 the name was once again changed,
        this time to the now familiar Project Blue Book.              Speculation as to the nature of
        UFOs was again rampant.                                                                                 I
