                               A MAJOR POLICY CHANGE

This month brings our last financial year to an end and subscriptions for the next year, up to
October 1998 will be due shortly.

The past year has seen a tremendous increase in our costs, for instance printing costs have
risen by over 25% since early in the year. This has caused numerous problems in trying to
maintain an interesting newsletter of reasonable length and content.         The OVNI has in fact,
been running at a loss for some six months and has been subsidised by your editor.             It is a
situation that cannot continue into our next financial year and therefore drastic action must be
taken in order to balance the group's finances.

Initially a number of options were considered, such as raising the membership fee from £ 10.00
to £ 12.00 per annum in order to cover the cost of producing the monthly OVNI newsletter.
The other consideration was to abandon the OVNI newsletter altogether and to keep in touch
with members either by phone or personal correspondence.            However, in view of the fact that
members often comment on their enjoyment in reading the OVNI, another solution has now
been considered.

The OVNI, is one of the very few UFO newsletters produced each month, many groups have
attempted this difficult task and have fallen by the wayside. It does take a great deal of work
and organisation to produce the newsletter monthly and it is a very costly exercise in both time
and money.

There is no doubt that a monthly newsletter keeps members right up to date with what is
going on. We are, after all, at the         'sharp end' of UFO investigations and our prime
consideration has always been, to keep members informed of current UFO events and activity,
both in the U.K. and world-wide.

The task of bringing this information into the public domain, has now been largely superseded
with the advent of the new 'Glossy' and expensive magazines, (although many still rely on
printing the old pot-boilers, such as the Roswell and Mantell incidents).
The proposed solution to our problem is this. The OVNI will remain in print, but will change
to a quarterly newsletter, while at the same time increasing from twelve to twenty pages. The
monthly meetings will continue at the Royal British Legion, Derby and the PRA membership
fee will be reduced from £ 10.00 to £9.00 a year. The exchange of the OVNI newsletter with
other UFO magazines and newsletters will also be greatly reduced.

This then, is our plan for the coming year and we hope that you will continue to be a part of it!

                                     4.15pm     28 August 1997

 Mr Nicholas Hayler contacted the PRA after witnessing a white/silver cigar shaped object in
 the sky over Derby city centre. Mr Hayler recounts the incident:

 I was on a Bartons public transport bus and we had just left Derby city centre, when I noticed
 what I assessed to be a UFO in the sky. It was cigar shaped and much larger than a passenger
 plane. It was a white/silver, metallic object, very bright and it was travelling far too fast to be
 an airship or balloon of any sort. Its edges were clearly defined and it was most certainly not a
 cloud formation phenomenon. Eventually it disappeared behind cloud and just then the bus
 changed direction and I lost sight of the object.

 Although the bus was densely populated, nobody else noticed the unidentified object
 phenomenon. At the time of the sighting, although I was tired, I felt a sense of disbelief and at
 the same time, great elation.

 I watched the object for a period of 1 to 2 minutes and I estimated its speed as being about
 750mph. I checked the time of my sighting on my wristwatch.

 According to Mr Hayler, the object was seen in the NE part of the sky and at an angle of 45
 degrees. He thought the object was unusual because of its speed, size, shape and altitude.

 The weather at the time was clear, with scattered cloud visible across the sky.

Readers may not generally appreciate that we are now in the throes of what is almost a 'Flying
Triangle' invasion. After something of an inauspicious arrival in the State of Missouri on the
18th November 1980, the FT's have been reported in increasing numbers World-wide.

Their arrival in Missouri and Kansas in 1980 caused consternation and awe, as hundreds of
witnesses, including policemen, watched a huge craft displaying a triangular pattern of lights,
move slowly over the countryside for a period of four hours. It was seen over 22 towns and
at one stage the huge craft appeared to be dropping, what a local truck driver described as

The whole event was tracked on Radar during the four hour period and must rate as one of
the most awesome sightings to date.       One witness describing the FT as being "as big as a
football field."   This then, was a portent of what was to come!

During January 1981 a huge boomerang shaped craft was seen over Arizona, with one witness
proclaiming that the object "was bigger than several football fields!"       The 'V' shaped craft
were later reported over New York State and Connecticut in March 1983. Once again the
craft was described as being "as big as two football fields" and this time, the craft flashed its
lights on and off, as though trying to attracting attention.

Later came the 'Belgian Flap,' when huge triangular craft visited this small country during the
period 1989/90.     The SOBEPS Group (Societe Beige d'Etudes des Phenomenes Spatiaux),
were inundated with hundreds of reports during this period. Such was the efficiency of their
organisation, that at one stage the Belgian Air Force asked for their co-operation and
assistance! With SOBEPS members being widespread throughout the country, they were in a
far better position to spot and report 'events' in their locality than the Belgian Air Force. It
seems that SOBEPS acted in a similar way to the U.K.'s 'Royal Observer Corps' and at one

stage had two F16 interceptor fighters placed at their disposal!       This resulted in the F16's
intercepting the 'Flying Triangles,' although they were completely unable to match the speed
and manoeuvrability of the FT's, as subsequent cockpit radar film footage showed.

On the 26th September 1993 a huge 'Flying Triangle' craft arrived over Bakewell in
Derbyshire. Although there had been isolated reports over the U.K. prior to this event, it gave
an indication of what we could expect in the near future.

On the night of the 26th September, the residents of Bakewell were treated to the sight of a
huge black silent craft flying slowly over their town. With a bright white light on each corner
of its triangular shape and numerous smaller lights running along the edges of the craft,      it
flew very low and at a speed of only 30mph. (a witness driving his vehicle at 40mph overtook
it!)   The FT craft then performed several slow manoeuvres over the town before returning to
its original course and flying slowly out of sight.        Just over three months later a British
Airways passenger aircraft was 'buzzed' by a glowing triangular craft as it approached
Manchester Airport.!

The 'Flying Triangles' then began to arrive in force, with 52 FT incidents being recorded in and
around Derby from December 1994 to May 1995. This included FT craft hovering at night
and shining down white beams of light onto the countryside.

My feelings were mixed, I was astonished and I also knew that I had witnessed something that
