Thunderlip is keeping rock disreputable


Scott McLennan | Telegram & Gazette
There was a time when such bands as AC/DC, Judas Priest and Mötley Crüe did not carry the imprimatur of respectability,
something that time has bestowed upon the like, effectively paroling former menaces into mainstream pop culture. The band
Thunderlip honors the debauched days, as the upstart group from North Carolina is hell bent on delivering the sound of harmonized
guitars, motorcycle-rumble rhythms and demonically preached lyrics with all the threat and sweat such music deserves.
“A lot of the music we love comes from the ’70 and early ’80s, stuff by Deep Purple, UFO and Thin Lizzy,” said Thunderlip guitarist
John Manning. “It’s really just about people having fun.”
As Thunderlip prepares to release its sophomore album “The Prophecy,” the band is hitting the road hard, already logging numerous
dates with the group Lucero and is now embarking on some gigs of its own, including one tomorrow at Ralph’s Chadwick Square
Diner, 148 Grove St., Worcester.
“The Prophecy” is due out Aug. 21 on Lucid Records, an imprint launched by former Thunderlip member Chris Broach. Lucid
previously put out Thunderlip’s self-titled debut, a frenetic snapshot of a band finding its footing amid an interesting rock landscape.
That first disc is a declaration of intent, with Thunderlip swearing allegiance to a nation whose flag is stitched from leather and denim
and testifying to the masses about the power of unbridled rock. There is nothing fancy to the album, at times echoing the most
careering efforts put forth by Motorhead, but the Thunderlip record ultimately conjures all manner of miscreant rock moments, right
down to an improvised outro song “Dead Horse Blues” that sounds like a stoned-out Brian Jones outtake from beyond the grave.
Manning said the newer material is better honed especially now that the band has been together for a while.
“We had been playing together for a year when we got signed and made the first record,” he said. “The new record has just eight
songs, but we spent a lot more time putting it together. We wanted to focus on the quality.”
Songs posted on the band’s MySpace site, as well as a sampler of new music mailed out in advance of Thunderlip’s arrival here, do
reveal some added depth to the sound, but not at the expense of the lunatic vibe that makes this band so interesting in the first place.
Manning said that Thunderlip’s live show has been its main calling card, and seeing pictures and primitive video footage (thanks
YouTube) of singer Chuck Krueger doing his thing, wearing a cape no less, hinted at what the payoff may be in catching this band in
its maiden voyage into Worcester.
“Facing a crowd for the first time is an interesting situation. It doesn’t matter if we go into a place where nobody has heard of us and
we play to 10 people. We’re going to do the same show we do when there are 300 people in front of us,” Manning said.
While a retro vibe has been shooting through underground rock for some time, most bands are finding inspiration from the era of
’60s garage rock. But there are those bumping up a decade and recasting the glory days of ’70s arena rock. The style has been
taken pretty far by fellow North Carolinians Valient Thorr, a band that Manning said has helped blaze the trail for the likes of
Thunderlip.
“We’re just trying to keep spreading what we think is good music,” Manning said.

Amid differing press releases, we printed some bum info about the upcoming tribute to singer Brad Delp happening Aug. 19 at The
Bank of America Pavilion in Boston. We have been assured that the following is where things stand: The show will include an
appearance by Delp’s most famous project, the band Boston, which now consists of founder Tom Scholz plus Gary Pihl,
Kimberley Dahme, Jeff Neal and Fran Cosmo. Original Boston members Barry Goudreau, Fran Sheehan and Sib Hashian
will join in on a version of the Boston classic “Don’t Look Back.” Guest vocalists for the event will include Sammy Hagar,
Stryper’s Michael Sweet and Starship’s Mickey Thomas. The bands Ernie and the Automatics, Farrenheit, Beatlejuice,
Godsmack, Orion the Hunter and RTZ will also perform. Dubbed “Come Together,” the event will also feature a film about
Delp, who took his own life earlier this year, produced by his daughter Jennifer, a graduate of the University of Southern California’s
film school. Proceeds from the tickets, which cost $35, will benefit The Brad Delp Foundation and other charities, including Scholz’s
The DTS Foundation. Tickets are on sale now through Ticketmaster.
