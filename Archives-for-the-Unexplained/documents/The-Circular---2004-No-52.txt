prepared by Montague indicating that it was a hoax by a local
youth , Jul ian Richardson, and his friends. The case was
supported by photographs by the hoaxers showing the
formation's construction in two stages . Unfortunately, the
evidence of the farming community was contradictory, and
consequently the CCCS council decided that no rating one
way or another could be made. In retrospect I personally believe
that Montague was correct, and I regret that at the time, as
chairman, I did not give him the support that he deserved. As
it was, the business split the CCCS council and Montague was
unj ustly publicly villified. He resigned as Scientific Officer
shortly afterwards in 1 994.

Unfortunately, Montague's treatment also made clear with this
landmark case that for an influential, vocal faction in the CCCS,
rational objective results were no longer a determining factor
in assessing the genuineness of crop formations. This tendency
has now reached a point ten years on where some of the largest
and most influential crop circle organisations in the south of
England (not the CCCS, I must hasten to add) do not even
pretend to carry out scientific research, but have turned into
circle phenomenon appreciation societies!

The irony of the Bythorn fracas was that Montague was not at
all the blinkered rationalist only concerned with mechanistic
science, as he was sometimes portrayed in certain quarters.
He was very much aware of what might be termed 'other states
of reality ' , as, indeed, his long-term membership of S P R
indicated. A paper ofh i s i n 1 993 published by the CCCS (Argus
1 992 - is science getting bogged down?) in some senses sums
up his position before he left the organisation. In many respects
it mirrored the problems that have dogged crop circle research
ever since, namely, that the subj ect is proving itself bigger
than purely scientific objectivity can address. In his concluding
paragraph he said, "Commitment to a scientific approach does
not exclude or make inadmissable other less orthodox attempts
to glean more information by more direct means," with a clear
reference to metaphysical sources of knowledge. In private he
said to me that the CCCS must, however, establish the objective
reality of the genuine phenomenon, before we can safely move
towards the exciting metaphysical fields that may lie beyond.

I will personally remember and appreciate Montague Keen as
a decent, honourable man of high intellect who was generous
to his professional friends and col leagues. Between 1 99 1 and
1 994 his sharp critical intelligence and hard work for the CCCS
