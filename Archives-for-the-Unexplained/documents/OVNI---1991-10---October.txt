The newly re-vamped " Unexplained" magazine includes an article on "The Crop
Circle Controversy" in its issue Vol. 1      Part 3    (October).   Priced at
£ 1. 50, it carries the story and photographs of the " Doug and Dave" hoaxers
(Today newspaper Sept. 9th).    The subject is treated objectively and a well
balanced account of the circle mystery has been produced.     This issue also
covers a number of old UFO cases, seen in previous issues ( 1980).

Thanks to PRA member Ray Swann, who spotted a story in        the "Coalville
Times", an investigation was made into the report of          a twin circle
"Dumbell" at Breedon-on-the-Hill, N. Leics.

The two page article "Magic Circle" was dated the 13th of September, noted
on the 30th of September and investigated by Omar Fowler on the following
day ( 1st October).

Mrs Dilks and her son Robin, were visited at their home, " Bramgate Farm".
After studying the available photographs and listening to their story, the
site of the circles was investigated.

The field, which is adjacent to a country road off the A447, was easily
accessible.  An open double gateway led into the field, which although now
cleared of the wheat crop, still had the stubble remaining.        The two
circles (approx. 13'dia. ) could be clearly seen.   The combined harvester
had cut into one and then stopped, the remaining circle was intact and
uncut.

The circles appeared to be roughly made, without any visible swathes and
the wheat stalks were mainly broken at ground level.   Although the circles
were formed on the night of the 23rd of August and had since been trampled
by numerous sightseers, the floor pattern of the circles did not appear to
be authentic.  When part of the top floor wheat layer was raised, a section
of crop could be seen pointing towards the centre of the circle.  This gave
the impression that a person could have walked to the centre initially,
before laying down the wheat in a circular form and covering the entry
track.

The circles were barely 50 yards in from the gate and only 8'- 10' from the
grass verge of the field.  Entry to the circle area would have been quite
easy, via the gateway by walking along the grass verge and then into the
wheat.

Inspection of the ditch by the gate produced, two beer bottles and a whisky
