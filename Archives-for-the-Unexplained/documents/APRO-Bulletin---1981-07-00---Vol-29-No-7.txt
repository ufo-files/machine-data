                                               CLARIFICATION
                                            By L.J. (Jim) Lorenzen

  During the early part of this year, Allen Benz and Bill English engaged in an effort to convert APRO's assets
to their control. Using positions of trust accorded them in what was purported to be volunteer work on behalf of
APRO, they made unauthorized use of the APRO mailing list and telephone in an effort to solicit support for
their ''takeover''.
   At one point, English intercepted a phone call intended for me and made arrangements for himseH to appear
(representing APRO) on a TV pilot which also included Dr. Hynek and Travis Walton. (He pocketed the funds
received for the appearance). Later, Dr. Hynek informed me that he found the experience difficult and
embarras sing as English had gone on at length in private conversations about how he and Allen Benz were
"taking over" APRO. Dr. Hynek expressed relief and congratulations to hear that I had the situation in hand.
  At the general membership meeting in Cleveland on June seventh a motio\1 was introduced by Dick Ruhland
seconded by Marie Bluhm and a quorum of APRO members voted to terminate the memberships of Benz and
English. The new APRO Board of Directors has reviewed that act of the members and has concurred
unanimously, changing the status of both Benz and English to that of subscriber.
  During early 1981, English had also phoned Walt Andrus of MUFON and imparted much the same
information, i.e., that he and Benz were taking over and that there would be a new board of directors at APRO.
Walt's response was to publish in his "Director's Message" a non-specific call to the new APRO Board to end
APRO's "isolationist" attitude. Unwittingly completing this rather remarkable bit of puppetry, newly elected
APRO Board      Member Alexia    Markham    read the passage from the        MUFON Journal at APRO's              July
membership meeting.
 As it turns out, the rumors of the take-over and of the isolationism are both greatly exaggerated. Readers of
the APRO Bulletin are aware of an on-going support of CAUS (Citizens Against UFO Secrecy), The Fund for
UFO Research, VISIT, Ray Stanford's Project Starlight International and Bob Gribble's Phenomena Research.
  Meaningful cooperation is generally not shouted from the housetops nor should it be. There exist some
differences in priorities, understanding, and emphasis, between UFO groups. I presume that that is why, in part,
there is more than one group.
  But where goals coiricide, cooperation should happen as a matter of course. And usually does.

                                            APRO IS ALIVE - AND WELL
                                                    By Coral E. Lorenzen

  The APRO Staff would like to thank the membership for their support during the difficult months just
passed. Many of you responded to my plea for monetary help and with a few adjustments in priorities, we are
well on our way to a stable group economy.
  Within the past few years Jim and I have thought of giving up our rather large home, in anticipation of fewer
domestic chores and less upkeep. It didn't come to pass, and because of it, APRO will again be quartered in the
Lorenzen home. The non-essential files which do not need to be dealt with day by day are being stored. A
couple of filing cabinets essential to the day-to-day business have been moved into the office which we have
maintained for our APRO work throughout the years. The rest, although available, will be accommodated in our
sun room. The squeeze is a little tight but we are no worse off than other groups who have had to face the
realities of inflation, and in some ways, better off.
  We will ask the indulgence of the membership in matters concerning file-searching for certain information, etc.
At present, the only full-time worker is myself. There will be some volunteer work done by some of the local
members (who will be mentioned at a later date), but we must remind the members at large that the office,
because it is in our home, cannot be available to just anyone at any time. Of course, we are always glad to make
the acquaintance of members, but would like ample warning of impending visits via letter, and then a telephone
reminder once the member arrives in Tucson.
  Also, try to renew your memberships on time so that we are not forced to mail reminders. Such considerations
will cut down the actual time that I will have to spend at day-to-day routine work. Besides the work which Mrs.
Panter did while in our employ, I have still to edit the Bulletin which is a sizable job in itself. Therefore, any
considerations in that respect will be much appreciated.
  Again, thank you very much for your help. Names and some of the letters of those who came to our
assistance will be published in up-coming issues.
