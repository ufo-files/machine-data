 INVESTIGATION TIPS AND FIELD EQUIPMENT USAGE

FIELD SAFETY: Accidents that happen in the field can be
prevented with a little forethought. Some research teams put
little effort to reduce risk in the field and to be prepared for
emergencies. Tripping over tombstones in the dark, falling
down steps and getting separated from your team are but a
few of the stories. All can be serious incidents. How about a
paranormal team member who neglected to tell the group of
medication he needed every 4 hours and almost died
because of his failure to do so. A few minutes devoted to
team safety before going out to investigate a report is not
only wise, it is essential.

   Phone numbers of local hospitals and emergency
    services for an area you are not familiar with.
   A first aid kit of the personal type is a must.
   An emergency strobe light attached to your gear or
    person can be a lifesaver if you are out and about in
    heavily wooded areas.
   A whistle is also a good carry item.
   Dress properly for the investigation and weather
    conditions.
   Watch weather for possible storms in the area you will
    be in.
   Carry a water vessel and an energy bar.
   If you start feeling fatigued sit down and rest. Heat
    stroke comes in many ways and can be deadly.
   Carry some hard candy in case a diabetic team
    member has an issue.
   Know your team’s medical needs. A bee sting can be
    as deadly to some as snakebite.
