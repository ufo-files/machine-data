There's booze in outer space.

No foo1 ing. Scientists probing areas of the Mi1ky Way with a power­

and dust called Sagittarius B 2.

Ethanol, for those who don't know, is ethyl alchol--chemical
formula C 2-H50H--the stuff that puts fire in firewater.

The discovery was made by a team of 13 scientists working at the
National Radio Astronomy Observatory at Kitt Peak, near Tucson,
Ariz. They were able to detect a substance 30,000 light years
away by picking up faint radio signals characteristic of the
chemica 1.

The work is part of a very sober inquiry into the formation of
stars. Present theory says stars are formed as swirling interstellar
clouds grow denser and denser and finally collapse to form a
star.

Unti 1 recentty, scientists thought the most complex substance in
the clouds was molecular hydrogen. Recently they have found more
complex molecules.   Ethanol, with nine atoms, is the most complex
found so far.

According to Dr. Barry Turner of the observatory, the chances of
finding a single molecule of Ethanol--which wouldn't go far in
making a cocktail--are about 1 in 1,000 at any given place.

Or, as colleague Dr. Benjamin Zuckerman of the University of
Maryland puts it:

"If Flash Gordon or Captain Kirk were to tow a net the size of
a football field through the whole cloud, you'd collect about enough
Ethanol to fill a shot glass. "
