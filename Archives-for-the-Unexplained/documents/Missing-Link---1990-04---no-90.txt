                          PERSONAL DREAM CONTEXT & REALITY

                                 By Brent Raynes, Associate Director
                                      Waynesboro, Tennessee


         The following event occurred during a personal period of spiritual searching and
      crisis, when I was desperately trying to make up my mind just what to believe in (even
      though I had formally accepted Christ in May 1975). It is of interest, I think, that
       about a couple of days before the following experience, I had silently asked God to
       give me a sign, and at the time that the experience actually occurred this was all the
       farthest thing from my mind.
         One night in the fall of 1975, I had a possible out-of-body experience. At any rate
       something happened. It seemed very real. I had just gotten into bed. Then the next
       thing I knew I found myself standing up, seemingly, walking away from the bed. Then
       about midway across the bedroom floor I found that I was unable to go any farther. I
       seemed silently directed by something to look out into the hallway. There appeared
       hundreds of tiny, pulsating, crystal-like translucent appearing white balls of light.
       Despite this odd perception I simply observed with interest, experiencing no fear or
       much of any other emotion. As I watched the lights seemed to cluster together to
       look like a four-legged animal facing in the direction of the closed bathroom door
       (where my father was at the time). This experience seemed to last only a few brief
       seconds,__ and _then suddenly I was back in bed wondering what had just happened.
         As I stated it seemed very real. In fact, I recall at first being puzzled as I seemed to
       be walking across the bedroom floor, thinking to myself that I didn't remember
       getting up out of bed, and marvelling a bit at that. But for some reason as the rest of
       the experience unfolded and transpired, I did not question it in the least.
          Shortly after this a UFO flap had erupted in Maine. One unusual case that
       commanded most of our attention at that time involved one David Stephens of Oxford
       as a central witness. Although on the surface of things it was a standard UFO case,
       with a large object rising from a corn field and following the car of Stephens and a
       friend down a narrow, winding country road in the wee hours of the morning,
       "visionary" elements also were involved. And, furthermore, I later examined the
       journal of a UFO sighter/researcher in Dover-Foxcroft, Maine, who had had standard
       UFO sightings herself for years and I found that she began having "visions," and many
       of them were similar to what Stephens had reported. (Unusual geometric, archetypal
       objects, patterns, and lis;hts.) Because these events had the earmark of "visions,"
       which for many are easily diagnosed as "hallucinations" (they clearly pointed to the
       mechanics of internal psychological processes no matter what the label or
       interpretation) one ufologist involved in this case cautioned me that I was focusing
       too much attention on this "visionary" stuff and ignoring the physical data. But as far
       as I was concerned I was taking up the slack. I had the distinct feeling that this case
       was very important in relation to the "visionary" events too, and later when I met the
       lady from Dover-Foxcroft I felt even more certain. Especially when I noticed the dates
       and saw that her "visions" began right around the time of the Oxford case.
           Later the Oxford case became somewhat of a "classic abduction" account,
       mentioned in books by Jim and Coral Lorenzen, and Budd Hopkins. This has
       certainly given me cause to pause and wonder about other "classics" that we hear
       and read about.
         Earlier in 1975, I had met Madeline Teagle of Ohio in my travels across country. I
       examined a lot of her written materials and heard first-hand the UFO experiences of
       her and others she introduced me to. In those cases too there were also these
      troublesome "visionary" elements. So that perhaps, I feel, made me more alert to
      them later in the Oxford case. In that case I felt that there was too much discussion
