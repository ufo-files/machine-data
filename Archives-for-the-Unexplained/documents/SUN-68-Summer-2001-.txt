The Japan Air Lines Alaska UFO Incident

         One of the more impressive UFO incidents reported at the May 9 press conference involved a Japan
Air Lines (JAL) 747 Jumbo-jet enroute to Japan from France (loaded with wine) as it entered the airspace
of Alaska prior to landing at Anchorage. Although the incident occurred shortly after sunset Alaska time on
Nov. 17, 1986, it did not get U.S. media attention until late December 1986 when the incident was reported
to the Japanese news media by the 747 pilot (Capt. Terauchi). The incident was described at Greer's
press conference (and in more detail in Greer's book) by John Callahan who said he was head of the
Federal Aviation Administration's Accidents & Investigations Branch at FAA headquarters in
Washington. According to Callahan, the incident prompted FAA Administrator Engen to hold a
special briefing which (allegedly) was attended by representatives of the CIA, FBI and President
Reagan's Scientific Study Team [???]. Callahan says that radar data and a transcript of radio
communications with the JAL crew were presented. But at its conclusion, CIA representatives
instructed everyone "that the meeting never took place, "according to Callahan. Also: 11Th is incident
was never recorded." (Emphasis added.)

        SUN's editor first learned of the JAL incident via CBS-TV news in late December of 1986.
We called the FAA's public affairs office to request a transcript of pertinent radio communications.
They promptly provided a copy-which was quite revealing. For instance, JAL first reported a giant
array of white and yellow lights directly in FRONT of the 747, estimated to be at approximately the
same altitude and only one mile away. The FAA controller said he saw no nearby air traffic on the
radar, but asked JAL whether it would like to change altitude (climb or descend). JAL replied:
~~Negative." [SUN Comment: A strange response if the pilot really suspected he might collide with
a giant unknown craft in a matter of a few seconds.) Five minutes later, the controller reported a
radar blip briefly appeared 11in trail of you [at] six o'clock position [i.e., directly behind the 747]. Do
you concur?" JAL replied: "NEGATIVE. [UFO is at] 11 o'clock, eight miles, same level." (Emphasis
added.) Curiously, this UFO's then-reported location relative to JAL closely matched the direction
of the planet Jupiter, which was extremely bright at the time, my investigation later revealed.

    Clifford E. Stone: "Spinner of (Very) Tall Tales"
            When SUN's editor first met former Sergeant First Class (SFC) Clifford E. Stone in Roswell
    (NM) in December 1991, and had dinner with him and his lovely Japanese wife, I learned of his long-
    standing interest in UFOs. (Since his then-recent retirement from the Army, Stone was working as
    a night security guard at a local factory.) He gave me a copy of his then-new, privately published,
    soft-cover 230-page book "UFOs: Let The Evidence Speak For Itself." The book's back cover carried
    the following Stone statement: "It is my hope that after you have had the opportunity to read this
    book, you will write your elected representatives in Congress and the White House demanding that
    the truth of what our government really knows about UFOs be made public." But never during our
    lengthy discussion in late 1991, nor during my nex! trip to Roswell in early 1994, did Stone mention,
    claim, or even hint, that he had been a member of an Army team whose mission was to recover
    crashed UFOs and their ET occupants-as he claimed at Greer's recent press conference.

             Fox News Channel showed Stone at the May press conference saying, "I was involved in
    situations where we actually did recoveries of crashed saucers ... and the debris thereof. There were
    bodies involved in some of these crashes, also some were alive. We know them to be of
    extraterrestrials. While we were doing all this, we were telling the American public there was nothing
    to it." In the lengthy interview with Stone in Greer's book, Stone refers to his duties as a member
    of the Army team which (allegedly) recovered crashed ET craft. During the interview Stone also
    claimed to have seen a live ET sitting at a table during his tour of a secret portion of the Pentagon's
    basement: "The entity was a little bigger than the 3 to 3-112 foot entities that are often reported."
    Stone claims that he and his Army team were sent to the USAF Bentwaters base in England in late
    December of 1980 to investigate a UFO incident. The incident was not reported to higher officials
    until roughly two weeks after Stone says his team arrived. A further challenge to this claim is that
    on Dec. 23, 1983, Stone made a Freedom Of Information Act (FOIA) request to the USAF for
    information on the Bentwaters UFO incident which he would have acquired during his (alleged)
    official visit in 1980. Further, Stone's request used his home address and did not show that he was
    an Army sergeant. Stone further claims that "another case we were involved with was the Iranian
    [Air Force] incident of Sept. 19, 1976." He falsely claims that the two IAF aircraft involved were
    disassembled following the incident, according to American field engineers at the IAF air bases.

              If Greer had obtained a copy of Stone's military record from the National Personnel Records
    Center in St. Louis, he could have learned that these and other such Stone claims were FALSE.
    Stone entered the Army on July 8, 1968, and after taking several months of "basic training" he was
    trained to be a "clerk/typist," later serving in Vietnam from Feb. 1970 until Aug. 1972 where his
    duties are listed as "clerk/typist." Stone then returned to the U.S to Fort Gordon, Ga., then to Fort
    Knox, Ky. His principal duty there was listed as "clerk/typist," but as of March 1975 his duty is
    shown as "administration specialist." In Aug. 1976, Stone was transferred to Germany where he
    served as an "administration specialist" until Aug. 1983 when he was transferred back to the U.S.,
    initially to Keesler AFB, Miss., and Fort Gordon, Ga. There his principal duty is listed as "Det. Sgt,"
    i.e., military policeman. In May 1985, Stone's last Army assignment was to the New Mexico Military
    Institute (a combination high school/junior college), where he served as "chief administrative clerk."
    [Strange assignments for an ET-craft-retrieval specialist.]

            In October 1987-roughly two years after Stone was assigned to NMMI-- his Army superior
    informed him that his work was unsatisfactory and that he should retire when he became eligible in
    mid-1988. When Stone refused, he was "relieved for administrative inefficiency" in January 1988.
    The primary basis for the criticism was Stone's personal UFO activities, including a feature story in
    the Roswell Daily Record in 1987 about his personal UFO activities and views, including his request
    for assistance from New Mexico's Sen. Peter Domenici.
