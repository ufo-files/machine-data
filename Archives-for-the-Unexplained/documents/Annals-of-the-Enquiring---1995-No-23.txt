        Initial reports were received with some incredulity , but the
fact that the craft had been sighted in several parts of North Cambs
and the surrounding district at different times, encouraged the belief
that some unknown airship was deliberately patrolling East Anglia in
the hours between sunset and sunrise.
        The authorities became particularly alarmed when an airborne
object appeared to frequently monitor troop movements in Gyppeswk
Park, Ipswich and rumour soon circulated that it must be German
spies. However, the mystery was never satisfactorily explained and
the unidentified craft eventually ceased its inexplicable night
manoeuvres.
        A flurry of nocturnal activity was reported throughout t he
length and breadth of England during February, 1913, when aerial
machines appeared again in t he skies; this time only a year prior to
the declaration of war between Britain and Germany.

witness Mr. C. T.Taylor and a companion, both of Scar borough, who

eventually towards Selby.

five miles. The object appeared to travel very slowly at times before
speeding up and after some 45 minutes, apparently reconnoitring the
neighbourhood, the light disappeared in the direction of Leeds.
