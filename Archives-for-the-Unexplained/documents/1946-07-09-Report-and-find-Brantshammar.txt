INFANTRY SCHOOL
SCHOOL EXPEDITION
DEPARTMENT 1
No. 258

To the air defense department
Östermalm street 87
Stockholm

On July 18, the State fiscal office in Sigtuna announced that an "explosion" had occurred in the vicinity of Brantshammar (12.5 kilometers North of Sigtuna) nine days earlier. Staff members from InfSS was tasked with investigating the site.

The following information was gathered upon arrival.

a) Mr. Karl Lundin, resident of Brantshammar, Knivsta, had acted as observer.

b) The site marked for investigation was a field at the South end of the "t" in Brantshammar.
 [? - translator's note.]

c) Time: July 9, 1946 14:45 PM. Mr. Lundin had initially kept quiet about his observation, since he figured that no one would believe him. However, on July 18 he was able to find two strange pieces (which he suspected to be bomb remnants) in this field. He then reported his findings to the state fiscal. InfSS personnel discovered a third piece during the same day.

d) According to the data we have received, the calculated direction of movement would have been from compass point 4900.

e) The tilting of the horizontal plane seems to have been around 15 à 20 degrees. There were no curvature or zigzag motion. The phenomenon was continuous and did not appear intermittently.

f) Sound effects: A strange hissing, which became louder at the time of the "explosion".

g) Visual effects: An orb (approximately about the size of a croquet ball), shining in all the colours of the rainbow. Its tail was estimated to be about 2.5 - 3 meters in size.

h) The "explosion" had apparently taken place approximately 1.25 meters above the ground.

i) The length of time ranging from the initial discovery to the "explosion" was 1 - 1.5 seconds. The "explosion" resulted in a shower of sparks. The observers had turned their back to the sun.

j) Apart from the above-mentioned remains, no interesting findings have been made.

These findings are alien to the surrounding area [pun intended – translator’s note]. Mr. Lundin has never before seen anything of the sort in the fields of Brantshammar. The fact that several phenomena was observed around the same time in various diverse places in the country leads to the assumption that it's all about meteorite impacts. The findings are sent in separately for examination.


Rosersberg, July 18, 1946

S. Ramström

B. Ahlström
