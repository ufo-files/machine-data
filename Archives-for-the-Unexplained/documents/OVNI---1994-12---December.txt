Last year, many parents bought telescopes for their children at Christmas. In January 1994, I
received dozens of letters from parents complaining about the instruments they had bought and
asking me for guidance on how to use them. Neither the children nor the parents could operate
the telescopes. Apart from parents wasting £100 or more, many children were totally
frustrated and baffled at not being able to use their new telescopes.

It would be useful to mention in the run up to Christmas that the cheap, nasty, little telescopes
advertised and displayed in many department stores and costing over £100 are totally
unsuitable for astronomical observation. If any parents are thinking of buying a telescope for
their children at Christmas (their child may just have a passing fancy in astronomy) they are
better advised to buy a decent pair of binoculars.

Binoculars will reveal many fascinating astro-objects of great interest, are relatively
inexpensive (compared with a proper astronon1ical telescope), are easy to store and carry, and
can be used for other activities.

When manufacturers/stores promote 'magnifying power' of telescopes, it is meaningless in that
'aperture' and 'light gathering ability' are far more in1portant than 'magnification'.
