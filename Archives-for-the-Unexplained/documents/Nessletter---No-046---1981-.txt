Cal ey Cruisers

  Caley Cruisers have just equipped one of their boats with some £ 15, 000's worth of
sophisticated sonar gear. This craft, 'New Atl antis', is a five berth cruiser and is
now avail abl e for hire by interested parties. The cost is £400 per week. Mr Jim Hogan,
director of Caley Cruisers, believes that this scheme, which was his brainchild, wil l
prove to be popular with scientist, students and school groups, although it is open to
anyone who wishes to search for Nessie.    They will have two sonar scanners and two echo
sounders to record any moving or stationary underwater objects in the loch. The charts
these pieces of equipment produce wil l be sent away for analysis. Anyone going on the
cruiser wil l be given four hours of expert tuition on how the equipment works as well as
being sent an instructional manual before they arrive. Then the parties will be abl e to
cruise around the l och, eat and sl eep aboard whil e carrying out their own survey.    Mr
Hogan feel s that the service wil l fill a need, and has said that every year he has had
reports of genuine sightings of the creatures.    Tim Dinsdal e has been involved in l aunch­
ing the project, and is confident that it will bring results.     He says .. I think it is an
excellent idea, in the past sonar has only really been on the loch for a few days but
this will be on board all the year round...
  As with the Loch Ness & Morar Project, it is hoped that a definite pattern will emerge
as to the animal's underwater behaviour. Then it would be possible to put cameras in
situations where they have very good chances of success.

Lake Champlain

Joe Zarzynski continues to keep me up to date with work on the Champlain front. He
has three reported sightings so far this year.   He has not had time to investigate them
thoroughly but sent brief detail s. Three women residents of Port Henry NY., watched
something large swimming in the waters of Bul wagga Bay for about 30 minutes on Friday
the 17th April. The weather was overcast and the lake calm. The women used binoculars
to watch the disturbance, and reported seeing something eel -like. One of them said she
could see humps. A fourth woman contacted by the three others, went to the shore and
also watched, she said she saw it surface four times and also saw a head.
  On April 19th there was a vague report of something being seen in mid-lake off Port
Henry, no further details.
  Four high school seniors from Elizabethtown, NY., reported that they had been within
30 feet of !Champ' on the 24th May. Cliff McDougal, Nat Morris, Bridgette Telkey and
Billy Kohen spotted something in the water from the beach of Kohen' s camp, and chased
after it in a motorboat.   McDougal described what he had seen, as about 20 feet long,
black, slimy, with a large hump with a jagged ridge. When they got to within 30 feet
of it, it submerged al l at once like a submarine. Not like a basking sturgeon, which
throws up its tail as it dives. Wil liam J Kohen, Bil ly's father and camp owner, said
he and a number of other adults saw the monster from shore but did not go out in the
boat.
  Joe hopes to spend a week at Loch Ness this year.   He wil l also spend much time at
Lake Champlain, as he has done in previous years. Last year he was helped by James
Kennard, as reported in earl ier Nessl etters. James operates his own sonar equipment,
and last summer had some further success. They found the wreck of another steamboat
in Lake Champlain, it was of the paddle-boat Champlain.   It had run onto a rock shelf
near Westport on the night of July 15th. 1875., the cargo and passengers were safely
got ashore. The hull was l ater stripped of all valuabl e bit5 and pieces and burned
to the water-line. It was then reported to have been towed into deep water and sunk.
James was very surprised to find the remains in just 25 feet of water. Of more interest
from our point of view, was the bl ip picked up under their boat in some 400 feet of water.
It indicated something 12 to 15 feet long moving in the water bel ow the boat. James
said it could have been a school of fish but rather doubted that, as the blip seemed
to be of something solid and large.

  The Sunday Post of 12th April 198 1 carried a report concerning a sonar contact by a
fishing trawl er. One day during earl y April , brothers Robert and James West, were on

and took the chance to try it out. The instrument is abl e to identify different shoals
of fish, their depth and size, and shows them in different col ours on a screen. James
was watching the set, when on the screen appeared a strange shape. The echo-sounder gave its
depth as 30 fathoms, and a size of 60 feet l ong and some 30 feet wide was indicated. The
brothers watched it for three minutes, as it moved al ong under the boat, it seemed to have
a tapering neck and a humped body. The brothers say the equipment proved beyond doubt
that it was not a shoal of fish, and are firml y convinced that it was the Loch Ness monster.
  This is a rather strange account, it woul d be very nice if we coul d accept it as a ' good'
Nessie contact. There have been few enough of them over the last two years. But while
it is bel ieved that there coul d be creatures as long as   60 feet, how can a width of 30
