true.'' A man who does not wish to be
named, who has lived and worked at
service stations across the Nullarbor,
believes the origin of the craft that
terrified the Knowleses is far more
earthly but perhaps no less sinister than
some people think. He points out that the
Nullarbor is close to several high security
areas - Maralinga, where the British
tested nuclear weapons in the 1950s, and
Nurrangar (near Woomera) where there
is a major US defence base, a focus of
controversy for many years. The man is
curious about Forr�st airfield, which is
80km from Mundrabilla. "Why is Forrest
so much off-limits?" he asks. "We see U2
spy planes flying in and out of there all
the time. Train loads of water and avia­
tion turbine fuel are transported to a
town called Cook which has a population
of only 200 people. "They can't, " he
says, "use two-and-a-half train loads of
water a week."
   Out at sea, he says, 22km from where
the incident took place, there are ships
moored 5km offshore with all their lights
on. They are not mother ships for
trawlers, he says, because there are no
trawlers about at the time.
   "I don't believe in little green men
with aerials sticking out their ears," he
says. What he does believe is that the
military and scientific community are
testing a new propulsion system that
involves electro-magnetic rotation - "in
other words," he says, "a flying saucer".
A theory involving the military "is the
only logical explanation to a lot of things
that have gone on. The police come
across electrical interference all the
time," he says. Radios blank out and
radar guns don't work. The man believes
the military are meddling with "ordinary,
everyday people" like the Knowleses as
part of a psychological experiment to find
out how far they can go ''before the
populace is terrorised". Since the inci­
dent, he says, a very effective disinforma­
tion campaign. has been operating.

THE KNOWLES FAMILY strongly ap­
prove of their car being on display at
Seven's Aussie Picnic. They want people
to see the car, Faye says, "so they can
see what happened to us". People, says
Patrick, "haven't seen anything like it
before". It also keeps the incident fresh
in people's minds. "Out here," says Colin
Norris, "this thing's fizzled out in an
absolutely appalling way.''
   "It seemed," says Patrick, "like a
nightmare, or something in a film. Some­
times I try to say to myself that it didn't
happen. But it's always there in your
mind. You get flashbacks, like we're on
the Nullarbor Plains again. There's a lot
of things happening out there that have
not been told."
   While looking at the Japanese UFO
magazine, one of Faye's sons asks her
about a particular photograph. The day
after the incident Channel 7 took the
Knowles family back to the Nullarbor and
shot some videotape. The picture comes
from that tape and shows Faye talking to
two men in a car. "Oh, I was talking to
American police. They were up there the
next day. They stopped me and asked me
all about it and they said they believed
me, what happened to us. They knew we
were coming over, what had happened."
   But, says Sean, they have Australian
police uniforms on and they are in an
Australian police car. "But," says Faye,
"they had American accents. It's weird. I
thought they were country coppers." •
