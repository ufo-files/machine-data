ON TROLL PATROL IN OHIO
     This weirdie from UAPA's home turf was reported by staffer Deborah
Winston of the Cleveland Plain Dealer on May 29th.   While it most pro­
bably deals with a hobo or derelict, it goes to show how some people
can turn the commonplace into the outre':
     When the police work up a composite on a suspect - and let's just
for argument's sake assume the suspect is a man - they have to rely on
witnesses' recollections of what the guy looks like.
     The cops want to know what he was wearing, his hair color and style,
his race, if he had any unusual physical characteristics (an "I love
Mom" tattoo on his right bicep, for instance), a guesstimate of his
height and weight and a description of his facial features.
     With that information, a police department is often able to come
up with a sketch and a written description that can be distributed to
the media with the hope that the suspect gets noticed and caught.   It
often works 1 ike a charm.
     Well, imagine the reaction at the Bainbridge Police Department last
week when they asked some fourth-grade girls to describe a suspicious
man they said they'd seen in the woods near K enston Intermediate School.
The girls said he looked like a troll.
     According to Police Chief James Jimison, the girls described the man
in the woods as a hazed-eyed, stocky, white and about 30 to 40 years old
