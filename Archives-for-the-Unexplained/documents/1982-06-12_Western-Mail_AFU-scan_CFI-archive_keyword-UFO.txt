                By JIM SMITH

.NEWLYWEDS Tony and Pam e la
-Williams are spending every spare
 minute of their brief honeymoon star
 gazing in West Wales.
    Unlike other happy couples, they have dif­
ferent reasons for holding hands under the
moonlight.
    They were brought together by a common
interest in UFOs and decided to spend their first
days of married life together on a "pilgrimage" to
the St Bride's Bay area of the Pembrokeshire
Coast National Park where many unexplained
sightings were reported five years ago.
    "It may seem a bit late but we are still
hoping to catch a glimpse of one," said 44-year­
old garage owner Mr Williams, who lives at Beck­
enham, Kent.
    He and Mrs Williams, aged 44, have been
married before but say that they were drawn
together after reading the same book which
dealt with reports on silver-suited space men and
strange objects being seen over the sky in the
Broad Haven area.
    "We are tracing the steps of the sightings
and hoping to speak to as many people as pos­
sible about the reports," said Mr Williams.
    Mrs Williams said, "I read this book about all
the sightings in Pembrokeshire and I later
loaned it to Tony."
