Uusi Suomi 16/7-1946.

9/6
Finland.

The observations of a light phenomenon that were made during the Pentecost couldn’t possibly have involved the occurrence of shooting stars. From what we can remember, a strange light phenomenon, directly assumed to have been derived from a human-invented device, was observed in the evening of June 9 in southern Finland.

PhD V.R. Olander at the University of Helsinki Observatory, who also happened to observe the phenomenon itself, stated that on the basis of his own observations, the incident proved highly unlikely to show evidence of a meteor strike.

Using the summary of observations made in different parts of the country as support for their hypothesis, scientists have stated that there is no way of proving that the object was, in fact, a shooting star. After having flown quite straight, the projectile suddenly turned and then continued back along a straight path. This fact completely excludes the possibility of a meteor, which makes no curves during its flight path, except possibly when it falls to the ground, which wasn’t the case in this matter. A human-built device must therefore be considered a far more likely explanation. In many respects, its significant speed is apparently based on entirely new principles. Interestingly enough, similar phenomena have indeed been observed several times during the summer (most recently yesterday morning, in Helsinki).
