                 AIRLINE PILOTS REPORTS TOP FANTASTIC WORLDWIDE SIGHTING$

     A UFO that paced an airliner; another that terrorized a family while they were
camping; an object that forced a truck off a road and another that paced a truck for
about 50 miles, "broke into segments, wandered around the sky, and then fused together
again"; and others that caused physiological effects, animal reactions, landed and
emitted sounds, are some characteristics of a fantastic international roundup of
sighting reports from five states and four foreign countries. There was even a UFO
that was reportedly photographed and pieces of a "saucer" that were found in a wit­
ness' yard after an explosion alle� edly ripped it apart. Witnesses include airline
pilots, a glider pilot, Air Force { AF) personnel, a police constable and a newspaper
man.
     Airline pilots flying over South Africa reported three different sightings of
UFOs in early July, according to the July 9, 1972, edition of the Sunday Express.
     During the first week in July, at approximately 8:45 p.m., Chester Chandler, a
South African Airways captain, was piloting his Boeing 727 over the Durban area when
he and his crew, including Graham Smith, senior flight officer, and G. Koekemoer,
flight engineer, saw a UFO.
     "It paced the p1ane for a whi1e before veering off to the southwest and whi1 e
this was happening -- at a height of 2,743 meters -- we gave ground control at Durban
a running coiTillentary on the object's moves," the pilot stated.
     The plane was flying at 250 knots and was preparing its landing descent when tile
sighting occurred.
     "The shape of the UFO was indistinct," Capt. Chandler continued. "It was a
bright light which ground control assured us was not emanating from any aircraft they
knew of. They also checked with the South African Air Force and with other authori­
ties, but drew a negative reply. Ground control was very excited about the object as
it is their responsibility to see that nothing is in our flight path. The crew and I
checked the flight of the object against the stars and against lights on the ground,
and came to the conclusion that it was not a star or aircraft."
     A few days earlier, Capt. Chandler spotted an object during a flight to Capetown.
It sped across the sky from north to south, but "didn't come anywhere near the
aircraft."
     On July 5, another South African Airways Boeing 727 crew saw a brown UFO that
flew under the plane during a flight to Capetown. Ground control personnel there re­
ported no aircraft in the area.
     During the same time period, "dozens of Johannesburg families" saw an orange,
glowing object cross the sky "at tremendous speed."
     UFOs also appeared in the skies over England during t1ay and June.

     •  April 3, 1972. About 12:30 a.m. Queenstown, New Zealand. Mrs. Donna Scheib
and "about eight other people" saw "nine balls of lights cross the skies," according
to the April 12, 1972, edition of the Invercargill, N.Z., SouthZand Times. Two or
three bright lights led the formation, but several others appeared to fade from sight
during the flight. Two metal balls found on a nearby Ashburton farm were believed to
be from the Russian satellite, Cosmos 482. Dunedin residents also reported seeing the
objects and some said they heard "booms," stated the April 7, 1972, edition of the
Dunedin Otago Daily Times. Later that day, at 8:30 p.m., a Napier woman reported see­
ing an object that burned itself out, the Napier DaiZy TeZegraph, April 7, 1972,
stated. (Possible explanations: reentry of Cosmos 482 or the breakup of a meteor).
