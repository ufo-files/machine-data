There is much disorganised and diverse activity before a science is born.
The many different theories and ideas eventually get weeded out until one
main core theory,               a paradigm,     becomes predominant. At this point,                 the pre­
science has become a normal science. Scientists do experiments and research
on one particular aspect of that paradigm. Eventually,                             enough observations
may have accumulated for the scientists to question the validity of the central
paradigm and a crj sis develops.                     There follows a period of revolution when
a new central paradigm is developed, and a new normal science develops.
     A "normal scjcnlisL" should be uncritical of the central paradigm.                                        In
a normal,     mature science there are very few disagreements over fundamentals,
only over small details relating to the paradigm. This distinguishes a normal
science    from     the         disorganisation       and    fundamental    disagreements      of    a    pre­
science. In a pre-science, there will be nearly as many theories as researchers
and this prevents detailed research into any one of them.
     Another philosopher of science,                    Imre Lakatos, has developed a different
"methodology       of     scientific         research programmes",         which   can   be   seen       as    an
extension of Karl Popper's falsificationism.                         It also includes some elements
of Kuhn's theory. In Lakatos's scheme, a scientific research programme should,
firstly,    have a degree of coherence whereby it can decide upon future areas
of research.       Secondly,            the programme should lead to the discovery of novel
phenomena.       (Thus,         Marxism and Freudian psychology are not sciences,                    because
they fail to meet the first criterion, and sociology is not a science because
it fails to meet the second.)
     In Kuhn' s scheme,                 the most important distinction between a science and
a non-science is the ability of the subject to support "normal science".
