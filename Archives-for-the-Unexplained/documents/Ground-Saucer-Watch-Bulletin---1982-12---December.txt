                           DIRECTLY SPEAKING
                        By: William H. Spaulding


The year 1982 ended as silently as it started, without the projected
influx of numerous saucer reports.       Throughout the years of flying
saucer incidents, especially during the high concentration periods, in
which sauce rs a re reported in high volume, there always has been a
certain cyclic pattern.    Saucer history (1947 to present), has shown a
marked increase in serial phenomena incidents, occurring approximately
at five year intervals.     If history maintains its pattern (?) then a
rash of reports . should start in 1983.

Saucer enthusiasts have in the past, and will most likely in the
future,  begin  to   prepare  for   next  year's  projected  increase  of
sightings.   There has been talk about the use of new field investi-
gation manuals,    quick-strike   teams  (the  ability  to  get  civilian
investigators into an area in short order) and improved analysis
methods.

If the projected "flap" of sightings does transpire it would be
paramount to determine the real cause of the cyclic regularity of such
events.    Past pattern studies, with and without computer support,
reveals that although saucer reports exit at almost any time during a
given year, it is the dramatic increase in sightings approximately
every five years that has created the false illusion of a recognizable
and established trend.    Many political situations, social aspects and
  -
even the position of certain planets have been associated with the
re-occurance of saucer events.      Few researchers take these studies
seriously, as none of these events can be substantiated.  Yet, it makes
a good point if someone was attempting to provide a correlation to
extraterrestrial visitations.

What could be the source behind these major cycles of activity?   There
are many things which could act as a catalyst to these occurrences and
some of them could be directly related to the saucer researcher
himself.    In the past, a particular spokesperson for a major UFO
organization or a "famous" independant investigator has been asked or
voluntarily told the media that "an upswing in reports is shortcoming'' ·
There may also have been mention of the 5 year cycle as an added
incentive.  This weak proclamation, on its own, certainly did not start
a build-up of saucer reports, regardless of how much publicity it
generated.   Yet, it has had a limited effect, possibly only psycho-
logically, to act as a stimulus.

On the negative side of this theorizing are the fundamental facts that
each time a saucer spokesperson (pro or anti in their belief) would
predict a rapid increase in sightings by the body politic, due to
entertainment stimuli of an event such as; Close Encounters of the
Third Kind or ET: An Extraterrestrial, no appreciable influx has been
recorded.    Although the effect of extraterrestrial visitations, as
presented in popular movies, could have a slight influence on empirical
observations of saucer-like phenomena, logically it shouldn't start the
masses from reporting numerous sightings.
What apparently has transpired is a "stronger" interest in the over-all
subject.   The real culprit for increased reports of saucer events is
the media.   News coverage in both respectable radio/television shows
and the tabloids ignite greater interest.  A new pattern study shows an
influx of saucer reports commensurate to the volume of favorable and
sensational news coverage.

The ultimate stimuli could easily come from an intelligence agency,
through   either   indirect   (misinformation)   or   direct  interface
(manipulation) of a few key events.   This could spark the interest and
create a parallelism with the media reporting on the subject.       The
events leading up to the flap of 1952 have revealed a definite
government invo.lvement, by having a high "unknown" rate of cases (20%)
and, in turn, giving the central leadership of the saucer investigation
to the CIA.

The inability to recognize "real" data patterns from the bogus, will
only add more confusion to an investigative effort overwhelmed by
rumors, theories and unprovable cases.

Ever since GSW started investigating the strong possibility that some
of the so-called bona fide UFO events were directly related to
government   testing,  numerous   quantifiable   incidents  have   been
discovered.  In late October (1982) the U.S. Army announced it would be
performing new flight tests on its Pershing 2 missile.      A test was
scheduled ind flown on November 4, 1982 and was the first of two
missiles to be fired from the McGregor range near El Paso, TX.      The
launches will be high-trajectory flight paths to a target approximately
100 miles uprange at the White Sands Missile Range (WSMR).

The target for the third launch will be augmented by radar reflectors
to aid in judging the performance of new guidance radar equipment.
Other tests will occur from a launch site forty miles southwest of
Mountain Home AFB (Idaho) and will be fired cross-statelines to WSMR.

These tests, if launched in good observational conditions, will most
likely be misinterpreted as flying saucers by unwitting witnesses.

On behalf of the entire GSW staff we wish everyone in the UFO field a

                   The Politics of . Saucer Researching


The rationale of a comparison of our basic political structure in
parties and issues within the U.S. Government can be a perfect
parallelism to the structures existing within flying saucer research.
It is extremely easy to see the obvious differences between the Far
Leftists and the Extreme Right in ufology, after only a brief exposure
to the personalities and their viewpoints.

The saucer field primarily consists of a small diversified army of
investigators,   theorists,  scientists,   hobbiests  and enthusiastic
readers of UFO literature that strongly support five major theories of
saucer origin.  Unfortunately, each of these theories are different in
scope and when interfaced with the raw emotionalism of the supportive
parties, the end result is one of non-logic and disarray.

Prior to commenting on the effects of these divisions, it is necessary
to define each of the categories which generate the basis of the
general saucer belief system.

The Far Left has a basic position that saucers represent a strange
phenomena    that  is  most  likely  related    to  psychic  or  mystical
experiences.    In this camp of Liberals one will find an abundance of
Contactees and a fringe of theorists who boldly link saucers with ESP,
monster cases, multiple dimensions and other paranormal delights.   Many
of the saucer believers in this category left the moderate extra-
terrestrial hypothesis (ETH) position simply because they became tired
of  this  not-yet-provable   theory.    To   the   Liberal, with extreme
inclinations, the mystical theories offer intriguing new hope for a
solution to the saucers and answers many of the difficult problems
associated with ETH.

Although many of those involved with the more "conventional" saucer
beliefs scoff at this segment of saucer enthusiasts, this division by
sheer numbers constitutes the vast percentage of the total saucer
movement.    Generally this category has little scientific research and
relies heavily upon unsubstantiated data and the testimony of many
unreliable   witnesses   by  any  standard.    Recently   some  Moderate
researchers, by definition, have promoted the cause of the Far Leftists
by placing an unwarranted importance on contact-type cases, which ten
years ago would not be remotely considered by the Moderate individual.
This fact alone has done more to enhance the Liberal cause by supplying
an added ''aura of importance" to saucer encounters of this variety.

Moderate  Position  (slightly  left)-  The  philosophy in   this  group
believes that saucers are real and most likely extraterrestrial space
craft.   Many feel that some additional research is needed to confirm
their obvious belief and that the U.S. Government is deliberately
withholding important information from the public which would prove the
existance of saucers.
Years ago when publicity permeated all forms of the media, this was the
category of the greatest activity.  The belief in ETH created a popular
grass-roots movement for years, until recently when the once-vast
numbers in this group started to view the saucer phenomenon from a
different perspective.    History has proven the entire civilian UFO
movement was managed improperly.   Nothing has been qualified during the
past thirty plus years to claim that the saucer phenomenon originates
from outer space.    Many of the "better" researchers have shifted
drastically to the right to form a majority of disgruntled/discouraged
believers.

Many of the individuals who are researching the crashed saucer cases
fall into this category. The highly sensational cases of crashed discs
have gained some respectability due to the reputation of Moderates in
this group.    The entire concept of crashed saucers implies alien
visitation, yet gains slight respectability, because reports of this
variety, involving contacts with aliens, is not as acceptable to the
Moderately Left group, as is the case with the Liberal element
perspective.

Moderate Position (slightly right)        This element of the saucer
movement has a basic position that saucers are unexplainable phenomena.
Most of these "serious" researchers claim that vigorous scientific
research is required to establish a substantial explanation to the
reports of saucers.

The few scientists that are interested in saucers have assumed a
position near the center, almost non-committal.       Ineffective research
due   to  a lack  of   formal   funding   is  obvious.     The  only   main
contributions to the UFO field made by the scientific community is some
work   done  on  soil   evaluations   from   purported   "landing"   areas,
photographic interpretation systems, statistical data analyses and the
short-comings of hypnosis and the polygraph as a viable tool for saucer
investigative work.    After all these years and all the reports, the
saucers still remain a mystery.

The Moderate Right has a position that can be classified as one of
cautious optimism.    To the hordes of disgruntled believers they are
still waiting for the Moderate scientist to take some positive stand on
the phenomenon which may rekindle their belief.   Other than that faint
hope, the discouraged perceives that the scientific community is doing
nothing.

The New Wave      Right Wing Thinking     This particular element of
thinking   maintains  a  basic  position  that  the   saucer phenomenon
represents a ruse, created by the U.S. intelligence agencies as a cover
for both weapon and psychological testing.    That the small residue of
bona-fide reports are real, at least to the observer, as part of a
preconceived saucer belief system.  Observed elements of this phenomena
include; illegal rocket firings across state lines, remotely piloted
vehicles, ECM, Stealth development vehicles, germ/nerve gas experiments
on animals (cattle mutilations) and psychological testing on unwitting
witnesses that may have generated some of the Close Encounters of the
Third Kind cases.
Although this approach to the saucer movement fills the gap between the
hardline groups of ETH believers and the extreme Conservative element
that advocate that reports are hoaxes and/or misinterpretations, only a
small segment of researchers are actively involved in this way of
thinking.

This  theory,  although extremely   tenable,  seriously questions  the
credulity of the saucer researcher and suggests that his entire effort
which he perceives as a scientific breakthrough for mankind, may be a
facade which he directly helped due to his involvement.

The Far Right - Conservatives - This extreme position is the easiest
path to take.    Simply stated, th~ Conservative believes that all
reports are hoaxes and misinterpretations and to help this cause, over
ninety percent of all sightings fall into this category. Conservatives
maintain that further research is pointless and that all of the
previous government studies have proven this point.

The stereotyping of the skeptics, that all saucer reports represent
nothing, is beginning to take a greater hold on body politic.     Since
the entire saucer movement is cyclic, i.e., both the popularity and
apathy rise and fall with the volume of publicized reports, the entire
movement is easy prey for the forceful skeptics that ridicule the
entire concept of flying saucers.   It's a pointless argument for those
advocating ETH to scream that their theory is correct when this segment
is divided with in-fighting, a lack of central leadership and a lack of
free thinking ability by its continual promotion of outer space
visitations.

The intent of the conservative element is to let the saucer movement
bury itself and that eventually the views of the Condon Committee, the
published findings of the Air Force and the negative findings of the
GSW and CAUS lawsuit will have its intended effect and that the
advocates of additional research will become tired of attempting to
promote further saucer research.

These writings will not attempt to categorize all of the more famous

a perception based on previous writings and comments of these figures.

    ARE FLYING SAUCERS MANUFACTURED BY THE UNITED STATES GOVERNMENT?
                    A CLOSE LOOK AT THE PROS & CONS


It would be advantageous to both the saucer skeptic and the grass-roots
believer to know that the United States or some other technology-laden
world power has built a flying saucer-type vehicle.   Initially it would
certainly   help  the   long-time  believer  in   the   extraterrestrial
hypothesis.   He could now claim that all though his basic theory of
origin was misjudged, the bottom line would be the substantiated fact
that a disc-like object does exist.     It would be a matter of simply
adjusting ones theory of origin rather than having to admit that the
saucer researcher has been wasting a lot of effort over nothing.

The skeptic as well would have to admit he was wrong, that some of the
reports   were  something  much  greater   th~n misinterpretations  and
hoaxes.  However, if a bona-fide vehicle, with a general description of
a flying disc did exist, he could easily play-up the absurdity of those
that zealously promoted outerspace visitations for all these years.
If . this hypothesis did prove true, all sides of the saucer mystery,
would win.    On the positive side, the scientific community which
previously treated the saucer phenomenon as so much ado about nothing,
would most likely become interested to the point of "leaving the
closet" and may even begin a new serious inquiry into the once-popular
subject.    Moderate-classified researchers would readily jump to the
opportunity to re-evaluate the older "classics" and the stubbornly
unsolvable unknown reports, which always, unwarrantly, obtained the
status of extraterrestrial in origin.

The negative effects of this hypothesis would be the remainder of the
players in the saucer field, who would easily say that a government-
built saucer would only represent a small percentage of the volume of
saucer reports.   To wit:  This theory woul4 not explain the reports of
saucers that simply "vanished" in flight or the scores of contact /
communication incidents with alien entities.   Unfortunately, there are
thousands of persons who, to some degree, support these misgivings and
would not change their thinking just because the United States admitted
it has been secretly manufacturing and testing a saucer vehicle.

To validate the potential of an American-built disc machine, it is
important to review certain quantifiable facts, basic logic and the
politics of such an undertaking.      The basic premise which we are
discussing is the building of a secret craft, the obvious clandestine
testing  of   the  subject machine(s)   and  the  capability of total
concealment of such a grandiose plan.  Can it happen?

Initially,   there   are  two  logical  elements  to   consider in   the
manufacturing of a disc.      One, the government has a super secret
underground base, with extensive manufacturing capability to build
circular airfoils.      This base which is militarily controlled, is
staffed by civilians with classified clearances, and has the ability to
remain covert.    The base must be underground, as it would be easily
detected by Soviet surveillance satellites.         The control of the
civilian work force would have to be perfect, as no one, to date, has
come forth with a "story'' about a secret factory with the capibility to
build saucers.   Even the cover of a "Manhatten Project" syndrome would
complicate matters to a further confusing end.    This would imply that
numerous divisions, permeating universities and the aerospace community
are working independently, each building a complex component for the
final assembly.

The second possibility theorizes that one or a combination of airframe
and engine manufacturers are building the disc at their plants, working
under a secret contract to avoid public disclosure and Congressional
inquiries.   The basic components are manufactured at their respective
plants or testing facilities.

Employee exposure to the project would be high and it would be nearly
impossible to keep full rein to prevent any leakage to the body politic
and media.   Surely a photograph, a drawing, a classified document or
rumor would be circulated.
The potential for maintaining secrecy on the subject project could be
enhanced if the machine(s) were built outside the United States.       A
good location to cover such a program · would be the vastness of
Australia.   This should not be scoffed at, since the recent saucer
reports have seemingly 'moved' to this continent.  Obviously, something
is responsible for the influx of reports of strange aerial phenomenon.

The basic idea of building a saucer sounds terrific and may be the
answer to the small residue of unexplained aerial events.      However,
there are just as many negative points against this possibility, and it
cannot be accepted as a blanket answer to the source of the saucers.
At times it is as absurd as the extraterrestrial mistruths.

The following   fun~amental   facts are pertinent to the proposed theory:

o    No data has surfaced to substantiate that      the U.S. Government has
     been building a flying saucer.

o    In culling the thousands of reports of purported flying saucers, a
     small percentile s t i l l defy any conventional explanation.   It
     appears that some bona-fide structured craft were observed.

o    Any extraordinary craft would have to be built with the expertise
     of the country's leading airframe and engine manufacturers.

0    Leading   aviation  specialists and  journalists       report  it  is
     impossible to cover-up the development of flying       disc craft for
     any appreciable length of time.

o    What would be the purpose of spending millions of R&D dollars to
     build a saucer and not use it for commercial or warfare purposes?

o    If it was a technological flop and the proposed disc did not meet
     the criteria for which i t was designed, then the reports of
     bona-fide saucer incidents should have stopped at the same time
     the development work ceased on the saucer craft (FNl).

0    The  U.S.  Government   does     not  have  it's  own  facilities   to
     manufacture secret craft.        It uses the facilities of McDonald
     Douglas, Hughes, Boeing, et      al.  It does, however, have its own
     design, testing, inspection      and semi-assembly facilities.    Even
     the top secret White Sands       Missile Base has civilian aerospace
     companies working on site.

Recently GSW has been receiving information from purported ex-military
officials, that claim to have seen a U.S.-built flying saucer.  In the
same manner of the crashed saucer incidents, much of their report
cannot  be   substantiated  and  little  supporting  evidence  can  be
uncovered.    In many of the incidents the converse is true, whereas,
more negative information is discovered that negates the witness'
claim.
Whether the rumors of U.S. built saucers is a plot of deliberate
misinformation,  a  hoax for    fun or profit   or  a  test  of  saucer
researchers credulity, the bottom line says, there is neither logic nor
facts to quantify the theory.

Although many things are possible, the mathematical probability is nil
for the U.S. Government to keep the "secret" on the development of disc
craft.     Massive  conspiracies   and  cover-ups   sound  interesting,
mystifying and offer an avenue of hope for those in this field who
cannot find the answers to the elusive saucers.     After all, it's so
much easier to blame the Government of a cover-up, than to cast doubts
on the basic belief system.

Yet, is it possible that hundreds or thousands of conspirators are
responsible for the perceived saucer cover-up?     How could all these
people keep so quiet for all these years?   Surely some credible bit of
information would have surfaced by now.   If a cover-up does exist, it
cannot be massive nor can it involve numerous people.  For a conspiracy
to be successful only a handful of collaborators could be involved.
This methodology lessens the chance for a leak of classified data and
controls the operation by knowing what everyone is doing.

The "small force of conspirators theory" hardly seems likely if the
U.S. is in the business to build a high-technology saucer craft.   What
does seem to make more sense, would be the development of numerous
secret   programs ranging  from  remotely piloted vehicles   (RPV)   to
stealth-capable airfoils.    News of these projects has leaked, in a

          SPECULATION VS SCIENCE:  WHO'S RIGHT -   WHO'S WRONG
                        By:  Kenneth Firestone


In all probability, the primary reason behind the lack of 'positive'
evidence regarding UFOs is due to the never-ending conflict between the
speculations of the interested public (saucer hobbiests, buffs) and the
scientific community.   For years the saucer buff and elements of the
scientific community and the government have been engaged in a battle
of "do the saucers exist or don't they?".       In the interest of the
subject; if it weren't for the opposing forces hashing about beliefs
and theories, t~ere would be nothing interesting or controversial about
UFOs.    In any discipline, for i t to be stimulating, there must be
competitors striving towards the same goal.    In the analogy of flying
saucers, there never seems to be an end to the game and victory is
apparently unimportant so long as there is enough interested fans to
keep the event going.

There has been a rapid decrease in saucer interest since the end of the
futile Air Force Project Blue Book endeavor.    I use the word "futile"
because the only apparent evidence they seem to have uncovered was the
stacks of near-worthless paper which they effectively stored deep
within their files.  There are some in the UFO field that believe there
is some important data, like the frequently referenced Hangar 18 rumor,
which would drastically change the course of ufology, if this "secret"
information could be realized.   Will we ever learn what is inside the
contents of that stored matter?     It is entirely possible that there
isn't any hidden material that contains important information.

As long as the Air Force was involved in the investigation of saucers,
people thought there was something highly important about the unusual
aerial objects.  The Air Force with their aircraft and space technology
was held in high esteem by the public when it came to dealing with an
aerial mystery.  But when the Air Force terminated their investigation,
(circa 1969), it put a damper on UFO interest.  By proclaiming that the
flying saucers really weren't anything at all, the Air Force contribut-
ed to mass apathy regarding the subject.  There were those who believed
UFOs were from an extraterrestrial origin and were here to save us from
self-destruction, but the lay majority of the populous saw their
interest diminish when the Air Force's inquiry faded.

With the Government's negative proclaimations paramount, the atmosphere
was conducive for speculating.    The writer use to feel there was no
room in the saucer movement for speculation.   Yet, speculation was the
next natural step, since no one in authority was offering anything but
negative comments on the subject and about those few who closely
followed it.    Speculation takes on an aura of added importance, in
light of the way science treated the subject.       One could say that
science failed in its half-hearted research attempt.          How could
anything be said scientifically about saucers, since no one actually
knows what they represent?     Science is a strict discipline, i . e . ,
chemistry, biology, et al, and since there is little known about
saucers they have been erroneously placed in a branch of "science"
similar to the occult.
Why did it take the Air Force, CIA, FBI and countless other government
agencies close to 22 years to arrive at zero results?     A series of
logical questions arises regarding the saucer phenomenon.

o   Why did the government use members of the scientific community to
investigate something that officially did not exist?   Was it to appease
the  small  portion  of  the  pop.ulous that were    claiming  that  the
government was covering up important data?

o   Were all of the people used to maintain a fictitious high level of
mystery to aid in the prolonging interest of the saucer subject?      If
so,  what  could  be   the purpose?    If  the   people  who  officially
investigated th~ saucers were just creating a ruse, there would be far
too many and therefore they had to be externally simulated with "false''
information.   Anything less than this possibility and surely someone
would have come forth with some information.   The purpose of creating a
facade, would be to simply keep the interest high to divert anyone with
serious research capability away from the subject.

If there was anything scientifically gained from the government's
"research'', then the data has yet to be seen.    The various programs and
Ad Hoc Committee sessions always concluded that the saucers were no
threat to national security.    For 35 years we have been listening to
the same old line.    At least that's what we have all been told.       Did
these high level sessions reveal more than this?     If the government has
never captured a saucer, then how do they know they do not constitute
violence?     Or is there a possibility that there is an outer space
vehicle locked inside the walls of a military base somewhere?        Still,
after all the years of saucer inquiry, I thought it was an insu~t to
our  intelligence   that  we  could  not   reveal   the  identity   of  the
phenomenon known as flying saucers.    It is a basic truism that America
possesses an impressive technology, which is capable of launching deep
space telemetry probes, manned space missions and new developments in
the field of medicine that hinge on the spectacular.

With these facts evident, an important question surfaces - why hasn't
any substantial  evidence  been obtained   on  a  simple  aero-dynamic
configuration    the  flying  saucer?    The  answer  may  not  be  so
complicated and most likely represents two separate philosophies as
follows:

A -   There isn't any strong supportive evidence to collect, since all
      saucer reports represent a small residue of hoaxes and a vast
      percentage of misidentified conventional objects and phenomenon.
      With this level of thinking, the more time spent on researching
      saucers the less knowledge one will gain.

B -   There appears to be a basic problem in the UFO field, as
      considerable evidence is placed on the saucer debate than with
      resolving the reports of UFOs.    This is due to the fact that
      "researchers" forgot or never realized their priorities/purpose
      and some selected to chase stardom, with public appearances and
      media interviews.
The early sixties     truly marked   the beginning of   popular  saucer
theorizing, with the pro-saucer element and the anti-UFO groups lashing
at one anothers ego.       The anti was claiming there were logical
alternatives to all UFO sightings and the pro element were forever
protecting their---r8vorite theory that UFOs were from the vastness of
outer space.    The entire UFO controversy reminds me of religion,
whereas the fanatic, though he hasn't all the answers is never wrong
and the skeptic inelegantly attacks the heart of the belief.

Did you ever wonder why researchers never worked in unity?   Maybe they
have a nuclear bomb or a pumpkin pie for Thanksgiving, but UFOs, never!
Simply because the saucers are only a belief and with any belief system
comes the argumentive controversy that supercedes the subject and any
attempt at a unified inquiry into the matter is impossible.

For those who have witnessed what they perceive to be a flying saucer,
I believe you, but the cold fact remains that no one can claim their
origin.    For years scientists have theorized the feasibility of life
visiting Earth from another planet/solar system and the type of machine
it would take to get here.    Some of the 'braver' scientists have gone
as far as to say that a real "mystery" exists.    That is as far as they
will   go,   they  cannot offer   substantial  evidence   to  prove  the
extraterrestrial hypothesis.

The pro-saucer researcher can only off er an opinion based on a belief
that we are being visited by space creatures.      There is no real means
of substantiating this theory - only a feeling that they are right.
The anti-ufologist is happy to tear down the pro-saucerian belief
because he is upset with all the publicity that has been generated on
the subject or for some other negative reason.        The only dedicated
researchers may be the 'debunker', a title given to a small group note
worthy of   their  true  intentions.     This   category,  at   least the
professional ones, have not taken a side one way or the other on the
basic belief system.  They simply try to ' discover the who, what, where,
when and why on the subject of sightings of UFOs.          By diligently
tracing leads, sources and at times, gut-feelings, the 'debunker' tries
to place in another piece of the saucer puzzle.       Generally, they are
not famous like the present celebrities within the field and it isn't
their intent to be popular.     Their function is to accumulate fresh,
provocative and factual data to aid in the identification of the UFO.
Had saucer research taken that stand 35 years ago, we may have already
known the truth behind the mystery today.

So, who's right and who's wrong?  The answer, of course, is no one. To
date either the speculative or the scientific side hasn't one single
shred of evidence to change the way of thinking on the subject of
flying saucers.  The debates and the confusion go on.

                            WHY FLYING SAUCERS?

                           By: Kenneth Firestone

                           ' t.• I ,


If you had ever seen a missile speeding its way through the sky and did
not know it was a missile, what shape would you say it was?      If you
guessed tubular than you are right, but what about those individuals
who may have witnessed a Pershing missile gone astray from the White
Sands Missile Range (New Mexico) heading on a track towards Colorado?
On November 19, 1964 i t happened.     The missile which overshot its
target by over 300 miles, crashed in Creede, Colorado.

In late October (1982), eighteen years after its launch, large portions
of the missile were found.   A White Sands spokesman stated the reason
that the missile malfunctioned was the failure of an explosive charge
implanted in the rocket's second stage to slow down the missile's
thrust.   It was this malfunction that allowed the ballistic weapon to
venture into Colorado instead of its original destination - the vast
plains of White Sands.

As the streaking projectile transversed over populated areas, what
might have been the thoughts of the witnesses?    Probably most of them
assumed i t was a flying saucer.      Not tubular, ball or triangular
shaped, but saucer-like.    From the conception of modern day saucer
sightings ( 1947) to present, misinterpretations of conventional and
non-conventional aerial configurations generated a profuse number of
saucer reports.  These misinterpretations have several causes:

o   The frantic psychological state of the                    observer     at   time    of   the
    occurance to relay any accuate details.

o   Inadequate knowledge of observational techniques.

o   The eager acceptance   by          so   many   UFO   witnesses   of   the   basic   saucer
    belief system.

Conditioning to the saucer belief system began with the popular Kenneth
Arnold sighting in June of 1947 when he t~ld the media the objects he
observed looked like    saucers skipping upon water".    Next the media
exploited the term "flying saucers      giving i t both dominance and
popularity over all other geometric shapes, sizes and types of outer
space vehicles.   With the public being influenced with 'saucers hype',
there was a time during the fifties and sixties, when every UFO sighted
had to be a flying saucer.

In the earlier days of the official and unofficial UFO investigations
the public became even more aware of flying saucers as the press, UFO
groups and the Air force often referred to the subject as     saucers ,
even in some official reports.  Due to this conditioning, using 'flying
saucers' as a stimulus, many of the objects were reported as saucer-
shaped, which otherwise may not have been.          At least a better
descriptive could have been used to report aerial phenomena.
If the general public wasn't so gullible and they were more educated in
the identification methods of UFO's then we may have had a more
understandable data base.   Unfortunately, the flying saucer was a tool
for protagonists to sell their products such as books, magazines,
newspapers, movies, etc. 'The term, saucer, was a stimulating, at times
captivating  device   and no   other  shape  could   lure the  public's
attention.

Some of the interest diminished after the failure of the Air Force
attempt to build and fly a flying saucer.       There were circulating
rumors that a jet powered disc and the Avro car were past the designing
stages and ready for test flights.      Unfortunately, none of these
concepts proved successful.    To a portion of the public this added
significance to some of the observations of flying discs/saucers.

Did the government ever really consider using the flying saucer as an
airfoil  to  replace more    conventional  shapes?    Did  the  military
deliberately induce failure of their trite flying saucer program to
create apathy of the saucer phenomenon?   The saucer had a definite role
in the UFO subject, mainly for solidifying the belief process.       The
saucer looked like a ship from space whose intelligence was years ahead
of us in technology.

However, common reasoning tells us that the saucer is a poor design for
outerspace use because of its limited space within, but of course the
"pilots" wre only four feet tall, at least that is what the moderate
element of ufology has claimed.      Logically, the most advantageous
design for outerspace travel would be a square due to its large area
for housing components and other facilities.     Yet the square design
doesn't seem extraterrestrial enough to generate UFO interest.      The
disc-shaped UFO did indeed create substantial interest amoung saucer
buffs which caused several erroneous beliefs to manifest.

The saucer, to some, has become something of a deity, like Jesus ·christ
who came to us from above to protect us from harm and then returned to
the heavens from which he came.     Such was the case with the flying
saucer in which too many believed.  They were sent here to save us from
some catastrophic incident.   Conversely, there were those who believed
otherwise, that the saucers were here to render havoc amoung the
inhabitants of Earth.       In either case the saucer stood as the
foundation for these two beliefs.        Saucer buffs, like religious
zealots, needed no proof of their belief - total faith outweighed all
logic.

Since the term flying saucers will apparently be with us for years to
come, we still must ask the question, what is the origin of flying
saucers?   Are saucers from space, are they a psychological mani-
festation, media hype or are they something other than what they appear
to be?  Even the most harden UFO skeptic will agree that in some of the
reported sightings people are witness to something mystifying.

A recent study has revealed that as high as 95 percent of all UFO
reports  have occurred within   500 miles   of  a  military  base/test
installation.   This  does  not   mean  that   extraterrestrials  were
 monitoring U.S. defense facilities.     A more rational alternative is
 that   there  simply  has  been  too   much  misinterpreting  of  known
 terrestrial objects such as many of the various kinds of airfoils being
 tested at military facilities.
                          •,"•I ,


 There is strong data that the press inadvertently created a protective
 shield for the government to hide their clandestine testing behind
 their sensational UFO stories. Many of the more popular saucer stories
 are frequently quoted from the likes of the National Enquirer or
 Midnight tabloids.

 Because so many individuals and UFO organizations are arguing over the
 varied flying saucer beliefs, the entire UFO matter has become a mass
 of entangled confusion.   To this writer, there is too much disillusion
 and assumption in some saucer beliefs for there to be anything other
 than a puzzle.   Eventually when the psychology of ufology is unraveled
 then there may be hope in uncovering the truth about flying saucers.
