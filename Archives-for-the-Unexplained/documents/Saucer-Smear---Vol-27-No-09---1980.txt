         A CONCLAVE OF HIGHLY-EVOLVED UFOLOGISTS was held at the Smithsonian
  Institute in Washington, D.C., on September 6th. The affair was in the
  form of a debate between J. Allen Hynek, Allan Hendry, and Bruce Maccabbee
  ( spelling??) on the one hand, versus Philip J. Klass, James Oberg,   and
  Robert Sheaffer. In spite of the $30 price-tag per ticket, the auditorium
  was nearly filled with reasonably enthusiastic UFO fans, some of whom had
  come from quite a distance for the event.
         Unfortunately, most of the participants seemed more concerned about
  squeezing their presentations into the strictly-enforced time limits, than
  about getting their ideas across to the audience. Hynek was by far the
  most scholarly ( and the mos� rushed) , whereas we must admit that ole Philip
  J. Klass came across best in regard to style.
         Ole Phil made two points that bear paraphrasing: ( 1) In today's poli­
  tical and intellectual climate, how can retired military officers really
  fear punishment if they come forth with stories of crashed discs? ( The only
  answer we can think of is that their paranoia overcomes their common
  sense.)   (2) Why don't we Ufologists take five or so of our very best UFO
  cases to the National Academy of Science, and let them decide on their
  value? If the Academy accepts one or more cases as valid, let the Govern­
  ment provide big money for further investigation of the UFO phenomenon. If
  not, we Ufologists should thereupon shut down and shut up. ( But whichever
  way the Academy's verdict went, there would undoubtedly be endless recrimi­
  nations, and nothing would really be settled!)
         Generally, little that was new to a seasoned UFO fan came out of the
  Great Debate, but then, what could one expect? Millions of words have al­
  ready been spoken and written on the subject without solving it as yet!
         In the Smithsonian-audience we came across a number of our loyal non­
  subscribers, including Harry Lebelson, Bill Moore, Stanton Friedman, Peter
  Gersten, Jerome Clark, Larry Bryant, Lee Spiegel, Elizabeth Phillip of
  "Second Look" Magazine, and others. Richard Hall was there, and snubbed
  us, as usual. Conspicuously absent was "Dr." Bill Mcintyre of MARCEN, who
  lives in nearby Maryland.
         After the public meeting, there was a highly-evolved private cocktail
  party at a Washington club for the speakers and other guests of the Smith­
  sonian. Since your humble "Saucer Steer" editor knows all six speakers
  personally, he tried to wrangle an invitation, but was nixed by Frederick
  C. Durant III ( no less) - a former CIA operative who acted as moderator of
  the debate. No doubt there were Great Truths revealed at this party that ·   ·


  we outsiders can only guess at!
         But seriously folks - the debate was a worthwhile effort, and one
  which may be repeated in somewhat different format in the months to come.
        P.S:  Stan Friedman will hate us if we don't mention that he was in
  town to give an unrelated UFO lecture that same night, at American Univer­
  sity in Washington. With him was Captain Bill Startup from Australia/ New
  Zealand, who was involved in the famous UFO film from that area a couple
  of years back. Startup is the author of a book about his experience, call­
  ed "The Kaikoura UFO", not available in this country.
