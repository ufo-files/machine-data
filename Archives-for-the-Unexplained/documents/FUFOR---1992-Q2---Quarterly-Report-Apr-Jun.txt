                                                  April - June 1992

              The most significant event during the quarter was the Fund's sponsorship of the Abduction
              Study ConferenCe, which took place June 13 - 17 at a major university in the Northeast. The
              Fund was chosen to administer the $48,000 contributed to support the conference; attendance
              was limited to 100 mental health practitioners, UFO investigators, and abduction witnesses.

              Immediately following the conference, we took the opportunity to gather together leading
              researchers and therapists for a panel discussion on the UFO abduction phenomenon. The
              discussion was videotaped and produced as an hour-long program, "Abduction Roundtable,"
              which is now available from the Fund (see the enclosed flier for details).

             Also during this period, the Fund~ s National Board approved a request fmm Michael Chorost
             for a grant of $2,000 to help support the costs for a trip to England to analyze soil samples to
             be taken from current crop circle formations. This was designed to be the first American
             scientific exploration of the crop circle phenomenon, and it was expected that this new
             research effort might discover characteristics about the phenomenon which would totally
             refute the hoax hypothesis.

             In April, the Fund received a proposal from a researcher in Russia to conduct an analysis of
             an anomalous mineral. It was the first research proposal from a member of the newly-formed
             Commonwealth of Independent States.

             Fund Chairman Bruce Maccabee, Ph.D., made a presentation on the UFO phenomenon to the
             joint meeting of the American Physical Society and the American Association of Physics
             Teachers in April in Washington, D.C. Executive Committee member Rob Swiatek addressed
             the INFO (International Fortean Organization) conference in May in Northern Virginia; and
             Secretary-Treasurer Fred Whiting participated in a debate on the UFO subject at a science
             fiction convention in the Nation's Capital.

             In May, the Fund was mentioned in articles in Omni magazine and The Wall Street Journal.
             Because of media coverage related to the May 17 - 18 CBS-TV miniseries, "Intruders," and
             the release of the Roper Organization survey on UFO abductions, Executive Committee
             member and primary Fund spokesperson Don Berliner reported conducting an average of one
             interview per week.

            Also during this period, the Board of Directors approved certain amendments to the
            organization's by-laws. One of them concerns the IsabelL. Davis Award, which is made
            annually "to recognize long-term accomplishments and excellence in promoting rational
            inquiry into reported UFO sightings and related phenomena." Board members will make
            nomiq,ations, and the names of nominees will be sent to our supporters, who are asked to vote
            for the individual whose qualifications make him or her best suited to receive the award. The
            ballot is printed on the opposite side of the enclosed Reply Form; we ask that you make your
