                            IN    PRAISE   OF   RESEARCHERS




          Most people, whether tolerant or intolerant, have an interest in UFOs

which may take the form of a dinner table debate or even an organized social

outing to a flying saucer club.



          Although the average person has heard of UFOs few are aware of the

established existence of UFO research.



          Organized research is the meticulous investigation and documentation of

UFO events which are made available to other researchers and interested individuals.

This interchange of information results in a solid education, making it consistently

easier to recognize misperceptions of natural and man-made objects and events,which

brings us ever closer to   the phenomenon that as yet defies explanation and may

quite possibly be "alien " in origin.



          Researchers require an abundance of patience for it must be recognised

that a final complete solution may be decades, even life times, away.



          It is because of the long term accumulative contributions of devoted

investigators that research has progressed to the point of scientific recognition

and involvement.



          Because serious ufology is a commitment of time and effort it cannot be

treated, by those who call themselves "researchers ," as a hobby which eventually

takes second place to more exciting interests when the novelty of the chase wears off.



          Fortunately, there exists throughout the world a core of investigators

whose curiosity has endured personal and communication difficu lties and who have

over the years contributed the wealth of their experience unselfishly to the subject.

These people are the foundation of UFO research not the myriad hobbyists who come

and so quickly go.l

                      ON   PICKLED   ALIENS   AND   STRANGE   HYPOTHESES


                                      By Mark Moravec



     I would like to comment on two items included in past issues of UFO Research Australia
Newsletter.

      Firstly, in "The Changing Face of Ufology" (UFORAN, Vol. 2 No. 2), David Reneke brings
up the topics of (1) recent advances in instrumented UFO research; and (2) claims of
alleged "crashed UFOs'' and the recovery of their dead "humanoid occupants. " Whilst I
agree with the author that significant advances have been made in instrumented research
(e. g. computer-enhanced analyses of UFO photographs), it should also be stated that such
techniques have not been developed to perfection.   lt is still a matter of some controversy
whether or not Ground Saucer Watch's techniques can accurately determine such factors as
the distance of the object photographed. We may not yet have the "perfect" UFO analysis
techniques, but that doesn't mean we should stop trying to develop them!

     On the topic of "crashed saucers," it is true that American researcher Leonard String­
field has gathered some 20 plus accounts of such alleged events.  However, there is absolu­
tely no evidence that any of these rumours are true.  None of Stringfield's informants have
been named; the various accounts are inconsistent with each other (despite Stringfield's
attempts to relate at least some of them to the same "incident"); and no supportive physical
evidence has surfaced.

      As I stated in my review of Stringfield's book "Situation Red:  The UFO Siege" (see
UFORAN, Vol. 1 No.2):  "The questionable part of Stringfield's work is his presentation of
unconfirmed (and probably unconfirmable) rumours of shot-down and crashed UFOs, with the
bodies of entities having been allegedly recovered and secretly refrigerated at an air
force base.   The circulation of sensational unconfirmed rumours has never contributed any­
th ing positive to UFO research and its respectability as an area of scientific investi­
gation. I feel Stringfield is too willing to believe in the stories of crashed UFOs. "

      As for those seemingly mysterious photographs of alleged UFO occupant cadavers in
USAF custody, Stringfield has admitted in a recent issue of the MUFON UFO Journal (No. 154,
Dec. 1980) that at least one of the photos has previously appeared in issues of both
"Official UFO" and "Ancient Astronauts, " the American magazines notorious for publishing
hoax stories in the guise of fact.    However, Stringfield also makes the incredible claim
that these face photographs are part of a conspiracy, by person or persons unknown, directed
towa rds un dermining the credibility of his "c rashed UF0" stories. Better to beli eve there
is a sinister conspiracy against oneself than to acknowledge one has been duped by hoaxers!

     lt is time to forget the dubious stories of "pickled aliens, " and instead to concen­
trate our energies on investigating the more promising, solid, contemporary UFO reports.

     The second item worthy of comment is John P rytz's artic1e ''An Approach to the UFO Maze:
Philosophy Based on Clarke's 3rd Law" (UFORAN Vo1. 2 No. 1). The author, in his attempt to
find the explanation which best fits the UFO data, proposes that a high strangeness pheno­
menon (UFOs) requires a high strangeness explanation. However, this approach suffers from
two major deficiencies:  (1) The resultant "high strangeness" UFO hypothesis becomes a
catch-al1 which can "explain" anything the author wishes it to explain, be it UFOs or any
imaginable anomaly.  There is no way to disprove the hypothesis, i. e. the hypothesis is
scientifically untestable and ends up not explaining anything. (2) There are counter­
examples which suggest that high-strangeness UFO cases may have low-strangeness explanations.

For example Keith Basterfield's image hypothesis attempts to explain CE3 and other high
strangeness cases as being examples of unconsciously-generated hallucinations during
states of near-sleep or tiredness. Surveys and experiments conducted by psychologists
have shown that such imagery is experienced by a proportion of normal, healthy people.

     As for picking out hypotheses of 11best fit,11 no matter what system one uses, it merely
reflects one's own biased beliefs. Thus the author found that the extraterrestrial and
parallel universe hypotheses were the best possible explanations for UFOs. If I were to
plug in my own values, I could quite easily come up with equally impressive, but completely
different results, e.g. psychological/sociological phenomena or psychic/religious phenomena.
In fact, rather than aiming for one main high strangeness hypothesis ( reflecting the author's
assumption that UFO reports represent a single, largely homogenous phenomenon ) , a number of
simpler but different hypotheses could explain the totality of UFO phenomena.

     In closing, I would 1ike to commend David Reneke and John Prytz for their contribut­
ions to the UFO debate. If my comments prove anything, it is that this debate is still
going strong. We are stil1 far away from any consensus as to what the UFO phenomena
actually represent. What we have achieved in recent years ( and it is a very significant
and positive achievement ) is a level of critical investigation where we can at least depend
on the UFO sighting data we now gather. We can, at last, have an informed debate on the
nature and origin of UFOs.l
