                                  Ronald Francis Jolly   18/2/1929-9/11/1996

It is with deep regret that we have to announce that Ron Jolly, TUFOIC president, died on the 8th of November. Ron
served TUFOIC for 17 years. He served as President from 1881 until stepping down due to ill health earlier this year.


Ron will be remembered for his dedication to finding the truth behind the UFO mystery and his always friendly manner.
His company will be sadly missed by all of the committee and members. We extend our sincere condolences to his
wife Mary and children.

Skywatch
The planet Jupiter still dominates the night sky. Looking West after sunset about 30 degrees high. Jupiter is
edging toward the horizon each night, and will disappear into the twilight at the end of December.


Comet Hale-Bopp is now winding its way around the back of the sun, and will not reappear                                                                               to us in the
Southern hemisphere until May next year, when, if all goes to plan                                              it should be quite a sight from a dark
location. Trying to estimate a comets brightness is a risky business at the best of times, and often leads to
disappointment, but all current indications are that this comet will be as good as Hyakutake was during
March of this year.


Saturn is currently roughly 50 degrees up in the Northern sky after sunset and is not difficult to find in that
part of the sky. Saturn is a yellowish colour and shines a steady light, as do the other planets, unlike the stars
that appear to twinkle. The distance, apparent size and our atmosphere all work together to produce the
twinkling effect. The planets being much closer and apparently larger only have our atmosphere to tend with,
so the twinkling effect is much less apparent.


Mars        rises in the eastern sky around 2.30 AM and does not get very much above the horizon when the
morning twilight takes hold.

                                        Snails go Cosmic
                                          By. SIMON BEVILACQUA
          The bizarre "cosmic slime" found in Tasmania this year was most likely snail eggs, says the state's UFO
investigation unit. The slime, found at Kempton and in the North-West of the state two months ago, was rumoured to have
been linked to a number of UFO sightings in the regions. The Tasmanian UFO investigation centre sent a number of slime
samples from different locations for laboratory analysis.
          Results returned last week revealed one sample to be the eggs of some form of mollusc and other samples to be a
jelly-like substance found in potting mix or fertiliser. UFO investigation centre spokesman Keith Roberts said the sample
taken from near a Kempton service station, which turned out to be mollusc eggs, sparked a series of slime reports across the
state.
          "Most of these things have reasonable explanations," Mr Roberts said A national UFO report body is still waiting on
its slime analysis.
          The UFO Information Hotline, a national phone operation which charges $3 a minute to receive reports, sent slime
samples to laboratories in Perth and Melbourne. Hotline spokesman Ross Dowe said be believed the slime to most likely be
man-made and to have been picked up and deposited by a storm Mr Dowe said the news of Tasmanian cosmic slime had
sparked a series of slime reports nationwide. "We expect to get results of the analysis hack before Christmas" Mr Dowe said.
