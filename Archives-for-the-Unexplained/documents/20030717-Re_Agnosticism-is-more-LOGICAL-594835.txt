  I understand what you are saying perfectly, and it is still balderdash.
  You say, "IF the definition we use for knowledge includes that one must know
  a fact to be true for it to be knowledge, THEN it leads to this infinite
  recursion problem, NOT with the real world, but with the term "knowledge" -
  it becomes a useless word because we can't tell when it applies and when it
  doesn't."
  That is total balderdash. What else could 'knowledge' possibly mean if not
  that a person can demonstrate that he has gained through experience a
  demonstrable understanding of the facts, the actual state of affairs
  relevant to the particular case? That is what science (Latin for knowledge)
  is all about, old boy, replicable demonstrations.

  Due to the generally acknowledged definition of the term 'knowledge' :
  (demonstrable understanding gained through experience
  http://dictionary.reference.com/search?q=knowledge ),
  the term 'knowledge' applies in any case in which, for example, a person
  can demonstrate, publicly, reliably, that a given statement like "There are
  no ETs" is false (is not in accord with the actual state of affairs in this
  particular case), by demonstrating at least one ET, publicly, for anyone to
  examine, don't you agree?
