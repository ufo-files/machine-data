                                 ANIMAL MUTILATIONS -- 1979

      Overall, it's not a pretty picture. The phenomenon involves the grotesque mutila­
tions of thousands of animals in the U.S., Canada, and several foreign countries. Most
of the victims have been cattle , but the afflicted also include horses , dogs, cats,
poultr y , and other animals. Even bestiality has been reported in several cases.
      The news media has been reporting the bizarre phenomenon for about the last five
year s , but there is evidence such incidents have been occurring worldwide for more than
two centuries. Even Sir Arthur Conan Doyle, creator of Sherlock Holmes and psychic
phenomena investigator, reportedly investigated animal mutilations in 19th-Century
London.
      In this country alone, since 1975, there have been an estimated 10,000 livestock
mutilations , and costs to ranchers could run as high as $10 million. About 30 states
are involved, including Arizona , Arkansas , Colorado, Kansas, Minnesota, Mississippi ,
Missouri , Montana, Nebraska , Nevada , New Mexico, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon , South Dakota,
Tennessee, Texas , Utah, Washington, and Wyoming.
      Theories range from dissections by extraterrestrials (ET) to black magic and
witchcraft or weird religious cults. Or predators. Or a powerful group of ranchers
using scare tactics in an attempt to force out smaller ranchers. Or a Central Intelli­
gence Agency operation testing the effects of radiation in different areas. Some said
there could be a relationship between the mutilations and nuclear testing or the close
proximity of nuclear power plants or uranium mining. One psychologist even suggested
the animals' sex organs may have been eaten by cult members in an attempt to increase
sexual potency.
      Whatever the reason, this year has seen a dramatic increase in the numbers of mu­
tilations. Characteristics include severed sexual organs, drained blood , and missing
tongues , eyes , and ears. In many cases , incisions were precise, as if done by scal­
pels. Though UFOs have been reported in some cases, this -- or any other single con­
nection -- is tenuous at best.
      As in the past few years, New Mexico seems to have suffered much. The situation
in the state grew so bad that, on April 20, a mutilation conference was called in Al­
buquerque by U.S. Senator Harrison Schmitt (R-N.M.), former astronaut. Earlier,
Schmitt had announced plans to probe the mutilations via the Justice Department because
some of the incidents occurred on federal land. Also, his constituents, particularly
the ranchers, wanted some answers.
