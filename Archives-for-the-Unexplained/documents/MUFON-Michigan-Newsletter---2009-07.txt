                          Investigators busy with UFO sightings
                                                By Brian X. McCrone

          BUCKS COUNTY. From flaming trees to six-sided, hovering aircraft, people in towns like Langhorne and Fairless Hills
are seeing things otherworldly — making the area the current ground zero of the UFO world.
         “I’m spending almost two hours a night on these sightings in Bucks County,” John Ventre of the Pennsylvania chapter of the
Mutual UFO Network said last week. “This wave that started the second half of last year was incredible.”
         Over 100 sightings since June more suited for Fox and Mulder than local police departments have attracted the attention of
the Discovery and History channels.
         The Discovery Channel brought its “UFOs Over Earth” series to Bucks County last year and the History Channel’s “UFO
Hunters” is now investigating some of the more credible sightings. “There was an incredible spike in 2008,” UFO Hunters producer
Kevin Barry said. “We’re still talking to people to see if we have something on our hands.”
         Unexplainable sightings are not new to Lower Bucks County, Barry said, noting a well-known 1973 UFO sighting along
Route 1 in Fairless Hills. “It’s interesting because there is a history there,” Barry said.
         Sightings last year include a Levittown woman who said she saw probes of light dropped into a tree in her backyard, Ventre
said, and a Doylestown man who saw a slow-moving six-sided UFO near his home.
         The new question — beyond where aliens will appear quickly and vaguely next — is what comes first: the appearance of
UFOs or the appearance of UFO television shows.
         “That’s an undeniable part of some stories. What makes people see these things?” Barry said.

                         Richard A. Lovett for National Geographic News May 6, 2009

                                                                      Cloaking devices, like the Star Trek technology that can make
                                                             whole Romulan warships disappear, came a step closer to reality last week.
                                                             Two independent teams have developed silicon-based materials that can
                                                             hide microscopic objects. The materials are the first to work in near-infrared
                                                             light, wavelengths very close to visible light.
                                                                      On a flat surface, an object covered with a piece of cloth would

                                                                      A team led by Xiang Zhang at the University of California, Ber-
                                                             keley, achieved this effect by drilling scores of tiny nano-holes into the
                                                             cloaking material. These holes change the material's optical properties, al-

          Small But Effective
          Both materials still have a long way to go before they're ready for stealth military operations. To start with, both cloaks work
only in infrared light for now. The next step is to try to develop a version that works in visible wavelengths. The cloaks are also capable
of hiding only microscopic objects. But even that has potential uses, Valentine said. In optical computing, for example, such cloaks could
be used to allow light to move more efficiently, by hiding the parts of a computer chip that get in the way of the beams.
          Also, expensive dielectric mirrors—special mirrors used to make printed circuits for electronics—can be ruined by tiny defects
in their surfaces. "You could cloak a defect and make it look like a perfect mirror again," Valentine said. Scaling up to hide an object the
size of a BB-gun pellet might be possible, but going larger will be difficult”, he said. In the Berkeley material, for instance, "the holes
have to be smaller than the wavelength of light, and many are required."
          From Theory to Practice
          The development of these working invisibility cloaks is exciting, but it's not a huge theoretical breakthrough, Ulf Leonhardt, a
theoretical physicist at Scotland's University of St. Andrews, said in an email. "Such an idea already appeared in my very first paper on
cloaking," published in the journal Science in 2006, he said.
          Carl Poitras, a researcher with the Cornell group, agrees. "Like a lot of things we can do," he said, "the theory is predicted ahead
of time, but the technology isn't there to show it."
          Now, for cloaking devices at least, the theoretical models are starting to become real-life products.

Lansing, on about September 1, 1954, Close Encounter of the 1st Kind --
This case is from a new presentation I’ve assembled on Michigan UFOs, benefiting from my continuing research in the files of the J.
Allen Hynek Center for UFO Studies in Chicago. The Center’s collection includes documents from the National Investigations Com-
mittee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP) and Project Blue Book, and contains material found nowhere else. I’ve incorporated these in-
sights into presentations on classic and lesser-known cases covering the gamut of UFO and related experiences. Here’s a case where a
UFO sighting was only reported much later, due to the witness responding to Dr. Hynek’s call for factual reports. In this instance the
Warren, Michigan, writer heard Dr. Hynek on a January, 1978 J.P. McCarthy radio show on station WJR in Detroit, and was relating an
incident that happened in September of 1954 in Lansing. The witness recounted a sighting he, his brother, and their parents had had on
or about the first of the month, near the north border of Lansing’s Potter Park, on the city’s near southeast side. The site is just a cou-
ple of miles from where I was living at the time, so I am familiar with the general area, although in 1954 I wasn’t yet into UFOs; actu-
ally I wasn’t yet into reading!

At about 8:30 to 9:00 p.m. on about the first of September the family was startled by a loud humming sound, something like that made
by an electric motor that is beginning to burn out. Frantically checking around the house to see whether something electrical was “on
the verge” of going bad, the family was alerted to the fact that the source was outside by the excited hollering of some neighbors. The
men in the family ran out of their house, to join a bunch of their neighbors gazing up at a huge round object slowly drifting east, about
300 feet above Bensch Street, and north of the Grand Trunk Western Rail Road tracks that course along the north border of Potter Park
(see sketch of UFO as it first appeared, sketch 1).

to head west over Potter Park itself. This maneuver certainly eliminated the simple unpowered balloon possibility. The object
next turned completely upon its side, presenting the appearance of a glass orange-juice squisher as it did so. As it now coursed
westwards, the thing appeared to climb, very slowly at first. The craft suddenly put on a burst of speed, and was gone in just a few
seconds! While wind speed was not noted by the witness, this last acceleration would seem completely to demolish the notion of a
powered advertising blimp. In all, the sighting lasted about four or five minutes, according to the reporting witness.

While the witness’ family probably did not report the incident to the police – after nearly twenty years the father couldn’t remember
having done so – the Lansing State Journal may have run a small article on this event, and it will repay some effort in consulting the
Journal’s newspaper morgue to follow up on this tale. The witness drew up a detailed map and color renderings of the object’s
appearance, which add to the impact of this remarkable report.

                                                               UFO Info on The Rise
                                                         When I began as Newsletter Editor in January of 2004, I was
                                                 hard pressed to find articles on the UFO topic. I mean serious articles –
                                                 without the “giggle factor” added by the journalist to show how down-
                                                 to-earth they were (pun intended). Besides my own editorial page, I
                                                 found myself writing other articles just to help fill the 20 pages. The
                                                 “Keeping it Light” feature was actually added as a filler but people
               David Twichell                    seemed to enjoy the lighthearted cartoons and now I couldn’t omit it if I
                                                 wanted to – and I don’t want to.
         Over time, I found more and more serious UFO articles popping up in periodicals like The Washington Post, The
New York Times and The Chicago Tribune. Documentaries that were the strict domain of the Sci-Fi Channel have found a
place of reverence on respectable airwaves like the History Channel (UFO Hunters) and Discovery Channel (MUFON’s
UFO’s Over Earth). Talk show icon Larry King has offered many shows dedicated the UFO topic. They began as a curios-
ity but as the ratings grew, the topic was addressed more often. King was an open minded skeptic until the night when the
December 1980 Rendlesham Forest UFO Incident was broached and James Fox, producer of “Out of the Blue”, was the
guest on his show. Halfway through the interview, during one of the many commercial breaks, King said to Fox in an
amazed whisper, “My God, this is real, isn’t it?” To which Fox simply replied, “Yes, it’s real.”
         CNN has covered the Phoenix Lights incident, the Disclosure Project’s 2001 press conference at the prestigious
Washington Press Club and made extensive mention of Dr. Edgar Mitchell’s assertion that Roswell was a real incident and
“we are not alone” in the universe. CNN news anchor Miles O’Brien has gone out on a limb to pursue the subject in a se-
ries of news articles. Even Fox News Channel’s Shepard Smith has gone from a know-it-all debunker to a cautious be-
liever based on the mounting evidence that occasionally passes over his news desk.
         President Obama’s campaign promise of government transparency offered a glimmer of hope to those seeking UFO
disclosure and he has surrounded himself with players that have been briefed on the subject. Hillary Clinton was allegedly
involved in the “Rockefeller Initiative” (http://www.presidentialufo.com/clinton.htm), and former Clinton staff member
John Podesta has publicly called for opening the books on the matter (http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bYvtqEkqG1M),
as so many other countries have done; the UK, France, Denmark, Canada, Brazil and Sweden just to name a few.
         Stephen Bassett’s Million Fax on Washington has prompted hundreds of thousands of faxes, letters and emails to
the president calling for UFO disclosure. Articles hailing the project have shown up in the most unlikely places. Bassett
has cataloged them all at his website, http://www.faxonwashington.org/. The next phase of the project is for the public to
write to the White House Press Corps urging reporters to address the subject in formal press conferences with the presi-
dent.
         Other sources of material can be readily found at George Filer’s “Filer’s Files” (http://www.nationalufocenter.
com), Linda Moulton Howe’s earthfiles.com (http://www.earthfiles.com/), Brian Vike’s HBCC UFO Research (http://
hbccuforesearch.blogspot.com/), and Jeff Rense’s website (http://www.rense.com/), just to name a few.
         A couple other online news sources that do not deal exclusively with the UFO phenomenon but include fair treat-
ment of the subject are The Huffington Post (http://www.huffingtonpost.com/) and The Examiner (http://www.examiner.
com/). At first I was a leery of the latter publication, thinking it the online version of the grocery store tabloid but I have
been assured that they are not one in the same. I suggest the reader take some time to search out the aforementioned web-
sites and decide for themselves.
         The point being, when I began as Newsletter Editor, I had to scavenge for worthy UFO articles, now I find myself
having to pick and choose. Is it the case that I’m getting better at this, or is it that the UFO phenomenon is finally gaining
the respectability it has deserved from the outset? Is it the case that the keepers of the UFO secret are loosing their grip or
are they purposely preparing the public for official disclosure? Whatever the reason for this boon, in this age of informa-
tion the truth cannot be hidden indefinitely.
         David Twichell
         MiMUFON Newsletter Editor.
