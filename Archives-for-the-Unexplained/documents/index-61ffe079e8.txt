                                                                Explanation of Ratings

  This book promises to provide a breakthrough on UFOs but fails to deliver more than a few new tidbits.
  Nonetheless, it is notable that a respected former New York Times reporter should tackle the issue.
  Howard Blum asserts that there exists within the government a "UFO Working Group" preparing for alien
  contact. He seems to have this conviction from inside sources, but one senses that they dried up before the
  book was completed. The first few chapters are intriguing, including an account of a NORAD radar
  incident in which an unusual object was tracked coming in from space. The remainder of the book feels
  like filler: it is a historical review of the UFO phenomenon from Blue Book to the MJ-12 controversy.
  Even if it failed in its goals, the book is probably worth the paperback price, at least for the subjects it does
  cover. It deserves a place on a serious researcher's bookshelf, if only because it is frequently mentioned in
  conversation. No index! -- Glenn Campbell

  About the Author

  Howard Blum, a former award-winning reporter for The New York Times, has written several highly acclaimed and
  bestselling nonfiction books that not only are headlinemaking works of investigative journalism, but also tell compelling
  true stories. In Wanted! The Search for Nazis in America, Mr. Blum told a real-life detective story‹and wrote a book
  that led to congressional hearings. In I Pledge Allegiance . . . The True Story of the Walkers: An American Spy
  Family, he wrote an espionage thriller about the most damaging spy ring in America's history. The book was the basis
  for the CBS miniseries, and Mr. Blum served as the drama's associate producer. And now in Out There, he reveals a
  true story about the future. Mr. Blum lives and works in New York, where he is presently involved in writing another
  true story.
