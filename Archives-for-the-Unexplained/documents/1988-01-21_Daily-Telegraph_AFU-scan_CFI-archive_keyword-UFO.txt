Family in fear
as 'egg from
 outer space'
   chases car
  . By Peter Wombwell
          in Perth
AN EGG-shaped unidenti­
 fied flying object appar­
 ently scattering black ash
 in its wake yesterday ter­
 rified a family driving
 across Australia's deso­
 late Nullabor Plain and
 the crew of a tuna boat
 50 miles out to sea.
The object chased the fam­
 ily's car along the lonely
 road 800 miles from Perth,
 and finally picked it up and
 shook it, they reported.
Both the family and the tuna
 fishermen said their voices
 became     distorted    ''as
 though they were speaking
 in slow motion" while the
 object was near them.
The tuna fishermen, w h o
 police said could not have
 known of the other incident,
 said the UFO had "buzzed"
 their boat in the Great Aus­
 tralian Bight.

     Lodged on roof
Mrs Faye Knowles was with
 her sons Patrick, 24, Sean,
 21, and Wayne, 18, when the
 "huge, glowing, egg-like
 sphere" appeared.
Sean Knowles said they
 stopped their car and
 walked back t o look a t the
 object. "It was weird, and
 we got frightened so we ran
 through the bushes and back
 to the car," he said. "But
 the thing followed us."
He said that later it lodged on
 the roof and lifted the car
 bodily off the ground.
His mother went into shock
 when she reached out of one
 of the car's windows and
 touched it. She told her sons
 it felt like a rubber suction
 pad.
When they spoke to police at
 Ceduna in South Australia,
 they said the car had
 seemed t o float i n the air,
 and their voices became dis­
 torted as though they were
 speaking in slow motion.
Sean said he had been driving
 about 70 mph when they
 first sighted the object, but
 even when he increased the
 speed to 125 mph the thing
 followed them.
Then it attached itself to the
 roof. After the car came
 down to earth, one of the
 tyres burst.
   'Not of this planet'
Mrs Shirley Lundon, of Mun­
 drabilla, where the family
 stopped after the incident,
 said they had scratches on
 their legs through running
 through the bush to escape
 the object, and there were
 also scratches on the roof
 and on one side of the car.
Police Sgt Jim Furnell said:
 "After investigating, we are
 treating the reports very
 seriously."
Mr Colin Norris, director of
 the UFO Centre, said: "It
 fits in with a lot of other
 sightings in the world. It
 appears to be an extra-ter­
 restrial encounter. It's not
 of this planet."
However, a Melbourne Plane­
 tary Society spokesman, Mr
 Matthew James, was report­
 ed to have put the light
 down to being a rare phe­
 nomenon known as ball
 lightning.
