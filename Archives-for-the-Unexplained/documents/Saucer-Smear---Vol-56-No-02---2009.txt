       Greetings once again,fellow saucer fiends!
       As we write thiseditorial in late January, UFO sightings are continuing all around
the world, probably at a faster pace than ever. So why do we print so few of them in our
zine?
      It's because our focus has shifted slightly. Very few recent or current sightings
have enough detail or even half enough detail to prove any sort of mysterious origin. And
we all know that photos or even videos no longer have much hope of being authentic, due to
modern technology. So why print material just because it is current?
      There are many great classic sightings from the 1947-1980 period, and there is still
a valid UFO mystery, which will probably be solved someday quite a long time from now.
      Meanwhile, there are other Fortean mysteries, some of them connected to UFOs and some
that are probably not. These still excite our curiosity, and we try tQ bring a few of them
to you - such as the "Miracle" of Fatima item in this issue. We have little hope of hold­
ing your interest unless we write about things that really interest�····

       For those of you still hoping to locate ET's home planet, there's good news in a new
generation of powerful, extremely sophisticated telescopes. One, called Kepler, is design­
ed specifically to look for inhabitable planets around distant stars.
       Telescopes coming on line in 2009 will let researchers see farther and more clearly
than ever before. They will open new windows on the heavens by using different technologies
and different wavelengths of light. They will be able to see things in the far ranges of
ultraviolet, infrared, or radio waves that are invisible in the narrow band     of   optical
light.
       One of these is the Allen Telescope Array, a set of 42 radio telescopes that will be
listening for extraterrestrial messages from possible civilizations around other stars.
       The International Astronomical Union, an organization of about 10, 000  professional
astronomers, has named 2009 The International Year of Astronomy. This is in honor of Gall­
leo, who was accused of heresy by the Roman Catholic Church for insisting that the Earth
moves around the Sun           •• •

son for the loan of a three-volume set of books about the exceedingly strange events near
the little village of Fatima, Portugal, in the year 191?. These books were published start­
ing in 1982 (65 years after the events! ) , and are translated from the Portuguese language.
They run over ?00 pages in all, so not even a proper summary is possible here.
       The important thing is that, possibly for the first time, the "miracles" are pursued
in an objective manner. These events are not "mass hysteria" or hallucinations as the skep­
tics would insist, nor are they supernatural events of a religious nature. Indeed,        they
seem to be UFO/paranormal events, similar to many other occurrences around the world during
later years. In fact, Fatima is sometimes called the first major UFO case of the 20th Cen­
tury.   It is amazing that it has received so little attention from ufologists, while Ros­
well, which is far less documented, has received so much!
       The story begins on May 13th, 191?, when three peasant children saw an apparition in
an oak tree of a humanoid being they took to be the Virgin Mary. The only trouble is that
the creature was little more than three feet tall, and looked like a female child about 15
years old. That's not your typical Grey, but it's not the Virgin Mary either!
       The creature stated that similar "miracles" would occur at the same place on the lJth
of every month until October, at which time it would reveal very important information
about World War I and the future of mankind. The "13th of every month" prediction turned
out to be absolutely accurate, which is amazing in itself. The three political well-publi­
cized predictions were complex and obviously heavily burdened by Catholic dogma by the time
they were revealed to the public. One simple prediction made on October 13th was         that
World War I would end that same day, but this did not happen. ( That war ended in late 1918 �
       On the morning of Oct. lJth, 191?, 60, 000 to 70, 000 people of all political and re­
ligious persuasions assembled in Fatima. In the early afternoon, apparently everyone pre­
sent saw something very weird, though the details of their descriptions vary. I t was the
"Sun", doing things absolutely impossible for the real Sun to do. We quote:
       "Phase One: Spinning upon itself, wherein the stabilized object showed a dull silver,
metallic color; Phase Two: Rotation on its periphery, wherein the object showed iridescent
borders of dark red, purple, and even black; Phase Three 1 Oscillation ( sudden movements or
tremors ) , tied to its rotation, wherein the object descended toward the ground and rose
again, producing a color sequence; Phase Four a Return to the stabllization achieved during
Phase One." The whole apparition lasted about ten minutes.
       There had been heavy rain, but when the object descended below the clouds, many peo­
ple claimed there was enough heat to dry their clothes. Yet, unlike the real Sun, this ob­
ject could be looked at directly without injury to the eyes.
       The three peasant children were relatives named Lucia, Janita, and Francisco.       All
three saw the "Virgin", but strangely only the two girls could hear her speak.       Apparently
members of the crowd near the children could also see the "Virgin", and it seems that she
descended from the craft overhead (  =  the "Sun" ) by aeans of some sort of ladder, and then
returned to it the same way. Other religious entities were also seen by some, and these
appeared similar to our concept of St. Joseph and the baby Jesus.
       Lucia was the leader of the three children, and was responsible for the predictions.
She very soon became a nun, and all of her statements during her very long life thereafter
were carefully controlled by the Church. The other two children died of natural        causes
w1 thin three years of the "Miracle".
       Forewords to the three volumes are written by famed ufologist Dr. Jacques Vallee; UFO
reporter Jim Marrs; and ( ugh! ) William Birnes of "UFO Magazine•• and Roswell fame.      These
books are not perfect, but they represent a sincere well-researched effort to find an ob­
jective solution to a very complicated series of events • • •
