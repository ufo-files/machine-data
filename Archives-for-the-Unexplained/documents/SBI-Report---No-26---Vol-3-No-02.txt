                     A UFOLOGIST'S LIBRARY

                           by Jerome Eden


     The newcomer to ufology has a most difficult time. His
orientation into this strange subject is made all the more diffi­
cult by the overwhelming mass ·of literature on the subject. He
cannot possibly read every thing, attend all the lectures, or
become familiar with every aspect of this multifaceted field,
where fact and fantasy are often separated by the breadth of a
hair, where the charlatan and the serious-minded researcher often
share the same platform.
     I thought it might be beneficial, therefore, to share my
UFO library with those who might accept some guidance. It has been
nearly thirty y ears since I read my first book on UFOs. From that
time until the present, I have tried to keep abreast of the never­
ending waves of books which continue to rain like a flood upon us.
Much that I have purchased and studied has wound up as fuel for
my woodstove. One learns from experience--often paid for by hard­
earned cash--to separate the wheat from the chaff. One develops
a "nose" that can discern .the bally hoo "baloney slicer" (who sells
his UFO fantasies at so much per slice) from the serious ufologist
whose main task is simply to present the factual truths about
ufology as best he can.
     So I went to my UFO bookcase and selected about a dozen of what
I consider my favorite books·. To me, a book is worth reading and
keeping for a number of reasons. My first criterion of an excellent
book in-ufology is my desire to read it more than once. Every book
I shall recommend is such a book. Second, each book adds factual
material to various aspects of this difficult subject. Some are
short works which can be read in a few hours, oth�rs may take
several day s. Some are by little-known ufologists, others by some
of the most prominent people in the field. A few are, from a
literary viewpoint, badly written, but the sincerity of the authors
and the material they present more than compensate for their lack
of literary skills.
     What the.authors of these books have in common is their
serious approach to this subject, their dedication to truth. Some
have suffered gravely for writing as they have. In brief, each
has "paid the price" for publishing his material: two were ridi­
culed and persecuted and died for their beliefs. Several others
have been mocked and sequestered by ttmainstream" ufology. Never­
theless, each author has display ed great courage and perseverance
in the face of incredible opposition.
     This list is by no means complete, for there are many other
UFO writers, both in the USA and abroad, whose works are worthy
to be mentioned. I do not mean to slight any one by omission. But
the purpose of this initial catalogue is simply to try to guide
the neophy te UFO researcher onto what I believe is the factual
path. Anyone.who studies these books, or at least a majority of
these books, will have an excellent grounding into some of the most
serious, sobering and factual material extant on the subject. A
few may be out-of-print or otherwise extremely difficult to obtain.
