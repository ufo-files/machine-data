Sample no 17

Sample received on Aug 8, 1946 at Rynninge, Örebro

Received at FOA on Aug 10, 1946, together with the following letter.

"With reference to telephone order from Fst/L on Aug 8, 1946 at 01.25 pm we wish to give you the following information regarding a finding at Rynninge, Örebro . The info was sent to TT  through Örebro-Kuriren.

The finding was made on Aug 8, 1946 at about 08.30 am by Mrs Hilma Wallin, Stadsvägen 21, Rynninge, Örebro. On a visit to Mr and Mrs Wallin the following information was received.

Mr and Mrs Wallin have a house of their own, surrounded by a large garden. When Mrs Wallin opened her verandah door in the morning of Aug 8 at about 08.30 she noticed a number of pieces of coke and slag lying around on the gravel yard. She called her husband, a retired workshop worker named Elis Wallin who picked up the pieces. He could see similar pieces lying around also on his neighbour´s gravel yard.

The Wallins have no children and children usually do not play on their yard. The yard was newly raked and possible traces after visitors, walking or driving, would have been visible. Mrs Wallin could not therefore explain how the pieces had ended up on the yards. In any case they had not been there the previous evening when Mr Wallin was raking the yard.

Mr Wallin confirmed Mrs Wallin´s report. Neither of them had seen or heard anything that could be connected to the findings.

The report was given during the morning by Mr Wallin in person to the editor in chief at Örebro-Kuriren, who informed TT.

An article concerning the finding published on Aug 8, 1946 in Örebro-Kuriren is enclosed.

The pieces found on the yard are being sent separately.

All newspapers in the Örebro area have been asked on Aug 8 1946 to report all possible observations or findings immediately to the Fo-staff."

The samples consist of about ten small coke-like pieces, three small pieces of slag plus a redbrown piece obviously consisting of some organic material. The slag and the partly burnt coke look exactly like the ashes that are being raked out of ordinary heating boilers.

All samples are impregnated on the surface with a grey-brown layer of powder (ashes or a fine dust from the gravel yard).

As to the pieces of coke we have examined the surface of fracture and it has the typical appearance of ordinary coke. Pieces of this kind are frequently found in gravel yards in wintertime when ashes are often used for sanding.

The redbrown piece of organic material emits, when heated, a typical odour of burnt fat. A fresh surface of section emits a smell similar to linseed oil. Also an ether extract of the sample emits this smell.

A qualitative analysis of the ashes with regard to calcium gave a positive result. The ashes dissolve in acid generating carbon acid which would indicate presence of chalk. The examinations indicate that the sample consists of a piece of dried cement or a dried lump of paint.
