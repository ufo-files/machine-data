One must first understand, in the investigative world we live
in there are only two (2) types of witnesses. The first is a
“FACT WITNESS” and the second is an “EXPERT
WITNESS”.

FACT WITNESS: A FACT WITNESS IS ONE WHO’S
TESTIMONY IS NOT BASED ON SCIENTIFIC,
TECHNICAL, OR OTHER SPECIALIZED KNOWLEDGE,
OF THE SUBJET MATTER UNDER INVESTIGATION. THE
MAJORITY OF THE WITNESS REPORTS THAT ARE
PRESENTED FOR INVESTIGATION ORIGINATE FROM
AN INDIVIDUAL IDENTIFIED AS A “FACT WITNESS”.

EXPERT WITNESS: AN EXPERT WITNESS IS
GENERALLY DEFINED AS SOMEONE WITH SUFFICIENT
SKILL, KNOWLEDGE, AND EXPERIENCE IN A GIVEN
FIELD SO AS TO BE CAPABLE OF DRAWING
INFERENCES OR REACHING CONCLUSIONS OR
RENDERING OPINIONS THAT THE AVERAGE PERSON
WOULD NOT BE COMPETENT TO DO.

SCIENTIFIC METHODOLOGY
The scientific approach is a principle of inquiry during the
investigation that forms a basis for legitimate scientific
processes. It is normally applied using the following steps:

RECOGNIZE THE NEED (IDENTIFY THE PROBLEMS)
DEFINE THE PROBLEM
COLLECT ALL AVAILABLE DATA (INDUCTIVE
REASONING)

DEVELOP A HYPOTHESIS AND TEST THE HYPOTHESIS
If you as a researcher apply the above methodology to each
and every investigation, you will find it provides for the
organizational and analytical process to culminate into a

EVIDENCE TYPES
During any investigation there are basically only four (4)
types of evidence, all of which in some manner relate to your
investigations. They are as follows:

  1. DEMONSTRATIVE EVIDENCE
  2. DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE
  3. TESTIMONIAL EVIDENCE
  4. PHYSICAL EVIDENCE

This article will deal with the “PHYSICAL EVIDENCE” only.

You have been assigned a case where you discover the
event that took place, left behind trace evidence that needs
to be recovered for future analysis. What do you do?
Where do you start? How do you handle the unknown
substance? How do you retain a sample? Upon your arrival
at the scene of the reported incident, if no one took the
responsibility and preliminary steps to preserve and protect
the scene, you must take that responsibility. Remember to
always, protect the scene, document the evidence, collect
the evidence and preserve the evidence.

CONTAMINATION OF PHYSICAL EVIDENCE
Can occur from improper methods of collection, storage or
shipment. Just like improper preservation of the incident
scene, any contamination of physical evidence may greatly
reduce or nullify the evidentiary value of the physical
evidence.

CAUTION
Unless proper care is taken, physical evidence may become
contaminated through the use of contaminated evidence
containers. For this reason, the investigator should take
every reasonable precaution to ensure that the “NEW”
containers are stored separately from used containers or
contaminated areas. An evidence collection container
should be opened only to receive evidence at the collection
point, at which time it should be resealed pending laboratory
examination.

CROSS CONTAMINATION
Most contamination of physical evidence occurs during the
collection phase. Always wear disposable plastic or rubber
surgical gloves. When it is necessary to collect more than
one sample, always dispose of the used gloves and utilize a
new pair prior to handling additional evidence. Each tool or
instrument utilized shall be cleaned prior to and after each
usage. Always clean the tool with sterile water between
each collection process.

EVIDENCE CONTAMINATION
   When collecting or even examining possible trace
    evidence, care must be taken to prevent cross
    contamination and loss of trace materials.
   If an investigation involves disciplines other than trace
    evidence, the involved investigators should confer

 Contact between items and/or the investigator, before

 Appropriate protective apparel, such as disposable

 Collect, package, and seal items individually in

  package until the item is processed in a controlled
  environment.
 Equipment surfaces used during collection and
  examination must be cleaned in an appropriate manner
  before processing begins and as often as necessary
  during processing to prevent contamination.
 Any contact, condition, or situation that could cause
  contamination or otherwise compromise the trace
  evidence examination must be documented and
  communicated between the laboratory analyst or
  analysts and the submitter.
 Trace evidence shall remain in secure, controlled-
  access areas, protected from loss, damage, or
  contamination. It must have a documented with a
  continuous chain of custody from the time of evidence

Paranormal Tips

Never take photographs without first removing the camera
strap. Loose straps have been the cause of many false
positives in paranormal investigation photos. Clean the lens
properly. Know how your camera operates before you start
taking photos. Do not smoke. Be aware of reflective surfaces
before taking your picture. Always carry fresh batteries for
your equipment. The use of a tripod and remote shutter
release are the preferred method of capturing a good photo.
