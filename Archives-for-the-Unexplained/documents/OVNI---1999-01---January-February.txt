                           THE MAN-MADE MILLENNIUM
Why all the fuss about the coming 'Millennium' year? We appear to be facing another phantom man-made
festival based upon vaguely calculated figures on the birth date of Jesus Christ. It is generally accepted that
the present calendar (introduced, 14 September 1752) is incorrect and that Jesus Christ was almost certainly
born four years earlier than had previously been estimated, therefore the 'Millennium' year commenced at the
end of 1996! We must also remember that this event only holds any significance with those referring to the
Christian calendar, in any event nobody knows the actual month of the birth of Jesus. It is acknowledged that
the Church changed the previous birth date of Jesus, to coincide with a mid-winter Pagan festival. A similar
situation exists in South America, where the converted native population worship Christianity, while still hold­
ing onto their old beliefs. Crucifixes can be seen standing outside small towns and villages, but they are also
adorned with skulls, bones and paintings of various types.

The Chinese will have no great interest in the 'Millennium, as they are probably entering the year of the 'Dog'
and the Islamic Calendar shows that the current year is 1419, changing to 1420 on the 17th of April. So why
all the fuss?   The only connection with 'our subject' is the fact that the Aliens have told more than one
contactee, that 'time is of our own making'.

Will we see the 'great revelation' in the coming year,        I don't think so.   The news of UFO incidents will
continue to be mainly ignored by the press and sidelined by military authorities. Lord Hill-Norton was unable
to receive a satisfactory reply to his tabled questions in the House of Lords in October 1997 on the subject of
the UFO landing in Rendlesham Forest in 1980 (see OVNI May/June 98). This took place near the military
bases of RAF Bentwaters and RA F Woodbridge and was the subject of Larry Warren's book 'Left At East
Gate'. Lord Hill-Norton was basically told that the M.O.D. received no such reports and in any case it was a
matter for the US authorities. If a person of such high standing as Lord Hill-Norton (a former British Military
Supremo and NATO Commander), could be fobbed off in such a manner, then their is little chance of anyone
learning much from the military authorities. To write to the M.O.D. and to expect a straightforward reply is
a waste of time and the cost of a stamp!

The past year has seen some interesting scientific advances, many of which would have been pure science­
fiction a few years ago.   It is interesting to note that several of the 'ridiculous' stories previously related by
abductees, no longer seem quite so outlandish. We have read of a tiny goat being born in an artificial womb,
cushioned in fluid and nurtured through tubes (doesn't that have a familiar ring?) 'Artificial Wombs In Ten
Years' screamed the headline in the Daily Mail. But while some experts hailed it as a major breakthrough,
others warned of horrific scenarios including 'farming' babies for experiments! (Even more familiar!)

Next, 'The Times' (5 October) told of a cybernetics professor who has been fitted with a computer implant.
The chip had been implanted in his right arm, allowing him to switch on lights automatically. The next step is
to have an implant that would allow him to develop telepathic communications, which may eventually allow
people to communicate without being in contact with each other. (It's all 'old hat' to the Greys!).

I read recently, that a scientist had managed to grow nerve fibres on a computer chip, with a view to this being
implanted in a brain at some future stage. Whatever is next...Cloned Humans?         Oh' I forgot...that is already
in progress.......Happy New Year to all our readers.

                           NEW MARS LANDER-EXPLORER LAUNCHED

A Mars lander craft, equipped with an arm and shovel, blasted off from Cape Canaveral on Sunday 3rd January 99.
It is on a quest to uncover frozen water near the planet's South Pole.

NASA's Mars Polar Lander soared aboard a Boeing rocket at exactly 3.21 p.m.            The rainy weather that launch
managers had feared, stayed away, allowing NASA to meet the instantaneous launch window, despite an overcast
sky.

The Polar Lander is bound for the frosty fringes of Mar's south polar cap. It's due to arrive in December, when it is
late spring on Mars and the sun never sets. It will be virgin territory: No spacecraft has ever ventured so far south
on Mars. As a result, scientists' expectations are high. Their goal is to find water-ice in the Martian soil. Where
there is water, NASA's top space scientist say there could be life!

''You've got to follow the water if you're looking for life," said Ed Weiler, head of NASA's space science division.
''We have a lot of reason to believe there's water on Mars in the form of ice," he added. ''But until you actually land
there and find it and measure it, you can't say for sure. That's one thing I think this mission will demonstrate."

The Polar Lander- 3 1/2 feet tall and 12 feet wide - has three legs as well as a 6 1/2 foot robot arm with a scoop on
the end to scrape beneath the Martian surface. The collected dirt will be heated, allowing any water that's present
to vaporize and be detected by laser. The lander craft carries no life-detection equipment. The only real way to
confirm life on Mars. Weiler says, is to fetch rocks and soil.

NASA plans to launch a soil-return mission in 2005; the samples would reach the Earth in 2008. The Polar Lander
is also equipped with a pair of probes that will peel away minutes before touchdown and slam into the Martian
surface at 400 mph to 500 mph, some 60 miles from the lander craft.

Like the lander, the twin probes will also hunt for water but at a much greater depth: They could penetrate as deep
as 3 feet. The question is whether they will survive the impact and radio back data. It's a high risk experiment called
Deep Space 2 that's part of NASA's New Millennium programme to test new technologies for future missions.

The Polar Lander is a companion to NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter launched last month. The two missions cost
$327 million and Deep Space 2 another $29 million.

        RETIRED NURSE PHOTOGRAPHS UFO IN TOOWONG, QUEENSLAND, AUSTRALIA

On November 6th 1998, retired nurse Madeleine Patterson, 63, had a sudden urge to go outdoors and snap a
photograph. Grabbing her camera, she stepped out onto the backstairs of her home in Toowong, Queensland,
Australia and snapped the shutter.

When the film was developed, she found the image of a silver disc UFO in the photograph.

''I didn't see the UFO at all," Mrs. Patterson said, ''What I had was a feeling there might be something strange to
photograph. It's something like when people get a feeling there's going to be a storm."

Retired nurse Mrs. Patterson, who is also a naturopath and nutritionist, has added the photograph to her growing
collection of cross formations and 'angels'. It includes six photographs of UFO's.
