       The English literature on this sub ject is very slim. There is a
short entry in Margaret Sachs' UFO Encyclopedia, a chapter in " The Invis­
ible College" by Jacques Vallee, and probably a few scattered articles in
Flying Saucer Review. I also understand that Wendelle Stevens published
a book about it, although I haven't seen it and cannot vouch for its re­
liability.
       The main source for UMMO is the prolific Spanish writer and ufolo­
gist Antonio Ribera, who wrote the classic " Un Caso Perfecto" with Rafael
Farriols, where they chronicle two UFO incidents near Madrid, which spear­
headed the UMMO affair by showing for the first time its characteristic
emblem ( similar to the astronomical symbol for the planet Uranus) on fly-
ing saucers.                                                            .
       These cases were the Aluche landing on Feb. 6th, 1966, and the San
Jose de Valderas landing and photo case on June lst, 1967. There were
several witnesses for both cases and, although CUFOS and GSW later label­
ed the photos a hoax, Ribera still stands by them. Another puzzling de­
tail was the appearance of little metallic tubes of nickel and other met­
als, containing inside sheets of polyvinyl fluoride. After extensive
analysis, the sheets were identified with a plastic produc t called " Ted­
lar" , which is manufactured by DuPont. The problem is that the product
was then restricted to military use and was not available on the open mar­
ket in Spain!
       The UMMO affair, however, goes well beyond these two UFO cases near
Madrid. As told in Ribera's book, " De Veras los OVNis N os Vigilan?" ,
starting in 1965 the Spanish UFO buff Fernando Sesma began to receive by
mail a number of technical papers relating to and supposedly written by
the OEMII, the inhabitants of UMMO. In the following years the network
expanded and the papers multiplied. Some of them were highly technical
tracts on the nature of the universe and of their planet; the biological
basis for intelligent life in the Cosmos (basically variations of          the
humanoid form); the propulsion of their spacecraft; etc.. All communica­
tions had the characteristic seal, similar to Uranus, of UMMOAELEWE, their
central government or Politburo. The envelopes were mailed from different
cities in Europe, but no return address was ever discovered. Most were
written in Spanish and a few in French.
       Skeptics and believers alike should be aware that the content of
the UMMO material is different from that of most contactee cases, because
it doesn't contain messianic messages of doom or salvation. The method
used is always by mail and not direct or telepathic contact. Unlike Ad­
amski, Siragusa, Meier, Rama, Ashtar Sheram, and countless other cosmic
souls trying to convert our confused human species, the OEMII write things
like, " In no way do we wish - and we sternly warn you about this - to see
you fall into the temptation of switching your religious, scientific, and
politico-economical ideas for ours....It would be a mistake for you to
adopt our ideas, concepts and statements at face value .... " Amen!
       To discuss the contents of even some of their papers is far beyond
the scope of this short report. Some of the interesting technical papers
- which excited a number of French scientists of some repute - were pub­
lished by Antonio Ribera in 1979 in his book " El Mensa je de UMMO " ,    which
contains also an UMMO-Spanish-French dictionary of 403 words, with lots
of OUUAS, OAWOO, etc. Unless you know this language, however, or at least
Spanish and French, you won't get far in this affair.
       To my knowledge, the mystery has never been solved as either a long­
range disinformation experiment or actual ET messages. The affair may al­
