Sappers  at fishing of ghost bombs in Lake Storsjön
In  the middle you can see one of two  embarkation-boats, with which  the specially constructed drag
are towed. At the right   “Captain of the Ghost bombs” of the(( Headquarter, Second command???)), captain L Falk, and at the far left sergeant Svahn, who are at charge of sweeping with the drag in lake Storsjön.
From  ÖP:s special correspondent.
Every day you can read  reports of observations of “ghost bombs” in different locations in the 
country and these observations have become one mystification.  Jämtland also have been paid visit to the phenomena  and in one location in the  neighbourhood  of lake Storsjön, where it was believed there was a crash some weeks ago, there have been eager searches for the projectile for ten days.
Despite the fact that you could limit the crash area rather well, the hunt have given no positive result. On the contrary  the mystery thickens.
The defence staff`s air defence section have been  compelled to establish  a special unit for putting together reports  of ghost missiles and  on the air defence section of((Andra militärbefälsstaben)) in Östersund captain  Lars Falk have considerable extra work with the spaceprojectiles that have been reported to have been passing different parts of our county. A couple of weeks ago some persons in the  neighbourhood of lake Storsjön observed such a ghost bomb, which with a whining sound passed  just over their heads and then seemed to crash into the lake. Report of the event immediately came to the hands of the  defence staff and captain Falk got the task to make certain investigations. From I5 was  detached two sergeants and five men from the sappers to sweep for the bomb and this work was still in full swing on the tuesday when ÖP:s co-workers was allowed to go with captain Falk on a inspection round.
The work is based on observations by no less than five eyewitnesses, whose  veracity  are in no doubt as the phenomena occurred in full daylight, tells captain Falk.  Three persons were placed at about the same space while the other two stood at different locations a distance away. Owing to this we have  been able to sketch  certain boundaries of probability resulting in an area limited on three sides in which the missile with greatest probability have crashed. Furthermore we have the heading of the projectiles trajectory fairly clear and thus we have had a moderately limited area to search.
