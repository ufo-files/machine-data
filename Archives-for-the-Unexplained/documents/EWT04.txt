STRANGENESS FACTORS
1. Introduction.
The essence of this research is a very restricted selection of Type 1 phenomena taken
from very strange cases and typical UFOs; potentially useful nocturnal lights, balls
of fire and other cases not quite so rich in information.
This selection of cases (in truth just 621 retained from a samplą of almost 3000 which
were examined) has been applied to a series of 103 indices of strangeness (1)
?. Primary documentation.
- A list of 103 "indices" which is distributed amongst 12 "characteristics" (lette-
red between A and W)
Each characteristic, with the indices, is then placed into the files, which then
display the different cases which have that particular index. (2)
3. Definitions
Strangeness
The strangeness is defined by an aspect of observation which appears
to us to be extraordinary (with reference to our normal perception)
Index of
Strangeness
A description element, situation or particular action which diver-
ges significantly from the normal course of an event or observation.
Characteristic
of Strangeness
Level of
Strangeness
A collection of strangeness indices.
The number of indices which are to be found in any observation
(varying between 1 and 8)

- All cases have an index which shows the correspondance with spience-fiction (S) and
mythology (M) These allow us to study the development of the UFO phenomenon alongsi-

of the enigma)
- The indices which show correspondance with science-fiction have been devised by

The indices which show correspondance with mythology have been determined by
J, Bastide (in his book'La Mémoire des OVNI").

Therefore we can make information widely available, by coding our entries onto perfo-

The intention is tto study possible correlation between the different indices; in
function and location, time and circunstance; also to possible discover a "scenario"
for the phénomenon, le the mechanics of its appearance, its reactions, its evolution
and in the end (ideally !) to find out what it is !
