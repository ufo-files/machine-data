Bein gs      in    Argentina -          Part     Two




                                                     Robert S.   Easley

           According to the Commission of Unidentified Flying Objects
in Argentina, two red-glowing UFOs appeared over a settlement of
civilized Indians, called Chaloc, on or about 22 February 1965.    One
of the egg-shaped objects landed on tripod legs on a near-by hill.
Three humanoid beings about 4� feet tall emerged from the craft and
proceeded to walk around it, as if inspecting it for damage.   The
beings wore opaque helmets over their heads.
           At this time one of the Indians hurried to police headquarters,
but by the time the authorities arrived the unknown object was leaving,
giving off a brilliant white light in the process.
           A policeman who saw the object succeeded in taking three
photographs of it.   Further investigation revealed three depressions
in the ground, supposedly where the object rested; and scorched brush,
apparently from the exhaust.
           The photographs, samples of the landing site, and the police­
man's report were sent to Buernos Aires for examination and analysis.
As yet, no official or unofficial word has been released from the
Buernos Aires government - despite the fact that the sighting is four
years old!
           On 30 March 1965 three objects were seen flying over the
town of Jose de San Martin.   Once outside the town one of the objects
appeared to touch d own.  Immediately local police went to the area,
and upon arrival, saw two humanoid-type creatures approximately 15
feet southeast of the cigar-shaped, bluish-white object.
           After apparently hearing the police car, the beings ran to
the object and scampered up a ramp leading to one of th e ends of the
'cigar'.   The object then rose straight up until it was lost to sight.
           Upon checking the area, the police found that the grass was
flattened where the object had sat.   No depressions, footprints, or
radioactivity could be found.
           1965 was a busy one, not only for the United States, but
also for many of the South American countries.   The saucers do indeed
have a global interest in our planet.
