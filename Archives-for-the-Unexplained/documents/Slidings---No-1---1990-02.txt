This is the first issue of SL IDINGS, the bulletin of SLIDE, and it doesn't
pretend to be anything more than some preliminary thoughts and
observations.   Hopefully, as we put together such bits and pieces, more
formal lines of direction may suggest themselves, patterns will emerge,
ideas will be generated - who knows where it may lead ?

What would really be a mistake would be for us to impose any kind of
direction or patterns ahead of gathering the data.  Equally, it would be
a mistake to impose any kind of filter on the data itself : at this stage,
we should note anything and everything that could conceivably have a
bearing on the phenomenon.

 So without further preamble, here is some of the input that has come in
 so far....


 Spontaneous     or deliberate    ?

 Most of those who report SLIDE effects reckon they can't do it to order ;
 but this is not universally the case.        Syd  Ellis of Hickory, North
 Carolina, who describes herself as 'a test technician in a security

        returning from my observation point, a mercury vapor street light
        went out as I passed it.    It has been SLI since then, regardless

            Usually, the lights go off by themselves, but occasionally I '11

        always on when I passed them the next day.

Another thought which will inevitably occur to some is whether SLI is·
linked to so-called 'poltergeist' manifestations.   Both seem to have in
common that they involve mental control over physical events. at a
distance from the individual.
       Harry Price, in Poltergeist over Englan d [Country Life, 1945,
p.335] refers to a case which may be relevant : it occurred in 1920, at
Salisbury, Wiltshire :
       ...Another incident was the switching on, in the night, of every
       electrical appliance that the 'Poltergeist' could lay hands on.
       The occupier's wife was ill in bed and the only staff was a young
       nursemaid who looked after a child of three.      The husband was
       first down in the morning and found the electric oven turned full
       on     the electric iron, on its side, was burning a hole in a
       portable ironing table      and a small electric cream-maker was
       going full blast.    The appliances that were not plugged in (e.g.
       the vacuum cleaner, etc) had not been interfered with.
The maid, though she was found 'sleeping peacably in her room with a
look of innocence on her face that disarmed all suspicion',           was
discharged and, though he does not explicitly say so, Price presumably
intends us to infer that the phenomena then ceased.       Today we would
want to know much more about the inter-personal dynamics of the
household before being so sure that we had identified the agent        it
might just as well have been the sick wife who was 'responsible',
perhaps out of jealousy of the nursemaid.

Suggested   explanation s


It would be premature to attempt to make a synthesis of ideas as to what
is going on, but that is no reason why we shouldn't note some of the
ideas that people have put forward.

George Egely:
      In my opinion during such incidents _some special, not presently
      known type of magnetic field is created around the body, which
      has an effect upon the structure of the materials.    Consequently
      their fundamental properties are changed temporarily    like their
      tensile stress, electric conductivity, magnetic momentum, optica1
      properties, etc.   The same effects are detected in the case of
      'metal bending' or similar features are observed sometimes around
      ball lightning.

Steven Yaple draws our attention to one of the few references to SLI in
the paranormal literature, in ESP, Hauntings an d Poltergeists by Loyd
Auerbac� (1986, Warner Books, p.32)
       In June 1984, while I was on WNBC-Radio's "Edge of Reality" show
       in New York, a caller reported the odd situation of street lights
       going off as he walked by them. Interestingly enough, I received
       a similar call on my return to California a week and a half later.
       While the experience could have been paranormal, it was highly

       probable that it was not.       Almost certainly what was happening
       was that headlights of passing cars were being reflected into the
       photoelectric cells of the lamps (the electric eye that automatically
       turns streetlights on and off as the sun sets and rises) .
A similar suggestion was made to Steven Yaple by jim Reeb, a PSINET
contributor, who added :
