   EXTRAORDINARY LIGHTS SEEN OVER PRESTON 2nd SEPTEMBER 05

                                  A report from Mr David Broderick*

In the early hours of the 2nd September 2005, I observed on looking through my kitchen window, an
object of unidentifiable proportions flying through the sky commencing at 0030 hrs and continuing until
0 1 00 hrs.
I had gone into my kitchen and looked up at the night sky, as I always do. The Plough was visible and
other stars, but there was some cloud that obscured the stars at times. Suddenly I saw a blue flash of
light go past! I thought 'what was that?' I then waited to see if it would come back and it did!
There were a series of oval blue lights, then as they came around again, they had changed to a V-shaped
object with an accompanying oval flying fast through the sky. (see sketch 1). The main craft had two
'tail' fins, one side shorter than the other and they all moved at great speed and their shape was sharply
defined and clear.
The objects were shooting right to left and then would stop for a fraction of second, making five or six
passes right to left, disappearing from view and then coming back in an arc over my house and away
over the River Ribble (see sketch 2). I opened my window but could hear no noise. I did not go outside,
thinking that perhaps they would disappear if I did.
I am not sure what height they were, but it was below cloud level. The objects were a light blue colour
like that of gas flame, it was a different colour to the stars. The wing shape seemed very large compared
to a plane and the oval at the tail end was always perfect in shape.
The lights continued to do this for half an hour and then disappeared. I waited a further 20 minutes but
they never returned. I was in awe and the hairs on the back of my neck were standing on end!
I have tried to think of an explanation and I can't. At first I thought of lasers, but there was no beam. I
have never seen anything like it before and was truly amazed.

                                  THE OLD PILOT'S ADVENTURE.

         The story of Major F.Turner-Bridger (Retd) interviewed by Omar Fowler in 1978



It was the autumn of 19 16, the little BE2c struggled for height as it headed for the German lines some­
where down in that shell pocked landscape. The little plane was piloted by Major F. Turner- Bridger and
for the first time, he had a companion behind him in the wind blown cockpit. The " 16th Air Reconnais­
sance Squadron" had been suffering appalling losses, the BE2c's had never been designed for combat
and were no match for the German Fokker Triplanes. The Major cursed at having to take on extra
weight, one man with his machine gun, a vain attempt to stop the slaughter of the air crews, over France.

Their difficult task was to "spot" for the artillery batteries, hidden somewhere below, to try and direct
their fire onto a suitable target. It was a job strewn with danger, there was the German anti-aircraft fire,
the real fear of being attacked by enemy fighters and of course the difficult job in itself of trying to keep
the little plane airborne with it's heavy load, two men and a machine gun                              .




The Hispano-Suiza engine thundered on, one hundred and fifty horse power pulling them gradually
skyward, higher and higher. The fact that they had no parachutes barely crossed the mind of Major
Turner-Bridger, he was intent on keeping an eye open for German fighter planes, he was not to be disap­
pointed. . . He spotted a small speck in the distance, now coming closer. Before long he was able to make
out the familiar tri-plane structure. The glint of the sun on the big black German crosses only confirmed
what he had thought, it was a Fokker Tri-plane! They were dead men unless his machine gunner could
scare the enemy plane off1
The Major reached behind him and jabbed at the gunner furiously, but the man had also seen the fighter
coming and had slid down onto the bottom of his cockpit floor. The Major jabbed again, but it was no
use, the gunner was terrified, he and his gun were just useless extra weight in the little biplane. He made
a snap decision, they were of no use, they would have to go!
The Major jinked his plane down and around as fast as he dare and headed down to the British "lines".
All he could see was a zig-zag line of trenches, shell holes and lines of barbed wire. Anxiously he
looked for somewhere to land. A small strip of bumpy ground by one of the leading trenches was the
only chance, he flattened out from his shallow dive, quickly touched down and bumped to a stop.

He turned around in his cockpit, grabbed the gunner by the scruff of his neck and threw him out of the
plane, his gun followed him. Then with the aid of a few British soldiers, he turned the plane around then
revved up the engine as much as he dare. The soldiers hung on grimly to the vibrating biplane and then,
at a signal from the Major, they let go. The biplane bobbed and bounced across the rough ground and
then with one final bounce, it was airborne.
This then was the calibre of the man, Major F. Turner Bridger, who was to encounter an even stranger
aerial machine 'twenty one years later' in October 1937   ...   ...See over page.

 MAJOR F.TURNER-BRIDGER'S UFO ENCOUNTER IN 1937
                                  (Related to Omar Fowler in 1986)

It was about September or October 1937 and about 4pm in the afternoon when my wife and I were
motoring back from Sussex to Haslemere (Surrey) where I then lived

On returning to the main road from Haslemere to Northchapel, I drove along a bye-way lane, where I
suddenly pulled up, as I saw a mysterious object on my left, at the brow ofBlackdown Hill. I pulled up
on the side of the lane, next to an unused field.

On the summit ofBlackdown Hill I suddenly noticed an unusual object hovering, I have drawn a sketch
showing the shape of the object and also a side view (see below).

The object had a hump in the centre and was surrounded by an orange flaming surround.

It so happened that whilst I was watching this curious object, I noticed another car had also pulled up
behind me. The object obviously noticed a car in this isolated lane

Suddenly the object flew back over the ridge of Blackdown Hill and out of sight. I waited some five
minutes or longer and then noticed the same object had re-appeared again some distance along this ridge
than previously.

I presumed that in this very lonely spot, the 'occupants' were surprised to find a car below and had
moved off, later to return to the ridge of the hill. I watched it in wonderment as it eventually
disappeared at terrific speed.

My thoughts then began to wonder and as Farnborough (Royal Aircraft Establishment) was not many
miles away, I imagined that it a very scientific object being tested out. At this point I may mention that
the view covered almost the whole terrain of Surrey.

I may add that I, as many people had since told me, I envisaged the coming war (WW2 1939-45). I even
went to Poland with my family, to verify my thoughts and my observations on my return were known to
many. I have since beentold that "if everyone had listened to you there wouldn't have been a war."

As an ex-flying pilot in the R.F.C. (Royal Flying Corps) in 1916 (having previously been in the trenches
in 1915 with the Kings Own Scottish Borderers), I though that this amazing object that I had seen was
evidence of a secret experiment being carried out at Farnborough.

Over these past years I have thought a great deal of this experience and was pleased to hear you mention
on the ITV Television that you would like to hear from anyone with a UFO experience, past or present. I
then telephoned you.

 From the "Bognor Regis Observer" Thursday 27 October 05

                                          SCIENCE @NASA
    'Earth is orbiting through a swarm of space debris that may be producing an unusual
    number of night-time fireballs.'

    November 3, 2005: "I thought some wise guy was shining a spotlight at me," says Josh Bowers ofNew
    Germany, Pennsylvania. "Then I realized what it was: a fireball in the southern sky. I was doing some
    backyard astronomy around 9 p.m. on Halloween (Oct. 31, 2005), and this meteor was so bright it made
    me lose my night vision."

    Bowers wasn't the only one who saw the fireball. Lots of people were outdoors Trick or Treating. They
    saw what Bowers saw ... and more. Before the night was over, reports of meteors "brighter than a full
    moon" were streaming in from coast to coast.

    Astronomers have taken to calling these the "Halloween fireballs." But there's more to it than Halloween.
l   The display has been going on for days.

    On Oct. 30, for example, Bill Plaskon of Jonesport, Maine, was "observing Mars through a 1 0-inch
    telescope at 1 0:04 p.m. EST when a brilliant fireball lit up the sky and left a short corkscrew-like smoke
    trail that lasted about 1 minute.

    "On Oct 28, Lance Taylor of Edmonton, Alberta, woke up early to go fishing with five friends. At about 6
    a.m. they "noticed a nice fireball. Then 20 minutes later there was another," he says.

    OnNov. 2 in the Netherlands, "The sky lit up very bright," reports Koen Miskotte. "In the corner of my
    eye I saw a fireball about as bright [as a crescent moon]."

    And so on ... .

    What's happening? "People are probably seeing the Taurid meteor shower," says meteor expert David
    Asher of the Armagh Observatory inNorthern Ireland.

    Every year in late October and earlyNovember, he explains, Earth passes through a river of space dust
    associated with Comet Encke. Tiny grains hit our atmosphere at 65,000 mph. At that speed, even a tiny
    smidgen of dust makes a vivid streak of light--a meteor--when it disintegrates. Because these meteors
    shoot out of the constellation Taurus, they're called Taurids.

    Most years the shower is weak, producing no more than five rather dim meteors every hour. But
    occasionally, the Taurids put on quite a show. Fireballs streak across the sky, ruining night vision and
    interrupting fishing trips. Asher thinks 2005 could be such a year.

    According to Asher, the fireballs come from a swarm of particles bigger than the usual dust grains.
    "They're about the size of pebbles or small stones," he says. (It may seem unbelievable that a pebble can
    produce a fireball as bright as the Moon, but remember, these things hit the atmosphere at very high
    speed.) The rocky swarm moves within the greater Taurid dust stream, sometimes hitting Earth,
    sometimes not.

    "In the early 1 990s, when Victor Clube was supervising my PhD work on Taurids," recalls Asher, "we
    came up with this model of a swarm within the Taurid stream to explain enhanced numbers of bright
    Taurid meteors being observed in particular years." They listed "swarm years" in a 1 993 paper in the
    Quarterly Journal of the Royal Astronomical Society and predicted an encounter in 2005.
    According to the International Meteor Organization, the Taurid shower peaks betweenNov. 5th andNov.
    1 2th (details). "Earth takes a week or two to traverse the swarm," notes Asher. "This comparatively long
    duration means you don't get spectacular outbursts like a Leonid meteor storm." It's more of a slow
    drizzle--"maybe one every few hours," says Asher.

    A drizzle of fireballs, however, is nothing to sneeze at. So keep an eye on the sky this month for Taurids.

                                                                      TALE ENDERS

SOLAR FLARES. October 7, 2005: Last month, the sun went haywire. Almost every day for two
weeks in early September, solar flares issued from a giant sunspot named "active region 798/808." X-rays
ionized Earth's upper atmosphere. Solar protons peppered the Moon. It was not a good time to be in
space. Or was it?
During the storms, something strange happened onboard the International Space Station (ISS): radiation
levels dropped. "The crew of the ISS absorbed about 30% fewer cosmic rays than usual," says Frank
Cucinotta, NASA's chief radiation health officer at the Johnson Space Center. "The storms actually
improved the radiation environment inside the station."
Even inside their ships, astronauts are exposed to a slow drizzle of cosmic rays coming right through the
hull. The particles penetrate flesh, damaging tissue at the microscopic level. One possible side-effect is
broken DNA, which can, over the course of time, cause cancer, cataracts and other maladies.
