               Not long ago, the interesting details of a little
             known fact has come to my attention. It concerns a
             Boston family who, sonie.250years ago, suddenly
             became objects of scorn and ridicule in their
             small, rather tight-knit community.
                So frightening are the implications of the unjust
             prejudices this family suffered at the hands of
             their once kindly and loving neighbors, it made
             me consider that if John Peece had been alive
             today, more that 2 centuries later, his fate, and
             that of his wife and children, would had been
             different.
                But this wasn' t to be- not in the year 1727.
             Instead, Peece was to exist in a time when fear and superstition reigned supreme,
             hidden only beneath a gauze-thin surface of neighborly understanding and sympa­
             thy, ready to show itself at a moment'. s notice.
               Peece, a carriage manufacturer, was 36 at the .time of the incident, which began on
             the night of January 22. His wife, Molly, two years younger, and pregnant at the
             time, had been a school teacher. Their two little girls, Agatha 3, and June, 5, like
             their parents, had suffered unpardonably as well at the hands of so-called enlighten­
             ed and educated townspeople.
               Their friends and admirers- many of whom had been at their wedding- number­
             ed most of the township.
                And then, Qn that fateful night it began, to last for six horror-filled months. It
             started with moans and rappings eminating from the walls of their cottage on Cedar
             Lane1 by forces and entities unknown.
                The furniture began moving of its own volition. Doors closed and opened again.
             Strange lights circled -the ceiling in every room, and on several occasions, Peece was
             knocked on the head and in the face with kitchen objects.
               It became so bad at times, that the entire family was driven into the street. Shortly
             before this happened, the family told of small fires which began in the main room
             "by themselves," and just as suddenly as they erupted, they vanished. Also,
             booted footprints were seen all over the house.
               The hauntings in the Peece household were considered th� devil's work and the
             family, including the children, were tarred and feathered and driven from the
             community. The house itself was set ablaze by hostile citizens and burned to the
             ground.
                Since that time, science has observed and investigated thousands of similar cases
             worldwide, and has come to term such bizarre incidents as poltergeists
             phenomena, and psychokinesis, (PK), moving objects with the mind.
                Although we still have no clear understanding of these psychological disturbances,
             science prods forward in its search for the answers, neyer ostraizing as outcasts
         ·   those afflicted, but with compassion for them. And since science is on the alert,
             people as well have become less biased and more tolerant of mysertious forces which
             have come to be known, perhaps somewhat jokingly, as ''things that go bump in the
             night.''
