     The Meeting began at around 9:20 and ended at around 19:50 . Firstly Mr. Amamiya briefly
introduced several recent domestic and foreign topics including Japanese mysterious failings of

     I (1952-) next lectured for around 30 minutes on the photos of "Lubbock Lights" (1951) taken
by Mr. Cart Hart, Jr. (see the last issue). This was the first long speech by me for the Meetings .

South America, in 1960. This tidal wave hit Northern Japan, and he, at the time a senior high
school student, witnessed it . Though his story was accompanied by no UFO, old records says that
a UFO saved a Japanese town from a tsunami in 1707 (see the 10th issue page 2) . His wife is Ms.
Harumi Kamata who heard a mysterious flying sound at the last Meeting (see the 14th issue page
1, the last issue page 4) .
     For the last part of the morning, Mr. Osamu Sato (1963-), the president of Sky People
Association, gave a lecture. He projected many UFO photos taken by himself onto the screen
(showing one photo, Mr. Sato gave a sour smile: this UFO was very much like a certain forged
UFO!). He reached a new stage: he receives a cloud, not cloud-like, as a UFO, if the circumstance
make him so. Objectivity can not satisfy a person who walks with UFOs.
     The first speaker for the afternoon was Mr. Akira Baba, a documentary-maker for various TV
stations. His plan to take up the UFO problem had been rejected by NHK, a virtual government­
managed broadcasting station. Some 9 years before, at Kawasaki City, Kanagawa Prefecture, he
saw a flat, triangular UFO composed of four triangles. Mr. Saba hopes NHK accepts Mr. Amamiya.

                                       Tadpoles Fall
                                 Falls of tadpoles created a sensation in Japan this June . The origin
                               was an event in Ishikawa Prefecture that faces the Sea of Japan:
                               early in the evening of June 4, at the parking lot of Nakajima Shimin
                               Senta (Nakajima Citizens' Center) in Nanao City, a male staff heard
                               the sound of "pon pon pon," and saw tadpoles fall with a light
                               rain (# 1). Around 100 tadpoles were scattered within a radius
                               of around 10 meters ( #2).
                                   In Hakusan City of the prefecture, on the morning of June 6,
around 30 crushed-tadpoles were discovered on a parking lot and near there within a radius
of around 5 meters. Neither water nor mud was there. The night before a woman who
lived across a street from the parking lot heard the sound of "don" (this expression is used for
an explosion or a collision) in her house and felt the house shake (#3).
    Fall-stories were reported one after another from several places in the prefecture, and it was
not long before many other prefectures, including ones face the Pacific Ocean, joined. Moreover,
frogs and fish joined. Quantity and state of creatures were various. However, not many people
saw falling itself.
    We will focus our attention on the first two events of Nanao City and Hakusan City. A tornado
can suck up things in the water. But weather data of the two days allow no tornado (#3).
Some whirlwinds may have been too small-scale to be caught by meteorological observatories.
But no strong wind was noticed (#4). A (gray) heron can eat around 50 tadpoles at a time
( #5). Were the tadpoles vomited by birds from the air? But no bird was noticed in the two
events (#5). Besides, bird-specialists are divided in opinion on the vomit hypothesis (#6). Were
                                                                                                           3
    the tadpoles dropped, as one weather observation company reasoned in real earnest, from
    unmanned balloons launched somewhere in the Asian continent ? ( #7) But a photo offered by
    Nakajima Shimin Senta to the press shows a not-crushed tadpole (see the above photo). How
    did the weak creature resist the shock of falling ?
         I noticed that parking lots and motorcars often appeared as falling spots (#8). That sound
    "don" of Hakusan City may have been a kind of thunderclap, and this electrical phenomenon may
    have had a tendency to like places where metal objects were.
         Mr. Akio Tanaka of CBA (Cosmic Brotherhood Association), Japanese legendary UFO group,
    once conjectured that the sky has a mechanism to make copies of a living thing. His theory was
    offered to explain how Jehovah had gathered numerous quails to rain onto the Israeli led by
    Moses. An ancient Egyptian papyrus, introduced by Prince Boris de Rachewiltz in Doubt magazine
    of 1953, describes fall of "fishes" that happened after the appearance of "fire circles" in the sky
    (see the 11th issue page 2). Mr. Tanaka supposed that an effect of UFOs to space had caused this
    falling (#9}.
         At around 4:05 of June 28, off the coast of the Noto Peninsula in that Ishikawa Prefecture,
    fishing boats and the patrol boat "Hakusan" of the Maritime Safety Agency saw a mysterious light
    (#10). This soundless object moved, shining with red or green, from the east to the west "like a
    obi (a wide belt for kimono, Japanese traditional clothes)." It was seen for 10-15 seconds or so.
    No radar at Komatsu Air Base, in the prefecture, of Air Self-Defense Force {Japanese air force)
    caught it. And no flight-maneuver was done by the base at that time. What may be the same
    object was seen also off the coast of Niigata Prefecture, next but one to Ishikawa Prefecture.
         Of course, this story is too vague.

                                Mr. Kazuo Shimizu Passes Away
    An executive of Nihon Uchu Gensho Kenkyu-kai (Japan Space Phenomena Society), an
    excellent, but now almost at rest, scientific UFO group by the second generation, Mr. Kazuo
    Shimizu, born in 1954, passed away of illness on July 3.
        I know Mr. Shimizu only through his works. He wrote a brave book UFO no Uso (Ues on
    UFOs; published by Deta-hausu, 251 pages, 1990) packed with dishonorable facts of Japanese TV
    stations, newspapers and some authors. Another important book, UFO to Zsei-Jin no Nazo (The
    Mystery of UFOs and Aliens; published by Ikeda-shoten, 239 pages, 1984), a joint work with
    Mr. Kinichi Arai (1923-2002}, one of the earliest UFOiogists in Japan (see the third issue),
    showed his rich knowledge of Western new UFOiogy.
        On the origin of UFOs, he was attracted by "the Psychic Projection Theory" for a period. But as
    time went on, he became one of leading skeptics in Japan. It is a great pity that he did not focus
    his efforts on the field of UFOiogy.
