                         THE HUMAN COST.


     It was the summer of 1989, the Network was only a little
over a year old. I had been asked by Bill Thornton to deliver
a talk at his book store, The Metamorphosis, located in SayvilLe
off of Main Street. The talk was to be on U.F.O.'s which was
to include both slides and a video presentation. The talk had
been planned some two months in advance with the hope of
attracting a large audience. Bill Thornton, as usual, is a great
supporter of the Network and had helped to plan this talk as
an effort to support the new organization, plus to promote his
book store. His store has become a focus for new age materials
and U.F. 0. books ·both old and new editions.
     The talk began around seven that evening with a modest
turnout of some twenty persons in attendance. The talk featured
slides concerning some of the cases that LIUFON had investigated
plus a video on UFOs in general. The talk was over by nine,
with questions being fired by the enthusiastic listeners for
answers concerning the phenomenon that we all knew existed.
Towards the end of the question and answer period the audience
began drifting out of the store, I began to pack up the projector
and the video equipment. At that one point, a young man in his
mid thirties came forward towards me to catch me in a private
conversation.
     "Thank you and the other UFO Researchers for keeping this
subject in the public's eye", he said. "My family knows all
too well the truth of the phenomenon and the fact that the
government is keeping a lot s e cre t from the people", he added.
"Your talks serve to bring attention to the truth of the reality
of all this," he proceeded to add while embarking on a story
that affected his family.
     It seems that his father was a Air Force Captain who was
involved in a 1957 chase of a UFO which involved his plane being
vectored into checking out a high flying unknown. His father
became involved after several other interceptors were unable
to catch the ob ject. When his fa�her's plane neared the object,
it crashed causing his father's instant death. The Air Force
refused to explain the circumstances surrounding his father's
death other ·than telling his mother that there was oxygen failure
on board the plane. His father was buried after a closed casket.
funeral under instructions from the Air Force. He told me further
that he, his brother and mother have been for the last ten years
attempting to get the Air Force to release all the records on
the case to no avail.
     He thanked me again for keeping the subject of UFOs alive
and under public scrutiny. He promised to keep in touch with
me on the progress on his legal case.
     During the forties, fifties, sixties and seventies, reports
have circulated that many pilots were lost by the Air Force
in vain attempts to catch these ellusive '  objects. Most if not
all such stories were flatly denied or explained away by the
Air Force. The result in either case was a high toll in human
lives . This gentleman and his family were the prime example
of the human cost of the phenomenon.
      When skeptics argue against the reality of the phenomenon,
let them argue away the human cost, but first let them talk
to the families of the dead, the missing, the abductees and
the medically impaired. Let the skeptics tell these people,
face to face, that UFOs don't exist and let us see if they can
convince the survivors of the human cost factor of the phenomenon
that UFOs are not re al. This young man and his family are just
part of the human factor which is forgotten._by the d�bunkers
and skeptics.
