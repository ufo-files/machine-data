       EXTRAORDINARY UFO SIGHTING OVER DERBY 4-2-2004
It was about 11.45pm on the night of Wednesday 4th ofFebruary this year when Mrs 'TB' was woken up
from her sleep by a loud "crackling noise". She thought at first that it might have been a firework, then
almost at the same moment, her bedroom "lit up". Thinking that the front door security light may have
been activated Mrs 'TB' quickly moved over to a side window and looked down, but there was no sign of
anyone there. She then thought that the light must have been the result of bright moonlight and she moved
to another window that overlooked the nearby 'Allestree Golf Course' for a clearer view.

It was then that she saw what she described as "An orange fireball" slowly moving across the sky. The
ball of orange light left an illuminated sparkly trail as it passed over the golf course, heading in the direc­
tion of Coxbench. It was flying fairly low and passed over the distant trees, when she lost sight of it.

"It was about the height and speed of a low flying aircraft" said Mrs 'TB', "But it was the loud crackling
noise that woke me up."

"I later looked on the TV news for any report of the object, but there was nothing. I then rang BBC Radio
Derby, who passed on the telephone number of the PRA".

               GHOST AIRCRAFT OVER NORTH YORKSHIRE ?
                                        (From the Craven Herald 23.1.04)
 A VISITOR to Barnoldswick was astounded to see a large grey aeroplane appear out of the mist near the
 Rolls- Royce factory- and then vanish.

 The plane looked as though it was going to hit both her car she and her partner were travelling in, as well as
 nearby houses, but a second later it had disappeared without trace.

 Moira Thwaites, a retired policewoman from Nelson, explained that she and her partner Malcolm Spensley, of
 Gargrave, were travelling towards Barnoldswick along Skipton Road at around 11.20am last Tuesday. As
 they approached Rolls-Royce's Bankfield factory both said they saw a huge, grey coloured plane - possibly a
 Lancaster bomber-type with four propellers - emerging silently from the mist to their right.

 A split second later they were past it, but saw nothing more.

 "lt was so low I fully expected it to hit us, or at least hit the houses near the Bankfield site," she said. "We both
 fully expected to at least hear the impact of a crash, but there was nothing. And when we both looked back
 there was nothing. V\lhatever it was had vanished.;;

 Mrs Thwaites added: ''There wasn't a sound from the engine at all. lt was really weird, but we both know what
 we saw and I just wondered if anyone else had reported seeing anything."

 To try and explain her experience, Mrs Thwaites contacted aerial phenomena expert Donald Cooper,
 president of the Skipton research society SE RI US. He is now eager to hear from anyone else who has seen
 the same image, or witnessed something similar at another time.

 "I spoke at length to Mrs Thwaites," said Mr Cooper. "She is a very sensible and intelligent person who,
 possibly because of her being an ex-policewoman, is not easily fazed.

 'We know from research that 'phantom' aircraft have been seen around Yorkshire.

 "lt is difficult to come up with a reasonable explanation that people can understand. There is a lot of electrical
 energy and physics that we are not even aware of surrounding the earth and a site like Rolls-Royce itself
 generates a great deal of electrical energy. Mrs Thwaites is adamant what she saw was a plane, but whether
 it was something breaking through another dimension or paranormal activity I cannot tell her," he added.

Following last week's story in which a couple told how they saw a Lancaster bomber-type plane on collision
course with houses near the Rolls-Royce site at Bankfield, Skipton aerial phenomena expert Donald Cooper
was inundated with phone caiis from people all over Craven who reported similar sightings.

Most told of what they described as a low flying Lancaster-type plane and several said they had seen it the
same day as the couple in the story, but in different locations.

Others were unsure of the day, but most were uniform in their description of the aircraft in that it was dark
grey, with no markings and, although the propellers were rotating, was making no sound.

"\must have taken 30 or more calls since Friday from a variety of people ranging from professionals to
members of the public, councillors to pub landlords," said Mr Cooper.

"Some have even come forward saying they saw something of that description several years ago. In particular
one elderly gentleman rang to say he had seen the plane the article referred to.

"He also said there used to be an old airfield at Greenberfield Lane (in Barnoldswick) which I didn't know
about."

Coincidentally, Mr Cooper pointed out that an article had also appeared in a national newspaper last Friday
about "time slips", which said there had been many reports of people claiming to have seen World War Two
aircraft as if re-running missions, often silently, from 60 years before.

Both Mr Cooper and his eo- researcher Joseph Skelton now want to know if anyone has any knowledge of
any aircraft making a forced landing near Greenberfield Lane in Bamoldswick during the last war. They can
contacted on 01756 791957 or 01756 795006.

                     REVELATIONS OF AN 'IFO' OVER DERBY
In the early hours of Sunday the 25th January this year, Mr Peter Brown was about to go to bed at his
home, situated in a 2nd floor flat at Chester Green, Derby. The time was 1. 15am when Peter decided to
glance out of his bedroom window before finally getting into bed.
He looked through the vertical blinds and was amazed to see "A cloud of dancing dots of light'' moving
slowly across the sky from west to northwest. The lights had formed into a circular pattern and were slowly
moving back and forth across the low cloud. Peter stood and watched the lights repeat their pattern for
some time "possibly 40 minutes'' he commented.
Puzzled by what he was seeing Peter decided to go outside for a clearer view and to watch the lights from
his garden. Now standing in the clear night air Peter wondered whether the dots of light were the result of a
laser light show somewhere, but was unable to see any beams reaching skyward.
Peter had described the light display as "spectacular and intensely bright" and he was mystified as to what
they were and how they stopped in the sky and then reformed before moving away and repeating their dis­
play on or above the clouds.
-He cannot recollect quite how long it was before the lights stopped, but he noticed that the clouds were dis­
 persing and as they did so the lights disappeared. Peter returned to his bed, somewhat mystified by what he
 had seen.
The following day, Peter began to make enquiries, first of all with the local paper and radio station and then
he decided to contact Derby University. It was here that the story began to unfold... Yes there had been a
celebration of some kind on Saturday night, but the organiser would not be available until the following
day (Tuesday). Anxious to find the solution to the mysterious lights Peter telephone the 'Gala' organiser
on the following day. He chatted for a while and the story began to unfold. The University had hired a
'light specialist' group called the 'Star Flower Searchlight Company' and they had set up a battery of
searchlights (not laser lights) to create a moving pattern of lights reflecting off the base of the low clouds.
This had gone on for some hours during the night and early morning. It had been so successful that the
Derby University group responsible for the celebration had hired the company for their next Gala on the
                                                                                                 ·



14th May. (Make a note of the date!) ... .Well done to Peter for finding the solution!
We can learn a salutary lesson from Peters findings. While we investigate reports from numerous sources,
we are seeking a solution and a rational explanation. We do not blindly accept that everything reported is
probably an extraterrestrial craft of some kind! Ed

              THERE'S SOMETHING STRANGE ABOUT 'IKEA'
                                  (Or rather that area of Nottinghamshire)
On page 5 we featured the 'IFO' report from Mr Peter Brown. It was while discussing this incident that
Peter revealed he had experienced a close encounter with a UFO many years ago.
It happened in the area where the 'IKEA' superstore is now situated. Strangely, this was also the scene
of a 'Flying Saucer' encounter on the 8th February 1999 (see OVNI May/June issue 1999).
In that instance the 'saucer' was viewed through binoculars and was close enough for hieroglyphic let­
tering to be seen on the craft.
Peter's report is given below:
This event happened over 30 years ago back in 1972. I had left my home in Amold to see my then girl­
friend in Langley Mill. I had a motor bike then and I was due to arrive at Langley Mill about 7.30pm.
On leaving  Kimberley, I was going downhill towards Giltbrook (this was before the_ Kimberley­
Eastwood By-Pass and IKEA had been built) when I noticed 'at 10 o'clock', a very bright object coming
towards me slowly, low in the sky. It was large bullet-shaped object and an intensely bright white, (see
drawing below).
I slowed down and stopped, shut my engine off and then put my visor up so that I could have a look at
what this was. By the time I had stopped, the object was slightly behind me and to the right.
After a few seconds, it accelerated from nothing to very fast and disappeared over the hills to the right of
me.
The whole thing lasted only 1 to 2 minutes. There was no wind on that night and there were no other
cars or people around. It was all countryside at that point and it happened near to where IKEA is now.
It was definitely not a plane or helicopter and there was no sound coming from the object at all.
Peter also added that the object could be·seen clearly and was sharply defined. It was also difficult to
judge the size of the object, but he said that it was "large". It had first appeared in the Northwest and dis­
appeared in a South-easterly direction.
An interesting point is that Peter stated the object had slowed down as it approached him and was
"almost stationary for quite a while". As far as he recollects he suffered no time loss and did not suffer
an subsequent stress as a result of the encounter. His final comment was "I had never seen anything so
intense or large like it before."
Peter later rang the Watnall Met. Station and told them about his sighting, but no one else had reported
the object.

                        A 'CLOSE CALL' FOR PLANET EARTH

                               (BBC News Online Science Editor)
          Astronomers have revealed how they came within minutes of alerting the world
          to a potential asteroid strike last month.
          Some scientists believed on 13 January that a 30m object, later designated
           2004 AS1, had a one-in-four chance of hitting the planet within 36 hours.
           lt could have caused local devastation and the researchers contemplated a
          call to President Bush before new data finally showed there was no danger.
          The procedures for raising the alarm in such circumstances are now being
          revised.
          At the time, the president's team would have been putting the final touches
          to a speech he was due to make the following day at the headquarters of
          Nasa, the US space agency.
           In it he planned to reset the course of manned spaceflight, sending it back
          to the Moon and on to Mars, but he could have had something very different
          to say.
          tf... the call had been made to the president it would flav� been disastrous
          Brian Marsden, Minor Planet Canter
          He could have begun by warning the world it was about to be hit by a space
          rock.
          Bush would not have known where it would impact - only somewhere in the
          Northern Hemisphere. Experts would have been bouncing radar signals off the
          huge rock as he spoke in order to get more information about its trajectory.
          At about 30m wide, the asteroid was cosmic small fry, not the type of thing
          to wipe out the dinosaurs or threaten our species, but still big enough to
          cause considerable damage after exploding in the atmosphere.
          Potentially, the loss of life could have been much worse than 11 September.
          In the end, Bush made no such announcement, but astronomers have admitted
          they were on the verge of making the call.
          Shall we call the President?
          In a paper presented at this week's Planetary Protection conference in
          California, veteran asteroid researcher Clark Chapman calls it a "nine-hour
          crisis".
          He explains how word reached the astronomical community of an asteroid that
          had just been discovered by the twin optical telescopes of the Linear
          automated sky survey in New Mexico.
          The Minor Planet Canter in Massachusetts - the clearing house for such
          observations - posted details on the internet requesting attention from
          astronomers, one of whom noticed something peculiar.
          The object was expected to grow 40-times brighter in the next day - a
          possible sign that it was getting closer, very rapidly.
          But with data from just four observations available, the uncertainties were
          large. There were many possible orbits the object could be on, and the
          majority of them did not threaten the Earth.
          VVhat to do? Tell the world about the uncertain situation or wait for more data?
          Fortunately for all concerned, shortly after the ominous Chesley e-mail, an
          amateur astronomer managed to dodge the clouds and take a picture of a blank
          patch of sky.
          This was significant because if 2004 AS 1 really was going to hit the Earth,
          it would have been in the amateur's sights. The fact that it was absent
          meant the rock would not strike us.
          But Chapman says in his presentation that if it had been cloudy, and no more
          observations could have been obtained at the time, he would have raised the
          alarm.
          Marsden disagrees. "If it had been cloudy and the call had been made to the
          President it would have been disastrous."
          Many astronomers recognise that a false alarm could have brought ridicule on
          their profession. They are calling for more planning and less panic if it
          should happen for real next time.
          And 2004 AS1? lt turned out to be bigger than anyone had thought - about
          500m wide. lt eventually passed the Earth at a distance of about 12 million
          km - 32 times the Earth-Moon distance, posing no danger to us whatsoever.
