              U.F.O.s have been seen the world over -and Norway is no
         exception as the above headline shows.
              It accompanied an article which appeared in the newspaper
         "Tonsberg Blad'• on January 13. The story was very kindly translated
         for f.I.G.A.P. by a Norwegian woman living in Tilford, near Farnham,
         along with several others.
              Here is a condensed version of the January 13 article:-
         u.F.o.s ••..•• have, during recent years, drawn great attention all
         over the world. A report from an American association a few years
         ago showed that Scandinavia, Norway in particular, was one of the ·
         places where U.F.O.s were seen very regularly. Confirmation of this
         has been impossible. But Wilhelm s. Simonsen, Chairman of the
         "Popular Scientific Association", says that observations are quite
         regular and that during the past year 40 separate sightings were
         reported to the association. Just over lOO Norwegians had seen
         U.F.O.s in the sky.
              Mr. 8imonsen told the n.Tonsberg Blad" that it was impossible
         for a single person or association to get the complete story on
         what had, or had not, been seen in the sky. "We do a great deal of
         work in registering activity in the sky over the whole country.
         But we are not the only ones. A number of other groups probably have
         information that we do not. We cannot present the total number of
         sightings in Norway, but we have a good picture as our numbers show.
         In the beginning of December a number of people in the Tonsberg area
         saw light phenomena in the sky and the "Tonsberg Blad" g-ot in touch
         with a number of astrophysical organisations in order to get an
         explanation. Scientists say it was either Venus, a star, or even
         a satellite".
             'We are aware that a .lot of scientists are willing to explain
         U.F.o. s away as natural phenomena'~ Mr. Simonsen comments, "but in
         our view such answers are classified as popular and easy. Those
         who go through all our reports and data will find that only a few
         of the reports fall into these categories. Of the 40 reports that
         we have listed, it can be seen that only two are likely misinterp-
         retations of natural light phenomena.
              "The balloon satellite Echo II caused a lot of U.F.o. reports,
         but it looked nothing more than a strong star. Meteors and
         satellites move quite quickly and evenly and they have a flame-like
         tail when they enter the Earth's atmosphere. But the strange thing
         is that observed U.F.O.s have changed course and speed, stood still
         and behaved f~r differently from a meteor or a satellite.

     Here is another report from the "Tonsberg Blad" translated
specially for s.I.G.A.P. This one is dated December 17, 1968 and
deals with part of the Norwegian U.F.O. flap at the end of the
year ~ -


     The glowing object which early on Saturday evening was seen by
a man in a car near the canal bridge and later followed the driver
towards Tenvik, was observed from several quarters the same evening.
"Tonsberg Blad" yesterday got directions from six people in Notte~oy,
plus one from Slagensmann, who at one time or another, saw the same
glowing thing. The information that people have given agrees
amazingly. In the Tolvarod area during the same period the T.V.
picture from Sweden was impossible to get properly and for about a
quarter of an hour the T.V. screen gave only glimmerings and waves
and a grating sound. Earlier in the evening and later there were no
disturbances, a lady told us, and lots of other people said the same.
The driver first discovered a U.F.O. and tells us that he first saw
the light when he looked north-westward from the canal bridge. The
light was hanging like a ball 300 metres over Teie. The U.F.O.
eventually disappeared out of sight but appeared again when the
driver had gone quite a way up the road. The ball of light then
hovered over the heights of Teiehoyden. The U.F.O. then settled at
about lOO metres from the car at a height similar to a helicopter.
The speed was about the same as his own - 50 to 60 km. per hour.
Just before Herstad it disappeared to\vards the west but then it
appeared again near Borgheim and followed the car some lOO metres
distant. The driver believed at first that thelight came from a plane
but soon realised that a plane should have several other lights than
the one that was hovering over Notteroy. The driver stopped to
listen for an angine, but there was no sound. He stood and watched
the ball of light hovering about lOO metres to the side of him.
He then turned towards Tenvik and discovered to his astonishment that
the ball had also made a turn and followed him. \N hen he got home
he fetched his wife and they both viewed the phenomenon for a while.
They continued to go to and fro from the window to watch the U.F.O.
But then it suddenly disappeared.
     Another man told our paper that a bright glowing object had
been seen by him at some crossroads about half-an-hour before the
previous driver had seen the ball at the canal bridge. The second
man discovered the U.F.O. when he stopped to see if the road was
clear towards Tonsberg. He described how he saw this bright "thing"
about 4oo metres away and between 200 and 300 metres up in the air.
The thing kept up a normal car speed he said, and it moved towards
Gipe. The driver thought at first it was a plane, but then he thought

the light was strange. He described the light from the object as
being white and flat. He started out at the Ekenes crossroads and
kept an eye on the "thing" and then it slowly floated over the sky
towards Notteroy church.
     A UoF.O. was also observed fro m Teie. A schoolboy told the
"Tonsberg Blad" that just before seven o'clock he saw a round ball
ef fire which came down over the mountains towards Teie and then
turned towards the middle of Notteroy. The boy said that the ball

it.
     A couple in the east saw a bright ball at about -8815 p.m., which
came in from the west at slow speed and disappeared in the east.
They thought it was a plane. Because of the speed and height both
witnesses thought that the light could have been from a single-engine
plane. They did not hear any sound.
     Late yesterday afternoon the "Tonsberg Blad'' received a phone
call from two more men who said they had seen a flying object on
Saturday night (December 14). One of the m saw it from an upstairs
window, glowing like a shining ball. Although the other witness saw
the object from another point he agreed with the description. It
flew off towards the west.
     A man from Slagenstangen told us that he saw a glowing thing in
the sky on Saturday at 6.45 a.m. in the morning, twelve hours before
the other four witnesses had seen an object over Notteroy. It was ·
very dark when the man saw the U.F . o. He said it came from the
direction of Notteroy and disappeared towards Horten. The object
moved slowly, like aplane that was coming in to land. It moved
slowly in a half-circle. It had a green shimmering colour. The
man thinks the object was about the si ze of a small car and during
the few seconds that he saw the U.F.O. he heard no sound.
     "Tonsberg Blad" spoke to a meteorologist at the Asmund Moene
Institute and was told th a t the weather situation discounted the
possibility that the object could h ave been a fireball, or electrical
phenomena in the upper atmosphere.
     An observer at the Astrophysical Institute at Blindern tells
the "Tonsberg Blad" that naturalphenomena could not account for the
object seen over Notteroy and although a fireball could account for
the ball of light following a car, one must have thunder before a
fireball could be seen, otherwise it could not have been possible.
     The Observatory also told us that they receive a great many
reports of U.F.O.s throughout the year, but they did not have the
time or opportunity to examine every case very seriously. First
and foremost it was difficult to examine something with only the
witnesses reports to go on. ''Through the years we have had reports
of Norwegians seeing objects in th e sky after hearing of U.F.O.
reports and stories of Martians", said the observer, "but they have
had a simple explanation". The observer said hebelieved that a lot
of the glowing lights se e n are weather balloons, aircraft or
satellites lit up by the sun. 'We have never had any reports of
U.F.O.s th a t we believe in. They can be explained away by the planet
Venus", he added ..
