                    Dear Mr. Melesciuc,


                          In response to your letter, I had a very small role in the analysis of the
                    Gulf Breeze photos, so small that my name was misspelled in the book.
                    Nevertheless I'd like to take this opportunity to answer the questions raised in the
                    order presented:


                          1) I did check for double exposure by measuring the brightness of the
                              darkest region of the UO and the surrounding sky. If the darkest region
                              within the object is darker than the sky a double exposure could not
                              have occurred because exposure is strictly additive. Due to background
                              variation in the sky the results were inconclusive.


                           2) I received a 9 track computer tape containing 10 digitized color images
                              of the Polaroid photographs. Bruce Maccabee would know the details.
                              He suggested that I look for signs of double exposure.


                           3) I received photos 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 16, 18, and 19.

                           4) In addition to the work mentioned in 1 ), I examined the images to
                              determine if the UO was in front of, or behind a tree. Again I could not
                              tell because little, if any of the object overlapped the tree.


                           5) The photos were sent to me due to my work in related areas. (I've
                              enclosed a reprint for your information.) My Ph.D is in Electrical
                              Engineering; my expertise is in image processing.


                    I hope this clarifies my role. As you can see it was a minor one. Good luck in
                    your search for the truth.
