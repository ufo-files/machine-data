          In the Observer for February 21st 1965, there was a short article
entitled 1Did the Romans march at right angles?'.   Mr. CePoH. Wilson of
Thetford, Norfolk has discovered that many of the accepted (i.e. proved)
Roman roads in Britain run exactly par£llel or exactly at right angles
to each other.  Mr. Wilson suspectR that there was a Roman Army grid system,
not marked, as ours is, on maps, because they probably had none, but on
stones in the ground.  Perhaps this is so, b ut it seems more likBly that this
                                                                                 •
is only another example of the Roman Roads taking over the more ancient
straight tracks laid out thousands of yoars before the Romans came to this
country.


          A very interesting book to read, if you have not already done so,
is 'A Lost Roman Road' by Bernard Berry.  Mr. Berry has discovered, by
following the line on the ground, the missing section in the Bath-Poole
Roman Road.  The book is very interesting and shows, with the aid of several
photographs which he took, how Mr. Berry followed the presumed course of the
road, and found traces of embankment, terracing, etc. as confirmation that
he was on the right track.  Mr. Berry obviously knows of the existence of
leys, for he mentions an old pond in the grounds of Longleat House known as
the Dod Pool, which is exactly on the line from the centre of Hamworthy
to the centre of Bath, and he thinks it probable that the Roman Road's
general alignment was based on a much older straight track between these
two points. Mr. Berry may well be interested in joining the Club, and
I will try and contact him.
