     September 14, 2003- La Discusion de Chillan, Chile- THIRD ATTACK ON SHEEP CLAIMS 20
MORE - Nocturnal attacks that end with large numbers of slain sheep in pens adjacent to homes have
acquired unaccustomed frequency, without owners being any the wiser until the following morning. The third of
these attacks in recent months occured between Thursday night and Friday morning in the Santa Lidia de
Cartago sector some 14 kilometers west of the city of Pemuco.
     Alfonso Mendoza, owner of the dead and dying animals, lost between 20 sheep and lambs. The first
attack was on Thursday and daimed the lives of 5 animals; the second was on Friday morning and took 7 more
plus left eight agonizing animals which must be put down. Among the wounded was the family's pet sheep,
which had been raised by hand after its mother's death, and treated with antibiotics.
     Despite the fact that the farmer believes dogs were likely involved, his experience and the difference in
the attacks on each day has left him wondering if a "leon" (puma) could have been responsible, since this
animal sometimes descends from the snowy cordilleran peaks, or else another type of animal.
     He was hoping to know for sure this weekend, which he planned to spend standing guard with a shotgun,
since he has already lost a third of his flock, which is an improved Suffolk stock. "If dogs are involved, they
must be eliminated, since they still attack even after being sated," he said. He notes that an event of this type
had not been witnessed here in three years, and that the "stunt" represents a loss of at least 200,000 Chilean
pesos to him.
     A situation that eludes his grip is that the first night, the attacking animals ate part of the sheep, but on the
second assault, they only attacked for the blood, which in his opinion is not customary in dog attacks.
     Earlier cases occured in Coletemu, where it was speculated that a puma attack would be very strange,
since said animals would be unable to reach the area by themselves.
     Another case occured on the road to Cato, where it was ascertained that a dog attack had indeed taken
place, since paw prints the size of a large canine's were stamped on the wood that served as the roof to the
sheep pen. -CREDIT: Liliana Nunez 0.
