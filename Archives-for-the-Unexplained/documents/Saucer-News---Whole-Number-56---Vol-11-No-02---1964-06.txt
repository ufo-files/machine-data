              As promised in the March issue, we are now revealing for the first
time the true reason behind      SAUCER NEWS'    long feud with Gray Barker -       a sub­
ject that has been the cause of much speculation over the years.
            Your Editor first met Barker in. the Fall of 1953 ,  shortly before
Barker began publication of his magazine,  then called  The Saucerian.  In the
months that followed,  the two  became fairly close friends.  They wrote each
other regularly,  and got together in Clarksburg as often as possible  to dis­
cuss UFO•s.  Both of them   took saucer research  ver.y seriously in those days,
and worked closely together   on a certain theory  they had jointly   arrived at
concerning the origin of saucers.
              Then,   in the summer of 1954,      about the time that the first issue
of SAUCER NEWS    ( then   called   "Nexus" )   was published,    your   Editor    visited
Clarksburg again -     this time accompanied by a young lady         whom he had serious
intentions of marrying.  Suffice is to say that Barker's charming manner suc­
ceeded in alienating the affection of this girl,  so that  the affair between
her and your Editor came to an abrupt end,  as did the friendship  between him
and Barker. Although Barker's attentions to the girl on that memorable weekend
were obvious,  he later claimed that his encouragement of  her infatuation for
him was unintentional. Eventually the girl married someone else - a person not
acquainted with either of the two researchers; but it was several years before
your Editor could fully forgive Barker for the incident.
              As neither Barker nor Moseley      wanted     to publicly admit     the real
cause of their feud,   they proceeded to attack each other  in their respective
magazines  about other things not related to the incident that destroyed their
friendship. They perpetrated hoaxes on each other, and made bitter accusations
against each other   by letter and in print.  Finally,  with your Editor's mar­
riage in 1962 , and his buying out of the Saucerian in 1963 due to Barker's fi­
nancial difficulties,   he became appeased, and decided to forget the past.  In
recent months  a superficial friendship   has again developed between   the two
men,  and it is hoped by both parties   that the last vestiges of the feud will
soon be gone.
             Actually,     it must be admitted that Barker and Moseley          were never
far apart in their personal beliefs regarding most aspects of saucer research,
and it would certainly have been better for the whole saucer field if both had
acted more maturely.  Saucerdom has wasted  too much time and effort  on petty
bitterness such as this; and were it not for the fact that researchers in gen­
eral are so hopelessly divided for       a variety of reasons,       the solution to the
saucer mystery might have been discovered and proven by now!

             Originally, Gray Barker' s highly profitable publishing concern was

              If we can presume to have interpreted the views of Mrs. Mundo cor­
rectly, she is saying that we earthlings have,for various nefarious and insid­
ious activities, been demoted from our former highly advanced planes of exist­

carnated upward to our prior level. of evolution. If, however, a degradation of
our physical and spiritual being should perchance occur, we shall be relegated
to the confines of worlds vastly inferior to this one.
              We are presented with the supposition that "Visitors" from planets
whose state of development transcends our own have arrived here     to assist us
in once again attaining the physical and spiritual refinement that we have in­
explicably lost. They have come now because of imminent planetary changes, the
precise nature of which has eluded us even after reading both versions of Mrs.
Mundo's book.
              In recounting her experiences with her other-worldly companions,
l1rs. Mundo details her encounters with various extraterrestrial entities with
wham we are quite familiar, including George Adamski' s infamous celestial co­

must speculate as to why the eminent "Professor" was permitted to remain igno­
rant of this amazing 11truth. 11

latches onto George Adamski's cosmic philosophy, which, stripped of its inter-

planetar.y connotations,  is a simple and straightforward plea for the abandon­
ment of the heinous devices of war in favor of eternal and everlasting broth­

             She unsuccessfully endeavors to assimilate various Biblical phe­

two common soldiers were patrolling the ramparts. They were simple men and did
not expect anything except a routine tour of duty. It was later established
that they had never even heard of " platos voldados, " or flying saucers.
            Their verbal report later stated that an orange glowing object ap­
proached until it was directly overhead. The object then slowly drifted down­

fort. The two sentries watched in amazement as the huge disc-like object hov­
ered overhead. It looked like a misplaced over-bright moon. The UFO was as
large as a Douglas airplane, and the glow illuminated the top of the fort as
it hovered like a huge helicopter. The sound of a normal aircraft was not
heard, but the sentinels soon became aware of a strange humming sound which
obviously came from the object overhead.
