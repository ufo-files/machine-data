DID A FLYING SAUCER LAND HERE? A Toronto commercial artist is convinced a flT­
ing saucer landed an, took off again from a farm near Ba llantrae, Ont.  Claus
Slade, who often visits his friend's farm say he first saw the land and take­
off site five years ago.
     He and his friend discovered the site while walking through the sumach­
covered hills or the area to the south and west of Ballantrae. When he first
saw it five years ago, the site cleared an area of about 50 feet in diameter.
     The outer edge or the circle seared the ground to a crisp, leaving bits
of charcoal. This outer circle is about three feet wide. And today, five
years later, no vegetation grows on it.   Inside the circle, small aumachs and
weeds are growing, but the grass has not sprouted through. Just outside the
outer circle, one can see flattened trees and toppled long grass, which is
still dead. Above the circle, tall, 150-foot high oak trees still grow, but
the branches which hang over the circle are dead.
     T here is a path to the north of the circle extending about lOO feet to
the south of the circle which is barren of leaf growth. All other vegetation
within a 1,000 foot radius of the circle is growing normally.
      The people who own the farm estimated that the seared circle occurred
about the beginning of June, 1964.   (The Era, Newmarket, Ont. Credit: David
R. Haskell, Newmarket, Ont., - editor of The Era.)
