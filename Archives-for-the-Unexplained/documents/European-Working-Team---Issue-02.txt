SOME BRIEF NOTES ON HUMCAT
I developed a special interest in the humanoid cases while I was employed at NICAP in
1968 and 1969, where I first met David Webb, my colleague. We bagan collecting CE3
case references (I had several first-hand cases to draw upon) and eventually we had
collected close to a thousand cases, from world wide sources, Our big wave of sightings
in 1973 added new material
and a new impetus to our work and, in 1974, we began work-
ing on a computer program that would provide ready access to the rapidly-growing case
files, In 1975 we located a researcher who had access to a computer and we began ente-
ring our many case references. This first part of our work was completed in mid-1976
with the inclusion of more than 1500 case references in the computer file, which we
dubbed "Hum Cat" defived from Dr Saunders' "UFOCat", upon which it was based. Our ini-
tial entries contained just the essențials givens of each case, such as a Serial Number
Date, Time, Country, State (or Province) and Town, immediate locale, number of witnes-
ses, and Witness Name. We are able to sort by any combination of these entries, which
provides a very useful index system. We haa, of course, a much more complicated pro-
gram to fevelop, but because of limited time and computer accessibility, we are so far
limited to developing this rudimentary system. It takes all our time just to say
abreast of all the updates. As of now, we are more than 2200 entries in the system
from world wide sources, dating from 1896 to 1979. There are probably anther 200
that are still waiting to be keypunched (1980 and 1981 remain to be entered, for exam-
ple, as well as many "new", older cases). We have in the past two years added two fea-
ture we did not originaly include in the system. The first is a rating for each case,
from o to 5, based upon the amount of information for any case, the kind of investiga-
tion and consistency of material and who the investigator was.
