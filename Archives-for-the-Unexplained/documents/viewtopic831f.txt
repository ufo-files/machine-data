    by DeanF » Sat Oct 02, 2010 10:26 pm

  It could be either.

  The route for cruise missiles to get to their destination is pre-programmed in, yes, they might have
  different targets but the 'safest' route into the target area could very well be the same.

  And launch systems do contain multiple missiles, without checking, it's quite likely they could be fired
  simulatenously. But I very much doubt you'd even want them flying in formation, I don't see any
  advantage. More likely to be seen, more likely to be hit.

  Plus, you've got several missiles flying completely independent of each other, without any
  knowledge/understanding of any other missiles that might be right next to them in the formation. It's too
  dangerous.

  First generation of cruise missiles used inertial navigation and used terrain mapping to re-calibrate the
  inertial nav. system. Over time during the flight, inertial nav. becomes in accurate. Newer variants of the
  tomhawk I believe now use GPS navigation. But to fly in such close formation with each missile not even
  knowing there's another missile just a few metres away is risky. The route planners would have to pay
  special attention to ensuring that the routes planned in to each separate missile would been a few meters
  translated laterally from the others. It's not worth the effort and risk.

  Far better, greater chance of success just to launch the missiles with the same route plan but seconds apart
  - building in some safety margin so that if a later launched missile is flying slightly faster than the earlier
  one it doesn't catch up on it and cause a collision.

  No, I suspect this footage is of a formation of manned fighter aircraft.
