                               EDITORIAL:          MIRRORS OF PROGRESS

                   by Allen Greenfield,              Chairman,       Newspaper      Committee


        It has occurred to me that the reflection of the progress of UFOlogy
        throughout the world has been shown,                      over the years,          in the publica­
        tions on this subject�

        Early UFO publications from the United States are usually poorly
        "knocked up" editions,             in some cases half science fiction and half
        UFOlogy.     Until recent years,               there were virtually no such publi�
        cations in Canada.


        UFO publications today cover a wide range                            of size and quality.
        Generally speaking,          UFO publications today are good,                      but a few
        specific publications are,               we feel,       worthy of mentioning as
        "classic examples" of what all UFQ publications                            should and can        be.

        In the U.S.    and    Canada such publications as                      FLYING SAUCERS MAGAZINE
        and SAUCERS,    SPACE,       AND SCIENCE are worth                   mentioning.     It should
        here be noted that some of the best and some of                            the worst examples
        of UFO publications can be found in America.                             There is great     incon-     ·-


        sistency    in quality       here,      but improvements are continually being
        made,    even on the poorest of publications.


        In Europe,    such publications as UFO NYT and UFO Nachrichten,                             to
        name only two,    are of excellent quality.                          In Australia,    THE

•       AUSTRALIAN    FLYING SAUCER           REVIEW is an enjoyable,               well printed,
        and well written       magazine.


        It occurs to us that          publications in the UFO field that are good
        are not necessarily the most expensive or most elaborate.                                Some
    *   of the best,    in    fact,       are done on a smaller budget than most of
        the worst.     This seems indicative of one fact:                          it is the editor's
        judgment that determines the worth of a publication.


        It also occurs to us that our publications are perhaps under­
        rated in their influence�                The public,         who can only judge by
        what they see,       often    are reluctant            to become a part of the UFO
        world,     even if they are           interested in the subject,                  because they
        happen to pick up a poor quality UFO publication and consider this
        an example of how the whole subject is organized

        We   think our point should be clear to you by now;                          even the best
        publications could stand a great deal of improvement.                                ALL should
        be very selective as to the material                      that they publish,          and ALL
        should make an effort to make their publication something that
        is representative of UFOlogy in its best tradition.

        There is no doubt that UFOlogy needs improvement.                                Anyone thinking
        that UFOlogy is in the best possible condition is plainly and
        simply wrong.        We need to improve allright,                      and maybe the best
        place to start is with the central organ of communication in all
        UFO groups,    our publications.
