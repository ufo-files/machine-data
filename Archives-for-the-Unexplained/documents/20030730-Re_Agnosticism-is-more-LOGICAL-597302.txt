  "Carlin" <carlin@tracassoc.com> wrote in message news:<vie0svmjejmae5@corp.supernews.com>...


         Not sure if I'm reading this right, but it looks to me like someone is
         saying that Agnosticism says that "God does not exist."


  Yes.


         By definition, Atheism believes that "God does not exist."


  No; some people use atheism in that way, but atheists themselves use a
  more generic version: Atheism is a 'lack of belief in God.' That
  includes all of (1) those who assert that there are no gods, (2)
  agnostics who don't profess to know whether there are or are not, and
  (3) those who have never heard of gods.
  Which is fine, but leaves a hole in the vocabulary: what does one call
  those who do assert that there are no gods? The point in contention
  here is, as you've surmised, one person's contention that that
  stronger position is precisely agnosticism:
  <quote>

         "Atheism is characterized by an absence of belief in the existence of
         gods." -- http://www.infidels.org/news/atheism/intro.html
         Agnostics like Thomas Huxley go one step further to deny and repudiate
         religious belief

  </quote>
  While Huxley is cited as the authority, the only evidence offered from
  Huxley's works is a statement that does not support this view at all:
  <quote>

         "That which Agnostics deny and repudiate, as immoral, is the contrary
         doctrine, that there are propositions which men ought to believe, without
         logically satisfactory evidence." -- Thomas Huxley, "Agnosticism and
         Christianity"   http://aleph0.clarku.edu/huxley/CE5/Agn-X.html

  </quote>


         Agnosticism
         believes that "There may or may not be a higher power, whatever name you
         want to call it."


  I'd put it as: for all we know, there may be, and for all we know
  there may not be. There is no sense believing one or the other,
  without satisfactory reasons.
