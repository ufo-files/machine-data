SKYWATCH
MERCURY is currently visible low in the western sky just after dusk. It reaches greatest eastern elongation of 23 degrees
on November 11 (Furthest point East of the Sun - the best time for observation).

VENUS is too close to the Sun for observation. It will reappear in the evening sky by the end of the year.

MARS is visible in the NE morning sky. It's moderate brightness and distinctive reddish colour make it easy to identify
as it moves steadily westwards against the background stars from night to night.

JUPITER is currently at its best for observation. It is a very prominent object visible for lnost of the night, lotaied high in
the northern sky in mid-evening, its four brightest satellites - Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto - can be seen in
                                                                                                         ;-·
binoculars. The Moon and Jupiter form an attractive, bright pairing on November 28.

SATURN reaches opposition on October 23 when it appears due north at midnight. To the unaided eye, Saturn is a
moderately bright whitish object and easy to identify in Pisces, but its magnificent rings cannot be seen without the aid of
a small telescope.

Meteor shower.
The Leonids Meteor shower this year has the potential to be a major storm, with the possibility of several thousand
meteors per hour emanating from the direction of the Constellation of Leo. The period of activity is from November 14 to
21. reaching a peak on the 18th. Leo will not rise above the horizon until about 3.30AM (Tas. Daylight time) when it
will be low in the NNE sky. The shower is predicted to peak at around 6.40AM, unfortunately Sun rise will be about
5.30AM. Keep observing for as long as possible, until it becomes too light to see any stars. If it lives up to expectations -
You will not be disappointed that you got out of bad so early. The Shower is due to the Earth passing though debris left in
the orbit of comet Swift-Tuttle.
