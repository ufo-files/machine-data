       Candy Jones, who died of cancer in mid-January of this year, was the
widow of,New York radio talk show host Long John Nebel, on whose program
your editor appeared more than a hundred times during a period of     many
years.   After John's death in 1978, also from cancer, Candy kept doing
talk shows of various formats on radio station WMCA until she became ill
in 1989.
       During the years just before Long John's death, Candy was co-host
with John, but would usually carry most of the 5-hour radio show alone,be­
cause John was in so much pain he could only sit at the microphone a few
minutes at a time.   She did this even though she and her husband were then
                                                                '
separated.
       Before marrying Long John in 1972, Candy had a successful career in
several fields, and at one time was the highest paid fashion model in the
country.   Her career goes back to World War II, and includes writing, lec­
turing, acting, and running a modeling school.
       We always secretly hated John because of his overbearing ways,though
he treated your editor well on almost all occasions.    Candy was a lower­
keyed person, and seemed the opposite of John in many ways.    We continued
going on her WMCA shows frequently until our move to Florida a few years
ago.   Our la£t contact with her was about two years ago, when we called
her at home one night, just to thank her for her many kindnesses.
       Long John began,.his radio career in the mid-1950's, and made a star
out of Howard Menger & other UFO personalities, even though John himself
did not believe a word of their stories.    On the other hand, Candy had a
keen curiosity about UFOs and many other subjects, though she was not a
Believer.

       What we remember best about our many appearances on WMCA after John's
 death was that there was a limousine service to drive Candy home, to her
 condo across Manhattan. However, for an endless series of peculiar rea­
 sons, the limo never came. (Finally we were bright enough to figure out
 t�at there probably was no limo.) So your editor would volunteer to drive
 Candy home, usually just after we went off the air at 5 a.m. Candy always
 made the drive worthwhile by coming out with some interesting bit of gos- ·



 sip about someone we both knew - not necessarily for publication, but just
 for interest. We always felt that these tidbits of information were a kind
 and deliberate effort to pay us back for the ride.
       One such morning, Candy told us a fascinating story about a vase she
 kept in t�e living room of her condo, where she then lived alone. John had
 always hated this vase, and had ur�ed her to get rid of it. Suddenly one
 night while she was asleep, there was a terrible crash from the living
 room, and when she got up to investigate, she found the vase shattered all
 over the floor. John, like Houdini, had said he would come back from the
 Other Side some day if he could, and Candy felt this was his way of doing
 it. Certainly this rather negative act of breaking the hated vase      was
 right in character for John!
       Our friend Tim Beckley of Inner Light Publications has written    an

     \ ON THE GULF BREEZE FRONT, we believe we finally have the proverbial
"smoking gun" regarding Willy Smith's educational claims. In a letter to
Jerry Clark dated Jan. 20th, 1990, Smith states: "...At the risk of being
repetitious, let me say once again that only a fool will fail to see the
ONLY mention of the University of Valencia (Spain) in my entry in AM&WS
(American Men & Women of Science) states that I was a VISITING PROFESSOR
there in 1973-74.   Which happens to be the truth, even if it destroys your
childish illusions about Mr. Ed and his cohorts..."
       However, we have photostats of the appropriate page of "American Men
& Women of Science", 1979 edition and 1986 edition, and under "Smith,Willy"
each states precisely as follows: "...vis. prof., Inst. Corpuscular Radi­
ation, Fac. Sci., Univ. Valencia, Spain, 73-74, chmn dept. astron. & phy­
sics, 74-77..." Without the abbreviations, this would read: "Visiting
professor, Institute of Corpuscular Radiation, Faculty (or Facility?) of
Science, University of Valencia, Spain, 1973-1974; Chairman of the depart­
ment of astronomy and physics, 1974-1977 ..."
       Since presumably Smith himself supplies the information used in this
text, it would certainly seem that he is claiming to have been chairman of
the dept. of astronomy and physics - though our information is that there
is no such department!

cluding Key West, where we bought a copy. (Wm. Morrow & Co., Inc., 105

Ed received a fair hearing in the UFO press, but we are proud that we were
able to supply Ed with a relatively unbiased forum in "Smear"; and we ap­
preciate Ed's appreciation.
