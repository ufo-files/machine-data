Mutual UFO Network of Ohio Formed
The Mutual UFO Network of Ohio was formed at an organizational meeting on November 14, 1998, as a nonprofit organization
to represent the Mutual UFO Network, Inc. (MUFON, Inc.) of Seguin, Texas in Ohio. At the kickoff meeting, eleven initial
members joined, the bylaws were accepted, and officers were appointed. The following individuals are founding members:

    Wendy Ban                   Terry Hamilton              Ted Kreit                   Richard Thielmann
    Bruce Budinger              Pete Hartinger              Richard Lee                 Don Weatherby
    Phyllis Budinger            Bill Jones                  John Timmerman


Purpose
The Mutual UFO Network of Ohio is dedicated to conduct research into the UFO phenomenon and to provide educational
programs and publications to the public. Our specific objectives are to:

    •   Establish an active network of people in Ohio interested in objective examination of the UFO phenomenon and related
        matters.
    •   Conduct investigations of possible UFO-related event and claims, both historical and current.
    •   Hold meetings and conduct objective educational programs on the subject of UFOs for members and the public.
    •   Publish a quarterly membership newsletter, a journal, and other publications to make the results of investigations avail­
        able to members and the public.
    •   Monitor the news media in Ohio to help ensure that coverage of UFO-related material is objective and fair.
    •   Provide a forum for non-MUFON and independent researchers to present and publish information as guest speakers or
        contributors to Mutual UFO Network of Ohio publications.


Membership Benefits
Besides having the opportunity to participate in group projects, to network with Ohio-based UFO investigators, and to help revi­
talize ufology in Ohio, members receive the following benefits:

    •   Free admission to the three yearly State Meetings, which will be held in various geographic locations around Ohio, and to
        other organization-sponsored events.
    •   The quarterly Mutual UFO Network of Ohio Newsletter, describing activities and announcing meetings and other events.
    •   An annual special Member's Issue of the Ohio UFO Notebook, MidOhio Research Associates (see below).

About the Ohio UFO Notebook
Now in its 18th issue, the Ohio UFO Notebook has been published since 1991 by MidOhio Research Associates, an indepen­
dent UFO investigative group spearheaded by Bill Jones, MUFON, Inc. Ohio State Director and Mutual UFO Network of Ohio
Director. Over the years, the Ohio UFO Notebook has published scores of sighting reports as well as selected articles, including
such notable examples as "Astronauts Speak Out About UFOs'' (#12), and "Interview With Retired Project Blue Book Director"
(#11).The most recent issue (with introduction by ufologist Robert J. Durant) features a reprint of NThe Fitzgerald Report," an in­
depth account of a classic multiple-witness close encounter in Sheffield Lake, OH, and arguments that an official Air Force
investigation was mishandled.

Group Research Project- Project Ohio: October 1973
Project Ohio: October 1973 has been designed to take advantage of the resources offered by an active group distributed over a
fairly wide geographic area. Bill Jones will manage this project, which will be the focus of the organization for 1999.

The year 1973 marks what was historically the last major UFO wave in the United States to gain significant media attention.
Although the wave actually extended into 1974, the goal of Project Ohio: October 1973 is to find and document as many Ohio
UFO reports as possible from the peak month of the wave. The true scope and extent of this wave is not known because sight­
ing reports have not been fully unearthed.

The project is modeled after researcher Jan Aldrich's Project 1947, a grass-roots effort to document the origins of the modern
UFO phenomenon by having large numbers of investigators collect newspaper articles, documents, and personal accOunts.
Phase 1 of Project Ohio: October 1973 will involve collecting Ohio media material from the peak of the wave. In subsequent
phases, the project will expand across the entire wave and will include efforts to interview the reporting witnesses. Results of the
research will be published in the Ohio UFO Notebook and on the World-Wide Web.

The project involves visiting your local newspaper office, library, or historical society to get copies of printed materials that docu­
ment the October 1973 UFO wave in Ohio. Newspapers often have collections (called •morgues•) that may include UFO reports.
The collections can consist of entire newspapers archived on microfilm or in bound copies, or clipping files organized by subject.
Many libraries and historical societies also have extensive newspaper collections and subject files.

UFO material may be filed under a variety of subjects, including: UFOs, unidentified flying objects, flying saucers, aeronautics,
airships, astronomy, extraterrestrial life, meteors, space events, spook lights, ball lightning, fireballs, flying discs, ghost lights,
odd occurrences, strange events. UFO material may even be filed under topics associated with parapsychology, such as appari­
tions, ghosts. Or, the material may simply be filed under phenomena.

Privately held scrapbook collections often contain valuable information available nowhere else. For instance, clippings from
now-defunct newspapers may be almost impossible to find except in such a collection.

Complete bibliographic references are needed for each document, including:

    •   Name of the newspaper (or periodical, newsletter, etc.)
    •   Section
    •   Page number
    •   Date (including volume number, issue number, etc., as applicable)

SPEAKER
In her profession as an industrial chemist, Phyllis Budinger has specialized in the analysis of unknown materials to
identify their composition. Her lab, Frontier Analysis, has analyzed physical trace evidence associated with UFO
events, including soil samples, angel hair, residue from a cattle mutilation, and stains from an abductee's skin. Her
lecture will cover the mission and goals of Frontier Analysis physical trace research, and the problems and pitfalls
encountered in this sort of work. She will describe what has been discovered to date in the field, and will present
real cases. She will also talk about the positive future of physical trace research, a field that Ted Phillips of the
Center for Physical Trace Research has described as an "embarassment of riches" since, though many significant
samples have been collected and preserved through the decades, relatively little professional analysis has been
done.
