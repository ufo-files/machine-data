3. MISSION. The Office of the Inspector General of the Department of Defense (OIG DoD) was established by
Congress in section 1117 of Public Law (PL) 97-252, "Department of Defense Authorization Act, 1983," which
is codified at Reference (c), as an independent and objective unit within DoD to conduct and supervise
audits, investigations, evaluations, and inspections relating to the programs and operations of the DoD. In
support of the mission of the DoD, the IG DoD performs the duties, has the responsibilities, and exercises
the powers specified in Reference (c).
• 5. RESPONSIBILITIES AND FUNCTIONS. The IG DoD shall:
• 5.b. Initiate, conduct, supervise, and coordinate such audits, investigations, evaluations, and
inspections within the DoD, including the Military Departments, as the IG DoD considers appropriate.
DoD Instruction 7050.03, "Office of the Inspector General of the Department of Defense Access to Records
and Information," March 22, 2013.
• 3. POLICY. In accordance with References (a) and (b), it is DoD policy that:
• 3.a The OIG DoD must have expeditious and unrestricted access to all records as defined in DoD
Directive 5015.2 (Reference (d)), regardless of classification, medium (e.g., paper, electronic) or format
(e.g., digitized images, data) and information available to or within any DoD Component, and be able
to obtain copies of all records and information as required for its official use once appropriate security
clearances and access are substantiated for the OIG DoD personnel involved.
• 3.b. No officer, employee, contractor, or Service member of any DoD Component may deny the OIG DoD
access to records. Only the Secretary of Defense can deny access to certain types of records or
information based on criteria listed in section 8 of Reference (a), and paragraph 6a(1) of Reference (b).

SCOPE AND METHODOLOGY
• The scope of this evaluation will include Offices of the Secretary of
Defense, U.S. Special Operations Command, U.S. Central Command, the
Services, Combat Support Agencies, other Defense Agencies, and the
MCIOs, as it relates to the actions taken to detect, collect, analyze, exploit,
and address UAP.
• We will conduct this evaluation by:
• Requesting information to understand the history, roles and responsibilities,
and any documentation regarding UAP, including policies and procedures for
reporting UAP and reports generated by DoD and subordinate organizations
regarding UAP;
• Conducting interviews and site visits, as necessary, to obtain clarification
regarding policies, procedures, guidance, and actions taken to detect, collect,
analyze, exploit, and address UAP activity; and
• Analyzing data collected and interviews conducted.
