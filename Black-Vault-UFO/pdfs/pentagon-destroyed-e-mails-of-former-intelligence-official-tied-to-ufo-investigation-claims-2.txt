Dear Mr. Greenewald:

       This is a final response to your August 11, 2022 Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)
request, a copy of which is enclosed for your convenience. We received your request on
August 12, 2022, and assigned it FOIA case number 22-F-1235. We ask that you use this number
when referring to your request.

       The Assistant to the Secretary of Defense for Privacy, Civil Liberties, and Transparency, a
component of the Office of the Secretary of Defense, conducted a search of their records systems
and located 89 pages determined to be responsive to your request. Ms. Lyn M. Kirby, Director,
PCLF, in her capacity as an Initial Denial Authority, has determined that portions of the
89 responsive pages are exempt from release pursuant to 5 U.S.C. § 552 (b)(6), as disclosure of the
information would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of the personal privacy of individuals.
Please note that we have considered the foreseeable harm standard when reviewing records and
applying exemptions under the FOIA in the processing of this request.

      In this instance, fees for processing your request were below the threshold for requiring
payment. Please note that fees may be assessed on future requests.

        If you have any questions or concerns about the foregoing or about the processing of your
request, please do not hesitate to contact the Action Officer assigned to your request, Daniel Mullin,
at 571-372-0465 or daniel.r.mullin3.civ@mail.mil. Additionally, if you have concerns about service
received by our office, please contact a member of our Leadership Team at 571-372-0498 or Toll
Free at 866-574-4970.

       Should you wish to inquire about mediation services, you may contact the OSD/JS FOIA
Public Liaison, Toni Fuentes, at 571-372-0462, or by email at OSD.FOIALiaison@mail.mil. You
may also contact the Office of Government Information Services (OGIS) at the National Archives and
Records Administration to inquire about the FOIA mediation services they offer. The contact

BLUF: I believe that we have exhausted all possible locations for search, and therefore we should uphold
FOID's no records located decision.
         On September 21, 2019, John Greenewald filed a FOIA request with FOID asking for emails sent
to or from Luis Elizondo, a former DoD employee, with the keyword "Unidentified." On September 27,
2019 the requester agreed to a narrowing of the scope with a date range of 2009-2012. On October 4,
2019 the requester further agreed to a narrowing of the scope to "unidentified aerial phenomena,
unidentified aerial phenomenon, unidentified flying objects, unidentified aircraft, unidentified UAS/
Unmanned Aerial/Aircraft Systems Unidentified UAV / Unmanned Aerial Vehicles." FOID conducted a
search by tasking the National Programs Special Management Staff (NPSMS) and returned a no records
response on December 13, 2019. The requester exercised their first set of appeal rights on December 16,
2019 and challenged FOID's search, citing the Luis Elizondo's TV show and his prolific interest in
UFOs.
        DPCLTD remanded the case back to FOID for a search of DOPSR, to ensure that no records were
missed on February 11, 2020. FOID subsequently reopened the case and tasked USD(I), and DOPSR,
who located no records. On April 1, 2021, FOID again returned a no records response. On April 6, 2021,
the requester exercised their appeal rights, challenging the adequacy of search. In his appeal letter the
requester argues that DoD should still have records of emails for three years based on the records
disposition schedules found at Tab H.
         According to the DOJ FOIA manual, Procedural Requirements, the courts require agencies to
take a search that is "reasonably calculated to uncover all relevant documents (page 43)." Courts have
also disfavored searches that "exclude files where records might be located" or are "based on
unreasonable interpretations of the scope of the request." Furthermore, courts can disfavor searches if
the agency "properly determined where records were likely to be found, or whether the agency
improperly limited its search to certain record systems." Finally, agencies "need to pursue a lead it
cannot in good faith ignore, i.e., a lead that is both clear and certain." We are also not required to go to
the ends of the earth, or disrupt agency operations to find documents.
         In order to understand this request, and in compliance with my NARA training, we first need to
understand who Luis Elizondo claims to be. He claims, in his History Channel Biography, to have been
the director of the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program. According to the New York
Times, the program was created in 2007 and shut down in 2012. The program began as part of the
Defense Intelligence Agency. Furthermore, we know that Steven Aftergood, of the Federation of
American Scientists, located a list of material produced by the project from a DIA FOIA request. Mr.
Elizondo resigned in October 2017 from USD(I), five years after the alleged shutdown of the AATIP
program.
         In reviewing the search taskers, both original and second, along with open source media on the
background of the request I believe that, if the accounts still existed, they no longer exist. Because the
subject, Luis Elizondo, left DoD in October 2017, even if his records were still available they have likely
been destroyed due the age of the request. If we knew his position level at USD(I) we could provide a
more accurate answer based on the records retirement schedules. Email of former, non-capstone
employees, are kept for at least 3 years according to OSD's records retirement schedule. So at a
minimum, even if we were to look again, we are not likely to find any kind of emails. In the first search,
conducted in 2019, National Programs Special Management Staff, conducted a search of their NIPR,
SIPR, JWICS, Shared drive, and JSP using the keywords provided by Greenewald, with no records
located. This was the best opportunity for finding records since the accounts should still have been
preserved according to the records schedule.
        In the second instance, FOID tasked USD(I), the last known location where Mr. Elizondo
worked, and DOPSR, since the Appellate authority believed that DOPSR would have material. In the
second instance of searches conducted in 2021, DOPSR had stated that they had provided all of their
records in prior cases and recommended USD(I). USD(I) in their search tasker claimed that "e-mails of
former DoD employees are not retained unless they are considered historical record and retained by the
NRC. Mr. Elizondo's e-mail accounts have likely been deleted and no longer exist." But, given
OUSD(I) and DOPSR records not likely to be found, I am not sure what, if any, records would remain,
given the requester's date range of 2009-2012.
         Given the timeframe, I believe the best course of action would be to search the retired files due to
the age of the narrowed request. There is a small possibility that the record requested may exist in RDD's
retired files. It would also satisfy a correct scoping of the request, as we are obligated to check systems
that may contain the records, and RDD is the only entity I am aware of that would maintain files in the
requested date range.
