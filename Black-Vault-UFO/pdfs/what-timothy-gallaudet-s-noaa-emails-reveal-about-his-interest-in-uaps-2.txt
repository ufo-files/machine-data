We have located 51 records (consisting of 245 pages) responsive to your request. A total of 167
pages of these records are being released to you in their entirety.

We are also releasing 78 pages that contain redactions under exemption (b)(6), to protect
personal privacy. Your request is complete.

You have the right to file an administrative appeal if you are not satisfied with our response to
your FOIA request. All appeals should include a statement of the reasons why you believe the
FOIA response was not satisfactory. An appeal based on documents in this release must be
received within 90 calendar days of the date of this response letter at the following address:

 Subject: Sent for Rod Rodriguez -- Notes from our New Orleans Board Meeting

 Dear Board members:

 Last week was a very long and successful week at the Foundation Board meeting in
 New Orleans. First let me thank all of you that joined the tour of coastal Louisiana to
 view many of our current and planned projects. It's impressive in scale, scope and
 planning. We appreciate the time of the CPRA leadership in joining our Board
 members.

 Elizabeth and I enjoyed having many of you for an evening at Avery Island. We are
 proud of our family's commitment to conservation and industry. We hope you enjoyed
 learning about the area's history. As well, it was good for us to meet with Secretary
 Zinke during our visit and understand his efforts to address infrastructure backlog at our
 Nation's parks and refuges. Secretary Zinke also joined us on Tuesday night at our
 Board dinner at the World War II Museum in New Orleans.

 We had significant operational and governance topics to review when we began our
 meetings to: 1. Plan for FY19-20; 2. Update governance bylaws and charters and
 Signature Delegation Authority Policy; 3. Prepare for the Enterprise Risk Management
 efforts for FY19-20; 4. Review the completion of FY18 financial activity; 5. Receive an
 update on the ongoing FY18 financial audit; 6. Discuss the Grantee Overhead review;
 7. Receive reports on the Gulf Expenditure Verification Program; 8. Review
 Government, Corporate, Foundation and Philanthropic Funding Strategies; 9. Complete
 compensation adjustments related to the senior management team; 10. Discuss the
 upcoming number of Board re-appointments and vacancies; 11. Discuss efforts to
 enhance the GEBF and Halliburton investment portfolios.

 As Jeff and team continue to work toward FY19-20, it was incredible to see the financial
 performance and level of activity in FY18. It is fair to say that it was our strongest year
 ever - as we ran an operational reserve of more than $5 million, raised record levels of
 revenues and made conservation investments of more than $330 million across our
 entire funding portfolio. Congratulations to everyone for this strong year. Conservation
 across America was the winner. If you have the opportunity, please congratulate Jeff
 and the entire team.

 As we look to plan for 2019-20, there is significant economic and political uncertainty
 that could impact our future operations. The team continues to monitor and adapt to
 unforeseen challenges and remains optimistic on future growth.

 We also had a significant 2018 programmatic grant slate we approved that included 235
 grants for a total NFWF investment of $73 million. Looking forward, we can expect the
 program plan to include approximately $200 million to be obligated in FY 2019 to
 support the Conservation Framework strategies through more than 680 projects across
 the nation. These investments will be allocated directly to our priority landscapes and
species, as well as distributed across the nation to support multiple geographies and
species.

The Board also approved $283 million in new projects under the Gulf Environmental
Benefit Fund (GEBF) - the second largest set of awards to date. Since its inception five
years ago, NFWF has now obligated over half of the $2.5 billion we have received in
accordance with the plea agreements. In addition, several Board members traveled to
Baton Rouge following the meeting to tour the new Center for River Studies that houses
a small-scale model of the lower Mississippi River. CPRA leadership provided a briefing
on how this model will guide their design and future operation of the large-scale river
diversion projects supported by GEBF.

Let me close this report by again thanking everyone that was able to attend. We have
always enjoyed strong Board interest and commitment, and we are proud of the high
attendance at our meetings. I am looking forward to seeing you in Puerto Rico in
March. If you need anything before our meeting, please just let me know.

Elizabeth and I want to wish you all good health and happiness for the holidays.

Best regards,

Rod Rodriguez

Dear Board members:

Last week we enjoyed a wonderful Foundation Board meeting in Dorado Beach, Puerto Rico.
truly believe it was one of our most productive meetings - but also in the most amazing location.

First let me thank Friedel and Carolina Stubbe, and Blas Fonalledas and Diana Perez for the
hospitality they extended to the Board and staff of the Foundation during our stay in Puerto
Rico. We were warmly received and we greatly appreciate the extra efforts put forth by the
staff. It was a great pleasure to welcome new Board Member K. C. Walsh and his wife Karen to
the NFWF Board of Directors.

We had significant operational and governance topics to review when we began our meetings
to: 1. Discuss Board of Directors appointments for upcoming Board vacancies; 2. Review
Enterprise Risk Management and the emphasis on cyber security to be discussed at the August
Board Meeting 3. Sub-recipient Indirect cost discussion; 4. Receive an update on the recently
completed FY18 financial audit - the 1oth consecutive clean audit and 5. Financial review of the
FY19 budget.

We also approved a significant Spring 2019 programmatic grant slate that included 60 grants for
a total NFWF investment of $9 million. With match, these investments come to $30 million for
conservation. Looking forward, we can expect the program plan to include an additional $195
million to be obligated in FY 2019 to support the Conservation Framework strategies through
approximately 600 projects across the nation. These investments will strengthen our
partnerships and be allocated directly to our priority landscapes and species across the nation."

The Board also approved 4 grants totaling $8 million in new projects in Florida, Mississippi and
Texas under the Gulf Environmental Benefit Fund (GEBF). Since its inception five years ago,
NFWF has now obligated over half of the $2.5 billion we have received in accordance with the
plea agreements.

Next Tuesday, March 26th , will mark the 35th Anniversary of the legal creation of NFWF. This is
a remarkable milestone and I am incredibly proud of the conservation impact produced by this
Foundation over the last 35 years. All of us should be proud - and we will celebrate the
milestone at our August meeting.

Let me thank everyone who was able to attend the Board Meeting. We have always enjoyed
strong Board interest and commitment, and we are proud of the consistent high attendance at
our meetings. I am looking forward to seeing you in Jackson Hole in August.

Wildlife Service, U.S. Forest Service, NOAA, Conservation Trust for Puerto Rico and the
