        Dear

        Thank you for your letter dated 23 August 2004.

        You will be aware from our previous correspondence that the MOD examines UFO reports, sent
        to us, solely to establish whether they present any evidence of a breach of UK airspace by
        unauthorised aircraft.

        With regard to your particular observation on 16 August 2004, I can confirm for that date, we
        received no other reports of 'UFO' sightings. We are satisfied that there is no corroborating
        evidence to suggest that the United Kingdom's airspace was breached by any unauthorised
        aircraft.

        The MOD is not saying that witnesses do not see things that they are unable to identify, just that
        we do not have any expertise or role in respect of 'UFO/flying saucer' matters to the question or to
        the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial lifeforms, about which we remain totally open-
        minded. Also, the MOD is often misquoted as saying that it does not believe 'UFO/flying
        saucers' to be a threat; this is a distortion of the MOD's stated position which is that to date, we
        have seen nothing which could be classed as proof that extraterrestrial life and or 'UFO/flying
        saucers' exist. Hence to date, no threat to the UK has been discerned which has attributed to a so-
        called 'UFO/flying saucer'.

        Your letter will be placed on our files.

                                               Yours sincerely

    Thank you for your letter of your reported sightings, dated 3 June 2004.

    I can assure you that the integrity of the UK's airspace in peacetime is maintained through
    continuous surveillance of the UK Air Policing Area by the Royal Air Force.
    This is achieved by using a combination of civil and military radar installations, which provide a
    continuous real-time "picture" of the UK airspace. Any threat to the UK Air Policing Area would
    be handled in the light of the particular circumstances at the time (it might if deemed appropriate,
    involve the scrambling or diversion of air defence aircraft). From that perspective, reports provided
    to us of 'UFO' sightings are examined, but consultation with air defence staff and others as
    necessary is considered only where there is sufficient evidence to suggest a breach of UK air space.

    With regard to your particular observation, I can confirm for the dates 2 April, 17'\ 19th and 30
    May, we received no other reports of 'UFO' sightings. On the 25th May, there was one other
    sighting, in Devon. We are satisfied there is no corroborating evidence to suggest that the United
    Kingdom's airspace was breached by unauthorised aircraft on any occasion.

    Your letter will be placed on our files.

     ' UFO' sightings for 2 June 2004 from anywhere in the UK. We are satisfied that there is no
     corroborating evidence to suggest that the United Kingdom' s airspace was breached by

reports of UFOs solely to establish whether they present any evidence that UK airspace might

should inform you that while the MOD remains open-minded as to the question of the existence
or otherwise of extraterrestriallifeforms, to date we know of no evidence which substantiates the
existence of these alleged phenomena.

                              THERAFDEPENDANTSFUND
  General

  1.         The RAF Dependants Fund was established on 1 Jul 67 with an initial grant of £50,000
  from the RAF Central Fund.

  2.          The purpose of the RAF Dependants Fund is to provide a single tax-free payment for the
  immediate need of widows, widowers, dependant children, or other nominated dependants of
  subscribing members of the Fund who die while serving on a regular RAF engagement or on specially
  designated Full Time Reserve Service.

  3.         The RAF Dependants Fund is a registered charity. The governing Trust Deed, Constitution
  and Rules have been registered in accordance with the Charities Act.

  Scope of the Fund

  4.          All officers, airmen and airwomen, including members of the PMRAFNS, who are serving
  on regular engagements may apply for membership of the Fund. Specially designated Full Time
  Reserve Service (FTRS) personnel may also apply for membership. Eligibility will cease on the last
  day of paid service regardless of age.

  Grant Payment

  5.          Because of the charitable status of the RAF Dependants Fund, the basic grant payable on
  death will be at the discretion of the Managing Trustees and will be related to the current financial
  state of the Fund. The amount to be paid will be reviewed by the Managing Trustees annually, or as
  dictated by exceptional circumstances, and promulgated to the RAF.

  Subscription

  6.         The subscription is 25p per month per member. Six months advance warning will be
  given to members in the event of any increase. Subscriptions will be collected monthly in arrears by
  AFPAA(Inns) and paid to the Fund.

  Income Tax

  7.         Subscriptions to the Fund do not qualify for income tax relief.

  Closure During Emergency

  8.            The Managing Trustees may close the Fund to new subscribers when war has been
  declared or when, in their sole opinion, war is imminent. In such a case, the dependants of those
  who were subscribers at the date of closure may be considered for assistance from the Fund for such
  period and in such circumstances as the Managing Trustees in their sole opinion shall decide. Should
  the Fund be so closed, the Managing Trustees will determine the day on which it shall re-open and
  will notify all serving personnel of the date of such re-opening.
