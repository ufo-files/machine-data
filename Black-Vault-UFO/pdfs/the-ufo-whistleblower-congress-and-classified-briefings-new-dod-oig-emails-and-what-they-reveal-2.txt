Charlotte, NC, June 5, 2023 - Working with The Debrief, investigative reporters Leslie Kean and Ralph
Blumenthal reveal for the first time that a former intelligence official turned whistleblower has provided
information to Congress and the Intelligence Community Inspector General on deeply covert programs involving
the retrieval of intact and partially intact craft of "non-human origin."
Kean and Blumenthal with Helene Cooper, co-authored the Dec. 17, 2017 front-page article in The New York
Times that disclosed the existence of a secret Pentagon program investigating unidentified aerial phenomena.
David Charles Grusch, a decorated former combat officer and veteran of the National Geospatial-Intelligence
Agency (NGA) and the National Reconnaissance Office (NRO), alleges that this information has been illegally
withheld from Congress. Grusch has also filed a complaint alleging illegal retaliation for his confidential
disclosures.

* A former intelligence official has provided extensive classified information to the Congressional General
Counsel and Director for Nation Intelligence Inspector General about covert programs and non-human craft
retrieval.
* Other intelligence officials, both active and retired, have corroborated the information independently.
* Whistleblower David Charles Grusch served as a representative to the Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task
Force and co-lead for UAP analysis.
* Grush says recoveries of objects of non-human origin have been ongoing for decades by the government,
allies, and detense contractors.
* Grush alleges illegal shielding of UAP-related information from Congress and illegal reprisals.
* Grush affirmed these extraordinary claims in signed affidavits to the Inspector General and in sworn testimony
to Congressional lawyers.
* This is the first time Grush and other U.S. officials have come out publicly, on-the-record, saying that the U.S.
has covert programs related to the recovery of unidentified aerial phenomena, some of which they assert has
been determined to be of "non-human origin."
* Grush's comments and quotes were reviewed and approved by the U.S. Defense Office of Prepublication and
Security Review (DOPSR) prior to publication.
Grusch's disclosures shed light on a long-hidden enigma with national security implications that has intrigued
the public and perplexed the military for decades.
The former intelligence officer provides evidence of a decades-long "publicly unknown Cold War for recovered
and exploited physical material," where near-peer adversaries engage in a competition to identify UAP crashes/
landings and retrieve the material for exploitation and reverse engineering.
Grusch, along with other witnesses, reports that the retrieved craft fragments and intact vehicles possess exotic
origins and display unique atomic arrangements, material science properties, and radiological signatures.
These materials challenge conventional understanding and strongly indicate the presence of non-human
intelligence.
Congress, which has conducted public hearings and classified briefings on unidentified aerial phenomena, is
pressing for answers. However, the current leadership of the All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office, tasked with
investigating UAP, faces a trust deficit among potential sources, hindering validation of the potentially explosive
information.
Grusch's decision to speak out has encouraged others with knowledge of the recovery programs to come
forward. Jonathan Grey, an intelligence official with the National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)
confirms Grush's claims, stating, "The non-human intelligence phenomenon is real. We are not alone."
In his effort to ensure government accountability through public awareness, Grusch left the government in April
2023. He is supported by highly respected individuals within intelligence circles, including Karl E. Nell, a retired
Army Colonel, and Christopher Mellon, a former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.
Grusch's attorney, Charles McCullough III, senior partner of Compass Rose Legal Group, who served as the
original Inspector General of the Intelligence Community, filed a complaint on behalf of Grusch in May 2022.
The complaint alleges the deliberate withholding of UAP-related information from Congress, and subsequent
reprisals against Grusch.
Congressional committees with access to Grusch's briefing have launched an investigation into the matter, and
the Intelligence Community Inspector General has deemed the complaint "credible and urgent." Grusch's claims
have been corroborated by several current members of the recovery program who have spoken to the Inspector
General's office.
