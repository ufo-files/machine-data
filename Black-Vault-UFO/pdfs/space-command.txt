UFO FACT SHEET (07/96)
On Dec. 17, 1969, the Secretary of the Air Force announced the termination of
Project Blue Book, the Air Force program for the investigation of UFO's.
The decision to discontinue UFO investigations after 22 years of gathering data
was based on an evaluation of a report prepared by the University of Colorado entitled:
"Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects." This report was a review of CU's
report by the National Academy of Sciences; past UFO studies; and the U.S. Air
Force's experience investigating UFO reports during that period from 1947 to 1969.
As a result of these investigations and studies and experience gained from
looking into UF reports, the conclusions of Project Blue Book are: (1) no UFO
reported, investigated, and evaluated by the Air Force has ever given any indication of
a threat to our national security; (2) there has been no evidence submitted to or
discovered by the Air Force that the 700 out of a total of 12,000 sightings categorized
as "unidentified" represent technological developments or principles beyond the range
of current scientific knowledge; and (3) there is no hard evidence to suggest that
sightings categorized as "unidentified" are extraterrestrial vehicles or spacecraft.
With the termination of the Blue Book, the Air Force regulation establishing and
controlling the program for UFO investigations and analysis was rescinded.
Documentation regarding the Blue Book investigation has been permanently
transferred to the Modern Military Branch, National Archives and Records
Administration, Eighth Street and Pennsylvania Avenue, NW., Washington, D.C. 20408,
and is available for public review and copying (charged per page).
Since the termination of Project Blue Book, no evidence has been presented to
indicate that further investigation of UFO's by the Air Force or other government agency
is warranted. In view of the considerable Air Force commitment of resources in the past
and the extreme pressure on the Federal budget, there is no likelihood of renewed
government involvement in this area.
There are a number of universities and professional scientific organizations,
such as the American Association for the Advancement of Science, which have
considered UFO phenomena during periodic meetings and seminars. Additionally, a
list of major private UFO organizations can be found in Gale's Encyclopedia of
Associations (edition 8, vol 1, pp. 432-3). Finally, the two major national centers,
National UFO Reporting Center, tel: (206) 722-3000, and the Mutual UFO Network
(MUFON) at (512) 379-9126, encourage the public to call them with reports of sightings.
