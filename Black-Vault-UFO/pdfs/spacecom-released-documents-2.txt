Dear Mr. Greenewald

        This letter is in response to your 26 April 2021 Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)
request originally submitted to Patrick Space Force Base and later referred to United States
Strategic Command (USSTRATCOM). You asked for a copy of records pertaining to the
Friday, April 23, 2021, launch of SpaceX Falcon 9, and a reported near miss with an unknown
object during the launch. USSTRATCOM assigned the request Case number 21-089 and
referred the responsive records to United States Space Command (USSPACECOM) for
processing. We assigned case number assigned 22-F-006 to your request. Please reference case
number 22-F-006 in any related correspondence.

        Rear Admiral William P. Pennington, USSPACECOM Chief of Staff and FOIA Initial
Denial Authority, determined the responsive records are partially releasable. The authority for
the applied exemptions can be found in the United States Code, Title 5, Section 552(b)(1), (b)(4),
(b)(5), and (b)(6).

           •   One document contains information exempt from disclosure because it is
               currently and properly classified in accordance with Executive Order 13526,
               section 1.4(a).

           •   Another document contains proprietary information exempt from disclosure under
               FOIA exemption (b)(4).

           •   Pre-decisional communications pertaining to the 26 April 2021 incident are
               exempt from disclosure under FOIA exemption (b)(5), after applying a
               foreseeable harm analysis. Several emails include draft versions of briefing
               slides. The final version of the Operational Review Board Report is fully
               releasable; however, we have withheld the draft versions to prevent confusion and
               to ensure the correct information is released. We also withheld information that
               would harm future decision-making processes.

               from disclosure under FOIA Exemption (b)(6) because release of the exempt
               information could constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy.

                                                                                                                      BLUF
At 23I1709z 18 SPCS sent NASA Collision and Launch Avoidance (COLA) results indicating a potential close approach
between the Crew 2 and an unknown object (85411 ). This was a false notification. The unknown object was an analyst
satellite, built for launch detection processing. As a result, SpaceX directed Crew 2 astronauts to don their suits based on
information from NASA Trajectory Operations Officer (TOPO) which was provided by 18 SPCS.
■   DFP: Why was the Active Mission Plan not reviewed by 18 SPCS crew during crew changeover?
■   RC: A failure to properly update and brief changeover slides, perform crew changeover for all crew positions, and
    collectively communicate the mission plan resulted in a false conjunction report being sent to NASA.

      ■   CF 1: All portions of crew changeover were not completed IAW 0113-01; therefore, all crew positions were not tracking
          current operations status


      ■   CF 2: The OSA did not inform the Crew CC of the ongoing situation


      ■   CF 3: The OSA did not review last 96 hour crew log


      ■   CF 4: OSA failed to immediately contact NASA to inform them of the false conjunction


      ■   IF: 18 SPCS will revise 01 13-01, Crew Operations, to review ALL mission plans & highlight changes for effective & active
          crew force comms & w/external agencies during execution


      ■   LL: When performing crew changeover, crews will ensure they comply with the updated 0113-01, Crew
          Operations, specifically reviewing all active mission plans so that all crew members and external agencies are
          aware of current operations status.
