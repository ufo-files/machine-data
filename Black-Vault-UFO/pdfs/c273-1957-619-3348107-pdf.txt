SUBJECT;        Strange object seen by Kenneth H. Hudson of Glenrock
                via Kempsey on 5/12/58, thought to land near KempBey.
REFERENCE;       Attached statement and rough plan.


               I have to report that at about 5 pm on the 5th
December, 1958, Kenneth Hugh Hudson, of Glenrock Plains, via
Kempsey, reported at this Station that he and his wife were standing
near their car painting it at about 11 am on that date, when
they heard a noise like a Jet aeroplane. Mrs. Hudson said to her
husband, "Look atthat thing", he looked in the direction she was
pointing and saw a strange object about 3 feet long and about
2'6" in width travelling in a South-east direction at a very high
speed.
                When questioned about what he saw, he informed me
that it would have come from a North-west direction and was coming
down at about 45 degree angle towards the MacLeay River, and
is believed by him to have landed in the river on the East side.
When asked if he saw any splash of water he replied that he did
not, but saw a great number of birds of different types fly from
the river as though they had been startled by something, and that
they flew from where he thought the object would land.
                 Mr. Hudson said that there was an embankment
blocking their view of the river between their home and the river,
but he felt sure that it would land in the river on the angle
it was travelling.
                 To further questions, he Sated that they were
painting their car at the rear of the house when they heard the
noise and his wife said to him, "Look at that thing going".
He then looked up and saw a strange object about 3 feet long and
2 feet 6 inches wide travelling at high speed, it w~s making a noise
like a Jet aeroplane and had disc objects on the end of the cross
member which appeared at a glance like an axle.    The discs were a
dark colour and the inner part of the object was a lighter colour,
he said that this col our could have been caused by the angle of the
sun on it, but he felt sure that it was a metal and man-made object.
                  From inquiries made it was heard by at least two
people from different angles and by myself at Frederickton but
was only sighted by the Hudsons.
                    Perhaps under the circumstances this report
and the attached Statement and rough plan could be forwarded to
the Department of Aviation for their information and any action         ~~
thought neces sary by them.

                                       (Sgd) A. Algie

                     INSTRUCTIONS TO BE OBSERVED IN THE COMPILATION
                                    AND USE OF FILES.


     I. Before opening a new file, care must be taken to see that a file dealing with the
        subject Is not already In existence. New File Covers must be completed with
        details for which space is provided.

     2. A new file ls to be marked " Part I " and when It has grown to the capacity of
        the cover a new part Is to be opened.

     3. Minute sheets are to be attached to the left hand inside of the cover and enclosures
        to the right hand side.
                                      I
     4. Minute Sheets are to be used for Internal administrative action-only long
        memorandum or papers prepared within the Department being filed as enclosures.
        Inwards and outwards correspondence Is to be filed as enclosures. Enclosures are
        to be given the same number as the minute referring to them without regard to
        the previous enclosure number. When an enclosure consists of only one document,
        Its number will be followed by the letter " A ". When two or more different
        letters or papers form one enclosure, each part will bear the same enclosure number
        but will be distinguished by letters "A ", " B ", "C ", &c.
                ..
     5. Minute No. ' I is to be on the printed side of the Minute Sheet and is to face the
        cover on the minute side of the file.

     6. Correspondence on subjects on · which files already exist must not be made on
        loose Minute Sheets-the file must be obtained and action taken thereon.

     7 Minutes must be add~d in numerical order and both sides of the Minute Sheets
       are to be t:sed. "' ••

     8. " Notes o; Action '' taken may be entered on           the   Minute Sheets, otherwise
        unaddressed minutes are not to be made.
'"   9. Minutes may be noted by Officers placing their initials over the reference to them
        at the Minute Head, and such words as "Agree " and " Concur " may be used
        to obviate separate minutes by individual ' addressees. An Officer's initials so
        placed will indicate concurrence with the minute where such Is called for, otherwise
        that the minute has been noted.?

     10. In columns marked I, 2 and 3 on the cover will be indicated (I) the pe rsons to
         whom the file is to be passed, (2) the number of the minute or enclosure to which
         attention is drawn, and (3) the date it is passed out by the person referred to at (I).

     II. When the pe rson indicated in column I has taken the necessary action on the file,
         he (or his representative in the case of senior officers) will place his initials in
         column 4, indicating that the necessary action has been taken.       He will then
         indicate on the next line, if not already indicated, to whom the file is to pass-
         completing column 2 at the same time.

     12. Files are to be passed from one Division to another through the Registry (unless
         transit slips are sent to Registry) which Is responsible for keeping a record of file
         movements .
                       •
     13. Directors and Officers using files are responsible for expeditious action on them
         and for their immediate retu rn to Registry.
