Dear All,

Welcome, everyone, to Rice University and our “Archives of the
Impossible” conference series. This year, we will be focusing on the
UFO — or UAP (Unidentified Anomalous Phenomenon) — an aspect
of the phenomenon receiving increasing coverage in the media and
government as new whistleblowers are stepping forward to share
credible testimony regarding their interactions with the impossible.
Officials operating within political and legal circles on the Hill have been
paying attention and are beginning to take the subject matter seriously.
Plausible deniability is, apparently, no longer plausible.

To address this emerging shift in the cultural dialogue, this year’s
conference will feature a host of colleagues from the sciences, the military,
the social sciences and the humanities. The conversation will be guided
by several questions: What role do UAP and experiencers play in our
understanding of the phenomenon? Might the influence of UAP or Non-
Human Intelligences (NHIs) be traced back to religious traditions of the
past? How does this possibility change our understanding of religion’s
past, present and future? What kinds of intellectual, sociocultural and
existential shifts does such knowledge ask us to make? What does the
inquiry lend to new conversations between science and religion?

We hope to host a fourth conference in spring 2027 on the subject of
Art and the Impossible in our new Sarofim Building that will host our
Department of Art (the building is going up now just across the street
from where we are meeting). Stay tuned for more on that future event.
The entire conference series, in which “The UFO and the Impossible” is
the third installment, functions as a community builder, connecting a
diverse international collective of individuals. May we all talk and share
now, creatively and constructively.

                                                     Sincerely,

                                                     William B. Parsons
                                                     Karin Austin

The media interest in this event and these topics has been, frankly, overwhelming
but also deeply satisfying. Everyone seems to appreciate the seriousness and the care
with which we are treating topics not generally given their intellectual due. That is the
point of the conference, of course — to demonstrate just how these phenomena can be
integrated and theorized in the academy.


We are recording the conference presentations and will have our own film crew present.
These videos will be available live for those who have registered online. They will be
posted on our website, impossiblearchives.rice.edu, really, on the YouTube channel of
the School of Humanities, following the event.


We encourage you to watch the following recent television series, all of which feature
the Archives of the Impossible at Rice University. They have appeared in this order:
