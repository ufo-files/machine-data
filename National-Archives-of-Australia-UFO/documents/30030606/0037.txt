Further to our discussion on the question of
investigotion of Australian reports on unidentified flying
objects, I would summarize my attitude as follows:
(a)
There appears to be sufficient evidence from
RAAP and US reports of investigation of UPO
sightings to indicate that some reports cannot
readily be explained by natural phenomena or
man-made activities.
Thorough investigation of
selected Australian reports of UPO sightings
seems to be varranted, but the effort should be
restricted to those occurrences that cannot
easily be explained.
(b)
The Department of Supply has personnel with an
appropriate range of scientific ond technical
expertise and laboratory and field facilities
that could suitebly be employed on UPO
investigations.
I suggest that Department of Defence should
consider passing responsibility for investigation
of Australien UPO sightings from the RAAP to the
Department of Supply under the folloving general
conditions:
(i) that & limited number of selected reports,
say six per year, be thoroughly
investigated by Department of Supplys
that at the end of two or three
years (or
earlier if suggested by the investigators)
the results of Supply investigations be
examined by interested parties, e.g. Defence,
RAAP and Supply, with a view to determining
whether there is any defence interest in UPO
sightings that could justify further
investigation.
(a) I believe that, although Defence should have general
oversight and broad direction of the UFO
investigation, significant JIO resources should not
be devoted to this until it can be clearly show from
the results of the investigation that a strategic
intelligence interest exists.
Liven then, the matter
would have to be related to other priorities; there
are many things that it would be desirable for us to
do but which cannot be tackled because of higher
priorities.
