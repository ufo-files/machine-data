Summary: In-flight observations by multiple crews of a light with color variations: very probable observations of the planet Venus on the horizon.
--- seg-ebb4a7cb79c02d7b4b5ab5c2 [needs-review] ---
Description: On April 14, 2010, at 22:44, a total of five commercial flight crews observed a variable-colored light point in the sky heading northwest. The analysis and hypotheses lead us to separate the conclusion into two groups:

- For flights RYR57ZB and RAM697, traveling significantly north of the other three (roughly between La Rochelle and Limoges), the observation appears very consistent with a misidentification due to Venus, which is visible on their side.

- For flights RYR8CW, RYR7CT, and EIN577, traveling east of Bordeaux, the visibility of Venus is in question due to a possible obstruction by the aircraft's nose. The presence of a short, difficult-to-interpret radar trace could lead to a conclusion of an unknown object, but the consistency remains too low without detailed testimony from the various crews. The observed twinkling in multiple colors is due to the dispersion of Venus' light as it passes through the different layers of the atmosphere, varying with fluctuations in those layers and the rapid movement of the airplanes in cruise flight combined with the Earth's rotation.

This case initially has a high degree of strangeness, but it ultimately reveals an average level. Its consistency is very good, given the multiplicity of testimonies, unfortunately very brief. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": very probable observations of the planet Venus at the horizon.
