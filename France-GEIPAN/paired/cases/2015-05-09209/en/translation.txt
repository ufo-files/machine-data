Summary: Silent passage at night of a V-shaped formation of bright points and detachment of one of these points: probable observation of birds (migrants in formation).
--- seg-cfcd1fc7f76869aab322840f [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 17, 2015, at approximately 11:16 PM, an observer looking up at the night sky was intrigued by the appearance of a series of bright points (the observer counted up to 8) that gradually formed a large "V." The entire formation flew silently at a low altitude. One of the points broke away from the formation, and the whole thing disappeared. No other witnesses were found. The witness submitted their testimony to the GEIPAN on June 16, 2015, through the GEIPAN questionnaire. A meeting with the witness took place on December 15, 2015, for a cognitive interview and a reconstruction. The oddity of the case arises from the "V" formation of the lights and their sequence of appearance and extinction. The "V" formation is rarely seen in only two scenarios: 
- a patrol of planes or drones flying in formation;
- a flock of migratory birds.

Regarding the "patrol of planes or drones," it would be surprising at night in an urban setting, and why would the lights of the vehicles follow such a sequence? The "group of migratory birds" hypothesis, however, explains this sequence:
- We are slightly outside of migration season, but gulls and seagulls sometimes fly at night when their roost is disturbed, for example, and they fly at low altitude; their white bodies, even barely illuminated by city lights, can create a luminous (and especially "white") impression. This could also be ducks moving at night and frequently forming "V"s.

The entry of the formation into a slightly brighter area could have produced this immediate appearance for each bird (from shadow to sufficient light) and a progressive formation of the "V." The sequence of lights as observed suggests that the "V" entered from the left, following a brighter area; 
- everything disappeared suddenly, indicating that the "V" left the lit area upon encountering a front of shadow (or less illuminated) transverse to its movement, while the brighter front causing the appearance was more aligned along the movement;
- one bird could have momentarily broken away from the formation. This is more peculiar but not contradictory, especially since we don't know how long this detachment occurred just before extinction. This hypothesis was explained with the help of an ornithologist expert. Nevertheless, it was not possible to understand how the lighting configuration of the neighborhood could produce such a clear and linear contrast at the altitude of the birds. This would mean that a very slight change in lighting can make a white bird appear or disappear in a night sky, or that a particular event that night (it was a Sunday evening) led to special lighting. The testimony is consistent. It is precise and was able to be reconstructed on-site. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable bird observation (migrating flock). The explanation of a "bird" phenomenon is recent at the GEIPAN, and it is possible that some past, unexplained (or poorly explained) observations (including less strange ones, without this particular sequence of lights) may actually fall under this phenomenon.
