Summary: Observations of the silent passage of numerous bright points forming a mass in the night sky: probable observation of a release of toy balloons (mylar).
--- seg-83ee9f869207a2e623387409 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MARLY LE ROI (78) 1993 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed (see the investigation report). On June 27, 1993, around 9:30 PM, during a family dinner on an outdoor terrace, several witnesses observed the passage from NNE to SSO, in a clear, dark night sky, of a multitude of white dots forming a luminescent mass without any precise geometric shape. Intrigued, some witnesses used binoculars, and one witness took two photographs (but they are not included in the case file at the GEIPAN archives). The entire phenomenon moved silently, and the observation duration reported ranged between 10 and 15 minutes. The very bright dots were then hidden by trees in the urban landscape. All elements of the testimonies are compatible with the hypothesis of a confusion with a group of balloons carried by the general wind at altitude. This conclusion is based on the following points:

- Slow and straight movement in the direction of the weaker dominant wind measured at altitude
- Relatively long observation duration compatible with the slow movement of the group of balloons
- Color and brightness compatible with that of light-colored balloons or those made of a highly reflective material (Mylar) exposed to the strong solar luminosity still present at altitude
- The impression of an amorphous mass and the overall behavior of this mass subject to the general wind (all balloons move at the same time and at the same speed in the same direction), while individual elements on its periphery are more exposed and move more
- The description of acceleration or high speed of the PAN at the moment of disappearance could seem more strange. Through experience, the GEIPAN knows that a decrease in the light intensity of the PAN (evolution of the atmospheric veil) is logically interpreted by witnesses as a rapid distance escape of the PAN. Additionally, one of the three witnesses made no note of this. The release of balloons was of great amplitude and should correspond to a significant festive event, as it was not customary at that time. The timing, on a Sunday during a sunny period at the eve of summer vacation, is, however, favorable for the positioning of the event. The fact that we have not been able to trace this release 25 years later cannot be decisive in either direction. At that time, the GEIPAN did not have experience observing the release of playful balloons. This experience has been consolidated today by the accumulation of a very large number of observations of Thai lantern releases. In particular, the collective behavior of a group of balloons subject to the wind is perfectly well characterized and does not need to be different if the balloon is made of Mylar rather than a lantern. The consistency is correct, with three relatively precise testimonies and an on-site investigation by gendarmes carried out shortly after the observation. However, we may regret the absence of the other testimonies (five missing out of eight witnesses) and the absence of the photographs taken by one of the witnesses. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a release of playful balloons (Mylar).
