--- seg-07c173c4b2e9d1478ea321d1 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the night of April 15 to 16, 2017, at 01:45 a.m., a witness was intrigued by an observation made with the naked eye and then with binoculars of a bright yellow PAN at low altitude. The PAN appears stationary, then moves. It will be lost from view behind trees. According to the witness, the observation lasted about twenty minutes. No further testimony will be collected. The phenomenon observed by the witness is consistent with the hypothesis of confusion with an airplane approaching Dijon-Bourgogne Airport (see the investigation report). This aircraft was likely observed from a distance, with landing lights on, heading towards runway 17/35 while gradually approaching the witness's position. In conclusion, while the presence of two steady red lights observed by the witness does not align with the explanation of confusion with a military aircraft, possibly a Transall C-160 in maneuver and/or landing at Dijon-Bourgogne airport, all other elements support this hypothesis, particularly:

- Although BA 102 was closed at the time of the observation, some military aircraft still used it in 2017 for refueling or exercises,
- The Transall C-160 and Hercules C-130 were still operational in 2017,
- The observed immobility and slow movement could be attributed to a combination of factors, including the aircraft's own speed, the distance from the witness, and its movement along an axis that was initially misaligned but later aligned with the observation axis,
- The duration of the observation is generally consistent with the hypothesis, implying both a slow aircraft movement near its stalling speed and an initial observation distance of several dozen kilometers,
- The yellow lights correspond to landing lights, and the red lights correspond to the aircraft's anti-collision lights ("beacon" type), although the witness did not mention them flickering,
- The absence of white anti-collision strobe lights is possible for this type of aircraft, either they are not present or they can be disabled by command. There are, however, uncertainties regarding the number of lights, with the only examples we were able to find showing two landing lights and a red anti-collision light for the C-160. The phenomenon observed by the witness remains compatible with the hypothesis of confusion with a military cargo plane approaching Dijon-Bourgogne Airport. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable misidentification with an airplane in approach to landing.
