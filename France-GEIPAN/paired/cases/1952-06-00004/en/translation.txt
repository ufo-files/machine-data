Description: On June 1, 1952 at 2:40 a.m., two individuals from the crew of a ship at anchor off Port-Gentil (Gabon) observed a luminous phenomenon in the sky. At first, the second captain very distinctly sees an object coming from the interior of the land that stops, turns to the right, and resumes its initial course—twice (the second turn having taken place vertically above the ship). He phones the commander who then looks with binoculars and sees for three minutes an extremely bright and phosphorescent orange circular light. This one moves at very high speed following a substantially straight trajectory. The commander loses sight of the phenomenon. The commander reports this observation. A sketch is made (author unknown). No other testimony is collected. The hypotheses of a bolide (meteor) or missile launch have been proposed but not confirmed. The GEIPAN classifies this case as D: Strange phenomenon, very strange/of medium to strong consistency.
