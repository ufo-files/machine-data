Description: On July 23, 2019, between 10:00 PM and 11:00 PM, while observing the stars in his garden, the witness saw a "glow" pass over him and disappear for a few minutes. During this observation, he went to get his phone to film the phenomenon. Only one testimony corroborates this observation. The case is of average consistency, due to a missing single testimony sometimes lacking in precision regarding important elements, and a video that is unfortunately very little exploitable. The observation is not very strange, given the fairly common description of the phenomenon (see the investigation report). The aeronautical hypothesis is unlikely due to the light signature of the observation (no flickering) and the details provided by the witness. The Thai lantern hypothesis should be rejected. Indeed, the wind was in opposition to the observation trajectory, and the speed was between 16 and 23 km/h on average. Furthermore, the light signature does not show any characteristic flickering, and the white color does not correspond to this type of observation. The hypothesis of a passage of the ISS (International Space Station) remains the most probable. Based on the objective elements defined in the investigation report, namely: - a PAN crossing almost the entire sky in a linear manner, - a round, white, fixed, and fairly bright shape, - the trajectory of the observed phenomenon approaching the trajectory that the ISS was supposed to follow that day around the time of the observation, - the duration of the observation (even if it does not correspond exactly, the approximations in the testimony can suggest that a margin of a few minutes is quite possible), we can conclude that this observation is likely related to the passage of the ISS between 10:08 PM and 10:18 PM that evening. This case should be classified as "B" for a probable observation of the International Space Station.
