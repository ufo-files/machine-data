--- seg-de80b3dee9dcf7c79bf40261 [needs-review] ---
**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined to enhance the conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The case of observation previously classified as D and named MORMOIRON (84) 1985 is part of a recent batch of reexamined cases. On January 14, 1985, two witnesses, T1 and T2, observed a peculiar light phenomenon. A sphere approximately 3 meters in diameter with a color that gradually shifted from white to orange. This bright sphere moved slowly and silently, appearing to follow a vehicle before disappearing. Another witness, T3, had previously observed on January 2, 1985, a glowing light that did not move. After a gendarmerie investigation and referral to the Geipan, the two observations were linked as a whole without an explanation, leading to a D classification. The case reexamination provides an explanation for each observation (see investigation notes). The reexamination distinguishes two distinct phenomena:

- PAN #1: Observed by T1 and T2 on January 14, 1985, after 5:15 PM.
- PAN #2: Observed by T3 on January 2, 1985, after 9:00 PM.

**PAN #1 on January 14:** The description of the PAN on January 14 is consistent with the appearance of the Sun near sunset (orange color) seen through a cloudy layer: the PAN is indeed a smooth sphere that occasionally produces a dazzling light. The position and movement of the PAN are also consistent. The presence of the Sun near sunset in the direction of observation is verified. The witnesses' perception of the PAN's movement is fully explicable by their own movement. It's a classic illusion known to the GEIPAN that contributes to the strangeness and non-recognition of a star for moving witnesses, known as the "following ball" illusion. The star's direction is, of course, fixed absolutely. When the vehicle follows a roughly constant course, the star appears fixed relative to the vehicle and moves across the landscape. Since the witness had already been surprised by the PAN's appearance and considers it at a close distance (hundreds of meters in this case), this movement along the landscape is interpreted as the PAN moving parallel to the vehicle, reproducing the vehicle's speed. On the other hand, when the vehicle makes turns, the perception of parallel movement no longer holds, and the PAN appears to be moving straight through. The starting point of the "following ball" misperception seems to be the non-recognition of the Sun when the witnesses are stationary. The reason is due to the cloudy conditions masking and veiling the Sun, creating rapidly changing aspects, light intensities, and colors (witnesses note the rapid movement of clouds), as well as more or less complete disappearances and reappearances. The variation in light intensity is classically interpreted by a witness who does not recognize the PAN as a change in distance, which is also a source of strangeness in this case. We also know that the Sun behind scattered clouds can create light beams, another obvious source of strangeness for someone who does not recognize the Sun. The perfect consistency between the true position of the Sun and that of the PAN, and the "following ball" effect, leave little doubt about the explanation. The impossibility of asserting that the sky was clear enough that evening for the Sun to be visible to the witnesses prevents us from considering this hypothesis as certain. However, it is highly probable. We can note that the witnesses were not familiar with the location, which may have contributed to the misperception.

Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Misperception with the setting Sun.

**PAN #2 on January 2, 1985:** The description of the PAN's pale orange color is consistent with Mars' appearance. The position and movement of the PAN are also perfectly consistent with Mars (within a few degrees). The observation was not particularly strange for the witness, and it may have become stranger when they learned 12 days later about the other observation (January 14) described in much stranger terms (and by military personnel) and with which it was possible to make a connection.

Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Misperception with Mars at sunset.
