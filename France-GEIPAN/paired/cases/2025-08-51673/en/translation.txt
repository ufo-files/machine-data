Description: On July 31, 2025, at 10:30 PM, a witness observed for a few seconds "a kind of bright blue light" moving very quickly, appearing to be at a very high altitude. The west-southwest observation direction is confirmed by a reconstruction photo sent by the witness. The described trajectory shows an initially ascending phenomenon that, after a short stop, makes a 90-degree turn before disappearing. Due to its peculiarity and average consistency (only one witness and no photos or other evidence to support the statement), this case appears to be the probable observation of the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid. The description of the PAN is consistent with the characteristics of a meteoroid: point-like shape, possible blue color, high brightness, very fast apparent speed, and observation for a few seconds. However, without other testimonies about this PAN, it is not possible to determine its exact location. A search in the FRIPON* database reveals multiple detections during the evening of July 31, 2025, particularly around 8:30 PM (UTC time) or 10:30 PM local time, without any direct recording of what the witness might have seen. The observed change in trajectory is explained by an illusion that occurred during the meteoroid's disappearance, combining a brief retinal persistence and a slight movement of the witness's gaze. The GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid.

*CONSISTANCE: According to the GEIPAN's criteria, consistency is the amount of considered reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.

*FRIPON: Fireball Recovery and InterPlanetary Observation Network, in French "Réseau de récupération de boules de feu (bolides) et d'observation interplanétaire."
