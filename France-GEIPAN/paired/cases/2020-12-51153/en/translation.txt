Summary: A brief daytime observation of a very bright, round phenomenon silently crossing the sky: observation of a bolide in a clear daytime sky.
--- seg-4cbdfc3fa62e797f6f5b2b83 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 19, 2020, around 10:15 a.m., the witness, accompanied by three colleagues, was driving a Traffic Master. He noticed in the sky a brighter point than a star, of fairly large size, which appeared static. The phenomenon remained still for a few seconds in the witness's line of sight through his windshield. He immediately called his colleague sitting next to him, who made the same observation. Then, very quickly, the phenomenon shrank, "became very small," and disappeared in approximately two seconds. The observation lasted about 10 seconds. No noise was heard. Only one testimony was collected for this phenomenon. The study of the case file and available elements suggests with strong presumption the appearance of a bolide in a clear sky during the day and its extinction (see the investigation report). The case has good consistency due to the information and details provided by the witness in the questionnaire. The briefness of the observation points to a rapid aerial phenomenon of meteoric type. No conventional device corresponds to the description given in the questionnaire, namely an extremely bright element in broad daylight disappearing quickly. The observation lasted only about 8 seconds (according to the witness). The disappearance is quite typical of the extinction of a bright diurnal bolide. It appears that the track of a bolide, perhaps sporadic but more likely from the Geminids or the Leo Minorids swarm in December, is probable. The GEIPAN is classified as A: appearance of a diurnal bolide in a clear sky.
