Summary: Presence of an object on a photograph without direct observation: photograph likely of a satellite flare.
--- seg-f0b569c139bc109ccf73fa5d [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The witness, wanting to take a star trail photograph, set their camera accordingly. The next day, he realizes that he has thus captured a glowing object that remained stable, unlike the other stars, which streaked across the sky during the 5-minute exposure time between the shots. A bright point in a long-exposure astronomy photo is more likely to reveal a brief light event than a fixed phenomenon lasting the 5 minutes of the exposure. This bright point turns out to be a small trail, revealing partial motion during the 5-minute exposure. The favored hypothesis is a reflection of the sun on a satellite: the "Calsky" report attached indicates that the NOSS 3-3A and 3C satellites were passing through the camera's field of view at that time, reaching a maximum of 27° above the horizon (it appears that the point on the photo is a little higher). It appears that the NOSS 3 -3C (28541) satellite, as seen in Satflare NOSS3-3C, may have a brighter and more variable glow than NOSS3-3A (28537), as observed in Satflare NOSS3-3A. The small black line observed under the white line when zooming in on the photo is likely a side effect of JPEG compression ("waving" of the white line): it is indeed a pity that the photos were not taken in RAW format. In the attached photo of the Pleiades, greatly enlarged, we see that each star image is surrounded by a black line: it is the same compression artifact. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable photograph of a satellite flare. (There is uncertainty regarding the satellite origin of the flare).
