Description: The witness is in bed when a bright light illuminates their room, followed by a loud noise. They later notice charred grass traces in a neighbor's field. No other testimonies are collected. The observation is extremely brief (1 second) and is solely of a light and a noise perceived from the bed in the bedroom. The witness did not leave the house and therefore cannot provide a description of a PAN (Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon) with any potential shape, position, etc. The light could thus have various origins (thunderstorm, spotlight, meteorite re-entry...). Additionally, the witness found a round mark in the ground the next day. Without observation of a PAN, there is no particular reason to investigate such marks in relation to a PAN, especially as round marks can also have various origins (e.g., fairy rings, traces of an old inflatable pool, motorcycle skids...), older or less obvious marks that may have escaped the witness (they are not immediately nearby the house) until they actively sought out marks. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Insufficient Observation Data.
