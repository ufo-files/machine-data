Description: On February 9, 2015, between 8:00 PM and 11:00 PM, a witness observed a "strange" star that he described as stationary, red, green, and blue. The long observation (one hour) of a fixed object in the sky, appearing like a star, suggests a distant astronomical phenomenon. The witness indicated the directions of Clayrac (azimuth ~ 165°) or Prayssas (azimuth: 142°). On the attached sky map, the star Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky, is visible at an azimuth of 143° and an elevation of 21°. The aspect described by the witness: changes in color from green to red to blue, vertical or horizontal oscillations, is entirely consistent with artifacts produced by atmospheric scintillation (see: is it a star? and a visual perception illusion called autokinetic illusion (see: perception of the movement of a bright point in the sky). The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the star Sirius.
