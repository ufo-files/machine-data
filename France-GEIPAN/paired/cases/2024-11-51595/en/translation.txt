Description: On the morning of November 26, 2024, while overflying the village of GAUJAN (32) during a flight between LOURDES (65) and TOULOUSE (31), a helicopter pilot (T1) and his flight assistant (T2) observed two to three bright points of light. These points moved erratically, appearing and disappearing without a clear pattern. After five minutes of observation, T2 checked for the absence of aircraft using Flightradar and ADS-B, and the air traffic controller at BLAGNAC (31) confirmed no corresponding radar signal. As they approached TOULOUSE, the medical passengers on board, a doctor and a nurse (T3 and T4), also noticed the phenomenon at the crew's suggestion.

The phenomenon was of low oddity and good consistency (multiple witnesses despite only one testimony, photo, and video of the PAN), leading to the conclusion that it was an observation of Starlink® satellite flashes, also known as cluster flares (as detailed in the investigation report).

According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. This phenomenon was noted on the GEIPAN website and can be accessed via the following link: New phenomenon explained: Starlink® satellite flashes.

The description and visual aspect of the PAN are perfectly consistent with this observation. A reconstruction using Stellarium confirmed that the PAN was in an area favorable for observing Starlink® "cluster flares," with the Sun below the horizon at -38°, making the PAN visible vertically from the solar position, at approximately 10° elevation.

Additionally, a reconstruction of satellite passes confirmed the presence of numerous Starlink® satellites in the area where the PAN was located. Since the identification of this type of observation following reports from numerous airline pilots worldwide, this is the first case of observing Starlink® flashes both in the air and from the ground, investigated by GEIPAN.

The GEIPAN classified the case as "A," an observation of flashes from Starlink® satellites positioned on the network of this mega satellite constellation.
