Description: On Friday, September 20, 2024, between 05:50 and 06:10, the witness, while traveling between Saint-Pompon/Daglan and Vitrac (24), observed an unusual luminous phenomenon in the sky. While the sky is still dark, a blinking white light point catches his attention. Its intensity is clearly much stronger than that of a star. At some point, the light source briefly ignites, resembling the appearance of a Japanese lantern or the burner of a hot air balloon, before returning to its initial flickering. Intrigued, the witness stops in Saint-Cybranet to record a video of the phenomenon, then makes a second one upon arriving at the parking lot of his place of work. A colleague, also a witness of the event, supposes it's a hot air balloon. However, the witness expresses doubts, stating that hot air balloons generally do not fly at night. The observation lasts about 20 minutes, though intermittently due to driving. The witness estimates the object is located in a west-north-west direction, at an elevation angle of approximately 45°, and appears stationary throughout the event. The case is of good consistency* with a detailed description, an observation over a long duration, and the witness providing 4 videos (see witness videos). *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The observed phenomenon for this case corresponds to a hot air balloon:

- The radar traces returned by CAPCODA (Air Center for Planning and Conducting Operations and Air Defense) confirm the presence of a hot air balloon (label F-HFBM) in the area and at the time of observation (see CAPCODA return map document).
- Analysis of video 20240920_060951.mp4 reveals the presence of the flame from the heating burner, used to heat the air in the envelope and ensure the balloon's buoyancy.
- No noise was heard.
- The observed speed is slow, giving the impression of immobility.
- The flight period of hot air balloons typically occurs early in the morning or late in the afternoon to avoid thermal currents and ensure optimal air stability. The case is classified A: observation of a hot air balloon.
