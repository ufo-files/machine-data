Summary: Multiple people observed a meteoroid entering the atmosphere, resulting in a bolide.
--- seg-eb144c4e554a93db35e5c19c [needs-review] ---
Description: The phenomenon observed on Thursday, April 9, 2015, around 7:10 a.m., was simultaneously witnessed by numerous observers across southwestern (Foix, Montauban, Saint-Orens...) and western France (La Chapelle-sur-Erdre, Plozevet), from Ariège to Finistère. Witnesses in the Spanish Pyrenees also reported the event (see the summary table of testimonies collected on this phenomenon). This phenomenon is referred to as a "BOLIDE," an old term meaning "in the shape of a ball," often used to describe a very fast vehicle, in reference to the speed of the observed phenomenon (see the generic explanation of the bolide phenomenon). Due to the intense light of the phenomenon, most observers, who are witnessing such an event for the first time, believe it is very close, just a few kilometers or less; in reality, this luminous phenomenon occurs at an altitude between 80 and 20 kilometers. That's why distant witnesses, several hundred kilometers away, can observe it simultaneously (see the report on a similar recent event: A large bolide observed over Spanish Catalonia). In the case of the bolide on Thursday, April 9, 2015, given the trajectories described by the witnesses, it is likely to have occurred over the Atlantic Ocean, about 100 kilometers from the coast (rough approximation). The fragments of the meteoroid, ranging in size from a few millimeters to centimeters, are very likely to have fallen back into the Atlantic Ocean. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: observation of a bolide, resulting from the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid. See other testimonies on the following sites: Ciel des hommes, Actucity, France bleu, La Dépêche with archive image. And a video on Dailymotion.
