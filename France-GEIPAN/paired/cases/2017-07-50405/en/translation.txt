Summary: Observation of the movement of several very bright and multiple-shaped objects flying in the clear sky: observation of Mylar balloons.
--- seg-9ed3d4798fdcc547181765bb [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 30, 2017, around 5:30 PM local time in LE CANNET (06), four individuals observed, both with the naked eye and through binoculars, the rapid movements of four very bright points in a clear sky. Numerous photographs were taken of these objects, which intrigued the witnesses greatly. The observation lasted approximately 13 minutes before some objects suddenly disappeared or others were lost from view, hidden by trees. Only one statement was collected. All objective data gathered are consistent with the hypothesis of festive balloons carried by the wind (see the investigation report). The photographs help clarify the chronology of the observation. The factors of strangeness experienced by the witnesses can be explained by:

- The brightness of the PAN : due to the Mylar-type surface, which reflects sunlight well;
- Sudden disappearance: due to the surface state (mirror-like), it is enough for sun exposure to decrease (passing clouds to the West temporarily blocking the sun) for the balloon to become less bright and indistinguishable, considering also the strong brightness of the sky background (blindness reported by witnesses) behind the balloon;
- High apparent speed: the photographs taken allow for estimating the actual speed within the framework of our hypothesis, and they fall within the range of wind gust speeds at that time. T1 considered (and did not see) that PAN 1 had moved over a long distance, but forgot that it could have been two different PAN (these balloons are often launched in multiples), as there was no continuous observation. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of Mylar balloons.
