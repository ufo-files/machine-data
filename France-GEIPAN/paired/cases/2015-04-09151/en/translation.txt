Description: On April 11, 2015, at 9:41 PM, a witness observed from the window of his kitchen a succession of three luminous phenomena that intrigued him. - A bright white flash at 8:41 PM, to the East, at an elevation of approximately 45°. At this time, several high-altitude planes are noted in the direction of observation (see the Flightradar24 record). The sun has set for about twenty minutes, but the planes at 13,000 meters altitude are still in sunlight: a temporary reflection of the sun on the underside of the aircraft's wings, or on its fuselage, can cause this temporary flash effect. The plane itself, too far away, is invisible to the naked eye. - an orange flash, lower down, moving for 2 seconds: the description closely matches a flash from an Iridium satellite, observed at a low elevation (redding due to the atmosphere): the Calsky log (attached) indicates 2 Iridium flashes in this direction at a time very close: one at 8:46 PM, magnitude -2.5, the other at 8:55 PM, probably too low to be visible in a mountainous area. - a third very short flash of half a second, whose origin may be a reflection of the sun on one of the multiple satellites visible at that time. This GEIPAN report is classified as "B": very probable observation of sun glint on an airplane and satellites.
