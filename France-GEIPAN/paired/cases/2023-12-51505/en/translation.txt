Summary: Multiple reports of light phenomena by flight crew during an Erevan-Paris flight: observation of Starlink satellite flashes in operational orbit.
--- seg-059c45e5a5dbd141291887d3 [needs-review] ---
Description: On the evening of December 13, 2023, during a TVF flight from Yerevan (Armenia) to Paris (France), a line pilot and his colleague observed strange luminescent phenomena in the sky through the cockpit windows. The observation began around 7:00 PM UTC, over the western end of Lake Balaton in Hungary, and continued until the beginning of the descent to Paris, around 8:40 PM UTC. Only one of the two pilots testified to the event. The unidentified airborne phenomenon (PAN) was constantly visible near the star Altair, northwest (azimuth of 310°). The observation took place over several European countries, including Hungary, Austria, Germany, Switzerland, and France. The PAN is described as composed of white spherical lights, appearing and disappearing with variations in intensity. Occasionally, these lights flickered. They could remain stationary or move straight. The observation lasted approximately 1 hour and 40 minutes, although the PAN was not constantly visible. At the end of the observation, the lights turned red, possibly due to atmospheric density. Numerous videos were taken throughout the observation, totaling over 4 minutes of recording. Two hypotheses are considered (see the investigation report): observation of an aerial military exercise or satellite flashes (reflections of the Sun). Although military maneuvers took place southwest of England during the observation period, this hypothesis is ruled out for the following reasons: - the PAN remains stationary relative to the star Altair throughout the observation, and its angular height decreases progressively at the same rate as that of Altair due to Earth's rotation. - the fact that the PAN always remains near the horizon, while the plane traveled a great distance during the observation, indicates that the PAN is very far away. Furthermore, during the descent to Paris, the witnesses noted that the color of the PAN turned red, likely due to atmospheric density (absorption of the blue part of the light spectrum). These elements suggest that the PAN is indeed above the Earth's atmosphere. The second hypothesis, regarding satellite flashes, is retained. The observation corresponds perfectly to other cases reported by pilots at the GEIPAN, explained as "Starlink cluster flares" (groups of satellite flashes from operational Starlink satellites). A check using the Stellarium tool confirms that the position of the PAN relative to the Sun is favorable for this type of observation (approximately 45° above the Sun and less than 10° above the horizon), consistent with descriptions and as illustrated in the Web article starlink-satellites-flaring-in-cassiopeia. This case, of low strangeness but good consistency (multiple witnesses, unique but very complete and detailed testimony, videos of the PAN), is therefore identified as an observation of "Starlink cluster flares." The position of the Sun relative to certain satellites favors periodic specular reflections on the satellite structures, which was perceived by the crew of this flight. The case is classified A: observation of Starlink satellite cluster flares in operational orbit. This observation has very strong similarities with other cases investigated by the GEIPAN and published on the GEIPAN website. [AERO AFR] from ABIDJAN (CI.AB) to PARIS (95) 08.04.2023. [AERO TVF] from AMMAN (JO.AM) to ORLY (94) 31.01.2023. [AERO AFR] from KINSHASA (CD.KN) to PARIS (75) 10.04.2023. These four cases serve as support for a technical investigation note and a publication in the News section.
