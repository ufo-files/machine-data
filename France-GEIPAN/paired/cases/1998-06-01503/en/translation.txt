Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its website: www.cnes-geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. In the night of June 27 to 28, 1998, around 1 a.m., a motorist suddenly experiences a complete breakdown of his vehicle (engine stop, headlights, radio). He parks on the side of the road and notices a green glow in front of his vehicle. The witness gets out and approaches but feels paralyzed. He then observes a stationary conical object floating above the ground and a being that enters the object, which has three seats, two of which are occupied. The object then quickly takes off vertically. As the witness gradually regains his composure, he gets back into his vehicle. The next day, the witness realizes he has a significant memory gap about what he did during part of the previous evening. He returns to the site accompanied but finds no trace visible. Only one testimony is recorded. The gendarmerie on the scene found no traces or clues. This testimony is of extreme strangeness: - conical craft without wings, floating above the ground and taking off vertically; - small beings with oval heads and four fingers entering the craft through a front passageway before takeoff. The case was then classified as D. In light of the current methodology of the GEIPAN (see the GEIPAN Classification Methodology), this classification is incorrect. Indeed, the consistency of the testimony is not sufficient to validate an unexplained or inexplicable character. In terms of information, a second independent witness and/or undeniable traces (photos or ground marks) would have been required here. Reliability is also in question since the witness does not remember what he did in the four hours preceding this observation, leading one to wonder if this observation is not the result of another type of memory alteration. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Unusable testimony.
