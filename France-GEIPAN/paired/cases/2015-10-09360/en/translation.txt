Summary: Observations of several seconds of three bright white fixed lights in the night sky and a red light: probable observation of a military aircraft in training.
--- seg-60e1fb1018a03d787594bb3c [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 21, 2015, around 9:15 PM, a driver and their passenger observed for a few seconds three bright white lights, described as diffuse halos, that were stationary, at a distance of several dozen meters above the tops of the trees. The witness stops observing, finishes their car journey, and then, from their home, spots the phenomena again. These are more scattered, more distant from each other. No noise is perceptible. The witnesses alert the gendarmerie the following day: a drawing is made by the motorist. No further testimony will be collected. The gendarmerie investigation indicates that no airship flew over the area at the time of the observation due to the strong wind that evening. The witness observes 3 fixed round lights, and a single red light (see witness drawing) for a few seconds. This aspect clearly suggests a plane seen at night, head-on (3 headlights + red position light to the left). The noise of this aircraft may not be perceived by witnesses who are in their car with closed windows. From this angle, the plane's wings are invisible as they are not illuminated. The presence of an aircraft at low altitude in this location, far from any airport, is unusual. But the military RTBA map (Low-altitude terrestrial network) shows no anomalies in the area at the time. (See map attached) reveals that this area named R166 is indeed a particular zone reserved for low-altitude aerial military exercises, particularly on Wednesday evenings. The weather report indicates overcast conditions, light variable winds at Bergerac at 21:00 (see Météociel). The high humidity in the air can explain the halo perceived around the aircraft lights. The GEIPAN did not handle the case quickly enough (one-month delay) to verify with the military air traffic control that an aircraft was indeed present in the area at that time. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of a military aircraft in training. The white points observed in the second part of the observation are probably other aircraft participating in the maneuver.
