Description: GEIPAN continues to publish all its archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. Its publications include older cases classified at the time (A, B, C or D) that are now being re-examined solely to improve the pertinence of the conclusions. Thanks to new technical resources (software) and the investigative experience acquired over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification explained in an investigation note. This observation case, previously classified D and named SOULTZ SOUS FORETS (67) 1980, is one of a group of recently re-examined cases. During the night of April 11 to 12, 1980, between 23:50 and 00:30, several witnesses observe a luminous phenomenon in the sky for a long time from different locations. The phenomenon, stationary or moving when the people move in their vehicle, intrigues them. The accounts in gendarmerie PV reports N°491 and N°307 have the characteristics of a celestial-body observation, now well known to GEIPAN through the accumulation of explained cases of this kind, particularly over the past ten years (see www.geipan.fr and search for cases involving celestial bodies, Venus, the Moon and the Sun): - Long observation lasting more than 30 minutes, low elevation in the sky and final disappearance of the PAN behind local terrain. - The PAN's position remains stable when the witness stops and moves when the witness moves, creating the perception of being followed. This is the "following ball" illusion. The celestial body's direction is of course fixed in absolute terms. When the car follows a fairly constant heading, the body remains fixed relative to the vehicle and passes along the landscape. Since the witness was already surprised by the PAN's appearance and considers it nearby, this passage along the landscape is understood as the PAN moving parallel to the vehicle at the same speed, here to the witnesses' left on the road from Hoelschloch to Merkwiller. On arrival at Merkwiller, the PAN naturally "goes around the church", which lies to the left of the car's direction; at the junction, when the vehicle stops, the PAN remains level with the junction. - The witnesses do not identify a conspicuous, very bright celestial body present in the observation direction, although the sky is described as clear and starry. Verification shows that this is indeed Venus, which at 23:50 is very visible, magnitude -4.4, at azimuth 301° and elevation 6.2°. - The sight lines identified by T2 and T3 agree: they place the PAN "above Liebfrauenberg", actually the château of that name. T1 uses a less precise reference, "near the radars of Drachenbronn base", and T3 mentions the same radars at the end of his statement while repeating that the PAN was above Liebfrauenberg: "I specify that when the ball was above Liebfrauenberg, in that direction, it was very close to and just above the airbase radar installations, to their left." This wording is confused, especially since the two landmarks differ by 20° to 30° in azimuth, depending on the width attributed to the Liebfrauenberg château grounds. T3 may mainly have wished to draw attention to military radars not being far from the OVNI. T4 says, "for about half an hour we could observe the object, which was apparently stationary above DRACHENBRONN base". T4 was not local, being on military service, and may have used T1 or T3's reference, since the witnesses were all together for at least part of the observation. This may explain "apparently", but he also abbreviates the reference, mentioning the base, which is northeast, rather than its radars, which are north-northwest. - Using T2 and T3's precise reference, not contradicted by T1 and T4, namely Liebfrauenberg château, and taking the observation point as the Merkwiller junction or the place called Hattenweg, the sight line matches Venus's azimuth, about 300°. The elevation profile in that direction, within +/- 5°, does not hide Venus. The obstacle with the greatest elevation, the Goersdorf forest massif, is 4.2 km from Hattenberg with a height difference of 200 m, giving a relative elevation of 2.7°. According to this estimate, Venus becomes obscured at 00:20, whereas the witnesses place it at 00:25 or 00:30; the elevation changes by 1.2° in 10 minutes. This lies fully within the margins of error in estimating or knowing the exact times of the observed events and the witnesses' precise positions, which affect the assessment of obscuration. Gendarmerie PV N°491 notes in its investigation summary and accompanying drawing that the PAN finally fled northeast after half an hour stationary above Liebfrauenberg. This would contradict the hypothesis. But T2 and T4 do not mention a final departure. T2's wording, "it remained above Liebfrauenberg until 00:30; I do not know which direction it took afterwards, I was not paying attention", suggests knowledge of accounts of a final departure without being able to confirm them. T3 says, "the ball left, leaving a yellow trail, towards the north". T3 may have experienced the usual illusion of a final reduction in the star's intensity due to the atmosphere, interpreting it as sudden recession into the distance. He may have approximated a departure northeast, with Venus then at 308°, as a departure north, just as he already approximated Liebfrauenberg and the base radars. T1 says, "The object remained motionless near the radars of DRACHENBRONN base. We observed the phenomenon until twenty-five minutes past twelve at night, when it moved away at very high speed towards LOBSANN." Since LOBSANN is northeast, this would indicate a clear final departure to the right, without the possibility of associating it with recession into the distance as for T3. Nevertheless, T1's account differs from the others on several points. During the car journey with T2 and T4, T1 describes the PAN as "high in the sky", whereas T4 and T2 see it to the left, or in an equivalent fixed direction, and T4 says that T1 pointed out the PAN on his left to the others. T1 alone describes a PAN consisting of "two discs joined by a line". Finally, T1 describes the PAN crossing the road from the right. A sensation of movement relative to the road could have occurred, but not in that direction or to that extent. The road's heading is not constant, particularly at the cemetery, where T1 says the PAN crossed the road: the heading changes from north to northeast, so Venus's direction relative to the road changes from the left, the 10 o'clock direction, to near the road's heading, the 12 o'clock direction. These distinctive and/or exaggerated aspects of T1's perceptions lead us not to retain them. Other apparently strange features, such as colour changes and impressions of the PAN descending in jerks or steps, are typical of observing Venus at low elevation through atmospheric layers that cause colour changes. The initial misidentification, failure to recognise it because of its glow and colours, causes the misjudgement of distance and ultimately the following-ball illusion. These aspects reinforce one another's perceived strangeness. The strong agreement of appearance and sky position with Venus leaves no doubt. The few opposing elements, a final departure eastwards, cannot be retained because they come from just one of the 4 witnesses, differing from or contradicting the other three. Finally, none of the witnesses distinguished Venus during more than 30 minutes, although the sky was clear and Venus lay in the observation direction and should have been a key reference for locating the PAN. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observations of Venus.
