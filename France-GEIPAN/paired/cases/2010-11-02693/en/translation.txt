Description: On the evening of Tuesday, November 9, 2010, at midnight, the witness, in the kitchen of their apartment, was observing the stars when they noticed a group of three yellow-hued lights in motion, forming a triangle. The observation abruptly stopped with the disappearance of the three lights, lasting only about two seconds. A few days later, the same observation occurred (date, time, duration, and circumstances not specified). The hypothesis of satellites does not fit at this time of night. The yellow color and the movement compatible with the wind direction could suggest a case of misperception with Thai lanterns seen as they were going out. These lanterns are typically released during celebrations, most often on weekends. The too-short observation duration does not allow for validating this hypothesis, which remains, however, possible. Finally, the witness does not specify if the observation was with the window open or closed. We can therefore also consider the possibility of a reflection from a kitchen light fixture where the witness is standing. Of low consistency and strangeness difficult to assess due to the very short observation duration, this case cannot be concluded. This case is classified C due to lack of independent testimony and cross-referencing.
