Description: On July 28, 2016, between 1:10 and 1:14 a.m., a witness opens their window and briefly sees several bright points forming a sort of "V" moving quickly. They initially think it's birds illuminated from below, then fighter jets, but hear no noise. The white-yellow points fade into the night. Only one testimony is collected. The observation is likely related to a flight of Thai lanterns:

- "Star-like" lights, but with perceptible size changes (diminishing here, probably due to the lanterns moving away).
- A formation flight (the lanterns move in the same wind current).
- A final pitch and fade: lantern extinction is often simultaneous and can cause trajectory changes.
- The wind measured in Lyon that night, according to Infoclimat, was from the west. The movement of the PAN in the sky indicates an eastward shift (consistent with the measured wind) and also northward, as the PAN are moving away from the witness looking north (angular size reduction and decrease in elevation or height in the sky).

We note that the observation took place in a city, very close to a major river, factors that can lead to local wind variations compared to the measured wind at a weather station, even a few kilometers away. The observation occurred on a summer night, which is conducive to festive events involving the release of lanterns. Therefore, the GEIPAN case is classified as B: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
