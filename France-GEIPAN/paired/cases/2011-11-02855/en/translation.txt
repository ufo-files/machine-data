Description: Unexplained cases may hold potential scientific knowledge, but their unexplained nature must be validated over time. Some cases may be reviewed again. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case was re-examined (see the investigation report). On November 27, 2011, at 6:50 PM, a driver and her children were intrigued by the presence of a stationary light phenomenon above the church in the village of Saint-Gatien-des-Bois (Calvados in the Basse-Normandie region). A fifth witness will also provide their testimony later. The conclusion of the initial investigation ruled out the hypothesis of a low-level cloud impact from skytracer-type projectors, based on meteorological data showing no cloud presence in the observation area. Or, a new, more advanced meteorological study (detailed in the annex of the investigation report) shows that low stratocumulus cloud banks were very likely present. It is now necessary to consider this hypothesis, supported by the following elements:

- The shape of the PAN is circular.
- The number varies as witnesses cannot recall, but remains consistent with the hypothesis.
- The color is white, compatible with the color that skytracers might use.
- There is confirmed presence of stratocumulus clouds at the location of the observation.
- High brightness is generated by the type of ray used by skytracers, concentrated over a small 8° diameter, and visibility is enhanced by the witnesses' familiarity with night vision.
- Exceptional horizontal visibility indicates very clean air with no fine particles suspended, allowing the beams generated by skytracers to be seen.
- There is no noise.
- The UFO(s) are located north of Saint-Gatien golf, which is nearby and known for hosting outdoor festive events that might potentially use skytracers. A contractor working at the time for the golf course offered shows using the skytracer model, whose characteristics proved to be all compatible with the characteristics of the PAN. The actual use of skytracers by this provider at the time of the incident could not be formally demonstrated, but operational tests were conducted in anticipation of future events. - The visual compatibility of PAN with what the assumed skytracer model would produce, located in the golf course and firing at a cloud layer at approximately 1000 meters altitude. This compatibility assumes that the observations of T1, T2, T3, and T4 on one hand, and T5 on the other, correspond to different directions of sky tracer shots and thus to non-simultaneous observations. This is perfectly plausible given the independence of these brief observations and the inherent agility of the skytracer. Each part of the witnesses only saw one of these sightings, the East or S.E. sighting of the skytracer seen by T5 may have escaped the attention of T1/T4 (the observation may also have been blocked by buildings to their right) while the South sighting observed by T1/T4 may have escaped the initial attention of T5 or occurred after he ended his observation (he immediately turns away and takes off running), - the date, a Sunday evening, is conducive to the use of such skytracers in a festive context. The period is much shorter, with November being unsuited for such use, having an outdoor temperature under shelter of approximately 9°. It is entirely possible that the material was used for testing purposes prior to a later display (Christmas?). The consistency is good because there are several witnesses, at least one of whom is independent of the others (T5), describing essentially the same thing. One can only regret the lack of angular data explicitly provided by the witnesses; this information is deduced solely from the photo reconstruction made by the first investigator (a priori based on non-recorded indications from the witnesses). The strong compatibility of the hypothesis with the appearance of the PAN and the cloud cover, its plausibility given the practices and equipment whose implementation is attested on the site and during the period, and the absence of any formal element opposing it, allow the hypothesis to be considered probable. The GEIPAN concludes in B: probable observation of impact spots in the sky from a skytracer.
