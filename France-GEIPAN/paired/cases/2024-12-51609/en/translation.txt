Description: The secondary witness (W2), leaving their home, spots a fixed bright object in the sky, initially interpreting it as a star. Shortly after, their spouse, designated as the primary witness (W1), arrives at the scene and conducts an independent observation of the same phenomenon. W1 returns inside the home to retrieve their smartphone, using it to take several photographs and video recordings. During this time, W2 pauses their observation. W1 describes the PAN as a blue-tinted, oval-shaped light performing rapid, varied, and non-rectilinear movements in the sky. The phenomenon remained visible for an estimated duration of at least eight minutes before suddenly ascending and disappearing at extremely high speed towards the sky. The consistency* of the testimony is very good, with two witnesses (but only W1 completed a GEIPAN questionnaire) and several photos and videos that could be utilized for the investigation. *According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The peculiarity perceived by the witness mainly relies on the visual aspect of the PAN and its movements, as observed in the provided photographs and videos. The explored hypothesis, that of observing the planet Venus, is supported by the following elements: Analysis of video sequences, particularly in clear and non-zoomed passages, reveals a single stationary white light point whose position in the sky corresponds to Venus at the time of observation. Notably, no additional light sources are visible nearby, which would have been expected if the observed phenomenon had an origin other than Venus. The apparent movements, oval shape, and blue-tinted appearance of the phenomenon are only perceptible in zoomed portions of the photographs and videos due to: - The use of W1's smartphone held at arm's length creating a shake effect responsible for the erratic movements visible in the videos and the apparent oval distortion of Venus' shape in the photographs, - The use of the smartphone's night mode generating an optical artifact, giving the PAN a blue appearance in the images. It appears that W1 equated the appearance and behavior of the Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon (PAN) based on images and videos that, as we have demonstrated, exhibit visual artifacts and incorrect interpretations. This hypothesis is strengthened by the testimony describing the disappearance of the PAN as a "spear-like movement towards the sky at extremely high speed," while the video analysis actually reveals a scrolling of fixed elements on the ground, perceptible on the screen. The case is classified as "A," observation of the planet Venus.
