Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website: www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified C case due to lack of information and named BONNIERES SUR SEINE (78) 1978 is part of a set of cases recently re-examined. On March 8, 1978, at 6:45 a.m., a driver noticed colored lights through the woods coming from a field. Upon arriving at the field level, she sees a very bright, metallic gray-blue object in the shape of a large upside-down bowl. Immobile and silent, it emits flashing lights from its base. Fearing for his safety, the witness leaves the area after one minute but returns at noon with work colleagues: a circular imprint on the ground at that location is observed. Notified by public rumor the following day, the gendarmerie contacts the witness and goes to the scene. No particular traces are observed except for those of a tractor. According to the witness, many curious people would have trampled the site. Prior note regarding a case where testimonies and/or traces relate to an explicitly described or perceived phenomenon on the ground: 
- In the absence of evidence that the phenomenon on the ground could only have come and/or gone by air, the search for an explanation exceeds the sole competence of the GEIPAN, and a GEIPAN conclusion can only be formulated if proof or near-proof is established; otherwise, the GEIPAN must conclude C: Inconclusive as it exceeds its sole competence. 
- Conversely, the absence of testimonies or traces of phases, or when the phenomenon is not on the ground, may prevent the GEIPAN from reaching a conclusion due to a lack of reliable information (classic C GEIPAN classification). We are in the first scenario here, as nothing allows us to exclude that the phenomenon arrived or left through the ground. The examination of the tracks in the field proves nothing in either direction. As a result, GEIPAN Class C: Ininvestigable as it exceeds the sole competence of GEIPAN. The analysis that the GEIPAN can perform within the limits of its capabilities and experience is as follows:

- The visual description is of moderate strangeness.
- The visual description is consistent with the presence of a lighted vehicle in a field near the road before dawn, the night observation, and the improbable nature of a vehicle at that time, which may have led to a misperception.
- The strangeness experienced in real-time by the witness appears limited as he did indeed stop (for one minute), but he did not wait for the phenomenon to evolve. He did indeed return to the site, perhaps also driven by the reaction of his colleagues to his disk hypothesis. On the other hand, the presence of a vehicle at that time is surprising and constitutes the main strangeness. It could be related to illegal activity, of course unverifiable, such as poaching, for example. The observation is characterized above all by low consistency both in terms of information and reliability: - the main testimony and those of the secondary colleagues are not spontaneous but at the request of the gendarmerie; - the evolution or disappearance of the phenomenon is not observed; - round or suspicious traces are not noted by the gendarmerie; - the strangeness for the witness may have increased due to the perception of unusual traces, the collective excitement (colleagues, journalists), and influenced some aspects of his subsequent deposition; - the non-spontaneous testimony of the colleagues, regarding minimal description and quasi-identical traces, opens up a possibility (non-verifiable) of prior coordination or influence.
