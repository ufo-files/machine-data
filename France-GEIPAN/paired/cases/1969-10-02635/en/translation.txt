Summary: Observation of a bright point during sunrise with a telescope.
--- seg-e3b59687da7f748d9fc28a27 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: A witness in 2010 reported an old observation made using a borrowed Admiral 1000 terrestrial telescope (zoom 15X-60X) from a friend. Two other people present at the time also observed the phenomenon. On Wednesday, October 8, 1969, at 7:10 AM, three individuals had their attention drawn to a bright point in the sky. The witness then observed through the telescope an object moving slowly from north to south in a back-and-forth motion. He described a disk accompanied by two satellites orbiting the main element. The observation lasted approximately twenty minutes. The witness paused his observation for a brief moment but saw nothing upon his return. No other testimonies regarding a phenomenon seen in October 1969 in the region or neighboring areas were collected at GEIPAN. Observing a bright point in the morning sky is in itself a common phenomenon; it could have been Sirius or a weather balloon in this case. The disappearance of the object is also mundane: the increasing brightness of the sky at sunrise made the object invisible. The observation with the telescope as described by the witness with enthusiasm is more peculiar (satellites orbiting around the object); however, zoom telescopes are known to be highly sensitive to optical aberrations (flare causing ghost images); the witness claims this telescope was of good quality without notable defects as he had previously seen Jupiter's satellites with it. The antiquity and uniqueness of the testimony, along with the inability to verify the quality of the telescope, do not allow for a clear conclusion; classification "C".
