Summary: Observation of the passage of two very bright red spheres in the night sky and extinction; then observation of two gray masses with different trajectories: mistaken for Thai lanterns.
--- seg-eed62209b6c78d318a4d44b0 [needs-review] ---
Description: On May 08, 2020, around 21:45, the witness states they saw from their home two very bright red spheres that were practically stationary or moving very slowly. The witness, in a moment of clarity, grabs his camcorder and films the scene. Its battery immediately fails and the video lasts only 16 seconds. The lights go out. He therefore continues the observation with the naked eye and notices the presence of two dark gray-black masses that then take different trajectories. One passes practically overhead at an altitude he estimates to be between 1 and 3 kilometers, while the other takes a curved, descending trajectory and appears to be coming towards the witness. He describes the first mass as being the size of a commercial airplane (type A320), without wings, and disappearing into a cloud. He was not able to observe that first mass well. The other, on the contrary, passes at a low altitude. It has an elongated shape, visually similar in length to a large commercial airplane, but it is not possible to discern its contours. The object disappears, hidden by the houses and trees of the neighborhood. Only one testimony is collected. The Thai lantern hypothesis is strongly considered here. The witness does not envisage a release of lanterns. However, the observation is compatible with these: - the appearance first: the PAN appear as two extremely bright spheres, orange in color as shown in the attached video. - then the witness sees two dark gray-black masses each with its own trajectory. These trajectories may seem surprising at first glance of the case, but they are in perfect agreement with the winds present that evening. Indeed, between 21:45 and 22:00, the wind turns and changes its trajectory. It is very weak, in the form of a breeze as the nearest weather station indicates 0 km/h (see annex: Winds). The initial wind is from a 31° sector: it is therefore oriented SSW, which corresponds to the passage of the first gray-black mass over the witness. The second one is oriented NNO, which again corresponds to the passage of the second gray-black mass. - The trajectory of the second gray-black mass is descending, therefore towards the ground, which is typical of a lantern that is turned off. - The gray-black color, one evening, is consistent with turned-off lanterns. - The burner heats the air inside the envelope and the lantern rises; the wind is very light and the movement is minimal; When the burner goes out, the air cools down, the lantern descends, the envelope can lean up to horizontal before touching the ground. That is why there are very few fires caused by lanterns. - The witness is remarkably precise on this point as well since he saw the extinguishing. The witness draws a rectangle for one of the gray-black masses; a dead lantern leaning long gives this exact description. The second is described as "square" with striations that again correspond to the lantern's frame as seen from below. He states: "Initially, the red spheres were between 1 and 3 km in altitude." Then once turned off, one of them was practically below my head, still between 1 and 3 km. The other was at an altitude of 300 to 500 meters and at a distance difficult to estimate (several km)." (See annex: Winds). The witness's estimate of the object's size (an airplane) does not align with this hypothesis, but it should be noted that estimating the size of an unknown object in a night sky without reference points is nearly impossible. This argument alone does not allow the hypothesis to be rejected. The present case presented a strong strangeness upon reading the testimony, but this is significantly reduced by the explanations above. The consistency of the file and the testimony is good, and the contribution of the video, even though short, adds value to the testimony, which is very precise in this case. It is an evening on a holiday, which is conducive to events of this type. The witness is surprised by changes in a very light wind that gives two different trajectories of extinguished lanterns. He indicates in his testimony, indeed, that they were not so easy to see. The case study concludes that it was a misidentification with sky lanterns. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Thai lanterns.
