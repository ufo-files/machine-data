Description: Unexplained cases hold potential scientific knowledge, but the unexplained nature must be validated over time. These cases are therefore reviewed. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this review sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The observation case previously classified as D1 and formerly named GIVET (08) on 20.07.2009 is part of a recent set of cases reviewed. On July 20, 2009, around noon, a person gardening suddenly noticed the presence of a bright, cigar-shaped object with a small fin. The object was silent and stationary above the houses in his neighborhood. The front of the object faced northeast, but the rest was difficult to discern. The witness described "a kind of agitation" above the object. The observation lasted about ten minutes, then the frightened witness went home. No other testimony was collected on this phenomenon. No flight incident was reported to the gendarmerie. The observation is very strange, given the elements described and the angular size (20 meters wide at 10 meters distance) of the entire described object (see the investigation report). Confirming the second point during a second encounter with the witness rules out any explanatory hypothesis and makes the observation even stranger. Indeed, the angular size, combined with the angular position (which is not vertical but within a field of view without lifting the head), makes it impossible that this PAN was not noticed (and reported) by the neighborhood during the day (at noon) in an urban area (a neighborhood investigation was conducted). The consistency of the testimony is not sufficient to validate an unexplained or inexplicable nature to this observation. It would have taken several independent witnesses or traces or photos. Here, the single witness, by his behavior in the face of such strangeness, is particularly questionable, as he stops the observation to go eat while the PAN is still there, "10 meters away from him and 20 meters wide," even though he has a camera. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Unusable testimony.
