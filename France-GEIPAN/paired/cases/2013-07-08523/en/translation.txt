Summary: Observation of a straight-line, silent movement of an orange-colored light point in the sky: probable observation of a Thai lantern.
--- seg-ee6d10a9b8d8e7adc9f7454b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Saturday, July 27, 2013, at 10 PM, two walkers were intrigued by the straight, silent movement across the sky of an orange glowing point. The observation lasted two to three minutes before the object disappeared over the horizon. The following day, witnesses discovered that other people had seen the phenomenon in the area, but no other testimonies were collected at GEIPAN. This testimony of a large orange glowing point is extremely well described; it is not particularly strange. The color, the linear movement, the time, and the day of the observation are consistent with a Thai lantern. The consistency of the movement with local wind is uncertain: weather reports available in Luxembourg and Metz indicate a south or south-west wind, but highly variable, which could potentially explain the object's change in direction. Unique Thai lanterns are not rare. The apparent "radial" speed of Thai lanterns by average wind is similar to that of airplanes. The apparent size and high brightness reported by the witness are difficult to compare to lanterns, given the variety of models on the one hand, and the observation conditions on the other. This observation is consistent with a Thai lantern, but not all the indicators to confirm it (wind, multiple releases) are present. A wedding was noted in Rehon at 4 PM on that day. With these indicators and uncertainties, GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a Thai lantern.
