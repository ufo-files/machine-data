Summary: Observation of the movement in the night sky of a red-orange glowing point with a halo: probable observation of a celestial lantern.
--- seg-e82e8427e243654f188bcf5e [needs-review] ---
Description: On May 14, 2022, around 11:50 PM, while walking down the street in front of his home, the witness observed a significant, red-orange glowing point surrounded by a halo, with an estimated magnitude of -6. The object remained stationary for approximately 30 seconds, then descended vertically, moved towards the south, and returned to a vertical position. Subsequently, the PAN accelerated and quickly moved towards the south, heading towards the Saint-Alban nuclear power plant (Saint-Maurice-l’Exil), before disappearing, obscured by buildings. These movements occurred without any audible noise. Over a period of approximately 10 minutes, the PAN traced a trajectory covering roughly 120° in the sky, with an initial elevation estimated between 25° and 30°, gradually decreasing to 20° at the time the object was lost from view. The witness created a photographic reconstruction. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, the consistency is average, with one unique witness, a photographic reconstruction, but no photos or videos. *Consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation concluded that this case was likely the observation of a sky lantern carried by the wind (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Point-like shape with a halo.
- Red-orange color.
- Relatively bright luminosity.
- General movement in the direction of the wind.
- Calculated movement duration consistent with a lantern located approximately 200 meters away and moving at the speed of the wind, roughly 1 m/s.
- The observation day, a Saturday, is ideal for a sky lantern release on the occasion of a local or private event, especially given the favorable meteorological conditions (weak wind, calm weather). We were also able to show that the differential movements of the PAN compared to the overall southward movement (initial stop then two brief vertical movements) could have been caused by the presence of urban heat wells generating local aerodynamic phenomena (zero and/or upward winds). The witness's perceived strangeness was due to the appearance of the PAN, which he did not recognize as a familiar object, and its behavior with a seemingly random movement. The elements mentioned above explain these peculiarities. Classification as 'A': Probable observation of a sky lantern.
