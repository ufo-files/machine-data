Description: This observation case is part of cases classified "C" in 2008 by the GEIPAN . It was the subject of a new investigation. (see document: Investigation Notes). On January 14, 1979, between 5:30 and 7:00 a.m., two witnesses observed, directly to the East in the sky, a luminous phenomenon that intrigued them. A bright sphere, larger than the stars, moves silently and at a slow speed in an East-South direction. The main witness did not provide details regarding the sphere's altitude in the sky. No other testimony was collected on this phenomenon. The case is consistent, but the description of the PAN observed is imprecise in the gendarmerie report. The sincerity and credibility of the witness were never questioned. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: Venus, which was present in the observed sky area. In the current classification of GEIPAN, this weak strangeness case is classified as PAN B, probable observation of the planet Venus, through a light layer of fog or by a halo effect.
