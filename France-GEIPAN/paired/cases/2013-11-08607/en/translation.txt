Summary: Observation of intense light and confirmation of light intensity on photographs: parhelion observation.
--- seg-dbafb7dc227dc6716dc38017 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On November 10, 2013, between 4:59 PM and 5:00 PM, a witness observed for 10 minutes a bright light that was impossible to look at directly. Taking photographs of the Sceaux Park, the witness then noticed on the pictures a white and then red light trail above the horizon that he could not explain. The observation of light phenomena at sunset leads to hypotheses related to the sun. The angle between the sun and the phenomenon was around 22°, an angle favorable to the parhelion phenomenon. Parhelion occurs in the presence of high-altitude ice clouds, which could be possible when the sky is filled with cirrus clouds and a weather report indicates cold and dry conditions; it can also occur on condensation trails of planes: the observed shape, and the heavy air traffic also make this origin possible. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of parhelion. NOTE: The green spots observed on the pictures are internal reflection effects within the camera's optics (lens flare).
