Summary: Sequential observations of a bright point in the sky: Venus observation.
--- seg-bb46245cf3c262f2a3ec5e5b [needs-review] ---
Description: A case of observation previously published under the name BRIEY (54) with a "B" classification, likely the observation of a star, was subject to a new analysis in 2009. Between October 20th and 24th, 1991, between 6 and 8 a.m., two witnesses were intrigued by the presence of a bright light phenomenon in the sky. One of the witnesses filmed with his camcorder the appearances of this very bright point. The gendarmes also noted the presence of a bright point in the same location in the sky. When taking a picture with the maximum zoom, a sphere with two "notches" appeared; this turned out to be a problem with the camcorder's lens. Following an astronomical verification, the investigation revealed that "the Shepherd's Star" was indeed visible in this part of the sky on the indicated dates and was in its maximum brightness phase with a magnitude that could reach -4.46 during this period. In light of the additional investigation, the GEIPAN classified this case as "A" for perfectly identified: successive observations of the planet Venus.
