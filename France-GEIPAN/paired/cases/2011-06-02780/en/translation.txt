--- seg-535253c56e495e64d901fda7 [needs-review] ---
Description: The witness filmed a lunar eclipse on June 15, 2011, and noticed the presence of three strange lights that intrigued them. The witness also noticed, upon later reviewing the video, a fourth phenomenon. The videos and photos do not clearly match the witness's testimony. The first appearance of the phenomenon at 3:48 corresponds to a "de-zoom" effect that reveals a bright point in the field of view that was not there in the "narrow field" mode. We then observe the "descent" of the bright point as the zoom factor increases again. It is likely a light source close to the ground (e.g., a street lamp). At 4:02, another bright point appears below the Moon: it is clearly a lamp post with part of its base visible. (The ephemeris included shows that the Moon was only 15° above the horizon, quite low, about 2 hours after its rise). The witness seems to have only observed these phenomena through the viewfinder of the camcorder, then realized it after video editing; the zoom/de-zoom operations deceived them. The photo showing two "L" shaped lights corresponds to a long-exposure photo (operator movement) of two bright points, likely the same two sources that impressed the video. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": indirect observation via video and photo of ground-based lamp posts.
