Summary: Observation of silent movements of a bright PAN with flashing colored lights: probable observation of a recreational drone.
--- seg-5517f0fd6e1dc5dd95489ace [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 28, 2018, between 9:03 PM and 9:10 PM, a witness observed the storm forming over the city from a road overlooking it. His attention was drawn to the silent movement of a white glowing point. Then, the glowing point stopped, and the witness noticed flashing lights of various colors. The witness returned home to fetch binoculars and a compass to determine the azimuth, but upon his return, he could not discern the shape of the PAN any further. The object then departed in the exact direction it had come from and disappeared over the horizon. Only one testimony was collected (see the investigation report). In conclusion, we have shown in the analysis that the witness likely observed a recreational drone equipped with lights and used under conditions that were not favorable, or even unauthorized (altitude). This conclusion is based on the following two main elements: - The behavior of the PAN, with a straight trajectory and a stop, is typical of drones; - The colors of the observed lights, white, red, and green, correspond to those usually used by drones and in aviation navigation. Furthermore, the witness could not hear any noise due to the significant distance between the witness and the drone, and the moderate noise emitted by the engine of this type of device. Also, due to the observation distance, as well as its small size, the shape of the drone could not be observed. We note that although the owner of this drone equipped his device with regulated lighting and respects the ban on flying his drone at night, the altitude reached does not comply with this regulation, which sets a maximum allowed altitude of 150 meters. We can also question the interest of flying a drone under such unfavorable weather conditions, risking damage to it. Some drone owners, in addition to not respecting the regulations, take sometimes reckless risks (possibility of material, or even bodily damage), unaware of the danger. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a recreational drone.
