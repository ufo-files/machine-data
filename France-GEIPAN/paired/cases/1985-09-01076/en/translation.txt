Summary: Observations of the silent passage of a luminous object in the sky; observation of a glow in a basin of the E.Herriot port in Lyon: probable observations of a meteoroid reentry.
--- seg-469134e9571e0cddc6c4fed2 [needs-review] ---
**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed to ensure more relevant conclusions. This reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named Lyon (69) 1985 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been re-examined. On September 3, 1985, four witnesses in Lyon (two witnesses) and also in Villefranche-sur-Saône (two witnesses) were surprised by the rapid passage of a luminous phenomenon in the sky, and for some, by the presence in the water of a basin with a particular glow. The description of PAN by various witnesses is typical of a meteoroid re-entry:

- Observation duration of a few seconds, high speed of movement,
- Glow and halo ("fluorescent green color (a bit like Bengal fires), surrounded by a white veil", "a pale bright green ball, surrounded by a dark green halo"),
- Temporal conjunction of nearly identical observations by distant witnesses (in this case, Villefranche-sur-Saône and Lyon),
- Consistent trajectory indications.

To confirm this hypothesis, it is necessary to explain some peculiar elements of this re-entry:

- The meteorite falls into the water and creates a glow from the water for several minutes: there are three PAN witnesses in the sky (T2, a security agent in Lyon, T3 and T4 in Villefranche-sur-Saône). None of them saw it fall into the water, but for T2 in Lyon, there is a match between the observed trajectory of PAN in the sky and the glow in the water observed by T2 and T1 (T1, a truck driver, did not see PAN in the sky). It can be assumed that the meteorite did indeed fall into the water. One should not attach importance to T1 and T2's estimation of the size of the glow in the water as "30 meters" (such a meteorite size would have caused a tsunami!). This estimation refers to the glow rising to the water's surface and cannot apply to the glow's source. Witness T1 also stated, "I saw a fairly large glow, yellowish-white color, similar to a diver illuminating from inside the water." while T2 estimates the size of the glow's source as a soccer ball.

- Firefighters recorded a slight radioactivity qualified as "negligible," both out of and in the water: this element did not lead to further investigations. Without a record of the time and spatial presence of this low radioactivity, it is not possible to assure it was caused by the meteorite. Even if it were, this would not rule out the meteoroid hypothesis.

- T1 noted this oddity: "The security vehicle's light was flickering." The gendarmerie report mentions this as observed by three witnesses (T1, T2, and another security agent colleague of T2 cited in the report but who did not testify) for 4 seconds during the fall. Except for this, it was only formally reported (deposition report) by T1 (T2, who used the vehicle, did not mention it in his deposition) and T1 observed it upon arriving at the scene after the fall and does not specify having seen the beginning and end of this flickering. It is common in situations of alert or emotion that a secondary anomaly is detected or associated with another main anomaly that, without the latter and the tension it creates, might have gone unnoticed or been downplayed (in this case, the witness, who is not a security agent but a truck driver, was indeed in a state of alert as he was sent to the scene by the security manager where security agents were already reporting by radio something unidentified. T1 also stated that he took a wrong turn to get there).

Consequently, with the conformity with a meteoroid re-entry confirmed and the peculiar elements of the testimonies explained, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable meteoroid re-entry.
