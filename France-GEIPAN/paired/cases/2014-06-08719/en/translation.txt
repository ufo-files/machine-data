Description: On June 7, 2014, between 2 a.m. and 4 a.m., an individual was surprised and intrigued by the observation of luminous phenomena in the night sky. The observation is divided into three phases:

- Just after 2:30 a.m.: Three objects moving from south to north; according to Calsky (see log), several satellite candidates at this time are IGS02, Cosmos 1782 Rocket, and NOS 3-6 rocket.

- Two flashing objects moving from south to northwest, and from north to south; according to Calsky, several satellites could have followed this trajectory. The flashing can be explained by the fact that some satellites are rotating on themselves and irregularly reflect sunlight. It could also be aircraft, as their anti-collision lights flash once per second.

- A "gigantic" light observed after 3:15 a.m. to the west and disappearing towards the south. Calsky indicates that the International Space Station (ISS) passes that day, visible from 3:16 a.m. until its rise to the west (WNW) until 3:22 a.m. when it disappears towards the south (SE). However, it is surprising that the witness uses the word "gigantic" for a light only as bright as Venus: the fact that the observation took place in the middle of the night might explain this. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of satellites and the International Space Station (ISS). The middle of the night is conducive to over-interpreting celestial phenomena, especially for emotional individuals, which seems to be the case for this witness.
