Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type "C" cases, which are now being re-examined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as "C" in 1993 by SEPRA.

During the night of March 1 to 2, 1993, between 1:15 and 1:20 a.m., a witness observed from their room a luminous phenomenon that they described as being large in size and resembling "two tears upside down." This case is consistent (one witness, but with a very well-detailed testimony), and the description of the PAN observed is very precise. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the Moon, whose light was filtered through a cloudy cover that partially hid it, giving it strange and variable shapes. Furthermore, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, while the witness makes no mention of it.

In the current classification of GEIPAN, this case of low strangeness is classified as PAN A, observation of the Moon through light clouds.
