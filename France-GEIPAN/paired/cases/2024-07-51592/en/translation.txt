Summary: Nighttime observation of vertical movements of white lights at the top of a hill: lights induced by nighttime maintenance activities on an antenna infrastructure (nighttime painting job on a pole to limit the risk of service interruption).

Description: On July 10, 2024, between 1:00 AM and 4:00 AM (for T1) and between 2:30 AM and 4:00 AM (for T2), from their home in Jézeau (Hautes-Pyrénées, 65), two witnesses (T1, mother, and T2, son) observed a PAN, in the form of a very bright white ball, moving near a relay antenna located at the top of a hill facing the witnesses, in the south-southwest direction. Initially observed from inside the house, then from a balcony, the PAN made irregular movements over a short horizontal distance, with occasional instances of doubling. Small white glowing balls sometimes escaped vertically towards the ground before ascending again. No noise was perceived. T1 recorded a video of several minutes. At 4:00 AM, the witnesses stopped observing, as the PAN was still visible. The case consistency is deemed satisfactory, although only one of the two witnesses (T1) reported their observation. The field investigation helped refine the understanding of the observation despite the low exploitable value of the video of the unidentified aerial phenomenon (PAN). The strangeness of the phenomenon for T1 mainly resides in the behavior of the PAN. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Several hypotheses were explored (see investigation report). One suggests it could be the observation of an aircraft, such as a helicopter or drone in flight. Another hypothesis envisages the presence of an object on the ground in the mountains, whose presence could be linked to activities in the Saint-Lary-Soulan (Hautes-Pyrénées, 65) ski area. Finally, a last possibility is night-time maintenance activity on a TDF antenna infrastructure, an operator of digital infrastructure and networks. This last hypothesis proved to be correct and was confirmed by TDF. The on-site investigation revealed fresh paint traces on the structure, confirming a recent maintenance operation. This nighttime intervention, intended to limit the risks of service disruption, actually took place on the night of the observation. A maintenance team was present on site to carry out a painting job on a pylon. The various observed movements likely correspond to the technicians' movements equipped with lamps, going up and down along the structure. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": lights induced by night-time maintenance activities on an antenna infrastructure.
