Description: Recent testimony (February 2016) of an old observation. On December 21, 2013, between 4:29 and 4:33 PM, a witness photographed a luminous phenomenon in a clear sky that they could not explain. No other testimony was collected. The observed phenomenon has all the characteristics of a parhelion, part of a solar halo: Parhelion . Parhelion . Météfrance (See photo 6). Since the provided photos are not the original files, the GEIPAN was only able to verify that the angle between the sun and the phenomenon is exactly 22°, but the appearance of the phenomenon, to be compared to the images from the above websites, leaves no doubt about its origin. At the time of observation, the sun is at an elevation of 6°30, which is consistent with this 22° separation between the sun and the phenomenon (see photos). The weather report from Toulouse Blagnac Météociel indicates a south wind accompanied by a sharp drop in pressure, thus announcing the arrival of a disturbance, accompanied by a cloud cover of cirrus or cirro-stratus clouds at very high altitude, therefore very cold, conducive to the parhelion phenomenon which occurs thanks to the ice crystals of this type of cloud. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A" for the observation of a characteristic parhelion.
