Description: On June 16, 2019 (not June 15, as incorrectly indicated in the questionnaire) between 12:15 AM and 12:30 AM, a witness observed the successive, 5-minute intervals of three identical red-orange light phenomena passing quickly and silently. The witness reports, "No precise shape, rather an intense light like a red-orange fireball." He sends us a link to the video taken. Only one testimony is collected. The hypotheses of a star Capella or an airplane remain too unlikely to be considered (see the investigation report). On the other hand, considering the defined elements, such as: - the estimated speed of the PAN in motion, exceeding that of an airplane,
- the repetition of the phenomenon, namely 3 times at intervals of a few minutes,
- the description of the PAN and analysis of its characteristics (color, shape, brightness, disappearance, etc.) via the video and IPACO software. Here is the translation of the provided GEIPAN text:

It is necessary to emphasize the very rapid light fluctuation observed in the video as well as the progressive disappearance. These are very important details: the trajectory of the observed phenomenon corresponding to the direction of the wind, and remaining a key criterion of this analysis, and to conclude, the fact that this observation took place on a summer Saturday night, a time conducive to festive events, we can therefore determine that this observation most certainly concerns the observation of Thai lanterns at intervals of a few minutes. These objects were, moreover, much closer to the witness than he thought (probably due to a very cloudy sky) and thus gave him the impression of high speed. This case is to be classified as "B" for a very probable observation of Thai lanterns.
