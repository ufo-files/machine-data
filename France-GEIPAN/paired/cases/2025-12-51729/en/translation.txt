Summary: Observation from the cockpit of an aircraft of a bright point in motion, then a sudden apparent "explosion," and finally a diffuse halo gradually expanding before disappearing: observation of the second stage Centaur of the Atlas V 551 rocket observed at the time of engine cut-off and associated venting.
--- seg-9fd3cc5c4aee97d64706b5cf [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 16, 2025 at 08:50 UTC (09:50 CET), a flight crew member, from the cockpit of an aircraft flying from Los Angeles to Paris, observed a luminous phenomenon to the right of the aircraft. At that time, the aircraft is flying at FL350, over the North Atlantic, south of Greenland. The phenomenon initially manifests as a white glowing point resembling a satellite, moving on a straight and slightly descending trajectory, from an azimuth of 120° to an azimuth of 90°. After a few dozen seconds, this point abruptly transforms into a bright white light, described as a "static explosion," then into a diffused spot with pink-violet hues that gradually expands before disappearing. The total sequence lasts about three minutes. A second witness, present in the cockpit, confirms the observation. The consistency* of the case is good. The testimony comes from an experienced observer in the field of aeronautics. It was transmitted the day after the observation using a detailed technical questionnaire, then supplemented by a cognitive interview. A second direct witness is mentioned, but only one questionnaire was formalized. The file contains no instrumental recordings or reconstructions. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The strangeness reported by the witness mainly concerns the succession of three phases: an initial movement resembling that of a satellite, followed by a static "explosions" of light, then the formation of a diffuse and colored halo that gradually extends. The absence of noise, the sudden nature of this transformation, and the atypical appearance of the diffuse phase justify the perception of an abnormal phenomenon. Various investigations (detailed in the investigation report) allow the following points to be established: aeronautical data confirm that the craft was flying at night, under Visual Meteorological Conditions (VMC), south of Greenland. Astronomical analysis rules out the Moon and bright stars present in this area. If a satellite was indeed identified in the sector during this time slot, its ascending trajectory is incompatible with the observed dynamics; moreover, this hypothesis does not explain the phases of static "explosion" and colored diffusion. On the other hand, the launch of an Atlas V 551 rocket from Cape Canaveral at 08:28 UTC, which is 22 minutes before the observation, offers a coherent explanation. At 08:50 UTC, the Centaur stage reached the engine cut-off phase (MECO-1), followed by a regulatory purge. This sequence accounts for the entire phenomenon: the moving light point corresponds to the aircraft in flight, the sudden "explosion" apparent is related to the venting (degassing), and the diffuse halo results from the illumination of the gaseous cloud by the Sun, which was located at an azimuth of 92° but below the horizon by -26°. The strangeness of the case is thus greatly reduced. The spectacular nature of the phenomenon is due to the observation geometry and the illumination of the gaseous cloud by the Sun below the horizon, creating the illusion of a static explosion during a nominal stage of the Centaur stage's flight. The observation corresponds to the flight sequence of the Centaur stage of the Atlas V 551 launch vehicle, including the engine cut (MECO-1) and the associated regulatory purge. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A" observation, second-stage Centaur rocket on Atlas V 551.
