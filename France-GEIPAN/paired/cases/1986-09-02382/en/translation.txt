Description: The GEIPAN received on 23.02.2008 an email from the witness informing him of his observation of a PAN on 15.09.1986 from the commune of VELIZY-VILLACOUBLAY (78). While walking to work at 7:30 AM, the witness is surprised to see in the sky the silent movement of a dozen identical green lights moving from East to West. Only one testimony is collected. Let's translate the key points of the witness's description of the PAN:

• Green color.
• Horizontal movement from East to West.
• No noise.
• A dozen identical lights make up the PAN.
• All these lights move in sync. All these points suggest either an atmospheric reentry or a fragmented meteor (see the investigation report). A search in existing databases and on the Internet at the date and time of the observation yielded nothing regarding the two hypotheses. However, by expanding the temporal search, we find other witness accounts of identical observations conducted in Germany, France, and Benelux around 7:30 AM, but on September 23, 1986, not September 15, 1986. In the Paris region, the press reported these observations the very next day. The newspaper "Libération" of 24.09.1986, for example, mentioned the presence of approximately fifteen bright points observed in the region. After investigation by several specialists in atmospheric reentries and astronomers, it was determined that the phenomenon was a meteoroid fragment. All its characteristics correspond to the witness's description: color, horizontal movement from East to West, composition of a dozen fragments... This testimony was given 22 years after the observation. There is every reason to believe that the witness erred in the date, but the time of 7:30 was indeed memorized. This error is entirely corroborated by the witness mentioning the following press release after the observation on September 23. "He told me that this very morning, on his way to work by car, he had heard a journalist on the radio declare that he had seen something unusual in the sky over Île-de-France." In conclusion, the witness observed a meteoroid fragmenting as it entered the atmosphere. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: bolide observation.
