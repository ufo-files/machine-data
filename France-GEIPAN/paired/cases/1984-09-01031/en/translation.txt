Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case previously classified as D is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On September 3, 1984, around midnight, two to three witnesses in a car were intrigued by the presence of a very bright red-orange phenomenon at low altitude. The witnesses spotted the PAN at several points along their route between Les Fontanettes (commune of Saint-Aubin-en-Bay), Le Coudray-Saint-Germer, and the Champignolle crossroads (commune of Sérifontaine). No sound emanating from this object was heard during the intermittent observations. Overcome with fear, the witnesses turned back. One of the witnesses (T1) had already made a similar observation in a car with his mother (T4) in July 1984. This case is consistent (three witnesses), the description of the observation location and the PAN observed is precise (see investigation notes). The described phenomenon has many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, etc.) with a known astronomical phenomenon: the setting Moon. The length of the observation period (half an hour or three-quarters of an hour), as well as the fact that the PAN appears to follow the motorists (following ball), suggest the observation of an astronomical phenomenon. The aspect described by the witnesses leaves no doubt: a large and bright object, low on the horizon to the southwest, orange or red in color. A witness precision ("It was always round but there was a missing piece on the left, as if a crescent was missing") even allows us to assume that the Moon was then in a gibbous phase, between the First Quarter and Full Moon. The presence of the Moon in the observed direction is verified. The witness indications are very precise thanks to the alignment with the road axis, and this corresponds perfectly. Similarly, the Moon is well according to the expected gibbous phase. Finally, the witnesses do not mention the Moon while it was indeed present in the various observed directions. The mistake with the Moon is frequent in the GEIPAN records (see case search on the www.geipan.fr website with the keyword Moon). What may be surprising here is the apparent absence of a cloud cover or clouds that could enhance the mistake. The Moon is well described as round or almost round, it is not about these curious red shapes caused by cloud gaps in front of the Moon. We note that the witnesses are young and this may be their first experience. Nevertheless, this observation created a strong strangeness and a state of shock: "I was panicked, I was very afraid," "I told XXX, let's turn around quickly," "I was really very afraid, I had tears in my eyes" (T1), "I didn't stop, I think it's out of fear," "All three of us wondered if we should continue," "We escaped to Le Coudray," "We were all three taken by panic. XXX was crying in the car because she was very afraid" (T2), "We couldn't go on," "XXX was screaming in the car" (T3). The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of the Moon.
