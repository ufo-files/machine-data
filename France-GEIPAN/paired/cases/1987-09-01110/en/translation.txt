Summary: Child's account of observing an orange glowing disk with a noise recording.
--- seg-ea8fd5808a06b1afba4f851c [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the evening of September 7, 1987, the eldest of two children in a farming family, aged 10, went to bed, listened to a cassette on his portable radio, felt tired, turned off the device, and fell asleep. Around 5 a.m., he was awakened by a noise: the child then distinguished a light through the blinds. He got up, took his portable radio, and turned it on to record the noise while observing an orange glowing disk with a flickering light. The recording lasted about twenty seconds, and the luminous phenomenon disappeared shortly after. During the GEPAN's intervention, several reconstructions were carried out without any being truly conclusive. Samples of the birch leaves where the light appeared were taken. The GEPAN also took possession of the original cassette for expert analysis. The analysis of the cassette focused on determining how the noise was recorded before attempting to identify it. The expertise, conducted with a device identical to the one used by the child, unequivocally showed that it was an internal recording of the device and not via its microphone. Therefore, the noise has nothing to do with the observed luminous phenomenon but comes from internal reception noise, possibly shortwave, but its identification is no longer relevant for the GEPAN's investigation. It is at best a mishandling, at worst a hoax. The biochemical analysis of the birch leaves was performed and yielded mixed results, one sample showing deviations in certain characteristics from the others that could potentially be explained by thermal or light stress, although no leaf deterioration appeared. These results are not truly compelling, as the analyses were not entirely consistent based on the sought-after parameters. The witness, a pre-adolescent at the time of the events, later admitted that he had fabricated this event to draw his parents' attention to him during a difficult period in his childhood. Subsequently, caught up in the events, he was unable to take back his words. Initially classified as category D, this case is reclassified as A: a perfectly explained observation.
