Description: On November 20, 2014, at 21:29, an amateur astronomer was surprised to observe through the ocular of his telescope a small, fuzzy, bright, warm-toned sphere. Of average strangeness and good consistency (unique witness, but with a very precise testimony), this case turns out to be the observation of the satellite Cosmos 1636, likely surrounded by a gas cloud from the remnants of its ergol tanks, released due to an undetermined incident. The trajectory of the PAN is indeed perfectly consistent with that of the Cosmos 1636 satellite, and its movement is quite characteristic of a satellite's movement. The hypothesis, however, cannot be confirmed without another witness for cross-referencing. It is not the visual perception of the witness that is in question, but the interpretation the witness makes of his observation through his feeling (low luminosity of the PAN, observation of a rare phenomenon). This case is classified B, probable observation of the satellite Cosmos 1636 surrounded by a gas cloud.
