Description: On Sunday, August 3, 2014, at 1 a.m., a person is startled to see through a window the silent passage across the sky of a fireball. Its trajectory is straight. Observation of the phenomenon is very brief. No other testimony will be collected. The observation for one minute of a fireball slowly receding into the sky on a weekend night suggests a Thai lantern likely released after a party. In this case, the proximity of a hall where a wedding was being celebrated makes this hypothesis highly probable. The witness sees the object take off in the direction of Saint-Etienne de Crossey, towards the northeast. A weather check on the Grenoble-Le-Versoud and Grenoble-Saint-Geoirs weather stations indicates a weak, very variable wind, therefore undeterminable in this valley area. Despite this entirely plausible hypothesis of a Thai lantern, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "C": no other witness, local wind undeterminable, due to lack of corroboration.
