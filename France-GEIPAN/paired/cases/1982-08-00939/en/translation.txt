Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MONT-SAINT-AIGNAN (76) 06.08.1982 (also known as ROUEN-SAPINS (76) 1982) is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. A witness at the corner of CD43 and Rue des Mouettes in MONT-SAINT-AIGNAN (76) observed on August 6, 1982, at 8:05 PM, the silent, S-N movement of a gray, metallic, oval object at 20 meters from the ground. The sun occasionally reflects off its rays. The witness sees on one side a geometric figure of red color resembling an "eye." The witness turns around upon noticing another witness already present, but when he looks back towards the object, it has disappeared. The witness provides a statement to the gendarmerie on August 11, unable to give a more accurate estimate of the distances. On August 12, gendarmes visit the site; no other testimony is collected, and no trace is visible. The physical characteristics (metallic appearance reflecting sunlight, oval shape, presence of a fanciful element) and dynamic characteristics (regular movement in the direction of the wind) are entirely consistent with the hypothesis of confusion with a metallic nylon balloon, type "Mylar" (see the investigation report). Such balloons already existed in 1982 and were often circular or oval in shape, adorned with a fanciful design that could be very variable and itself circular. It is a Friday evening during the summer, which is conducive to a festive or recreational event of this type. It is possible that such a fanciful design is predominantly red, and when observed from a distance illuminated by the setting sun, appears to the witness as a glowing red eye. Although the terminology used by the witness is not very explicit on this point ("... I specify that at one point I turned around to look at this person, and it was when looking again in the direction of the object that it disappeared."), it is possible to assume that the disappearance of PAN occurred as the witness turned his gaze to see the other person observing PAN. We do not know how long the witness turned around, perhaps long enough for the witness to be unable to find PAN in his field of vision or for it to have disappeared. The consistency is rather weak, with few elements allowing to rule out (unknown duration of witness's turn) or firmly confirm an hypothesis that, in its current state, is nevertheless sufficiently probable to be retained. During the observation and initial investigation, GEIPAN had (or little) experience of misunderstandings caused by Mylar balloons (see on the website www.geipan.fr the cases recorded since, search for cases with keywords "Mylar" or "balloon"). GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a Mylar balloon.
