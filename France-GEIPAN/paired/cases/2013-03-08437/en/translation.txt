Summary: Observation of the silent movement of a bright red light phenomenon in the sky: probable observation of the passage of a Thai lantern.
--- seg-efc0faad738166a4783e98de [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Sunday, March 10, 2013 at 12:30 AM, a witness is intrigued by a bright red light moving slowly above Fort l'Infernet, without any particular noise being heard. The phenomenon disappears as it moves towards the East, where the witness identifies a very bright star. No other reports of this phenomenon were collected. Astronomical verification shows that it is likely the star Vega that the witness, looking towards the East, saw rising in the northeast. Meteociel service does not indicate any weather records for that evening. Bourg St Maurice's weather report shows a very unstable, weak wind—this could suggest similar weather conditions in Briançon. The detailed and precise description of the phenomenon perfectly matches the appearance of a Thai lantern. The bright yellow part corresponds to the paraffin burner. This burner burns for 3 to 10 minutes, exceptionally 20. The time and day of the observation (from Saturday night to Sunday) are common for this type of observation. The object's movement does not conform to the felt wind: this can easily be explained by the fact that ground and low-altitude winds can be different, especially with weak winds and in mountain valleys. The temporary disappearance of the object is likely due to the lantern passing behind a building or pole, and its final disappearance due to the burner's extinction. This case is classified as "B": probable observation of a Thai lantern.
