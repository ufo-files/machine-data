Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named ROYAL CORME (17) 1996 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined (see the investigation report). On September 16, 1996, around 5:00 a.m., a witness in a car is on his way to work. On his route (observation point 0), he suddenly notices a rather large and bright point. A few kilometers further (observation point 1), he observes on his left side a stationary oval-shaped craft with three square portholes emitting a strong orange light. The witness continues on his way, and near Saintes (observation point 2), he observes in the sky two identical forms, as he did in his second observation. Only one testimony was collected. The very first part of the observation (observation point 0), which the witness describes as: "I first noticed a rather large point in the sky, emitting a bright light, while I was driving towards Nancras. I was intrigued by its strong brightness and its completely unusual size," the witness likely observed the planet Venus rising in the east-northeast, opposite his direction of travel. The gendarmes mention in the protocol the presence of a radio relay with three orange lights (the witness described three portholes), there is also another antenna. Both are visible from observation point 2, but not from observation point 1, even though the witness seems to have seen the same characteristics. This does not seem to be a possible explanation. For observation point 1, there is no explanatory hypothesis. The description of PAN by the witness, its apparent immobility, do not allow for any explanatory hypothesis to be made. On the other hand, the data collected during the witness's hearing are not sufficient in terms of quality and reliability. A reconstruction on the spot, at night, would have allowed for angle measurements (position and angular width) to question the witness more thoroughly and precisely, and to further explore and perhaps establish other hypotheses. More than 20 years later, it seems illusory to restart such an investigation. In light of the current classification methodology A/B/C/D1/D2 (see the classification methodology explanations in GEIPAN), this case would be of a D1 strangeness level, but its consistency is too low to validate the unexplained character of the observation. Consequently, the case is classified as "C" due to a lack of reliable information.
