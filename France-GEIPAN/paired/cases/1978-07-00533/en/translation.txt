Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named CASTRES (81) 30.07.1978 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed.

During the night of 29 to 30 July 1978, around 3:45 a.m., a motorcycle rider (T1) returning from a party in CASTANET-LE-HAUT (34) spotted a large orange glow in the sky while located at a place called "Le Devès" in the commune of MURAT-SUR-VEBRE. The PAN was also observed by another witness (T2) from the same location. The observation lasted for five minutes, after which the PAN disappeared at a moderate speed. A gendarmerie patrol became aware of the case on 1 August 1978. Both witnesses were immediately questioned. The on-site investigation did not yield any additional witnesses or ground traces. The direction indicated by both witnesses corresponds precisely (within one or two degrees) to that of the Moon (see investigation notes). The red Moon at sunrise or sunset is often the source of misunderstandings for witnesses (see the GEIPAN annals on www.geipan.fr, search for cases with Moon phenomenon). Generally, it is the presence of clouds in front of the Moon that creates misunderstandings, obscuring it enough that it cannot be recognized but still allowing its glow to appear through gaps that can take on various forms, some of which are strange. The complex shape indicated by witness T1 (disk and domes) could be one of these. The available weather information 40 years later does not confirm or deny such a cloud configuration. The swinging movements of the PAN can be explained by a perception error related to the movements of cloud shapes in front of and around the Moon. The final movement of the PAN to the right observed by T1 required a contact with the witness. T1 spontaneously mentions what he finds most strange: "it disappeared suddenly" and when asked where? He responds, "forward," "in front of me." He remembers a distance escape. When reminded of his testimony about the movement to the right, he says there was a little bit of that, but it was mostly forward. T1 thus fell victim to the classic perception error consisting of interpreting an intensity change of the PAN (here its decrease due to the closing of the cloud veil) as a distance change of the PAN, in this case, a distance escape. This point once again illustrates that witness expressions should not be taken literally as they also contain interpretations. The very strong consistency between the true position of the Moon and the position of the PAN leaves no doubt about the misunderstanding. This case is consistent (two independent witnesses), and the description of the PAN observed is fairly precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is at issue, but the interpretation they make of their observation in the face of the unrecognized phenomenon. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of the red Moon.
