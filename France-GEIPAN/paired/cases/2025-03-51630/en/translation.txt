**Case Description:**

On March 6, 2025, around 1 a.m., a witness located in Vicq (52) took their dog out onto their terrace when they observed two bright white lights relatively low in the sky, just above other buildings. They immediately went inside to retrieve their smartphone and took two photographs: one without zoom and another with digital zoom. Approximately fifteen minutes later, they attempted a new observation from their house's upstairs level, but the phenomena had disappeared. The consistency of this case is good, despite relying on a single testimony, which is supported by two photographs used in the investigation.

According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Analysis of the photographs provided by the witness was conducted using the IPACO expertise software, which is used by GEIPAN for image processing and interpretation. The analysis concludes that the two observed light phenomena are very likely the Moon and the planet Jupiter, then in their setting phase. This identification is based on several consistent elements:

- The color, white, matches both celestial bodies.
- The shape of the Moon could not be distinctly seen by the witness, as it was partially obscured by trees.
- The witness's description of a small, "lying in a V" light shape is likely a posteriori interpretation of the photographs, where such a shape can appear due to a slight movement of the camera during capture. This shape is not identical between the two images.
- Since both celestial bodies are in their setting phase, their very slow apparent motion is not perceptible over a short period, which is consistent with the observation conditions.
- The perceived movement of the smaller PAN (Jupiter) relative to the larger (Moon) between the two images can be explained by a simple change in the witness's position, creating a parallax effect.
- The planisphere tool integrated into IPACO confirms that the astronomical position of the Moon and Jupiter at the time of the captures (01:01 and 01:02) precisely corresponds to the apparent location of the two PANs on the photographs (see Annexes 1-3).
- At the indicated time, both celestial bodies are still visible above the horizon but close to being occulted by nearby landscape elements. Fifteen minutes later, their visual disappearance is consistent with their position being obscured by trees and surrounding rooftops.

Finally, the witness mentions, uncertainly, a third object higher in the sky, visible only on the non-zoomed photograph. IPACO analysis very likely identifies this object as the first-magnitude star Capella. The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as A: observation of the Moon and Jupiter.
