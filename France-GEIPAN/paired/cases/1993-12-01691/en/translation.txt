Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named PRIVAS (07) 1993 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On December 11, 1993, around 8:50 PM, a driver was intrigued by a very bright light that was spinning in the sky and then disappeared. A little further along, the light reappeared, and both passengers in the vehicle also observed the phenomenon. Upon arriving at a parking lot, the witness pointed out the PAN to another driver. The witness reported that for approximately twenty minutes, the light spun around itself silently. Several other lights (between 6 and 10) were noticed below. The PAN appeared and disappeared, moving forward while spinning in all directions. Only one witness reported their observation to the Gendarmerie. The description has characteristics similar to a laser light show from the ground that reflects in the atmosphere (due to water droplets or a cloud layer):

- Sky patterns with one or more bright points or segments, in one or several colors, according to a fixed or most often variable geometry.
- Rotation, scanning, or pivoting of the patterns, with the possibility of large and rapid excursions in the sky.
- Depending on atmospheric conditions, visible beams rising from the ground (in this case, only the rising beams were sometimes visible without projecting patterns in the sky).

Today, laser light shows are frequent and easily recognizable in the testimonies received by the GEIPAN, but they were rarer in 1993. It is not possible today to investigate the origin of this light show at the site or in its proximity (near Avignon). However, we have an observation from that same year, by chance on the same day but in a completely different location, where the investigation allowed the light show to be found and highlights that equipment producing such effects was sold to discothèques in France (see the Gendarmerie report for the observation case ILE BOUCHARD (37) 11.12.1993). The GEIPAN classifies the observation case as B: probable (and not certain, as it was not possible to identify the installation or verify the compatibility of the meteorological conditions) laser or "Sky Rose" observation.
