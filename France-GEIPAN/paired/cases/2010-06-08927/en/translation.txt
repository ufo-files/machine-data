Summary: Pilot observed a very bright point moving in a straight line to the south: no smoke or fuel trail observed; observation of the International Space Station (ISS).

Description: On June 27, 2010, at 6:05 PM, a pilot and his copilot, flying from Nouméa to Seoul on an Airbus 330 at 40,000 feet (12,500 meters), spotted a very bright "1 o'clock" light moving quickly along a straight trajectory headed southeast. No smoke or combustion traces were observed. The speed was estimated at Mach 6 at an altitude of at least 80,000 feet (around 25,000 meters). As the light approached its 3 o'clock position (90° to the right), the pilot made contact with air traffic control in the area based in Guam, without providing a precise position or heading. The controller, initially surprised, responded in the negative before contacting a minute later an American Airlines flight in the sector to ask for confirmation of any possible unreported air traffic. The American Airlines flight crew confirmed the observation. The observation ended after 5 minutes when the light passed into the blind spot of the ongoing flight's crew. Only one testimony was collected. This observation has all the characteristics of a satellite observation, and the most probable hypothesis is the observation of the International Space Station (ISS). The sky position, date, time, and duration of the observation are consistent with the proposed hypothesis. As always in such cases, the distance and speed estimates made by the witness, even an experienced pilot, cannot be accurate. Without references, it is absolutely impossible for a witness to estimate the distance, size, and speed of an object they are unfamiliar with. Only the angular velocity of the phenomenon's movement should be considered precisely. Since the ISS is much brighter than other satellites, the crews would have perceived it as closer and at a lower altitude. The sky position, date, time, and duration of the observation were accurately taken into account and are consistent with the proposed hypothesis (see the detailed investigation report). The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the International Space Station (ISS).
