Description: On Sunday, May 17, 2020, at 1:00 AM, a witness observed for 10 seconds an object with a very unusual appearance, in the shape of a triangle, flying at a constant speed with a face parallel to the ground and featuring 3 white glowing points at each of its angles. Its color seemed to blend or slightly distort the appearance of the sky in the background. Only one testimony was collected. The witness very likely saw 9 Thai lanterns. The day and period are compatible criteria: Saturday night to Sunday morning in the spring are typical and conducive to outdoor lantern releases as part of local or private celebrations (weddings, baptisms, etc.). Similarly, the point-like shape of each light described by the witness is entirely plausible, the distance of the lanterns preventing their shape from being distinguished. In addition, the weather station at Château d'Olonne, located 8 km from the observation site, reports a wind of 8 km/h from the north to the south at the time of observation. The speed and wind direction are also compatible with a release of lanterns. The brightness of the points is also compatible with this hypothesis. Indeed, the witness states "the brightness of a medium-sized star in the sky," which is quite comparable to the apparent brightness of lanterns. The white color of the bright points is not the most common, but it is frequently encountered in established cases of lanterns. The absence of noise and the number of bright points are also consistent with this hypothesis. The perceived strangeness by the witness resides in the triangular shape observed. When an observer sees a set of glowing points, it is common for them to interpret this set of points as a single object bounded by these glowing points. This illusion, named "form or contour illusion," occurs with independent lights, typical of a lantern release. It can also occur with an airplane when the wings are poorly visible: the witness then reconstructs a limited black volume bounded by the visible lights. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
