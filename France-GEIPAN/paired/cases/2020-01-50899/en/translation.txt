Summary: Multiple observations in the same night of a white light moving in the sky: misidentification with the observation of a laser ("skytracker") on clouds.
--- seg-12d5f9a326f34f3da1dfa271 [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 11, 2020, starting at midnight thirty minutes, a witness observes, in a clear sky, a phenomenon that intrigues them. For 40 minutes, they witness the successive appearance of "a white light" resembling a large white tube, moving rapidly on a curved trajectory. The witness does not see any laser beam on the ground and questions the phenomenon, which seems to occur over the countryside of Verlinghem (59) or Prémesques (59). The tired witness goes to bed, having filmed the PAN with their smartphone. Only one testimony is collected. Many elements remain consistent with the hypothesis of a confusion with a laser ("skytracker") used during the night from Friday to Saturday (see investigation report), likely by the disco "Le Manoir", located in the commune of Bailleul (59):

- The elongated shape and diffuse appearance of the PAN remain typical of a cloud impact spot. A perspective effect is present and produces this elongated shape, the beam being round at its exit from the projector.
- The white color, commonly used by projectors.
- The beam itself is not visible to the witness with the naked eye nor on the video as is. With excellent horizontal visibility, very few, if any, particles capable of supporting the light emitted by the projector were present in suspension in the atmosphere. However, we were able to highlight, after enhancement, the presence of such a beam in the video, which was indeed present but too distant and/or not bright enough to be directly visible to the witness and on the video as is.
- The rapid, regular movements typical of an automated system. Some passages are not visible, the projector being aimed further away or outside the cloud base, and some are closer, as at the beginning of the observation where the witness describes the PAN as being above their position.
- The observation day is favorable to the hypothesis: the weekend is indeed conducive to the use of such projectors in a festive context, such as for advertising purposes for a particular event in a disco.
- The range of skytrackers can reach, or even exceed, 20 km, which aligns well with the hypothesis of use by a disco located within these limits and along the observation axis. However, we were unable to confirm the actual use of such a skytracker by the disco in question on the night of the observation. Although the investigator was unable to demonstrate the use of such a projector on the evening of the observation, the explanatory hypothesis remains very probable in light of the other testimonial elements. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: very high probability of a misperception with the observation of a laser ("skytracker") on the clouds.
