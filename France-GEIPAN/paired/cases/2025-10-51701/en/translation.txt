Description: On Tuesday, October 3, 2025, around 6:56 PM, the witness was walking on La Saline beach, Rue des Mouettes, in Saint-Paul (Réunion Island). The sky is clear above the sea, with some clouds present on the land side. The environment is calm: no notable noise, traffic, or airplane engine sounds. It was then that he noticed the presence in the sky of a unique luminous phenomenon, round in shape, white in color, with a constant and particularly intense brightness. A glowing halo surrounds the sphere. No noise was perceived during the entire observation. The object moves slowly from the sea towards land, appearing to pass over the habitats, without emitting any sound. Its intense brightness remains constant throughout its trajectory. The observation lasted approximately four minutes. The phenomenon continues its trajectory until it disappears behind a cloud formation, where its light remains perceptible for a few more moments before completely fading out. During the observation, the witness photographed and filmed the PAN using an iPhone 11 Pro. The consistency of this case, in terms of GEIPAN* criteria, is good, despite a single witness. This is widely corroborated by several photos and a video of the PAN. One of these photographs was analyzed for the investigation. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The only hypothesis explored, that of the observation of the Starlink-3084 satellite, is corroborated by the following elements: - Verified presence on the inthesky.org site, in the sky of Réunion between 6:55 and 7:00 PM (18h55 et 19h00) of the Starlink-3084 satellite (annex 1) illuminated by the setting Sun for the observer. This event was particularly intense as the satellite was in the process of re-entering the atmosphere with an orbit at ~170 to 220 km (annex 4), while the operational STARLINK® orbit is at ~550 km. - The position was confirmed through the processing of a photograph transmitted by the witness, using the photo analysis software GEIPAN, IPACO* (annex 2), compared to available data on trajectory modeling via intersky.org (annex 3). The sky map time indicated in inthesky for the position is 18:58, approximately two minutes earlier than the photograph time, a difference explained when comparing a modeled trajectory with reality on an object in atmospheric re-entry. - The straight "ascending" trajectory, perceived from west to east, is consistent with the satellite's movement direction (from south southwest to north northeast). - The point light, white, very bright is the result of the sun's strong glare on this satellite. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies this case as 'A': observation of a Starlink® satellite in orbital decay, en route to atmospheric reentry.
