Summary: Multiple observations in France of a bright phenomenon passing through the night sky: observation of the atmospheric reentry of a third stage of a PROTON launch vehicle.
--- seg-9a7a6f61b652240b4823b6e0 [needs-review] ---
Description: On the evening of September 25, 2008, around 11 PM, numerous witnesses in southern Brittany or southern France observed a spectacular light phenomenon crossing the sky from northeast to southwest. Many reported their observations to the GEIPAN via phone (5), email (5), or questionnaire (3); some even gave depositions to the gendarmerie, which forwarded 3 testimonies to the GEIPAN. Multiple witnesses described a very powerful light phenomenon that traversed the sky in 1 to 4 minutes (mostly) heading south or southwest (as detailed in the investigation report). We have compiled the essential elements of the observations on a single chart (attached). A great variability is evident in these elements, regarding the color of the phenomenon (white, yellow, orange, red, etc.), its shape (ball, point, rocket ship, even a rectangle), estimated distance (300m to 8km), direction of movement (mostly north to south, or northwest to southeast, west to east), and observation duration (primarily less than 1 minute, with a few cases over 2 minutes). Nevertheless, the simultaneous occurrence of the phenomenon and some common characteristics, such as fragmentation into over 10 elements, leave little doubt about the uniqueness of its origin (except for the case in Pontoise, possibly). Experience shows that such variability is common in all mass observations; it is also important to consider that witnesses, scattered and far apart, did not observe the phenomenon from the same angle or for the same duration, which explains the variability in their accounts. In this type of case, the multiplicity of witnesses at the same time and their significant geographical separation prove that the phenomenon was very powerful and at a high altitude: the hypothesis of atmospheric reentry becomes certain. Proving the nature of the reentering object is more challenging: in this instance, given the phenomenon's orientation towards southeast and its duration of less than a minute, the hypothesis of atmospheric reentry of a satellite debris is much more probable than that of a natural meteoroid reentry. American space surveillance information confirmed this hypothesis. This case is therefore classified as "A": atmospheric reentry of a third stage of the PROTON launch vehicle.
