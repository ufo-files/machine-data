Summary: Observations of a glowing disk in the sky that seems to follow the observers: observations of the Moon.
--- seg-ce7b311dcaf4bdb768e5a38e [needs-review] ---
Description: On November 12, 1986, starting at 2:45 a.m., several people in Vendin-Le-Viel (62) and Henin-Beaumont (62) witnessed a puzzling light phenomenon in the sky. This observation of a glowing disk that seemed to follow the observers is a well-known type to UFO researchers: a misidentification with a star, in this case, the Moon. It was noted that this was the initial interpretation of one of the witnesses: "My colleague told me, 'The moon is very low today.'" This interpretation was relevant but contested by his colleague: "So I said to him, 'No, it's not the moon.'" To verify the hypothesis of a Moon, one must ensure that the direction of observation aligns with the Moon's actual position in the sky. The direction indicated by the witnesses is poorly described in the hearing report as they do not specify which side of the road they were observing; however, a precise detail is given: "Axis of the road from Henin-Beaumont to Sallaumines," which means directly west (Azimuth 270°). The sky map shows the Moon at 261° at 2:45 a.m., at an elevation of 3°30, very close to the observation axis described. Later in the testimony, it is noted that the witnesses did not recognize the Moon, despite its presence in their field of view: "One has to say it: there was no moon." The witnesses' drawings accurately represent the Moon, in its gibbous phase, temporarily and partially obscured by clouds. The description of the phenomenon's movement, slowly descending at an oblique angle, perfectly matches a moonset. The witness reports its disappearance at 3:07 a.m., while the actual moonset is at 3:11 a.m. (center of the star at the horizon, in absolutely clear terrain), which is entirely consistent. This typical case of a Moon misidentification observed while driving is classified "A" as perfectly identified.
