Description: On Saturday, August 8, 2015, at 2 a.m., an individual, from their home, observed the silent passage of approximately fifty small, milky-white (then gray-bronze) discs in the sky. The phenomenon was observed with the naked eye and then with binoculars for 8 to 10 minutes; as the discs moved very slowly and wavered on a straight trajectory, some gradually detached from the front and back of the group. The entire group slowly disappeared from the witness's field of vision. No other testimonies were collected. The observation of approximately fifty glowing discs crossing the sky in a few minutes, on a weekend night, strongly suggests festive, wind-carried luminous objects: light balloons or Thai lanterns, likely released nearby after a private party, birthday, or other event. The white then gray color suggests light balloons, illuminated by LEDs, and filled with helium, similar to: light balloons. Unlike Thai lanterns, these light balloons do not pose a risk of causing fires. The movement was observed from northeast to north, which is consistent with the weak north-northeast wind recorded in Cannes Mandelieu. As the light breeze is almost facing the witness, the flying objects appear to be moving very slowly (refer to the METEOCIEL record). The GEIPAN did not receive any other testimonies of this phenomenon. Investigating to find the perpetrators of the release is extremely difficult in an urbanized environment. GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of light balloons.
