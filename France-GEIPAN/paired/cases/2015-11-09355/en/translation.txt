Summary: Long night observation of a colored, flickering, and stationary light phenomenon in an overcast sky: probable observation of the star Sirius.
--- seg-6682c23586c333477a17585e [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On November 7, 2015, at 12:20 AM, a witness outside their home is intrigued by the presence in the overcast sky of a stationary, brightly glowing, colored phenomenon. The phenomenon has a high luminosity and appears to flicker. The witness then notices that their nearby electric meter emits a stronger hum when the phenomenon is visible. The observation lasts for 20 minutes but is discontinuous. Only one testimony is collected. Based on the witness's indications and the significant duration of the observation (20 minutes), the hypothesis of an astronomical misperception is considered (see weather report and astronomical chart in the investigation document). Sirius is located at the azimuth given by the witness (SE) and at an elevation between 5 and 10° (see reconstruction). The fact that the witness was able to estimate a higher elevation (20-30°) does not constitute a true discrepancy (deviation within the usual tolerance when the witness does not refer to a specific measurement technique). The intermittent observation may result from the significant cloud cover, which could be non-continuous that evening. Similarly, the green and red light could be due to atmospheric scintillation of Sirius at low elevation. The increased noise from the nearby electric meter, synchronized with the observation, may be related to the tension or surprise created by the observation on the witness ("I've never seen anything like it," strange thing). The experience indeed indicates that the perception of noise can be altered (amplified or diminished) depending on the tension of the visual observation. The GEIPAN considers it is very likely an observation of SIRIUS through an overcast sky. Consequently, this testimony is classified as B: probable observation of the star Sirius.
