--- seg-7241467edbbe36ff441ed6a4 [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 21, 2012, a driver and his passenger were traveling on the A13 highway towards Caen. Between 4:30 p.m. and 5:15 p.m., they observed a silent, silver, shiny, cylindrical object in the sky, initially stationary and then moving very quickly on an east-west trajectory. The object disappeared, leaving behind a trail of cloud. Only one testimony was received at GEIPAN. Several hypotheses were considered for this observation: a cylindrical airplane or hot air balloon (gray or transparent) that temporarily reflected the sun. GEIPAN requested radar traces from the Air Force to verify the hypotheses, but only one trace was recorded in the area, which was of poor quality and did not match the testimony. Furthermore, the witness initially stated in the questionnaire that the object "came from the east, going west," but later, in email exchanges on March 22, 2012, he said it came from the northwest. The exact time of the phenomenon is known only within a half-hour margin, which is significant when correlating with airplane passages. The observation location during the highway trip is also imprecisely reported. Observed airplanes do not appear on radar, and the single radar trace produced is unusable, leaving no solid elements to confirm or refute any hypothesis. GEIPAN classifies this case as "C": no decision due to lack of reliable information.
