Description: On March 26, 2026, at 21:47 UTC, the pilot of a Gulfstream G650 (private jet) reported a discontinuous observation of multiple intense white lights evolving in formation at an altitude higher than that of the aircraft. He described lights with unpredictable trajectories and extremely abrupt height changes, far exceeding the capabilities of current top-tier fighter jets. The event began at the Afghanistan-Pakistan border and continued intermittently until India. Despite proximity estimated at around 18 km by the pilot, onboard radars detected nothing and no collision alert systems were triggered. With low strangeness and good consistency (2 witnesses, but a single, unique testimony with exploitable PAN photos), this case turned out to be an observation of Starlink® flares (as detailed in the investigation report). *CONSISTENCY: According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. If the description of the PAN by T1 suggests the observation of military aircraft overflying a particularly tense geopolitical area (war in Iran, Pakistan-Afghanistan conflict, proximity to Kashmir), the investigation crosses this interpretation with the hypothesis of an observation of Starlink® flares. The images provided by the witness match the reported characteristics: the conditions for the appearance and disappearance of the PAN described in the note "THE 'FLARES' OF STARLINK® SATELLITES (OR 'FLARING SATELLITES')" seem to be present, supporting this hypothesis and reducing the strangeness perceived by the witness. GEIPAN classified this case in category "A", observation of Starlink® satellite flares.
