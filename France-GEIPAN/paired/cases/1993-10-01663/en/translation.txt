Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named NOEUX-LES-MINES (62) 30.10.1993 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On three occasions, over a period of about 15 days in early November 1993, the owners (T1 and T2) of a house found traces of crushed and laid-down grass in their garden: a first wavy line of approximately 8 meters long and 40 cm wide, then a second line of about 10 meters, and finally a circle at the junction of the two lines. They had these various traces witnessed by a neighbor (T3). T1 specifies that between November 8th and 14th, around 1 a.m., she saw a yellowish light moving at a height of 0.60 meters from the ground. T3 also noted that these dogs barked abnormally for about 15 days around 1:30 a.m. The witnesses provided their testimonies late, and the gendarmerie visited the site on November 17, 1993. This is an observation of ground traces without observation of an aerospace phenomenon. Indeed, the observation by T2 (and only T2) of a "yellowish light at a height of 60 cm that was wandering around 1 a.m." cannot be associated with the traces. It was seen after the detection of the traces and does not seem unusual in itself, especially the position on the photo does not allow to locate this light above the witness's plot or on the ground much further away in other plots. This light would probably not have been reported if these traces had not already caused concern among the witnesses. It is not within the mission of the GEIPAN (and therefore not within its expertise) to seek an explanation for ground traces without a concomitant testimony of an aerospace phenomenon at the time of the discovery of these traces. Indeed, suspicious ground traces can have a great variety of non-UFO origins. Some examples (without claiming they are eligible here): mushrooms, thieves, wild or not animals, neighbors' pranks or jokes. The GEIPAN should not have concluded this case as PAN D as nothing indicates that it is an aerospace phenomenon given the collected information. In conclusion, the GEIPAN revisits this case as C: unexploitable as it exceeds the sole competence of the GEIPAN.
