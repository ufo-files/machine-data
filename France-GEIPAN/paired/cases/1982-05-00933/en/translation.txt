Summary: Observation of an object shaped like a half-moon moving at varying speeds and appearing to follow the witnesses: astronomical observations of the Moon.
--- seg-43bc7bdd2dc10c20959c22c3 [needs-review] ---
Description: This observation case is part of cases classified as "D" in 1982 by the GEPAN. It was the subject of a new investigation (see document: Investigation Note). On May 16, 1982, around 3:45 a.m., a driver and her passenger were intrigued by the presence of a bright mass in the sky described as a half-moon, "of orange color." After remaining stationary above the town of Suippes, the PAN seemed to follow the witnesses by performing horizontal jumps, and this until the village of Somme-Suippes. No noise was heard during the observation. The two witnesses lost sight of the PAN upon arrival, as it is hidden by the landscape or buildings. No other information was gathered on this phenomenon by the gendarmerie. The described phenomenon has many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color...) with a known astronomical phenomenon: the Moon at sunrise. Moreover, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, while the witnesses do not mention it, except to say that they initially thought of the Moon (which indicates that the PAN resembles our natural satellite). It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue and anxiety). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of weak strangeness is classified as PAN A: Astronomical misperception of the Moon.
