Description: On the night of September 1st to 2nd, 2024, the main witness (T1) and his spouse (T2) were sitting on their terrace facing south in Sallaumines (62), hoping to observe northern lights. While T1 was taking photos with his phone, he spotted three black, triangular-shaped __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s arranged in a line, moving quickly and silently towards south-southwest. He immediately reported these __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s to T2, who initially thought they were geese. However, the absence of sound, speed, and orange color of the objects led the witnesses to dismiss this hypothesis. The observation ended when the objects disappeared behind a wooded hill at approximately 30° elevation. T1 noted the altitude and apparent size of the objects, which did not match any recorded flights on Flightradar24. To document the event, T1 created a visual reconstruction and provided the GEIPAN with photos taken before and after the observation, as well as a picture of a meteor crossing the sky later that evening.

Due to its peculiarity and average consistency (two witnesses, but a unique testimony, absence of direct photos of the PAN), this case appears to be a probable observation of nocturnal birds illuminated by street lamps. This hypothesis is supported by the following:

- The triangular shape corresponding to the body of birds observed under poor conditions, at night, for only three seconds, with low contrast against the sky background, for a small angular size.
- The partially orange color of the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s can be explained by the reflection of orange lights emitted by urban lighting on brighter parts of the birds.
- The grouped, linear movement is entirely consistent with bird behavior.
- The data provided by T1 is fully compatible with birds 10-20 centimeters long, flying at a moderate altitude (15-20 meters) at a speed of approximately 30 km/h. This could be, for example, mallards or winter teals, which are abundant in the area at this time of year (taking into account a possible margin of error, especially regarding size).
- Finally, the observation area is surrounded by wetlands capable of hosting many bird species that can move at night from their feeding grounds to their roosting sites, potentially disturbed during the hunting season for water fowl.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B," a probable bird observation.

*Consistency: According to the GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.
