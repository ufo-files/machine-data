Summary: Observations and photographs of multiple phenomena in the sky with different shapes: misidentifications with various planes observed at sunset.
--- seg-af44fc0bab934610ba245f0b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 3, 2016 at 4:45 PM, two witnesses at their residence observed in the sky numerous bright rectangles that intrigued them. Using binoculars, they observed and described PAN in the shape of gray/green saucer-like discs with a lighter, very pointed dome. Continuing their observations, they then described three oval phenomena with small, dark gray wings surrounded by flickering orange light moving from southwest to northeast; a deep rumble was heard. One witness also saw a grayish sphere. The witnesses ended their observations around 5:10 PM. Three photographs were taken with a tablet. The GEIPAN received all the testimonies in 2017. This case appears to be a very probable misidentification with different planes observed at sunset (see the investigation report). The description of the PAN is indeed perfectly typical of this type of observation. The photographs taken by T1 allow for a detailed comparison (see attached report) with air traffic records. This leaves no doubt about the identification of the "bright rectangles" or "gel capsules" described by the witnesses. The identification of the last phase of the observation is more uncertain as it was not photographed and the absence of military air traffic records (the testimony is too late in relation to the observation to benefit from these) does not allow for a comprehensive record of the traffic. However, the description is once again typical of a misidentification with aircraft. Only the sphere described by T1 remains unexplained, but its existence is questionable: T2 did not see it while T1 and T2 were observing together, the sphere does not appear on the photos, contrary to what T1 claims. Furthermore, there is no reason why the sphere would fade on the one or more photos (as T1 says), if it is indeed real. The consistency of the testimony is good (2 testimonies, photographs), except for the component related to the sphere, which cannot be considered reliable. The case is classified as A: misidentification with different planes observed at sunset.
