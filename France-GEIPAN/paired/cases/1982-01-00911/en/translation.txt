**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the accuracy of conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or explicit changes in classification in an investigation note. This previously classified D case (formerly named MARTIGUES (13) 1982) is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases.

On January 12, 1982, between 5:45 and 6:15 a.m., two witnesses observed, from partially open curtains in their bedroom, a bright light. The object was described as a white glowing sphere with a green halo, remaining motionless and silent. Each appearance lasted a few seconds. A third witness's testimony, reported by T2, mentions an observation made around 7 a.m. The direct witnesses (who signed a gendarmerie protocol) observed the light from their bed through the partially open curtains. It was a fixed, fleeting light seen multiple times, for a few seconds, between 5:45 and 6:00 a.m. The gendarmerie protocol indicates that the bedroom window faces the north-west motorway bridge, 150 meters from the observation location. T2 places the PAN "at a height of 60 meters, near the motorway bridge. The bridge's peak is at 45 meters." These details suggest a low-altitude PAN in a north-west direction. The PAN's height would be 60 meters - 25 meters (8th floor level) observed at 150 meters, approximately 12 degrees of elevation.

One hypothesis could be the observation of Capella. This star moves towards an azimuth of 320 degrees and an elevation of 15 degrees, placing it in the north-west sector. Capella can appear, due to atmospheric turbulence at low elevation, with variable colors compatible with those observed (white and green). T1 mentions it was raining. The sky might not have been entirely clouded, allowing Capella to appear fugitively. The passage of a cloud layer can vary the star's intensity, giving the impression of a closer or farther distance from the witnesses. Despite this, the witnesses reported a strong brightness for a Capella observation: T1: "of very high intensity. I thought it was a lightning flash or a 'factory flash'." T2: "the central point very violently illuminated," "illumination that enveloped the entire neighborhood." In fact, they made the observation from their bed through the partially open curtains (T1 states they didn't move from the bed, and T2 observed before getting up; there was no PAN during the subsequent observation from the balcony). This observation condition may have heightened the impression of surprise and intensity of light. However, this same condition suggests a restricted angular field of view around the building's normal, reducing the likelihood of the Capella hypothesis, though it's not entirely excluded (as shown in the attached reconstruction).

Another hypothesis is a ball lightning strike. The rainy weather, colors, halo, and short duration suggest this type of phenomenon, which can appear during stormy conditions (yellow + pale green halo). See the article "Aspects, manifestations, and classification of ball lightning." This would more directly explain (than the Capella hypothesis) the perception of a strong glow and also a ball or halo (though these latter perceptions sometimes accompany star observations as well). We do not have the statement from the third witness mentioned by T2, but only what was reported in the protocol. Between 7:00 and 7:15 a.m., he observed a light in the shape of a half-sphere, blue-white in color. This very bright and silent light lasted two seconds. The observation seems inconsistent with Capella due to its direction (see reconstruction), but is consistent with ball lightning. Ball lightning can manifest fugitively during stormy conditions, even an hour later than the T1 and T2 observations. The observation is not strange because the light could be from a star or more likely from ball lightning. The very poor observation conditions, particularly the a priori reduced visual field (from the bed through partially open curtains), did not allow for a proper observation of the PAN's appearance, movement, and disappearance, which are crucial elements for further investigation and confirmation of the hypotheses (especially for ball lightning). The third testimony also lacks consistency (precision, reliability) due to its indirect nature.

In conclusion, these are low-strangeness testimonies that could potentially correspond to a star or more likely to a ball lightning manifestation, but they cannot be properly exploited due to their low consistency. The GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information. Observations made on other days, mentioned by T2 and reported in the protocol, are exploitable (lack of information, no precise date).
