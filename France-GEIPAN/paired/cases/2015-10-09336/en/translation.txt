--- seg-5b510d1643d8e911ff18fb74 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 8, 2015, at 5 a.m., a witness was intrigued by a strong light in a clear sky. He decided to take photographs and noticed the presence of another object. Only one testimony was collected. The witness reported a very bright point to the East (a phone conversation with the witness clarified that he observed the phenomenon "under the Moon, to the left"). The numerous photos show a blur, more or less significant despite the use of a tripod: the witness took the pictures by pressing the shutter release (without remote control or delayer), which was enough to cause a significant blur due to the use of a long focal length (300 mm). The uncropped photos show several other bright points, undoubtedly characterized as the stars 31 Leo, Pi Leo, and HIP 48876 and 48734, confirming that Venus is the subject of the photographs. In the cropped and enhanced photos (7298 to 7301), we notice an odd saucer-like shape; if we look at the thumbnails of these photos, using an appropriate tool (e.g., photo software Xnview) that shows the original framing, we see that these photos correspond to a decentration of Venus in the image; the odd shape might be a photographic artifact, called a "flare" or parasitic reflection, or a real lenticular cloud (diagnosis ongoing with photo and weather experts). The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation and photography of Venus, with a probable photographic artifact.
