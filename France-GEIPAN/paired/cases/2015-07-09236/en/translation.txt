Summary: Observation of a small, blinking star at a regular frequency, then moving in a straight line, blinking again, and disappearing into a clear sky; likely misidentification with a rapidly rotating, out-of-control satellite.
--- seg-c7eb15d1ffa1a5c8a84685c4 [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 12, 2015, between 3 and 4 a.m., a witness located on the banks of the Hérault observed a small star that blinked at a regular frequency, then moved in a straight line, blinked again, and disappeared into the clear sky. No unusual noises were heard. Only one testimony was collected. Due to its low strangeness and consistency (single witness, lack of precision in the time), the case appears to be a probable misidentification with an out-of-control satellite rotating rapidly on itself (see the investigation notes document). The PAN indeed exhibits all typical characteristics of such an object: brief and intense blinking, short observation, slow movement, and the appearance of a star or satellite. Unfortunately, the lack of precision in the testimony regarding the timing prevents formal identification of the satellite, although the Iridium 73 satellite, known for its regular blinking, passed directly through the area indicated by the witness at a very close time. This case is classified B, probable misidentification with an out-of-control satellite rotating rapidly on itself.
