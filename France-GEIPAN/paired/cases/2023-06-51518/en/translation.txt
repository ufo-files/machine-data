**Report:**

On June 28, 2023, around 7 PM, in Evry-Courcouronnes (91), a witness inside the Parc des Loges stadium observed a PAN moving in the sky. They immediately recorded a 4-minute video. Based on the video and the witness's testimony, the following description of the PAN's appearance and behavior is provided:

- A round, black object with no additional features like a halo or specific structure. The witness noted a metallic appearance, not visible on the video.
- The object appeared to be at a low altitude (at the level of one of the stadium's lampposts), moving slowly and silently. It disappeared after a few minutes, moving away while ascending into the sky.

According to the witness, its trajectory was described as "straight and ascending, with apparently random movements at high altitude." Only one testimony was collected. Using visual cues from the video and the witness's schematic indications, we were able to determine the PAN's movement direction, which was from east to west. However, some uncertainty remains due to difficulties in accurately positioning the PAN at the beginning of the observation.

Applying the GEIPAN criteria, the case's consistency is deemed good, despite the initial lack of azimuth data, which was later reconstructed, and the fact that there is only one witness. The witness provided a usable video of the PAN.

The explored hypothesis is a simple helium-filled balloon, and the following elements support this:

- Round shape, consistent with a balloon's appearance.
- Black color is plausible. The metallic aspect reported by the witness suggests the balloon could be made of a metallic-coated (Mylar-type) envelope, a material commonly used for helium-filled balloons.
- Slow, straight, and ascending movement, consistent with a normal balloon's behavior.
- More erratic movements observed at higher altitudes are consistent with irregular, even shear, winds at these heights. However, these phenomena cannot be formally proven with the available meteorological data as they can occur on a very small scale.

Regarding the movement direction, meteorological data indicate that the wind was blowing lightly, consistent with the PAN's slow movement. The wind direction was somewhat northeast, aligning with the observed direction of the PAN despite various uncertainties. (See the meteorological document for UT time, with a two-hour offset from observation time; DD symbols indicate wind direction and FF indicates wind strength).

Initially, the witness considered the possibility of a balloon but dismissed it for the following reasons:

- "The sphere seems rather full as it moves": This impression is likely due to the black color, metallic aspect, and the balloon's perfectly round shape, which can create the illusion of a solid, metallic, and full object.
- "We perceive the object moving differently when changing direction": As explained, this change in direction, which was not constant during the entire observation but only when the PAN reached a certain altitude, is due to changing and more irregular winds encountered at that altitude.
- "Its shape does not correspond to a common helium balloon, given there was no string present": The presence of a string is not universal, or it may have detached from the balloon or been cut. Regarding the spherical shape, it is, on the contrary, common for a Mylar balloon filled with helium, which is generally available for sale online.

The balloon's origin could not be determined during the investigation. However, it's worth noting that the area is surrounded by numerous educational institutions (schools, colleges, and high schools) to the north and east. Thus, it's possible a balloon was released or lost during the holiday period at the end of the school year.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as A, observation of a balloon.
