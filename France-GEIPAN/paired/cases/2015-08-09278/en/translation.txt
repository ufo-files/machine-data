Summary: Daytime observation of a pale white light phenomenon crossing the sky and sudden disappearance towards the Vosges: likely reflection on the cockpit window of a military plane.
--- seg-31dacd2bbdfa48e53142ab02 [needs-review] ---
Description: While waiting for his daughter who is about to make a parachute jump, the witness and his wife, at the Colmar airport on August 21, 2015 at 11:05, observed a pale white ball crossing the sky from north/northwest at an apparent speed faster than that of airplanes in flight for jumps. The phenomenon abruptly stops near the Vosges Mountains. The witness discusses it with the person who made the jump with his daughter, but the latter saw nothing. Only one testimony will be collected. Analysis of the elements at our disposal: 1) the observation is static, outdoors, on the tarmac of the Colmar airport; 2) the phenomenon is unique, luminous, white in color, pale, round in shape, with blurred contours, small in size (estimated 1°), without noise or trail, spotted to the North, then disappearing to the Northwest, moving in a straight line in the sky at 220° (reconstructed from the witness's indications), at a speed of 4°/s, with a descending elevation from 40 to 20° (unclear if this is due to distance, a real decrease in altitude, or a combination of both); 3) the weather conditions recorded at the airport in Colmar on August 21, 2015 at 11:00 AM, as indicated by Meteociel, report a light easterly wind of 6 km/h on average, with no precipitation. The witness reports "Clear blue sky and sunny"; 4) the date: it is a Friday, late morning; 5) the environment: open area near the airport, north of Colmar. The trajectory report map of the phenomenon is found in the file "carte report trajectoire.jpg". ANALYSIS: This phenomenon initially suggests a reflection of the sun on an airborne object, either metallic or on a windshield. The detailed analysis below will show that the observation conditions are very favorable to this phenomenon. The ephemeris from the observation location gives the sun's position at 11:05 at 127° (southeast) with an elevation of 43°, see file: Éphémérides Soleil.jpg The witness estimated the phenomenon's trajectory from north to northwest at 220°, with a decreasing elevation from 40° to 20°, directly opposite the sun. The map in the "Hyp Carte reflet soleil.jpg" file positions these elements. These elements allow us to note that: - The sun's incidence is almost perpendicular to the phenomenon's trajectory; - The sun's elevation at 43° is of the same order of magnitude as that of the phenomenon estimated by the witness at 40°. All these elements are favourable to the generation of a sun reflection, as geometrically validated in the file "Hyp Géométrie reflet soleil.jpg", a reflection that disappears when the incidence angles between the sun, the object, and the ground witness deviate from favourable conditions. This explains why other witnesses performing a parachute jump, at a different altitude and location from the witness, did not see this phenomenon, which required a precise location to be observed at that exact time. Now the question arises as to the type of flying object that was originally responsible for this phenomenon. Several hypotheses are developed: Hypothesis A: a civilian aircraft on approach to Basel-Mulhouse Airport: - the trajectory recorded is compatible with an aircraft on approach, turning, to align with the runway of Basel-Mulhouse Airport, located to the south at 170° at 55 km, a runway oriented at 150°. The file "Hyp A approche Bâle-Mulhouse.jpg" illustrates the observed trajectory by the witness (in red), the track (in blue), allowing for interpolation of the trajectory between the observation and landing (in black). This leads to a sun reflection on the fuselage of an airplane in flight, in a circular arc around the witness, the reflection disappearing when the object resumes a straight trajectory, in alignment with the Basel-Mulhouse runway. Now let's study the approach trajectories for Basel-Mulhouse Airport. It appears that aircraft passing near Colmar are at an altitude of 10,000 to 12,000 feet, which is 3,000 to 3,600 meters. As for speed, it is between 300 and 400 knots, which corresponds to 600 to 700 km/h. Let's corroborate this with the observation data: the angular velocity as reported by the witness, 60° covered in 15 seconds, leads to an angular displacement of approximately 4°/s. Under this hypothesis, an aircraft is actually at an altitude between 3,000 and 3,600 meters, and given its elevation of 43°, corresponds to a distance between 3,000 and 3,600 meters. At 3,000 meters, this corresponds to a linear speed of 3,000 * tan(4°) ~ 200 m/s, or 720 km/h. At 3,600 meters, that corresponds to 860 km/h. The analysis of this hypothesis makes it credible, taking into account a distance approximation error by the witness, the phenomenon being at around 3,500 meters distance, not twice as close. In this case, it moves at a speed of approximately 800 km/h, which is consistent with an approach at this distance from the runway. But, unfortunately, this hypothesis does not withstand the reality of civilian radar observations. There was no civilian aircraft following this trajectory at that time. More surprisingly, there is indeed a civil aircraft, flight AB7653, but with an azimuth diametrically opposite to 038° (+180°=218° ~220°), approaching Baden-Baden, located to the northeast at 035° at 90 km, with a runway oriented at 030°. This flight has a ground trace 1.3 km northwest of the witness, at an altitude of 4,000 meters, placing it at an elevation of approximately 70°, which could not generate a reflection, unless the witness mistakenly reported the direction of movement while completing the questionnaire. Civil radar detection in the "Hyp Abis approche réelle Baden-Baden.jpg" file. Hypothesis B: A military aircraft approaching Air Base 116 Luxeuil-Saint-Sauveur: This base houses the 2nd Fighter Squadron, which has a single flying unit, the 1/2 "Cigognes" Fighter Squadron, and its Mirage 2000-5F aircraft. It is located 80 km at 250° from the witness, whose track is aligned at 290°. To land, passing through Colmar, an aircraft should have a trajectory further south than 250°, which corresponds to the 220° observed, in order to make a turn to the West (at 290°) to align with the runway. In this case, it would be the pilot's windshield that is causing the reflection, with the plane rolling slightly towards the witness for an arc-shaped trajectory, the fuselage being treated to obscure the plane. The photo "Hyp B Verrière mirage 2000.jpg" shows the importance of the greenhouse. A request for military traffic, not reported on civil radar logs, was made in May 2017. Unfortunately, we have no response to our request to validate this hypothesis. Hypothesis C: Diurnal Iridium Flash: Celestial bodies are visible to the naked eye during the day when their magnitude becomes less than -4. This is the case with the planet Venus during its maximum elongations. Unknown phenomenon to the general public! Iridium flashes can be much brighter, reaching up to magnitude -8 (which is about 30 times brighter than Venus' light), and some are visible during the day. As for the color of the reflection, despite the yellow color of the sun, visible stars during the day are white in color. The moon visible during the day in its quarter phase is of this color. This Iridium flash hypothesis is plausible, but unfortunately, we no longer have ephemerides for this date and location to confirm this hypothesis. In conclusion: The consistency of the testimony is good, with multiple witnesses (one deposition) and reliable information provided. In the absence of certainty with a response from military authorities, we prioritize the hypothesis of a military aircraft approaching Air Base 116 as the most likely, with a proposed classification of B: probable reflection on the cockpit of a military aircraft.
