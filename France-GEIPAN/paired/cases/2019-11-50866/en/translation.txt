Description: On November 29, 2019, around 10:00 PM, a witness observed from an outdoor terrace what appeared to be a very bright star, but with flickering effects that intrigued them. Nine people were called to come and see the PAN and gathered on the terrace, observing the various evolutions of the phenomenon (appearances, disappearances, division and separation of parts). The witness attempts in vain to film with his mobile phone. Only one testimony will be collected. During the investigation, another concise email testimony will be provided, but with videos showing the PAN (see the investigation report). Of fairly strong strangeness but average consistency (multiple witnesses, but unique testimony, and PAN videos), this case proves to be non-identifiable due to a lack of data, although the case presents characteristics of an astronomical misperception. According to the witness's statements, his observation at 21:50 for approximately 80 minutes was made in the southeast at an azimuth of 123° "below the constellation of the Little Dog" between 30° and 60° of elevation. The primary hypothesis under investigation is a case of astronomical misperception due to: 
- the very long observation duration (approximately 80 minutes), which is typical for this type of misperception,
- the appearance of the PAN on videos, which is consistent with that of a bright star that is difficult to focus on due to the night environment,
- the twinkling effect, which is characteristic of astronomical misperceptions: this effect is caused by the star's low position on the horizon, through which its light passes through atmospheric turbulence. However, the investigation determines that: - Sirius, corresponding to the azimuth 123°, cannot be retained as the observation time (rise at 22:12) was not lifted, - Procyon, a star in the constellation of Canis Minor, is located on the eastern horizon at 21:50 and is not in the direction indicated by the witness or at the elevation indicated by the witness, - furthermore, the movement of the lights is neither confirmed by his cousin, the only witness who could be found during the drafting of the investigation note, nor by the videos of PAN. The angular height of the PAN is largely imprecise. The case is classified "C", an unidentified case due to a lack of reliable data.
