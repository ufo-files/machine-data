Summary: Observations by two independent witnesses of successive orange light points crossing the sky of Albi: likely observations of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-2e2218a51587466908b102bd [needs-review] ---
Description: On September 02, 2017, around 21:20, two witnesses, located 1.3 km apart in Albi, observed the silent passage of an orange ball in the sky. A few moments later, a second orange-lit ball was observed on the same trajectory as the first. One of the witnesses (T2) managed to take a photo of the PAN before it disappeared. With low strangeness and good consistency (2 independent witnesses, photos), this case appears to be a very probable misidentification with 2 Thai lanterns (see the investigation report). Based on the characteristics of the PAN and the photos taken by T2, the two witnesses likely observed the passage of 2 Thai lanterns over Albi, and it is even more than probable that the observed lanterns were the same. One of the witnesses rejected this hypothesis because, in his opinion, the trajectory of the PAN did not correspond to the direction of the wind. However, it turns out that the wind was very changeable at the time of the observation. The Albi station, which is the closest to the two observation locations, shows that at the time of the observation, the wind was blowing from the West-North-West. Yet, when comparing the trajectory of the PAN from both testimonies, it appears that the PAN is heading towards Southeast. This is very close to the direction an object carried by the wind would take. Furthermore, the observation took place on a Saturday night, without rain, and with a very light wind, providing perfect conditions for the launch of Thai lanterns. The case is classified as B, probable misidentification with 2 Thai lanterns.
