Summary: Long night-time observation of coloured lights in motion at low altitude: probable observation of a recreational drone.
--- seg-c786373652c48067bcf3479d [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 3, 2025, around 3:00 AM, a witness in Bazas (33) was awakened by intense white lights on the ground pointing south. These lights, sometimes swirling and brighter than car headlights, were observed by the witness for approximately 1 hour and 15 minutes. Around 4:15 AM, the witness then observed in the same direction a kind of "string of lights" composed of lights of various colors, either steady or blinking, which remained in the same position for 15 to 20 minutes before suddenly disappearing. A video of just over one minute was recorded by the witness on this occasion. Finally, around 4:30 AM, two other blinking lights, one white and the other reddish, were observed respectively in the south/southeast and southeast, at an altitude. Observed for approximately 15 minutes, they then abruptly went out. These last two observations lacked sufficient details to permit identification. In both cases, it was a single blinking light that could be the result of numerous difficult-to-identify phenomena, which was not filmed or photographed by the witness, had no proven link to the first two observations, and only showed a weak degree of strangeness. Therefore, these last two observations were not further investigated in this inquiry. The consistency* of the testimony for the first two observations (especially the second) is good, despite a single but complete testimony given two days after the observation, and a video, for the second observation of PAN, which was studied and added to the investigation.

*According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The witness considers it possible that it could be a drone before dismissing the hypothesis, as it connects all four observations into one: "I would be surprised to see three drones flying over Bazas at 4:00 AM on January 3." After eliminating the hypothesis of an aircraft (see the investigation report), GEIPAN studied the drone hypothesis, which appears plausible for the following reasons:

- The lights, of various colors, steady and blinking, are not aeronautical (red, green, and white) and are therefore quite typical of "customized" drones by their users.
- Along the line of observation, there is a large field, easily accessible and far enough from residences while offering a wide view of the village, from which the user(s) of the drone could have tested and flown it at will, while keeping it in sight and without disturbing the neighborhood, despite the ban on night flights. However, the regulations regarding the maximum flight height of drones seem to have been respected, as PAN remained close to the ground, located at the boundary between the prohibited area and the maximum authorized altitude of 60 meters.
- Video analysis conducted with the specialized software of GEIPAN, IPACO*, shows that this drone must have been located between the user and the witness, at a distance of between approximately 100 and 340 meters, for standard drone dimensions between 30 and 100 cm. We should note, however, a violation of the regulation that bans drone flights at night. A violation, however, frequently committed by enthusiasts eager to test illuminated equipment. GEIPAN classifies this observation case as "B": probable observation of a recreational drone.

*IPACO: Image analysis and processing software of GEIPAN (IPACO.fr).
