Description: On February 2, 2015, at 1 a.m., a witness in their courtyard observed a red-orange ball in the sky for 3 minutes, initially thinking it was an airplane. At 1:40 a.m., the witness observed a large, black object flying very low and without lights over neighboring buildings, describing its front as having a rounded triangular shape with rectangular windows. As the object approached slowly, the witness noticed a large red-orange ball beneath it, resembling a position light. The object, moving very slowly, hovered then emitted a thick fog and disappeared from the witness's view as it flew backward. No sounds were heard. Around 1:55 a.m., the witness observed a bright white star for 1-2 minutes that suddenly accelerated and disappeared from view. The witness believed it to be the previously seen object. Only one testimony with a photograph of the observation location was collected. Since this observation, the witness has seen unidentified flying objects (UFOs) in the sky of Perpignan both day and night. These observations present unusual characteristics, especially as the witness reports the phenomenon repeating. Consequently, the GEIPAN requested a local investigator to interview the witness. On September 30 in the evening, despite very cloudy conditions during the visit, the witness told the investigator they saw light phenomena passing through the sky, which the investigator did not witness. In 25 minutes, the witness reported seeing 31 UFOs in the form of white dots passing, hiding, and stopping. The witness stated that other people to whom they tried to show the phenomena also did not see them. The witness reports that synthetic crystals were implanted in their eyes shortly after the February observation (they were wearing glasses at the time but almost blind.) It was after the operation that they started seeing "armadas," "myriads" of UFOs every night over Perpignan. This observation case is clearly the result of ophthalmic issues, both before and after the operation, for the February observation and the recurring observations afterward. The witness seems excessively focused on UFOs to the point of excluding that their observations are the result of a vision problem. GEIPAN classifies this case as B: the witness's vision was very likely impaired.
