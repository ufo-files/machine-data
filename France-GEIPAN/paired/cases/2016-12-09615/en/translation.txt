Description: On Saturday, December 17, 2016, around 8:45 PM, three individuals in a parking lot observed approximately thirty white (with an orange tint) glowing points silently moving across a cloudy sky. A video was recorded. Only one statement was collected; the witness did not want the video released. It is highly likely that this was an observation of Thai lantern releases. The visual aspect in the video aligns with this. The wind direction in Nîmes that evening (from the Infoclimat record) was north to south, consistent with the movement of the PAN (the camera generally faces east and observes the PAN passing from left to right). The witness's objections to this hypothesis include the disparity in trajectories between PAN and the fact that some may be joining others. However, the video shows that the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s indeed succeed each other in the same direction from left to right, but urban conditions can create local wind irregularities with faster currents. The video also displays several dimmer fixed points that are likely stars, as the cloudiness that evening, according to the Météociel record, was low (1/8), and some stars were visible at low elevation, particularly in the eastern sector (Procyon, Betelgeuse, Rigel). In the video, the mobile glowing points that extend beyond the dimmer or less clear fixed points are probably lanterns passing in front of stars. The other reported oddity or phenomenon of a panache or halo around glowing points when the camera zooms is without meaning and a result of optical or video aberrations. Note that this occurred on a weekend evening, which is always more conducive to festive events involving lantern releases. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
