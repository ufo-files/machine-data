Summary: Long night observation of a colorful light phenomenon in a clear sky: astronomical misidentification with Sirius.
--- seg-809bc62c933ac84a905e3720 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On April 3, 2016, at 10:10 PM, a witness, standing on their doorstep, was intrigued by a brightly luminous phenomenon seen to the right of the church bell tower in their village. The phenomenon emitted lights of various colors (blue then red). No noise was heard. The phenomenon eventually disappeared from view at 10:45 PM. The witness took two photographs. Only one testimony was collected. The witness saw Sirius. At a low elevation and under certain atmospheric conditions, the lights of Sirius can be variable in color and misleading. Geometric reconstruction from the witness's photograph and the estimated location of the photo allows for a 1-2 degree coincidence between Sirius and the PAN. Furthermore, the trajectory of the PAN (direction and speed) as described by the witness is fully consistent with that of Sirius. The alternative possibility of a lantern, which could have been easily launched on a Sunday evening, can be eliminated. Indeed, this would imply that the witness did not see Sirius and that it is hidden by a cloud, while the testimony indicates a clear sky. The wind on that day (infoclimat) is compatible with the direction of movement but with an intensity (10 km/h) resulting in an angular velocity of movement at 200 meters altitude and 600 meters distance (calculated from the elevation) much greater and entirely inconsistent with the observation (even if the wind were 10 times less intense than what the weather station a few kilometers away indicates). Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies this observation as A: Sirius.
