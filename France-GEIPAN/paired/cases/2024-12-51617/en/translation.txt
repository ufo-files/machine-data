**Description:** Around 11:24 PM local time on December 22, 2024, while in his garden in La Turballe (Loire-Atlantique), the witness observes a luminous phenomenon he describes as a "flaming band with orange and yellow hues" appearing from the northeast at an elevation estimated between 35° and 45°. The visual aspect of the phenomenon reminded him of a waterfall of fire seemingly pouring onto the ground. The observation lasted only a few seconds. The witness reports that weather conditions were particularly favorable at the time, with a completely clear sky. Minutes after the event, he notified the gendarmerie by phone and recorded the GPS coordinates of his observation point and the corresponding azimuth via his mobile phone.

On February 2, 2025, the witness submitted a Technical Questionnaire (QT) to GEIPAN filled out in full. The case includes the following documents: a screenshot from his phone showing the recorded azimuth and GPS coordinates, a photograph pinpointing the exact location of the observation and the direction of the phenomenon, an illustration of the observed scene overlaid on a photo of the site. The case consistency is good. While based on a single testimony with no photographic or video evidence, the phenomenon description and location data are reported with great precision.

*According to GEIPAN's criteria, case consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The witness was precisely in the line of departure for the trajectory of a meteor, observed by dozens of people. A total of 73 witness reports were recorded in the International Meteor Organization (IMO) database under event reference #8095-2024.

According to data published by Vigie-Ciel.org, a remarkable meteor was observed on December 22, 2024 at 11:24 PM local time (CEST, or 10:24 PM UTC) in northwest France. Visible for over 10 seconds, it was described by many witnesses as particularly spectacular. One witness in Flers (Orne) reported: "I thought it was a shooting star, but it kept going straight, passing through a cloud: it turned green, moved at incredible speed, and then fragmented like a fireworks display, turning orange." Dozens of online observation reports were recorded. The meteor, with an apparent magnitude of -8 (about 30 times brighter than Venus), was also captured by four cameras in the FRIPON (Fireball Recovery and InterPlanetary Observation Network) system. Video analysis allowed for reconstruction of its trajectory and estimation of its physical and orbital characteristics.

The object responsible for the phenomenon is a meteorite from the outer regions of the main asteroid belt, located between Mars and Jupiter. With an estimated diameter of 15-20 cm and a mass of around 20 kilograms, it entered Earth's atmosphere at a relative speed of approximately 13.6 km/s (nearly 49,000 km/h), above the department of Mayenne.

The meteor began emitting light at an altitude of 75 km, approximately 15 km southwest of Laval, continuing its trajectory towards the northeast with an azimuth of around 44° and a slight inclination of 18° from the horizontal. This slight inclination explains the exceptional duration of the phenomenon's visibility, as it traveled over 140 km in the atmosphere before fragmenting and dissipating at an altitude of around 32 km, near the northern limit of the Orne department, about 15 km west of L'Aigle.

**Source:** Vigie-Ciel.org – Meteor of December 22, 2024 at 11:24 PM:  [Link to original report](https://www.vigie-ciel.org/metier-du-22-decembre-2024-a-11h24/)

The PAN (Unidentified Aerospatial Phenomenon) description provided by the witness, particularly the northeast observation direction and the precise timestamp of 11:24 PM, allows for reliable correlation of this observation with the meteor passage reported by numerous witnesses on the International Meteor Organization (IMO) platform. The phenomenon was also detected at the same time and along the same axis by four cameras in the FRIPON (Fireball Recovery and InterPlanetary Observation Network) surveillance system, significantly enhancing the credibility and coherence of the testimony.

The witness's perceived strangeness results from the rarity of such an observation in the line of departure and descent of the meteor, creating a visual effect resembling an orange/yellow waterfall, amplified by fragmentation phases followed by the gradual extinction of the phenomenon. The meteor's trajectory, characterized by a slight 18° inclination from the horizontal (according to Vigie-Ciel calculations), allowed for an exceptionally long observation duration, estimated between 7 and 10 seconds, which is atypical for this type of atmospheric event. The case is classified as "A" by GEIPAN: observation of a meteor.
