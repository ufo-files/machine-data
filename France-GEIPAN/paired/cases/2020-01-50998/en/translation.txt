Summary: Bad weather observation of three bright red rectangular lights in motion in the sky: unexplained phenomenon (case classified D1).
--- seg-cace6e1ee1c01bb58cf0adb5 [needs-review] ---
Description: The witness is driving early in the morning on a small rural road located on the Crozon peninsula. The weather is bad with strong wind gusts, a low overcast ceiling, and rain. The witness observes, partially masked by the top of a pine bush located on his left, two identical horizontal red rectangles. After slowing down, he notices a third identical red rectangle appearing to the left of the other two, with the same spacing. The entire object, which appears to be of significant size to the witness, is located relatively high in the sky and moves, maintaining the same appearance, along the road, always on the witness's left, and in the direction of an intersection a little further ahead. Then the three PAN hover at the intersection, facing the witness who stops just before the intersection to observe them. As they drew closer, the witness estimates that they occupied two-thirds of the length of his windshield, slightly to his left. He must lean forward in his seat to observe them in full. He describes them as resembling bright red, glowing tail lights of trucks, well-defined, and consisting of small, moving beads inside. These PAN, despite the gusts of wind shaking the witness' vehicle, remain stationary. After a few seconds of observation, the three PAN depart towards the south, facing the witness, maintaining their appearance and formation while visually decreasing in size. This departure first occurs at a moderate speed, bouncing while descending in apparent height until the level of the horizon, before accelerating and disappearing almost instantly into the sky, without interacting with the clouds. Regarding this observation case, the consistency is good, with a very coherent and well-detailed testimony. The observed phenomenon is of high strangeness. The information gathered during the remote and on-site investigation allowed several explanatory hypotheses to be considered for this observation. Deepened in an expert committee on GEIPAN, the investigation allowed several hypotheses to be considered: - Drone(s) - Helicopter(s) - Light from a lighthouse/semaphore - Lighting from wind turbine markers - Laser or nightclub projector - Reflection on the windshield of an internal light in the habitable space - Airplane - Reflective plates or position lights or navigation lights from a truck. The investigation deepened the first three hypotheses: drone(s), helicopter(s), and light from a lighthouse. Other hypotheses have been eliminated by justifying them. The LANVÉOC-POULMIC Air Force Base, located nearby, was consulted regarding a possible aeronautical observation and indicated that none of its aircraft were in maneuver at that time. On the other hand, even though the shape and color of the PAN might suggest a form of lighting signage (marking) for which we, moreover, could not find any examples, all other parameters are little or not at all compatible with the hypothesis of drones or helicopters. In fact, the significant angular size of the PAN suggests a relatively close position to the witness, who, especially for helicopters, would have undoubtedly heard the sound of their engines with a carrying wind, even under unfavorable observation conditions (in a car with the engine running, closed windows, and in rainy and windy weather). It seems, moreover, difficult to imagine that three of these craft could have been permanently observed while maintaining the respective distances separating them. The hypothesis of observing three drones is a little more consistent in that it allows them to be placed a little closer to the witness while having a clearly lower noise emitted. It implies, however, that these drones be of great size for a remaining distance to the witness being low. Additionally, a drone instructor from the French Navy was consulted, and he states that the appearance of the PAN cannot be likened to any lighting signage used by a military drone. Finally, the LANVEOC-POULMIC Air Force Base confirmed that they did not conduct any drone exercises on the night of the observation. It could, however, be drones from another base or used by a private individual, which, under the observation conditions, remains nevertheless unlikely. Regarding the semaphore/lighthouse hypothesis, it is very unlikely. A semaphore is not designed to emit light signals, and there is no sufficiently powerful lighthouse in the area capable of producing fixed, permanent red rectangular lights for two minutes, directed along the observation axis of PAN and moving in a "rebounding" manner. Moreover, a beam, in rainy conditions, should have been visible below the rectangles, which was not the case. On the other hand, the lighthouse lighting is rather diffuse and in no way allows the creation of three well-defined red rectangular shapes on the base of the clouds. Finally, the witness, accustomed to the area, would not have failed to recognize the lights of a lighthouse, including under the weather conditions of the observation, typical for the region, lights that he would have also observed before in similar weather conditions. The GEIPAN remains cautious due to the technical questionnaire drafted and sent five months after the observation, as well as the field investigation and reconstruction conducted one year later. By experience, GEIPAN these time delays can disrupt or alter the elements of the testimony. A visual reconstruction of the observation using a video simulation tool was carried out. It will be published associated with this investigation report. In conclusion, this investigation was very rich in content and hypotheses studied. The GEIPAN conducted its most comprehensive investigation methodology, first with a remote investigation, then on-site investigation, and finally the analysis by the GEIPAN expert committee. Several hypotheses have been studied. The GEIPAN considers that none provide a satisfactory explanation regarding the testimony. Therefore, we publish all the elements brought to our knowledge by the witness as well as the entire scope of our investigation and this observation is classified as 'D', unexplained phenomenon.
