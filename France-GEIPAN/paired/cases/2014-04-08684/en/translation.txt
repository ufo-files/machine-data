Description: On April 16, 2014, around 9:30 PM, a witness observed from their home three glowing objects in the sky (between the constellations of Boötes and Virgo) moving in formation before disappearing. A few seconds later, the witness observed a PAN resembling an atmospheric reentry. The total observation duration is estimated between 30 and 50 seconds by the witness. Only one testimony was collected. Of average strangeness and consistency (single witness but detailed report), this complex observation case turns out to be a misidentification with a particular trio of satellites: a triple of Chinese military satellites Yaogan 17 (see investigation notes). The PAN indeed exhibits all typical characteristics of such a trio: brightness, visual aspect, short visibility due to a flash, trajectory and timing perfectly consistent. The last part of the observation, not strange, can be explained either by a meteor or by the flash of satellite Iridium 40 that occurred that evening in Petit-Quevilly. It is not the witness's visual perception at stake, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (observation of a particular type of satellite, simultaneity of two different phenomena). This case is classified A, misidentification with the Yaogan 17 A, B, and C satellite trio.
