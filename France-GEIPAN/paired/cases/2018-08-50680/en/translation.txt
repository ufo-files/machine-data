**Description:** On August 6, 2018, around 10 PM, a witness was observing the stars from a hill overlooking the village of Sermur (23). Intrigued by five aligned and very close stars, the witness took a photograph and, upon enlarging it, discovered that these five light sources formed a flat rectangular structure. The PAN in motion was described as consisting of five white glowing rectangles bordered by five red glowing points. No other witness statements were collected. One hypothesis is favored: a misidentification with an airplane (see the investigation report). The appearance of the PAN visible in the photo (not publishable at the witness's request) strongly resembles that of an airplane at cruising altitude in a photographic pose: the elements are perfectly symmetrical, and the visible colors (red, white, and green) are perfectly consistent with aeronautical lights. Notably, the photographic pose lasted six seconds, and the visible elements on the photo (white line, red point) are repeated five times, resulting in a rhythm of approximately one second. This duration is precisely that used by anti-collision lights on an airplane (see the article "An Airplane?"). Anti-collision lights consist of brief red flashes separated by one second, which perfectly matches the red points visible on the photo. The white lines correspond to white anti-collision lights. A closer examination of the PAN photo reveals that the continuous line connecting the white lines and red points is green, corresponding to the starboard position light of an airplane (see the Wikipedia article "Light and Fire (aeronautics)"). It should be noted that, according to the witness's statements, the PAN was moving from north to south while being east of the witness's position: an airplane moving on this trajectory shows its starboard side, which is perfectly consistent with the greenish continuous line. Even though it is not possible to formally identify the airplane photographed by the witness, it is worth noting that international flights departing from Paris and bound for Africa follow a trajectory consistent with that of the PAN. Furthermore, the angular displacement of the PAN during the photographic pose is approximately 5°, which corresponds to a speed of about 1°/second. This speed is perfectly consistent with that of a commercial airliner. The very strong similarity between the aeronautical elements and those of the PAN leaves no doubt about the misidentification. This case presents average consistency as it involves a single witness. The PAN photo likely identifies a commercial airliner, although the lengthy delay between the receipt of the testimony and its processing by GEIPAN prevents formal identification of the photographed airplane. It should be noted that the witness is mistaken in stating that the PAN's trajectory was diagonal and top-to-bottom. In reality, the PAN's trajectory relative to the stars shows it was roughly parallel to the horizon. This misinterpretation is likely due to the witness's sole reliance on what they saw in the PAN photo, rather than what they actually observed. This misinterpretation is also probably due to the fact that the testimony was provided relatively late (observation made in August, questionnaire completed in December). This misinterpretation also explains why the witness speaks of a single flat rectangular structure. The hypothesis of a misinterpretation is further strengthened by the fact that the witness initially described five aligned stars, as seen in the photo: if five glowing points had moved together, the result in the photographic pose would have been a much stronger colored line. In conclusion, this case, with its relatively low strangeness and average consistency (single witness, but PAN photo), turns out to be a misidentification with a commercial airliner. The PAN photo shows elements repeating at very regular intervals during the photographic pose. However, it turns out that the rhythm of these colored (red and white) light flashes corresponds to that of anti-collision lights on a commercial airliner, and the angular displacement of the PAN also corresponds to that of a commercial airliner. Other clues identify the PAN as a commercial airliner: a green line corresponding to the starboard position light, and the PAN's trajectory matching that of an airplane departing from Paris. The case is classified as A, misidentification with a commercial airliner.
