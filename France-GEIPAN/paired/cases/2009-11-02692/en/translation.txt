Testimony: Given one year after the observation and triggered by a television broadcast. On November 16, 2009, around 7:45 PM during a car ride towards Villefranche-sur-Mer (06), two individuals observe the movement of a bright red light in the night sky. The PAN changes trajectory and is then lost from the witness's sight. The witness has time to take a photograph and call a relative residing in the direction of the PAN's disappearance. The witness reports that their close ones saw a reddish object land on the slopes of Mont Boron hill, then ascend and quickly disappear in a white flash. Only one testimony is collected. The trajectory perceived by the witness suggests an arrival of the PAN from the east (Monaco), then a vertical change in trajectory towards Nice upon reaching the witness's observation point (Villefranche-sur-Mer). The change is relatively minor, around 30° to take a southerly-western (Nice) component from an eastern arrival, and is associated with a perception of descent ("nose-diving") towards Nice. The witness initially thinks of a Thai lantern (a bright red light very similar to a Thai lantern) but appears to reject this hypothesis due to the speed, primarily. However, the measured wind is consistent with the trajectory. Closest to the observation point (3 km in Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat), the wind blows towards the south-west. At Nice Airport (9 km), the wind is variable at that time, shifting from south-west to south-east, indicating that in such a relief and coastal environment, wind can be very changeable from one point to another and at different times, with likely horizontal wind currents and sudden "nose-dives". The wind is not light, at 10 km/h with gusts up to 30 km/h. The perceived strangeness in terms of speed may result from the inability to accurately assess the distance. The photograph provides no new information that would validate or invalidate the lantern hypothesis. The observation's continuation comes not from the witness but from his mother, reported by the witness. What the mother initially observes is consistent with the lantern hypothesis as she sees a red object land on the hills. We are no longer dealing with the "flaming red" seen by the primary witness, but a red compatible with the lantern's extinction phase. Note that the very changeable wind could have blown the lantern towards the hill while it was still well lit. However, the final phase is no longer consistent: the slow movement could still be plausible if the lantern was still lit and managed to rise after being pushed to the ground by the wind, but not the final disappearance in a white flash in less than a second. We do not know if the mother observed the PAN continuously (she called her son to inform him of this end) or if she confused it with another nearby phenomenon. Until this last element, the observation is consistent with a Thai lantern flight. The strangeness of this observation (compared to a Thai hypothesis that, otherwise, would be considered) concerns only elements not directly reported to the GEIPAN, despite the GEIPAN's request for a testimony from the mother via the witness. Therefore, the testimony is inconsistent. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies it as C: lack of reliable information.
