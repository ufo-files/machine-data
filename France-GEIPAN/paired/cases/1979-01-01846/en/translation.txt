Description: On January 13, 1979, starting at 8:00 PM, several witnesses observed in the sky a luminous object of various colors emitting reflections. The observation lasted approximately thirty minutes. The object was located to the right of the Moon, but there is no information on the distance between the Moon and the observation. The witnesses likely made an astronomical observation that could be either Procyon (magnitude 0.40), particularly visible during this time of year, or Sirius, whose reflections of different colors are well-known and, in this case, enhanced by observation with binoculars. Jupiter is also particularly visible, but to the left of the Moon, which does not correspond to the testimony. These witnesses were absent for three hours and upon their return around 12:00 AM, they spotted the same luminous phenomenon but farther away and which had moved towards the South, corresponding to the natural movement of the stars.
