Description: On July 7, 2021, at 11:53 PM, while observing the sky, the witness reported the passage of two luminous spheres moving parallel above his home on a west-east trajectory. One of the spheres crossed paths with the other. These bright objects, of pale white color, moved quickly, with an observation duration estimated between 5 and 7 seconds. Only one testimony was collected. The consistency of this testimony is average: a single witness, no photos or videos. According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The relatively fast movement of the PAN and their appearance immediately rule out any meteorological, astronomical, or astronautical explanatory hypotheses. As the wind is blowing from the northwest and the PAN are moving from the west-southwest, they cannot be passive objects carried by the wind, such as balloons or Thai lanterns. The investigation revealed that the witness likely observed a small group of white-plumed birds, probably gulls, moving silently from their feeding site to their roost (see investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The witness made the observation from an ornithologically rich area near the Regional Natural Park of Camargue. The presence of wetlands conducive to bird nesting suggests that birds passing at night from their feeding grounds to their roosts, possibly near ponds located to the northeast.

- Silent and straight movement in a small group (gulls are gregarious birds that are usually silent at night).

- The actual shape of the birds and their wing beats were not observed, likely due to the observation distance. The birds' movement may have also been in a gliding flight during the very short observation duration.

- The visibility of the birds' white plumage is mainly due to contrast with the black sky background and the residual light emitted by Aigues-Mortes city streetlamps (30), giving them this dim appearance.

This conclusion is tempered by the fact that the witness, accustomed to observing the sky, might have recognized the birds, and it is difficult to reconstruct favorable lighting conditions for this observation. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of white-vented gull-like birds moving in a small formation towards their nocturnal roost.
