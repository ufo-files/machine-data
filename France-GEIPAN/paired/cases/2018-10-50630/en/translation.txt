The witnesses are unsure of what they saw. The case consistency is fairly good (see the investigation report): - Two direct, non-independent witnesses (friends), - An incomplete subjective reconstruction (unclear time and location), - No additional technical elements (photos or videos). The strangeness is relatively low with only one hypothesis considered. The general description of the PAN strongly suggests a meteor, given its luminous aspect, its very high apparent speed, and its very short observation duration, but no meteor could be linked to the observation, despite the various dedicated surveillance networks currently in place. Therefore, the hypothesis is probable but not certain. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of a meteor.
