Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named CORTE (2B) 2003 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On Saturday, January 11, 2003, around 6:15-6:20 PM, a driver and his passenger in a car on the RN193 heading towards Corte (2B) were surprised by the quick and silent passage in the sky of a bright green light with white flashes. The witnesses describe a circular shape. During the observation, which lasted a few seconds, the object moved on a south-north trajectory. The witnesses' description, in a clear night sky, is typical of an observation of an atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid: - short duration of a few seconds; - high angular velocity; - intense multiple colors, green (fluorescent) and yellow with halos. Here, the witnesses do not describe streaks but rather a round shape. T1 distinguishes in this circle various internal details, such as openings and position lights. T2 does not notice any details in this bright circle but describes it at the end as a "ray of light." There is no perception of noise, but the witnesses were in a car, and meteoroids are often silent for witnesses. Since 2003, the date of the observation, the GEIPAN's experience with testimony of this type has significantly increased, also in collaboration with specialized sites for collecting testimony (IMO, BOAM, FRIPON, REFORME). Given the frequently present descriptive elements during proven meteoroid reentries (observed with many overlaps over large distances on the ground, thanks also to the aforementioned sites), there is a strong probability that this is the case here. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a meteoroid reentry.
