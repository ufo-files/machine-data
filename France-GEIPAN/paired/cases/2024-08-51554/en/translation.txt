Description: The main witness T1, accompanied by her husband (T2), are returning from a concert in Basel. T1 is driving on the A35 motorway while his wife sleeps beside him. Around 23:52, just before the exit at Kembs/Sierentz, T1 noticed through his open left window three blinking red lights in the sky, as well as a white point identified as a star. These lights form an almost equilateral triangle, and flickers appear intermittently. T1 wakes her husband who tells her that he sees these lights and thinks it's an aircraft. T1 asks him to film the red lights. Half-asleep, his wife manages to record a video before putting her phone down and falling back asleep. T1 continues observation and notes that the red lights, initially stable, begin to shrink, appearing to move away towards Sierentz. After taking the Kembs/Sierentz exit, T1 gets out of their car at 23:55 and does not see any lights in the sky anymore. According to the witness, the observation lasted approximately 3 minutes in total. The case consistency is average: 2 witnesses are mentioned, but the PAN video, though several minutes long, only allows its identification for a few seconds. *According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation was able to demonstrate that there were actually two distinct, yet quite similar phenomena, which likely contributed to the confusion of T1: the presence in the area of an aircraft and reflections on the windshield (see the investigation report). • The examination of aeronautical charts shows that at the time of the observation, a military aircraft was in the observation axis, following the highway where the witnesses were located, going in the same direction (the witness states that the PAN was sometimes hidden by the trees bordering the highway). We were unable to formally identify this aircraft, but the fact that it moves in a zigzag pattern, that its IFF code is typical of a military code, and that its disappearance from aeronautical maps occurs in a straight line near the Luxeuil Air Base are elements that support the hypothesis. The lack of perceived noise is explained by the observation distance, the aircraft flying at high altitude, and the ambient noise emitted by the witnesses' vehicle on the highway. • The appearance of PAN and its apparent movement on the video suggest reflections, an hypothesis already considered by the gendarmerie investigation, even more so since the video is taken in the direction of the witnesses' vehicle windshield. An analysis of the video allowed for the formal identification of the source of this reflection: lights from an electric window lift control panel. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a military aircraft for the PAN observed visually and as "A": light reflections from the car's cabin for the PAN in the video.
