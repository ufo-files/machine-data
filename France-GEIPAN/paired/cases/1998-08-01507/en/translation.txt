Summary: Observation of the slow passage of a luminous sphere in the sky: observation of the Mir space station.
--- seg-4f62d8c0235befdfd795a40f [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type "C" cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as "C" in 1998 by the SEPRA previously named Cintegabelle (31) by the name of the gendarmerie brigade. On August 9, 1998, at 10:10 PM, a couple on their terrace observed the slow, silent, and zigzagging passage of a silver-light sphere in the sky that abruptly went out. The trajectory of the PAN was directed towards the Moon. Another witness in a city several dozen kilometers away also reported seeing this phenomenon: this witness could not be located. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, trajectory) with a perfectly known astronautic object: the Mir space station. Furthermore, Mir was indeed present in the observed sky area, while the witnesses did not mention it. At the end of its trajectory, Mir entered the Earth's shadow, marking its visual disappearance, as indicated by the witnesses. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (surprise, fatigue). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this weak strangeness case is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception with the Mir space station.
