**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases previously classified (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions. Through new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MONTHERME (08) 1980 is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases.

On October 12, 1980, a teenager reported an observation made the previous evening from her home to the gendarmerie. She placed a PAN in a field 200 meters from her residence. The entire family was interviewed. On October 11, 1980, at approximately 11:40 PM, T1 (14 years old) saw from her bedroom window a ball on the ground 200 meters away, resembling a flying saucer or a large cigar with a lighter yellow border than its orange center. After two minutes of observation, the PAN rose, projecting blue lights, and with an engine sound, heading towards "La Roche at 7 o'clock." 15-20 minutes later, it passed over the house, illuminating the garden and heading towards La Roche. T2 (her 15-year-old sister) came to T1's room when she called her parents. T2 saw a large, brightly yellow-lit ball illuminating the garden and the neighbor's garden, and "La Roche at 7 o'clock" located 500 meters away. The PAN stayed for a few minutes in the field behind the house before heading towards La Roche with a car-like sound. A few minutes later, it passed over the house, heading towards La Roche. Around 11:30 PM, T3 and T4 (the parents), notified by T1, observed 10-15 minutes at 200-300 meters a long-range, rotating, blue-colored light beam. A short starting noise was heard by T3 (but not T4, who is a bit hard of hearing). The ball was not seen by T3-T4 as it was obscured by trees. The gendarmerie found no evidence or related observations in the neighborhood.

These testimonies are notably strange only regarding what the children, T1 and T2, reported seeing—the ball that caused the lights. It is likely this is the reason for the initial classification of the case as D. This re-examination notes this strong strangeness, as well as the low consistency of testimonies, as nothing explains why the parents did not see the ball on the ground or in the air.

- For T1 and T2, the PAN remained on the ground for only 2 or a few minutes before taking off, while T3 and T4, who started observing after T1, could observe (with T1) for 15 minutes rotating lights and beams whose origin was not visible ("hidden by trees for T4") and either implicitly or explicitly (for T4) on or near the ground.
- T3 and T4 stopped observing without ever seeing the PAN take off. T3 does not specify what ended his observation, while for T4, it was the extinction of ground lights that led him back to his room.
- For T1 and T2, the starting noise is associated with the PAN's takeoff and flight. For T3, the noise is linked to the beginning of his observation of lights that never rose, and T4 (who did not hear the noise but is a bit hard of hearing) understood that the noise came later, once he had returned to his room.

It is worth excluding the possibility that the PAN had already departed when T3 and T4 arrived, and that they focused on residual ground lights. Indeed, T1 and T2 do not mention residual ground lights, and they would have drawn their parents' attention to the strangeness in the sky, leading them to see what T1 and T2 were observing.

It is curious that T3 and T4 describe lights seen (on the ground) without mentioning in their testimony the observation of a flying PAN by their children, even for sequential placement of observations or to report their children's emotions and statements. There is a form of distance from the parents regarding the children's testimonies. They strictly stick to what they observed.

Additionally, note that the testimony was initiated by the child, T1, at the gendarmerie. The gendarmerie then requested the deposition of other witnesses. T4 (the father) refused a first convocation to the gendarmerie. There are too many inconsistencies between testimonies to validate the main strangeness of the observation, consisting of these lights, balls, or cigars that fly and overfly the area. The most probable explanation is that there were indeed ground lights for an unknown reason. The observation of flying lights is highly suspicious.

Therefore, GEIPAN classifies the case as C, due to a lack of information.
