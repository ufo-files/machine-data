Summary: Observation of the passage of a bright point in the sky and a particular zigzag movement: observation of the passage of a satellite and autokinetic illusion.
--- seg-a46f183e0437a5dbaea14afb [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 4, 2011, at 10:59 PM, a witness observed a bright point moving from south to north across the sky. Intrigued by the object's zigzag movements and changes in intensity, the witness heard no noise during the observation. The object disappeared from the witness's field of view. The witness described it as a white glowing point crossing the sky from south to north, reminiscent of a satellite passage: at that time and on that trajectory, the satellite IGS1B, with a magnitude of 2 (like the North Star), was passing. Its brightness varied during the passage, depending on its exposure to the sun. Of course, from the ground, a satellite moves strictly in a straight line (or slightly curved), which does not correspond to the witness's testimony. The zigzag movements described by the witness could be due to an optical illusion, physiologically called autokinetic illusion, which occurs when the eye follows a glowing point on a totally black background. See pages 26 and 30 of the document: http://www.cnes-geipan.fr/typo3conf/ext/dam_frontend/pushfile.php?docID=9932 This case is classified as B: probable observation of a satellite passage with autokinetic illusion.
