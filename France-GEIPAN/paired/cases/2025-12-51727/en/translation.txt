Description: On December 10, 2025 at 10:22 PM, from the terrace of his home in San-Gavino-di-Tenda, South Corsica, the main witness (T1) observes to the southeast a PAN of point-like shape, with changing colors, shifting from white to blue, green, and red, and exhibiting particularly intense brightness compared to surrounding stars. According to the witness's estimate, this silent phenomenon is located at an elevation between 20 and 25 degrees. The witness (T1) filmed the PAN with his smartphone, keeping it perfectly still to avoid any parasitic movement, using a strong zoom. This method allowed for the observation of a slight lateral displacement of the phenomenon to the right, of a few millimeters. The night was particularly clear, offering optimal observation conditions. The observation lasted 30 minutes during which the PAN moved towards the north before returning to its initial position. A second witness, T2, residing in the same village also confirms the presence of the phenomenon. The consistency* of this case is judged good, although only T1 completed a questionnaire GEIPAN, thanks to the availability of an exploitable video of the PAN for the investigation. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The strangeness experienced by the witness is of little importance, as the PAN did not present any particularly exceptional characteristics to them. The hypothesis of an observation of the star Sirius, as an explanation of the phenomenon, is supported by the following elements: - Spherical shape in the videos, an effect caused by using a strong zoom, for a point-like object. - Significant and rapid variations in brightness and colors, giving the impression of a flickering. These elements are typical of the twinkling of a low star on the horizon as light from a star is scattered by the turbulences of the atmosphere (layers of varying density and temperature) in favor of this twinkling effect for the observer (Annex 1 - Figure 1). The "particularly clear" night reported by T1 is confirmed by weather records from the Bastia (2B) weather station, which show exceptional horizontal visibility (60km) at 10 PM, decreasing to 35.7km at 11 PM while remaining significant (Annex 1 - Figure 2).

The astronomical situation, as depicted using the Stellarium software, shows the star Sirius in the rising phase in the southeast at the time of observation, moving towards the south, therefore to the right, as reported by T1.

Its elevation varies between approximately 14° and 19° between 10:22 PM and 11:02 PM, close to the elevation estimated by T1 (20° to 25°) as shown in Annex 1 - Figure 3.

The described movement "to the right" in the QT, therefore towards the south, is confirmed in Annex 1 - Figure 3 and Annex 2, which presents an animation of the observed movement from the video processing provided by the witness.

The disappearance was not observed, as T1 stopped the observation on his own, with Sirius continuing its course across the celestial vault. The residual strangeness after comparing the observation to the developed hypothesis leads the GEIPAN to classify this case as "A", observation of Sirius.
