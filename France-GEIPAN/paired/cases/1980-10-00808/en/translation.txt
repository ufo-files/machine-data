Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D (also known as MONTAUBAN (82) 1980) is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On October 6, 1980, at 6:00 a.m., a driver, stopped at a red light, spotted a low-flying, bright light emitting white neon-like light. In the shape of a rocket, this craft moved in a circular arc above the houses. After a period of inactivity, the object took off vertically and appeared to the witness as an upside-down plate. The object then disappeared completely. No sound was heard during the observation, which lasted a few seconds. No other testimony was collected on this phenomenon. The witness provided numerous unexploitable indications (1.50m or 5 feet in size or diameter, approximately 50m distance, movement speed comparable to that of a watch trotter...) in the absence of angular references or known landscape markers. Excluding these indications from the analysis, there is little left of the testimony that can be exploited; it is thus possible to summarize it as follows:

- Observation for a few seconds of a bright white PAN in the shape of a "rocket" moving silently in a circular arc, then pausing, taking off vertically while assuming the shape of an "upside-down plate" and disappearing.

- We can infer from the testimony that the PAN was likely at a low angular height above the horizon: "it was at the level of the trees or the roofs of the houses." Although the gendarmes moved, no other exploitable information was collected. It would have been beneficial to clarify the following points:

- The exact position of the witness in his car. Was he in front of his home or elsewhere?

- The exact azimuths of the appearance and disappearance of the PAN;

- The initial and final elevation;

- The angular size of the PAN;

- The exact manner in which the PAN disappeared? Did it suddenly go out, gradually? Or did the witness lose sight of it?

In the face of the missing parameters, we cannot rule out a helicopter moving on the horizon, a bird illuminated by a spotlight, or even a much stranger phenomenon. This lack of data does not allow for further analysis. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as "C": Lack of reliable information.
