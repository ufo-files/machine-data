Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time and now being reexamined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation report. This observation case previously named PLAN DE CUQUES (13) 1994 was among cases classified "D" at the time and recently reexamined. On July 31, 1994, around 2:30 PM, witnesses observed in the sky a cylindrical shape associated with two round shapes of metallic color. One witness noticed two protrusions with blinking lights. The object moved silently, flying in all directions at a fairly high altitude. Two witnesses reported their observations to the gendarmerie. Many elements suggest a confusion with a fantasy balloon, blown about by the wind at high altitude (see investigation report):

- The direction of movement, consistent with that of the wind. Witness #1 provided fairly precise geographic indications allowing the trajectory of PAN to be reconstructed, which therefore moved according to a generally south-north axis, entirely consistent with the direction of winds recorded by the Marseille-Observatory station.

- The way PAN moves. Witnesses described it as: "It flew quite slowly, and made rounds on itself. It had the attitude of a dead leaf, while keeping its direction" ... "flew quickly in all directions" ... "the object that kept swinging."... This description is typical of light objects carried and blown about by a light wind.

- The description of PAN. Witnesses spoke of "a cylindrical shape, about the size of two vehicles, metallic in color, with two types of ears on the side. I also noticed two round 'things', like car headlights" ... "a more or less round shape of metallic color" ... "above this object [were] two protrusions from which blinked lights." The mention of a "blinking of lights" and to be related to the mention of a "metallic color." If the object is partly made of a highly reflective material, such as Mylar, and other non-reflective materials, its swinging in the wind, causing it to spin in all directions, and the random reflection of sunlight on its surface, will give witnesses the impression of "blinking lights." It could therefore be a beach fantasy balloon, escaped from Cassis and its surroundings, which are located in the extension of the trajectory of PAN towards the south, and where many places are suitable for leisure activities in the summer (beaches, coves...), especially during the day, on a summer Saturday. No other testimony was collected, even though the observation took place in broad daylight, on a summer Saturday, in an inhabited area (the village of Allauch). This also supports a low strangeness hypothesis, such as the one, argued elsewhere, of the fantasy balloon. The consistency of testimonies is rather weak. Although we have two testimonies, they are very brief and from dependent witnesses (friends). Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: balloon.
