Summary: Observation of the silent passage of three orange lights in the night sky: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-5cd1797e9c75bffea3be9973 [needs-review] ---
Description: On Friday, August 26, 2016, at 10:39 PM, a witness and his wife were surprised to witness the silent, NO-SE passage of three orange lights in the clear night sky. The size of these lights was compared to that of a golf ball held at arm's length. The three lights were positioned in a triangle. They were then obscured by trees in the landscape. Only one testimony was collected. The witnesses very likely saw a passage of Thai lanterns. Indeed, releases are frequent on summer evenings, and especially on weekends, and it is common for them to appear as an orange light triangle as described. The wind, recorded at 4 km/h on Météociel, is from the north, consistent with a real or perceived movement from NO to SE. The reported observation duration is low (5 seconds), but the observed angular movement of the PAN in the sky is also necessarily limited (30 or 40° between the house to the south and the tall trees to the north at a distance of ten meters). This remains consistent with a movement of lanterns at low altitude (50 or 100 meters) considering the uncertainties or errors possible in PAN observations: here, a probable overestimation of the double (factor 2) or more of the reality of wind speed (6 km/h recorded at 4 km/h) or observation duration. The witness's main objection to this hypothesis comes from the feeling of having seen a solid surface between the three bright points. This is also classic of observations of point lights at night: the eye fills in the void! This objection is based on the observation of hidden stars during the passage. This very precise observation seems difficult to ensure given the speed of the entire observation, and since it did not appear sufficiently clear and strange for the witness to immediately report it (and not secondarily). Consequently, the very probable explanation is that of Thai lanterns. The Geipan classifies the case as B: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
