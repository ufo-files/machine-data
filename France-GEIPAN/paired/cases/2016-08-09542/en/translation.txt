Summary: Sequential observations (with the naked eye and then with a telescope) of a bright point in the night sky with movement and varying colors: observation of the star Capella.
--- seg-8c59b9e5524773208c1e6522 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: A witness reports that since August 22, they have been intrigued by a stationary glowing object in the sky seen from several different locations. The witness sees with the naked eye a colorful glowing object that moves from left to right and then returns to its position. He also distinguished, with the naked eye and telescope, colors red, blue, green. Other people are witnesses to the phenomenon. Only one testimony is collected. The witness saw a star. In the direction indicated (N-NE), it is Capella, but the witness later states that through his astronomy club, he concluded that he had seen Arcturus, which is full west and higher in the sky (30°). Considering the witness knows the directions from his house, the GEIPAN would rather hold Capella. This observation is interesting as it illustrates a typical (and acknowledged by the witness) optical illusion of a star's movement when fixed upon ("I noticed it was moving from left to right, then down, then back to its place"). As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Capellla.
