Summary: Observation of the appearance of two constant glowing balls moving silently and at the same speed, followed by two more balls: observation of celestial lanterns.
--- seg-77e9d743faf436e289306aa0 [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 7, 2019, around 12:30 AM, the witness is in their garden. Looking north, they observe two fairly large orange orbs moving parallel to each other, silently, without flickering, with constant brightness, at the same speed, on a west-east trajectory, and at a distance estimated between 500 and 1000 meters. Two additional orbs join the first two, forming a trapezoid in the sky, flying in formation. Subsequently, these orbs disappear one by one into the sky. Although there are two witnesses, the consistency of this case is average: one testimony, no technical questionnaire (only a gendarmerie report), a photo but without its original source. (see the investigation report). The investigation concluded that the observation was of sky lanterns. The description of the PAN is entirely typical of this type of observation:

- Date and time: The observation occurred around 12:30 AM during the night of a Saturday to Sunday, a traditional time for releasing sky lanterns during private celebrations, such as a wedding.
- The orange color of the orbs is entirely typical, confirmed by the use of the IPACO photo analysis tool.
- The apparent trajectory of the PAN, from west to east, is perfectly consistent with the wind direction.
- The gradual disappearance of the lanterns is explained by their simple extinction, reaching the end of their lifespan. The witness observing the second group of two lanterns joining the first is explained by a simple difference in distance, the second group likely being closer and moving visually faster than the first until they joined.
- Finally, the absence of rain and the light wind at the time of observation are ideal conditions for a sky lantern release without risk.

The witness was informed of this hypothesis on July 8, 2019, and on July 13, they refuted it for the following reasons:

- "The spheres were larger, of identical size, orange in color without wavering or difference in light intensity between them, moving on a straight trajectory, west-east, at the same speed, in a perfect trapezoid formation, two by two at a constant altitude."
- "Furthermore, we provided a photograph showing a light trace of the phenomenon. Have you considered performing a spectrographic analysis of this light?"
- "Have you also considered asking aviation authorities if an observation was reported by a commercial airliner, or if they have radar echoes at that time?"

Regarding the first two points, it has been shown that, on the contrary, these elements are typical of the description of sky lanterns, including the color analysis of the photo. Regarding the last point, the time elapsed between the observation and the start of the investigation did not allow for the recovery of radar traces from the Air and Space Force. Given this, the hypothesis of sky lanterns is robust enough to exclude the aeronautical hypothesis. The case is classified as A, observation of sky lanterns.
