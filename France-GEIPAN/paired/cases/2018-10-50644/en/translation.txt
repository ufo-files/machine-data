Summary: Observation and photography of a very bright PAN in the daytime sky: Lack of reliable information.
--- seg-c063f301acdbb0d3738bb019 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 13, 2018, a witness at a playground was intrigued by a bright light in the sky. He described a PAN that appeared to be composed of two sun glints on a central object. The witness quickly took photos with his smartphone. Only one testimony was collected. It is particularly difficult to characterize the PAN in this case (see the investigation report). Indeed:

- The absence of reference points in the photographs does not allow for precise localization of the PAN in space, nor does it enable assessment of any potential movement, let alone the distance it is from the observer, or its dimensions. It could be very far away (as the witness suspects) and of great size, or conversely, much closer and of smaller size;

- The PAN itself appears blurry, its shape hard to discern. This is due to the poor quality of the optics and sensor used (those of an iPhone 4s), which produces a significant amount of background noise. This smartphone is also unsuitable for taking photos of small and distant objects; under these conditions, any potential depth-of-field analysis that could have better defined the dimensions of the PAN and its distance from the witness, cannot be performed;

- The PAN being of very small angular size (less than 0.5°) and observed against a uniform blue sky background, without reference points, it is understandable that the witness had difficulty assessing its shape and any potential movements satisfactorily.

The witness, however, put forward a hypothesis: confusion with a stratospheric balloon. We sought to deepen this lead online and with the dedicated balloon study service at CNES, without result. Although dimension and distance estimates made using the IPACO software on the photos could be consistent with the hypothesis, the absence or imprecision of all other parameters do not allow it to be validated. The lack of consolidated data does not allow for a satisfactory conclusion. The GEIPAN classified the case as 'C': lack of reliable data.
