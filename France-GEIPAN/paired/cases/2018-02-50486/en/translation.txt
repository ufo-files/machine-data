--- seg-3097193b3d7401d85bb4facf [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The witness was completing a bicycle tour in the commune of Staffelfelden (68) on February 10, 2018 at 06:50 when they observed in the sky for approximately 5 seconds two very bright white orbs surrounded by a halo moving quickly and silently from east to west, leaving behind a short trail. The case consistency is average with only one witness and no photos of the PAN. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The description of the unidentified aerial phenomenon (PAN) closely matches the characteristics of a meteor, due to its very high speed of movement and its brief visibility duration, limited to a few seconds. This hypothesis is supported by the same-day reporting of a meteor observed from Colmar (Haut-Rhin), a geographically nearby location, with a similar trajectory to that described for the PAN (see investigation report). However, there are some differences between the two testimonies, particularly regarding color, brightness, and the absence of fragmentation mentioned in the meteor observation. There is also a gap of approximately 50 minutes between the reported times. This gap could be explained by a combination of unverifiable errors: possible confusion between local and universal time by the meteor witness, and a late estimate of about 20 minutes by the witness of the PAN. The zigzag observed at the end of the PAN's trajectory could be explained by a kinetic effect or by the witness's own head movement following the meteor passing directly overhead. The case is classified as "B", probable observation of a meteor.
