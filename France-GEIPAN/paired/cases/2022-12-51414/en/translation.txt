## GEIPAN Case Report Translation

**Description:** The main witness (T1), a helicopter cockpit pilot and flight engineer (T3) were conducting a training flight in the mountains. They were equipped with night vision goggles (JVN). The flight was planned to last between 45 minutes and 1 hour. Around 6:35 PM, while flying over the town of Oloron-Sainte-Marie (64), the helicopter was at an altitude of approximately 2,000 feet en route to Pau Airport. At that moment, the pilot noticed a horizontal moving light trail ahead, slightly higher than his altitude and located north of Pau. This trail was preceded by a "large, slightly blurred oblong shape," which the pilot estimated to be "larger than a commercial airliner." The light trail was roughly twice the length of the shape and connected to it, while its speed was remarkably high. Using his night vision goggles (JVN) to observe the phenomenon, the pilot noted that the light trail appeared white, while the shape was dark (with these goggles, colors reveal objects through green contrast ranging from pale green to very dark green near black).

Witness T1 followed the trail and shape with his gaze, querying T3. Then, the trail and shape "evaporated" to the west of Pau Airport, lasting approximately 4 seconds. Oriented northwest, T1 observed another light trail appearing to the south, moving on a north-south axis between the towns of Orthez and Lacq. He estimated the height of this object to be "approximately 1,000 to 2,000 feet" above his altitude, moving in a level flight path. The speed of movement was very fast, and the object vanished again to his left rear as it moved towards the mountain range. This phenomenon lasted approximately 2 seconds. About 30 seconds later, the same phenomenon occurred exactly on the same axis. Same height, and it disappeared to his left rear again. T1's attention then shifted to the left side of the craft. About a minute later, T1 observed the same phenomenon again, also noticed by the mechanic (T3). The mechanic described this PAN as a glowing light trail with a more rounded head, gradually stretching out. He compared its light to "some sort of combustion" and did not observe a dark mass at the front of the trail. The movement of this PAN was above the horizon to the west of the helicopter's position, following a horizontal trajectory at very high speed, but "less than [that of] a shooting star."

The witness then contacted Pau Tower Control to check for any information regarding rapid aircraft movements in the area. Tower control indicated they were unaware of any military air activity in the area and no aircraft were detected on radar screens. After a few moments, controller (T2) reported he had also just observed, with the naked eye, a glowing light trail that quickly faded to the west of his sector. T2 described this PAN as a fixed glowing point moving horizontally southwest on a north-south axis, observed for 2-3 seconds before gradually fading away. This time, neither T1 nor T3 saw the phenomenon. The total duration of observations by T1 and T3 lasted approximately 3-4 minutes. Although no photos or videos of the PAN were taken, consistency is very good with three complete witness accounts, particularly that of T1, and an investigation conducted by the Air Transport Gendarmerie. The witness accounts are generally of excellent quality, complete, and recorded relatively soon after the observation (approximately three weeks to a month according to the witnesses).

**Consistency Analysis:**

According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a given testimony. Two hypotheses were considered (see investigation report):

1. **Aeronautical Hypothesis:**  This could not be fully explored by GEIPAN due to the late receipt of the testimony. However, it was examined by the Gendarmerie, who consulted the National Air Operations Center (CNOA) and reported that radar trace analyses did not match the identified observation.

2. **Double Observation Hypothesis:** This hypothesis was chosen, based on the following elements:

   - Extremely short observation duration (2-5 seconds).
   - Witnesses' descriptions of a combustion impression, ejecta of material, and sparking of sparks are typical of meteors.
   - Straight-line trajectory. The horizontal orientation of this apparent trajectory is caused by a simple perspective effect, being perpendicular to the observation axis. The trajectory of the first meteor is typical of a bolide, and those of the following three are consistent with the position of the Gemini radiant, low on the eastern horizon to the northeast.
   - The observation occurred a few hours after the peak of the Gemini meteor shower, one of the year's most significant showers, with a theoretical maximum hourly rate of 120/150 meteors.
   - Speed calculation for the first PAN (approximately 20 km/s) falls within the lower range of known bolide speeds (between 11 and 70 km/s). This speed aligns well with T1's impression of relative slowness compared to shooting stars he is accustomed to observing.
   - The oblong shape followed by a glowing trail, typical of a meteor, although T1's description of the first PAN using night vision goggles (a dark shape) is unusual.

   We unsuccessfully searched for evidence of these meteors in automated video capture systems (FRIPON and BOAM) and the International Meteor Organization (IMO) visual testimony database, typically used by GEIPAN. The early observation time (when night was not fully established) and the low brightness of the meteors (about which the witness provided no details) might explain the absence of detection by FRIPON and BOAM cameras, as their detection threshold might not have been reached.

For these reasons, GEIPAN classifies this observation as a "B" - probable observation of a meteor atmospheric reentry.

**Note:**

* FRIPON: Fireball Recovery and InterPlanetary Observation Network
* BOAM: Base des Observateurs Amateurs de Météores
