Description: On June 5, 2017 (not 2015, as incorrectly indicated by the witness in the free text) between 11:58 and 12:03, a witness observed in the clear sky a phenomenon that intrigued them. The witness sees in the northwest at an average altitude, 2 lights projected side by side, like 2 beams of headlights. He distinguished behind these two headlights a kind of cylindrical fuselage, all progressing towards the East horizontally. "The type of vessel" progresses at the speed of a low-flying aircraft, then stops once in flight appearing stationary, then climbs in altitude practically vertically. A medium-altitude commercial aircraft is coming from the northeast and appears to intercept the initial trajectory of the "ship." The aircraft continues its course, the "ship" resumes horizontal flight with its original heading; then it seems to stop a second time, only to climb almost vertically again, until it completely disappears from the witness's view. The witness believes he is dealing with an unknown vessel that was disturbed in its trajectory by the medium-haul aircraft and requests the GEIPAN to investigate. Only one testimony is collected. As indicated in the attached aeronautical traffic log, the witness is located in an area of high activity (at least at that time) with aircraft at all altitudes, including recreational aircraft making low-altitude passes. Under these conditions, one does not see where the strangeness would lie in the witness's description. A low or medium altitude aircraft is distinguishable by its headlights (which can be lit during the day as well) and its fuselage. The low-altitude aircraft can perform all types of maneuvers (for example, school maneuvers) including speed reductions, heading changes that the witness interprets as significant speed reductions (when the heading changes from "transverse to the sight" to "along the sight") as well as sudden altitude changes. The stationarity or vertical ascent cannot easily be assessed from the ground, indeed the witness uses the words "it seems" or "almost." Under these conditions, the strangest part of all this is indeed the scenario the witness imagines, where the low-flying plane is an unknown vessel that alters its course to evade (or the vision of?) the commercial plane's trajectory. Once it is verified (through an aeronautical context analysis, for instance) that the observation itself does not report anything unusual, the GEIPAN does not dedicate any further resources to exploring or invalidating the scenarios put forth by the witness to interpret or explain an unsubstantiated oddity. Note that, if necessary, the observation was made in broad daylight, in an urban area, and thus a genuine oddity would have been difficult to miss by the large number of potential witnesses on the ground and... in flight. Finally, note that the same witness made a similar testimony two months later (EMERAINVILLE (77) 04.08.2017). As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: aircraft.
