Description: While commuting to work on the morning of September 18, 2019, a witness observed in the sky a white equilateral triangle object with a yellow area at its base. The PAN remained stationary and was observed for 10 seconds. The witness continued on their way, but when they looked up again, the PAN was gone. Only one statement was collected. While the two most plausible explanatory hypotheses, based on the collected elements, are compatible with the behavior of the PAN (apparent immobility), they prove insufficient in light of the provided description (see investigation report). Indeed, these two hypotheses, respectively an "extender on a crane" (or "pinnion") and a "condensation trail of an aircraft," only partially account for the witness's description. As a result, the strangeness remains quite high. Despite the investigations conducted, both remotely and on-site, the amount of information gathered is rather satisfactory. However, the absence of other witnesses and the lack of any other objective elements (photos or videos of the PAN) prevent the case from being strengthened. The witness is somewhat imprecise (doubtful about their own research?) in their estimates to describe the phenomenon: some data involuntarily evolve during the investigation (apparent size, azimuth, altitude, shape, etc.). Finally, even though the witness's cooperation in the investigation was concrete, the reliability of the testimony seems compromised by the fact that it may have been influenced by a UFO environment related to their personal research to understand the observation, both before and after the event. They are undoubtedly sincere about having observed something unusual, but it seems they are trying to convince (and convince themselves) of the oddness of their vision. Due to its unexplained residual strangeness, this case has real interest, but its reliability has likely been affected, reducing its consistency. The GEIPAN case is classified as C due to a lack of reliable information.
