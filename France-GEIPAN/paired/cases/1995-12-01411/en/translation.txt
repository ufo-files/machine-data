Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case previously classified as D and named SAINT-GERMAIN EN LAYE (78) 1995 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On Saturday, December 23, 1995, around 2 a.m., a witness and his friend observed a huge, round object in the shape of a saucer slowly spinning on itself and moving up and down over a house, eventually stabilizing over a field. The witnesses observed lights all around. No noise was heard during the observation. The witness, overcome with fear, returned home, looked out the window, but the object was gone. Only one testimony was collected. The PAN observed by the witness is likely just a local disco laser, also known as a "sky tracker," projecting its beams onto the low cloud layer present that day (as detailed in the investigation report). The description of the phenomenon: the witness describes "a huge round shape" ... "not very high off the ground" ... "below this object, we could distinguish lights all around, it was slowly spinning on itself, moving up and down, and then it got closer to me and stabilized over a field" ... "it didn't make any noise." This description is characteristic of the appearance and behavior of a skytracker, as lasers can spin on themselves, rotate in a circular motion, move back and forth, and stop. Laser beams are not always visible. This depends on the atmospheric conditions between the ground and the base of the clouds, which can contain suspended particles, such as water droplets, dust, pollen, or pollutants. No information is available on this for the period in question.

Location: The witness indicates a house located in a known place to the northwest of his position, which faced the phenomenon. However, along the extension of this known place, at a few degrees off and a bit farther away, is a disco called "L'Arlequin," which may have used a skytracker (they already existed in 1995). The existence of this disco is documented as early as 1993. The observation took place one weekend night, when discos are most active. The conditions allowing the visualization of laser beam impacts are quite specific, involving the type of atmosphere, clouds, and ambient lighting. A single disco can create its effects in different locations within a 10-20 km radius around it.

The GEIPAN website (www.geipan.fr) contains similar cases (perform a case search using keywords like "Skytracker," "laser," or "Sky Rose"). Therefore, the GEIPAN classifies this case as B: Sky Rose.
