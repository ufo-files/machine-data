**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions' relevance. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This previously classified observation D, named LES OLLIERES (07) 1979, is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases.

On July 21, 1979, a first group of people (T1 and his wife) observed from 10:45 PM to 11:28 PM a stationary luminous phenomenon with colored lights at the top of a ridge line in the direction of "Serre de Pieroulet." The PAN was described as a "beacon" with a powerful yellow-orange light source projecting a blue beam that rotated regularly over 360 degrees, scanning the ground. During the 45-minute observation, no unusual noises were heard. T1 was about to take a photograph when the PAN suddenly went dark.

The following day, these initial witnesses learned that their closest neighbors (T2 and his wife), while closing their shutters around 11:00 PM, had noticed a luminous PAN in the same direction. T2 and his wife observed the PAN for about ten minutes before going to bed. The Gendarmerie was notified the next day by T1. Only T1 and T2 provided testimonies.

The PAN was exceptionally well described by the main witness, T1. His report allowed us to realize that the appearance and behavior of the PAN were very peculiar and did not correspond to any known phenomenon, natural or artificial (see investigation report). The only plausible hypothesis would be a helicopter (as the PAN is clearly stationary in the sky) using a spotlight, but inexplicably, its beam would be blue, a different color from the source, and it would rotate constantly without any surveillance or lighting objective.

Unfortunately, T1 is no longer alive, making it impossible to further advance the investigation, which could have included cognitive interviews or on-site reconstructions. The other three witnesses could not be located, but it's also possible, almost 40 years later, that they are also deceased. T1's scientific expertise domain is closely related to light and color, as well as their impact on the environment. Notably, his observation concerns luminous and colored phenomena that fall precisely within his expertise domain. This coincidence reinforces the consistency of the facts, given the precision and completeness of the description:

- The witness uses descriptive parameters that he is familiar with;
- In terms of reliability, the wide recognition T1 already enjoyed does not allow us to imagine a particular desire to add strangeness to an observation for personal gain. On the contrary, the attitude of recognized scientists generally encourages the greatest distance from the PAN.

In this context, the testimony is representative of a genuine strangeness experienced and a knowledge stake for this scientist. T1 did not take any photographs. He is a priori in possession of a camera, but it makes sense that the witness takes his equipment when the PAN disappears. We can regret the poor quality of T2's testimony, which is vague and lacks the testimonies of the other two spouses.

However, T2's testimony, in addition to placing the PAN in the same direction and at the same time as T1, confirms his description on the main points of strangeness: fixed yellow/orange glow, blue light scanning the ground. T2 does not mention the beam, and the strangeness experienced seems minor (witnesses stop observing and go to bed).

Following the current GEIPAN methodology, this level of strangeness and consistency requires a "field investigation" with the witness for a reconstruction and cognitive interview. This last step can positively or negatively impact the evaluation of strangeness and consistency. However, it is not possible for a case revisited so long after.

Thus, the evaluation of this case is made according to the current criteria for considering strangeness and consistency (see GEIPAN methodology), as if a field investigation had been conducted and was completely neutral (no additional input, positive or negative). Sometimes, this is the case. Conversely, to mark this methodological gap, the classification is based on A, B, C, D, not the current A, B, C, D1/D2 which corresponds, for unexplained classifications, to a stronger validation of the unexplained character.

Here, the case is the boundary between an unexplained case and an exploitable case due to a lack of consistency in the current state of the file, lacking additional input from a GEIPAN on-site investigation. Consequently, the case is classified as D: Strange phenomenon with average or strong consistency.
