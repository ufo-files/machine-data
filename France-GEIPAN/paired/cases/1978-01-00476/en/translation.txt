Summary: Observations of the slow movement of a rectangular, red-orange light phenomenon in the sky; presence of a halo on the long sides of the rectangle: observation of a sun pillar.
--- seg-987391964a31eda5d8ffddb9 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: This observation case was subject to a new review (see the investigation notes document). On January 13, 1978, around 5:40 PM, a driver and then her husband observed the slow movement of a rectangular, reddish-orange light phenomenon in the sky; a halo on the longer sides of the rectangle was noted. No unusual noises were heard during the observation phases. No ground traces were found, and no other testimony was collected by the gendarmerie regarding this phenomenon. This case is consistent (two witnesses), and the description of the PAN observed is accurate. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a solar phenomenon: a sun pillar. Additionally, the Sun was just setting below the horizon directly in the axis indicated by the witnesses. The winter season and the presence of a few clouds favored the observation of a sun pillar. The issue is not the witnesses' visual perception but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (surprise, observation of a rare light phenomenon). In the current GEIPAN classification, this low-strangeness case is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception with a sun pillar.
