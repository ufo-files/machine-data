Description: On October 7, 2021, at 6:00 PM, the sole witness of this case, driving their vehicle on D5E4 at the exit of Porge (33) towards Saumos (33), observed through the left side window, in a northwest direction, several circular cloud formations. He stopped on the side of the road to photograph the PAN and then continued on his way, without being able to observe them further, as trees blocked the view. Upon arrival in the commune of Sainte-Hélène (33) around 6:40 PM, he again observes, in the direction of the west, a phenomenon identical to the previous one, but with "three curved branches." The witness photographs, once again, the PAN, before ceasing observation. According to GEIPAN criteria, the consistency* of this case is considered good: one late testimony, but several photographs of PAN produced by the witness, which could be analyzed (*consistency is the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony). The investigation determined that the PAN observed were likely condensation trails produced by a military aircraft participating in an ongoing exercise in the observation area (see investigation report). Analysis of the photographs reveals that the appearance of the PAN is consistent with condensation trails. The circular shape of the PAN is consistent with aerial maneuvers such as loops, typical of military exercises. In addition, a military exercise named "Volfa 2021" was taking place in the geographical area nearby at the time of the observation. An independent internet user based in Arcachon (33) photographed formations similar to PAN, identifying them as circles created by planes. Military officials contacted by email confirmed that the observation area corresponds to the location of the "Volfa 2021" exercise and that the observation is likely related to this activity. However, the investigation did not allow for the precise identification of the aircraft responsible for the PAN. The late witness contact with GEIPAN, several months after the observation, prevented the obtaining of an air traffic report from the CNOA (National Center for Air Operations). The only aircraft located in the area by radar trace (source: Globe.adsexchange.com) were flying at insufficient altitudes to generate condensation trails as observed. The case is classified "B": very probable observation of condensation trails from the propulsion of one or more aircraft.
