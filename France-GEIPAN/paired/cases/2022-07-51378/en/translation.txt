Summary: Observation of the movement of a PAN bright light with peculiar movements in the night sky: observation of the ISS (International Space Station) coupled with a kinetic effect.
--- seg-8672b2955962772f2cb99023 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 28, 2022, around 10:30 PM, the witness (T1) was outside on his terrace in Senas (13) when he noticed the presence in the sky of a PAN resembling a satellite, but much larger and brighter, moving in a straight line from west to east. He observed that its movement consisted of a series of slowdowns, accelerations, and brief stops. After 30 seconds of observation, he called his wife (T2), who also confirmed the presence of the phenomenon. The PAN eventually diminished in size in place and then disappeared, briefly turning a red color. The consistency* was average, with only one of the two witnesses reporting their observation to the GEIPAN and no photos or videos of the PAN taken. *According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation revealed that the witness very likely observed the International Space Station (ISS) (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Round shape, typical of the description given by witnesses of a brightly lit satellite like the ISS.
- White color.
- Generally straight movement.
- Presence, at the time of observation, of the ISS confirmed by orbital data provided by the Space Surveillance Service of the CNES.
- Observation duration of approximately one minute for an effective visibility of a little over three minutes. Possible underestimation of this duration by the witness.
- Initial and final elevations and azimuths provided for the ISS by the Operational Space Surveillance Center of the CNES generally correspond to those given by the witness, who may have had difficulty accurately orienting these positions at night during a brief observation.
- Disappearance due to a very low elevation, making it more visible to the witness, and a brief color change that turned red due to atmospheric dispersion effects.

The witness's perception of jerky movements of the PAN is considered relative to the perception of saccadic eye movements. It is analyzed that this perception could be caused by a physiological phenomenon called micro-saccadic eye movements or autokinetic, an illusion caused here by the dark sky, poor in reference points, leading to eye drift (see a detailed description available on the Méprises du ciel website). The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the ISS.
