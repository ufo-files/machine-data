Description: On September 17, 2017, between 10:00 PM and 11:00 PM, a witness is at their home (top floor of an apartment building, in an urban area) lying on a mattress on the floor and observing the sky through the window. He sees three faint lights arranged in a triangle that question him by their unusual appearance. The witness then sees detaching from a cloud, the outline of a dark and triangular object. As the PAN is about to exit the window frame, the witness stands up to open their window. The PAN quickly disappears behind the roof of his building. The observation lasts less than 10 seconds. Only one testimony will be collected. Given the following objective elements: • Black night, scattered cloud cover, light to moderate wind; • Short observation duration (10 seconds); • Several dim, yellow-orange lights observed with a dark mass; • Regional environment with high density of civilian and military aeronautics, at the intersection of civilian and military air routes; the investigator's favored hypotheses are: misperceptions with Thai lanterns or civilian or military aircraft, or a civilian or military drone (see the investigation report). Regarding the Thai lanterns, the main argument in favor of this hypothesis is the appearance of the PAN (several low-intensity lights moving jointly) and the witness's perception of a single object, which is typically described in reports of Thai lantern releases. However, the hypothesis of Thai lanterns, partially compatible with the description, is not considered probable given a wind opposite to the PAN's trajectory at the time of observation. In an area with high aeronautical density and near a military air base, the airplane hypothesis is also considered by the investigator. The hypothesis of a civilian aircraft cannot be maintained, as the observation angle from the window does not allow for a clear view of aircraft on the existing aeronautical route, and the radar echoes are not in the correct direction. The hypothesis of a military aircraft could explain the main factors of strangeness perceived by the witness, the unusual aspect of the lights arranged in a triangle, low intensity. This could lead one to think of a military flight in training from the military air base located south of Louviers, at very low altitude (not radar echo). But, in this case, the noise of the plane's engines would have been perceived by the witness (open window). Additionally, there is no confirmation of a military flight from the base. The airplane hypothesis is therefore not considered probable. The last hypothesis proposed by the investigator is a drone flight, either civilian or military. The variety of shapes, sizes, and appearances possible for drones in general allows for consideration of the perceived strangeness by the witness (an unusual sight for someone accustomed to seeing planes overhead). The North-South orientation of PAN could suggest that it originated from the military air base located south of Louviers. But, the absence of an authorized air traffic corridor (especially at night) near the observation zone also tempers this possibility. Additionally, there is no confirmation of a drone flight from the base. The trajectory of the PAN does not present any strangeness (straight line, constant speed). However, the appearance of the PAN raises a strong question in the witness's mind (3 dim lights in a triangle with a dark mass in the center) that the hypotheses considered cannot explain. The lack of additional information (another witness, photos, video, time details) does not allow for further investigation of this case. This case is classified in category "C": lack of reliable information.
