**Description:** The witnesses are by their pool, near their home in the commune of Cessieu (38), on July 16, 2023, at 4:30 PM, when they observe several PAN in the sky, moving slowly from southeast to northwest. Witness (T1) describes these PAN as a line of 4 or 5 objects, themselves composed of three equal-sized luminous spheres, with a larger luminous sphere in the center. Two other identical PAN are nearby, outside the formation, and move in the same way. The consistency is good: there is only one testimony despite two witnesses, but a video of the PAN was provided. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation was able to show that the witnesses likely observed a cluster of Mylar fantasy balloons, moving at high altitude carried by a light breeze (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Spherical shape, frequently reported in such observations and common for such balloons,
- PAN grouped in clusters of 3 individuals, themselves aligned or moving alongside a main group,
- Brightness mentioned by T1 obtained by reflecting sunlight off the highly reflective surface of the balloons, as the observation was made during the day on a sunny day,
- Initial trajectory of the cluster of PAN straight and stable, the balloons being carried by the altitude wind.

The exploitation of fine-grid AROME* wind data provided by Météo France confirmed the existence, at altitudes between approximately 100 and 250 meters, of a wind consistent with the direction of movement of the PAN, from southeast to northwest. The speed of this wind is also low to very low, consistent with the slow movement of the PAN. *Application of Research to Operations at Mesoscale) is a model with a very fine mesh (1.3 km mesh) for forecasting in France (METEO France).

The witness expressed what they found strange about this observation, and each element of this strangeness could be explained in light of the studied hypothesis:

- The disappearance and reappearance of some of the PAN is entirely typical of balloons rotating on themselves during their movement, temporarily offering a less reflective surface to the Sun,
- The witness's mention of some PAN isolated from the main group suddenly moving in the opposite direction of the overall movement of the whole is entirely consistent with the existence of micro-gusts at high altitude (wind shear) that can occur at slightly different altitudes from that at which the main formation is located. However, it was impossible to formally demonstrate this, as the AROME data resolution did not allow it,
- Finally, regarding the a posteriori observation on the video, the lower resolution of smartphones compared to human eyes explains the witness's disappointment, as they do not see as many details on this video as they would with the naked eye. The clear or dark spots observed, moving quickly around the PANs, are likely video artifacts. Note that the day of the observation is a Sunday in the summer. It is possible that a party took place in the direction from which the PAN came, but the investigation did not confirm this. We could not question the pilots of the gliders flying nearby to see if they had seen these balloons.

GEIPAN classifies the case as 'A', observation of Mylar balloon type, carried by the wind.
