**Description:** The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to ensure more accurate conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The observation case previously classified as D and named PARIS (75) 1997 is part of a recent batch of cases re-examined.

On September 7, 1997, at 1:43 AM, a witness observed from their balcony a large triangular shape with two blue and one green light moving slowly on a straight trajectory. The observation lasted 12 seconds. No noise was perceived, but the witness felt vibrations in their body, and when they attempted to take a photo, their camera froze. Only one testimony was collected.

The testimony describes numerous oddities that, taken at face value, led the GEIPAN of the time (now SEPRA) to classify the case as unexplained. This new examination does not confirm this classification. The testimony lacks sufficient consistency, especially in terms of reliability, to validate its strange nature and is thus unusable. The elements of strangeness (unexplained) are strong and numerous but present significant differences and inconsistencies across their accounts: the gendarmerie's hearing report the next day and the witness's published account three months later, both provided by the witness to the gendarmerie.

The witness is the editor-in-chief of a "Strangeness and Mysteries" magazine and publishes their observation in their own publication. It's notable that they have an interest in their magazine having mysterious testimonies. However, we first focus on the inconsistencies between the two accounts of the observation:

- **Triggering the observation:** In one case (gendarmerie report), the trigger is visual—the witness first sees the triangle, observes it with binoculars and a camera, then feels vibrations in their body and on the windows. In the other (publication), the strange event starts with vibrations, not on the windows but on numerous plants on the balcony, with specific details about the synchrony with vibrations in their body, then they see the triangle in the sky. It's surprising the witness doesn't know exactly what started the strange event, and equally surprising that what appears to be the strangest element (vibrations around in the absence of noise) pertains to such different elements (windows, plants).

- **Duration:** 12 seconds in one case and over 17 seconds in the other, where it's noted that this was measured with a stopwatch. Why is the stopwatch mentioned three months later to provide a different information?

- **Angular size:** The UFO is covered by 3 fingers in one case, 4 in another.

- **Camera:** In one case, the witness reports the camera being unable to capture the image, stating only (without expressing any strangeness) that they didn't take any pictures. But three months later, it's a genuine strangeness: the camera refuses to take a photo when the shutter button is pressed, even after switching to manual focus.

These are enough elements to question the reliability of a single human testimony without a photo or other evidence, especially given the high level of strangeness reported, which (according to GEIPAN methodology) requires a very high level of reliability to characterize the observation as unexplained.

Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Unusable testimony. The fact that the witness provides their testimony in their own magazine about mysteries and the strange, and states their conviction about the reality of the strange and a conspiracy to hide it, is another element to question the reliability, although the inconsistencies noted are already sufficient to conclude on the reliability.
