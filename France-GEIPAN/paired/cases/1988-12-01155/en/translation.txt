Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This previously classified case D, named VERGT (24) 1988, is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On December 1st, 1988, around 8:20 a.m., a witness, accompanied by his dog, was walking along the road near his home. He saw at 60 meters a bright light, more intense than an arc welder. The witness described a round shape of blue-violet color in a gradient moving in a straight line S-N, silent, and at very high speed. During the observation of 3 or 4 seconds, the witness felt the effects of pressure and received an electric shock. The dog ran away towards the home, no longer responding to its master's commands. The witness made two statements to the gendarmerie, one on the day of the observation and another 10 days later. The elements of the first statement, as well as those collected on the scene, allow for the hypothesis of an electrical phenomenon because: - the glowing ball was observed in immediate proximity to the intersection of two power lines and a transformer. - the electric ball followed a movement aligned with one of the power lines. - a sudden and brief drop in voltage was observed by neighbors at the time of the observation. - the witness felt a pressure effect and an electric shock. A glowing ball can be produced by the ionization of air and molecules suspended in the presence of an intense electric field (here a priori resulting from a malfunction). (The glow of a neon tube operates with a gas ionized by an electric field). This type of phenomenon following power lines has already been observed, particularly after a storm (see the video). The initial analysis in 1988 did not provide an explanation as it took into account the second witness statement, which described several balls and located the phenomenon in the field, much farther from the road and the intersection of power lines. We now prefer to retain the first statement made on the same day, as the second one may have been influenced (including unbeknownst to the witness) by numerous contacts or solicitations from neighbors and the media. The knowledge of similar cases of lights along power lines also strengthens the hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies the case as B: observation of an electrical and plasma phenomenon.
