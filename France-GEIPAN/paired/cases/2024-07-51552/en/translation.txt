Description: On July 11, 2024, at 11:30 PM, a witness (T1) and his spouse (T2) observed a PAN from the backyard garden of a vacation home in PIA (66). They cannot explain what they saw. They describe "a triangle of 3 glowing white spheres." The triangular shape remained constant throughout its movement at a relatively high speed. Near the end of the observation, 2 light sources switched positions. The observation lasted about 10 seconds before everything went dark. The case consistency is average. Several information provided in the QT were modified, and there is no photo or video. However, the witness answered all the investigator's requests. He agreed to an exercise in size estimation and provided a sketch of the observation sequence. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Several hypotheses were explored (see the investigation report). Those of illuminated birds by urban lights and skytracers (laser projectors) were dismissed due to the intensity of lights perceived by the witness. The aircraft hypothesis is challenged by the actual size of the PAN estimated by the witness, in light of the distance to the identified plane on the CAPCODA* map and whose trajectory could correspond. The hypothesis of 3 festive lanterns is compatible and probable (multiple lights moving in the same direction, position change, sudden disappearance), and the direction of movement could be explained by a local micro-current of wind. A local festival took place that day, but a lantern release could not be confirmed during the investigation. It should be noted that unauthorized lantern releases are prohibited in the Pyrénées-Orientales (66) since 2008 for safety reasons (drought, heatwave, fire risk). Therefore, it is difficult to find people claiming these lantern releases. The case is classified as "B": probable observation of Thai lanterns. *CAPCODA: Air Planning and Conduct of Operations and Air Defense Center (Air and Space Force).
