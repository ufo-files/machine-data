Description: On November 2, 2025, around 23:25, from their home in Ellecourt (Seine-Maritime), two witnesses observed a series of unusual nighttime light phenomena in the sky. The observation, initially through a window, was continued from outside to obtain an optimal field of view on a dozen intermittent light sources slowly moving. These bright points followed a general trajectory from south-southwest to northeast, with an initial elevation of approximately 30° above the horizon and a passage near the vertical of the observation location. Although the general heading appears constant, observers noted perceived chaotic individual movements during a sequence lasting between five and ten minutes. The visual aspect evolved during the event: initially red and flashing, the sources turned white and fixed before gradually disappearing towards the northeast. No noise was perceived by the witnesses. The three video recordings made with a smartphone during the observation confirm the presence of blinking white points, although the image resolution does not allow for the distinction of precise morphological or structural details. The case consistency is judged average, due to the overall coherence of the testimony: only one of the two witnesses (T1) completed a QT, three videos confirm the light sources in the sky, however, their limited quality did not allow for in-depth analysis or formal identification of the observed phenomenon. According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. After analysis, the residual strangeness remains moderate. The phenomenon presents unusual characteristics for the witnesses (multiplicity, silence, light evolution), but does not highlight any abrupt maneuvers or behavior incompatible with known aerial objects. Several hypotheses were examined during the investigation. The hypotheses of aircraft types such as airplanes or helicopters were ruled out due to the absence of noise, the high number of light sources, their dispersion, and their non-conforming light behavior to these types of craft. The aerial map provided by CAPCODA* does not allow the overlaid tracks present to be aligned with the trajectory of the PAN. Astronomical or satellite hypotheses have also been ruled out, as the observed characteristics (low apparent altitude, chaotic movements, color changes, flickering) are not compatible with this type of phenomenon. The hypothesis of a drone group was prioritized. It appears compatible with several elements described by T1: multiplicity of objects, apparent slow speed, absence of perceptible noise, overall coordinated movement, as well as the change in light signature during its movement. The cartographic reconstruction of the trajectory made by T1, as well as the analysis of the local environment, do not reveal any major inconsistencies with this hypothesis. Verification steps were taken with companies operating drones capable of operating in the affected sector, but the responses provided were negative. No radar information regarding unconventional aerial objects was highlighted during the considered period, as civilian radar systems are not adapted to detect small drones. In view of all the elements collected and analyzed, the GEIPAN classifies this case as C, the phenomenon remaining unidentified due to a lack of objective data, despite the examination of a hypothesis consistent with the reported observations. *CAPCODA: Air Center for Planning and Conducting Operations and Air Defense (Air and Space Army).
