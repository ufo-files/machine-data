Summary: Observation in the sky of a luminous phenomenon in the shape of a ball with a flat upper part: mistaken for a gibbous Moon.
--- seg-32f4d51bad4204b2e9b08ccb [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 19, 1977, around 2 a.m., a motorist observed a luminous phenomenon in the sky while driving on D109. The PAN (unidentified aerial phenomenon) appeared as a ball with a flat top, emitting a pale glow and seemingly stationary above a valley. The witness decided to head in that direction and noticed the PAN moving at a moderate pace towards the West. The witness followed it for approximately thirty minutes without catching up. No other testimony regarding this phenomenon was collected by the gendarmerie. This observation case, formerly named Arinthod (39), is classified as "C" due to lack of information and is currently being re-examined (see document *Investigation Notes*). The case is considered consistent (one witness, accompanied by a dog that reacted to the presence of the PAN), and the description of the observed PAN is fairly precise. The witness's sincerity and credibility have never been questioned. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the setting Moon. Moreover, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, yet the witness made no mention of it. This is not a matter of the witness's visual perception but rather the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, driving at night). In the current classification of GEIPAN, this case of average oddity is classified as "A": observation related to a misperception with the gibbous Moon.
