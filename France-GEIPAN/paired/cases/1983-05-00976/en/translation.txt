Summary: Long observation by 7 witnesses of a luminous phenomenon in the sky: astronomical misidentification with Venus.
--- seg-35fbdd98197de5018cdc4808 [needs-review] ---
Description: In its publications, it includes old cases classified at the time as "Type C" cases and now being re-examined for the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified "C" in 1983 by GEPAN. In the night of May 9, 1983, between 10:30 PM and 11:30 PM, several witnesses observed, with the naked eye and binoculars, a luminous phenomenon in the sky. This brightness emanates from a ball that moves slowly and disappears several times in the landscape. When viewed with binoculars, the shape becomes triangular, and different lights appear. Seven witnesses will be interviewed by the gendarmerie. In the legend of the map provided by the gendarmerie, investigators indicated for the red arrow "direction taken by the phenomenon" while it only refers to the direction of observation. The witnesses indicate a movement either towards the NW or towards Saint-Jacques, that is, to their right when looking at the object. Contact with the Saint-Michel Observatory confirmed that night the passage of a comet. However, according to astronomers, the descriptions of the witnesses do not correspond to the description of the comet. This case is consistent (seven witnesses), the description of the PAN observed is quite precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witness have never been questioned. The described phenomenon has many common characteristics (long observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: Venus. Moreover, Venus was exactly at the angular height (17.5°) and azimuth (290°) described by the witnesses. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feeling (fatigue, surprise, presence of relief). In the current classification of GEIPAN, this low strangeness case is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception with Venus.
