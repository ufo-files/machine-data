Summary: Observations of silent movements of white balls in the cloudy sky: probable observation of laser impacts.
--- seg-ba40e8f1293bac9617a1906b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Friday, June 21, 2019, at 11:40 PM, a witness (T1) on their balcony observed several white balls rapidly moving, especially in circles, in the cloudy night sky. The witness called their son (T2), who in turn saw white balls spinning in the sky and filmed the scene. No noise from the phenomenon was heard during the observation, which lasted about ten minutes. The first witness (T1) left their apartment and exited the building but no longer saw the phenomenon. The witnesses likely observed laser impacts in the clouds. All described elements are compatible, if not characteristic, of this hypothesis: - a blurred, undefined light shape, appearing as if "behind the clouds." Indeed, the reflection of the beams is not clear or very bright, the effect is similar to that of light behind a light cloud (witness perception); - regular geometric movement (here circular), multiple lasers and impacts (here three), pursuit games between them. Note that T1 himself makes this hypothesis, but rejects it due to the alleged too high speed of movement in the sky and the absence of ground lights in the neighborhood. These are poor reasons. Indeed, laser impacts are known to move very quickly, and this is even one of their interests in animation. The visibility of laser beams from the ground rising into the sky depends on humidity and atmospheric pollution conditions, while the presence of clouds (which was proven that night) ensures a luminous impact of the beam in the sky. Furthermore, the distance between the vertical impact and the device location on the ground can be several dozen kilometers. It was a music festival night in Lyon, with many events. Some concerts may have used light spots, as shown in one of the photos in this link. The witness videos do not contradict this hypothesis. We do not see the spots in circular motion that the video could not or did not capture, while the lights crossing the field quickly can have various origins (for example, nearby insects illuminated by the flash) and produce an effect distinct from the visual description of the witnesses. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable laser observation.
