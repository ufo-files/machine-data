Summary: Nighttime observation of several silent, orange-glowing lights moving across the sky: probable observation of celestial lanterns.
--- seg-12572d156f11bf2349ecf6c1 [needs-review] ---
Description: The witness is outside their building in Oullins (69) on May 20, 2016, at 11:15 PM when they observe three bright orange triangular lights in the sky moving slowly, without flickering or noise. The witness films the scene with their smartphone and notes that they align, with the PAN at the bottom left, the brightest, gradually moving to the center of the other two and eventually becoming the only visible one, while the others dim gradually until disappearing. A fourth very bright, orange PAN is then noticed by the witness, in the bottom right of the others, moving towards their position. The witness starts a second video at this moment and observes that the fourth PAN moves from north to south, passes behind trees before moving away to the west. The entire observation lasted 5 minutes. The case consistency is good: despite a single witness, they were able to record two videos of the PAN which were analyzed. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation and video analysis revealed that the witness likely observed a group of sky lanterns (also known as Thai lanterns). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Point-like shape given the observation distance. The fourth PAN, the closest and brightest, shows a spherical shape, which is consistent with sky lanterns.
- Orange color, typical for lanterns.
- The light of the PAN flickers, clearly visible on the videos. This is entirely typical of lanterns, which consist of a candle placed at the center of a rice paper envelope with an open base, and thus subject to aerological conditions causing the candle flame to flicker.
- The observation duration, 5 minutes in total, is less than the known maximum lifespan of lanterns, around 20 minutes.
- The observation period, on a Friday evening in May, is suitable for releasing lanterns for a celebration.
- The movement is noted by the witness as being from Oullins towards Chaponost, thus an east-west or east-northeast to west-southwest movement. The wind recorded at the nearest station (Lyon-Bron Airport) was weak and variable (see Annex 1).
- The speed of the PAN movement is low, consistent with the weak wind speed (0 km/h and 4 km/h in gusts).
- The apparent movement differs between lanterns due to each one's varying altitude.
- Ascending trajectory without direction change: consistent with objects rising due to heat produced by the candles.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of sky lanterns.
