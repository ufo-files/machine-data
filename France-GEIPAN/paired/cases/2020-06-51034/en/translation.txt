Summary: Nighttime observations of the silent, straight passage of a PAN primarily consisting of two blue lights: phenomenon unidentified after investigation.
--- seg-37660dad799c5e4a3685dc3e [needs-review] ---
Description: The three witnesses are in a family garden in the middle of the countryside in the commune of Aunay-les-Bois (61) on June 3, 2020, when they observe, between 00:00 and 01:00, the silent passage of a PAN for a duration estimated, according to the witnesses, between 5 and 10 seconds. The PAN is described as primarily consisting of two blue luminescent sources, of comparable brightness, surrounded by a halo or several similar secondary luminescent points. The whole moves according to a horizontal trajectory, at a constant speed, and at a low elevation above the horizon (estimated at 15°). The witnesses' visual field is partially constrained by lateral hedges, but they observe the PAN approaching from south-southeast, then temporarily disappear behind a row of poplars before definitively exiting their field of vision towards the east. This case is considered consistent: several witnesses completed a technical questionnaire, made detailed sketches, and participated in cognitive interviews. However, it is noted that some answers to the GEIPAN questionnaires appear to have been provided after consultation between witnesses, as indicated in the investigation report. The strangeness of the observation persists after the investigation, in the sense that the phenomenon only partially corresponds to the characteristics of the explored hypotheses. Additional analyses (cognitive interviews, on-site visits, IPACO* analysis) did not allow the investigation to be oriented towards any of these hypotheses, nor to reduce the strangeness:

- Hypothesis of an atmospheric entry: Although the description may evoke an atmospheric entry, particularly of a fragmented body (possible blue color, also possible elongated shape and rectilinear horizontal trajectory consistent with the hypothesis), no observer or automated camera database has recorded such an event, which, in our opinion, should have been listed given the significant dimensions involved.

- Hypothesis of wind-carried objects (lit balloons): The wind direction is poorly compatible, if not incompatible, with the direction of movement of the PAN. This hypothesis indeed assumes that the balloons evolve at a sufficient altitude to roughly align themselves with the dominant wind; however, at this altitude, the measured wind speeds are significantly lower than the estimated speed of movement of the PAN, invalidating this lead.

- Hypothesis of one or several drones: The calculated speed is compatible for one or several drones located at most a few hundred meters from the witnesses. Civil or military drones can carry lights of various colors. However, civilian drone flights are strictly prohibited at night. Furthermore, a Marine National expert, drone instructor, was consulted regarding the lights used by military drones; he states that blue lights can be used but they are flashing. Finally, according to the angular data provided by the witnesses, it is impossible for it to be a single drone, since the PAN was seen passing behind a row of trees 200 meters away, which would result in a dimension greater than 9 meters. The use of several identical drones is still possible, but was considered unlikely. Without additional data, no hypothesis is satisfactory for this observation case, which has good consistency and maintains its high strangeness after investigation. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "D": Unidentified phenomenon after investigation.
