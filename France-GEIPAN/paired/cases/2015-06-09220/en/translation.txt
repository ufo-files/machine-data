Description: On Sunday, June 14, 2015, at approximately 5:10 a.m., a witness looking out their window was intrigued by the successive and silent passage of 7 to 8 orange, spherical, and luminous phenomena across the sky. The observation lasted approximately 10 minutes before they disappeared into the clouds. The witness had time to take photographs; unfortunately, they will be unreadable. The witness will go to the gendarmerie. No further testimony will be collected on this phenomenon. No explanation was seen in the local press in the days that followed. The observation of orange lights crossing the sky in a few minutes, on a weekend night, suggests Thai lanterns, likely released nearby after a private party. The movement is observed from southwest to northwest: if these are wind-carried objects, it would then be a south or southeast wind. The Meteorology Report (see the weather document), taken at the exact location of the observation (Dijon Air Base), states that the wind was very variable and indeterminate at 5:00, light from southeast at 4:00 to east at 6:00. This weather report neither confirms nor denies that the observed objects are indeed wind-carried objects. On the other hand, the GEIPAN requested a primary radar trace log around Dijon at that time: the log indicates only high-altitude commercial airliners. The photos taken by the witness prove to be unusable, evidence of the weakness of the lights observed. Despite a testimony compatible with Thai lanterns, the GEIPAN does not have enough elements to confirm this hypothesis, and classifies this case as "C" due to lack of information and cross-references.
