Summary: Short nocturnal observations of the silent passage of a dark PAN composed of triangular elements: probable misidentification with birds.
--- seg-4f0ef2a9972da928c72db220 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 30, 2017, two friends were sitting in lounge chairs on an outdoor terrace, gazing at the stars. They were suddenly surprised to see a dark, silent PAN with many triangular components moving across the clear sky. The whole thing quickly disappeared behind the house. The strangeness of the observation lies in the geometric shape attributed by T1 (much less by T2) both in the overall triangular aspect of the PAN and in the also triangular shape of its components. The mode of appearance and apparent transformations of the PAN add a bit of strangeness, but this is not incompatible with the favored hypothesis (see the investigation report). The testimonies are quite consistent as the witnesses observed calmly and testified very precisely during the on-site investigation. The accounts confirm each other despite inevitable differences in estimations between witnesses. The initial apparent strangeness diminishes greatly when considering the ornithological hypothesis, which fits well with many aspects of the described events. This hypothesis is all the more convincing as it accounts for both the discrepancies in description between the two witnesses and the differences in reactions and feelings revealed by cognitive interviews. The hypothesis of a misidentification with a flight of migratory birds appears the most probable, even if the strangeness remains significant. This is due to uncertainties in angular size and distance estimates, and the fact that it is very difficult to objectively assess a posteriori the reality of a bird passage. Therefore, there are no certainties or explanations regarding the witnesses' failure to recognize the birds, leading the GEIPAN to classify this case as probable misidentification. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable misidentification with birds.
