Summary: Observation of the joint movements of two white light points in the sky: mistaken for a duo of US military satellites NOSS-3 6.
--- seg-e163beeafbfa01b23274460c [needs-review] ---
Description: On June 13, 2014, at 12:56 AM, a witness was stargazing with their new telescope and noticed two bright points moving together (same distance apart). They lost sight of it permanently in the very starry sky. The witness reported their astonishment at the unusual movements of the Pan. Only one testimony was collected. The witness provided sketches and a photograph. Due to its low strangeness and good consistency (single witness, but numerous astronomical references and a photo), the case turned out to be a misidentification with a peculiar pair of American military satellites: a military satellite doublet NOSS-3 6. The PAN indeed exhibited all typical characteristics of such a doublet: brightness, angular separation, trajectory, and timing were perfectly consistent (see investigation notes). The photograph is unlikely to show the PAN, but only the area where it disappeared. The double star 16 Cygni, appearing here as two close points, was probably interpreted by the witness as the PAN. The PAN's stop phases can be explained by the discontinuity of observation: temporary disappearance of the PAN due to the moon's brightness and the presence of numerous stars. This case is classified as A, misidentification with the pair of American military satellites NOSS-3 6 (A) and (B).
