Description: On the evening of July 14, 2022, in the garden of his home in Avrieux (73), the witness was waiting for fireworks from neighboring villages when, around "22:15" or "22:23" according to his testimony, he saw a white ball appear in the sky, moving in a straight line. The PAN moved from southwest to northeast and was observed by the witness for approximately 5 minutes before being obscured by a mountain. The witness had time to record a video of the PAN with his smartphone. The consistency* is average: one unique witness and a video of the PAN that is difficult to use, with no context data present or visible. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation established that the witness had observed the International Space Station (ISS) (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The described shape: a "ball," typical of the description given by witnesses of a brightly lit satellite, such as the ISS. The possibility that the witness only describes this shape through what he sees in the video, rather than with the naked eye. However, there is no indication in the testimony to confirm this.

- White color.

- Approximately 5 minutes of observation duration, for a maximum possible total visibility of the ISS of 6 minutes 30 seconds.

- The actual presence of the ISS in the sky around 22:32, the time the video recording provided by the witness was created.

- Straight-line movement, according to a trajectory that is consistently aligned with the one estimated for the witness (appearance in the southwest and disappearance behind an east-northeast mountain, as the ISS had not then completed its entire trajectory).

We have also demonstrated that the observation environment, consisting of high mountains, limits the witness's visibility, who cannot observe the entire movement of the ISS with an initial and final elevation of 10°, which is undoubtedly located behind the mountains at these moments. The PAN is indeed lost from view, hidden by one of these mountains. The video recorded by the witness turns out to be unfortunately difficult to use, as it does not allow the movement of the PAN to be assessed. The device used, not having been properly focused, the PAN appears blurred. Classification as "A," observation of the ISS.
