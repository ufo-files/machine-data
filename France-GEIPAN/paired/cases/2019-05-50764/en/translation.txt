**Report:** On May 23, 2019, at 7:30 AM, the witness, while aboard their boat approximately 5 nautical miles (around 9.3 km) off and south of Cap Cepet, in the commune of Saint-Mandrier-sur-Mer (83), observed a black sphere emerging from the west at an elevation of 15°. This sphere appeared to be at an altitude comparable to a commercial airliner, but at a higher speed. Midway through its trajectory, the object changed color to become "very bright chrome" and accelerated significantly while climbing towards southeast, tripling its initial speed. The witness lost sight of the object to the south at an elevation of 45°. The observation lasted between 5 and 7 seconds, with no perceptible noise, halo, trail, smoke, or spark. The consistency of the testimony is considered average due to the presence of a single witness, the absence of photos or videos of the PAN, and incomplete or missing data provided by the witness, particularly regarding the apparent size of the PAN and the angular distance traveled.

According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony. The explored hypothesis is the observation of a weather balloon, supported by the following elements:

- The observed colors, "black and chrome," are typical of weather balloons, which can have two different finishes: a painted or aluminum-coated Mylar® envelope.
- The round shape is characteristic of balloons, allowing sunlight illumination on the east side, creating a shiny appearance due to reflection on its aluminum-coated Mylar® surface.
- Weather data analysis revealed that the initial movement of the PAN, from west to east, aligns perfectly with wind direction at 750 meters altitude. We also observe that this wind describes a wide curve over the sea, suggesting a possible origin from the coasts of Marseille, Martigues, or Port-Saint-Louis-du-Rhône. Given the excellent weather conditions, it is plausible that the weather balloon was released or fell from a beach or cove along the coast.

The perceived strangeness by the witness is mainly due to their perception of the speed and altitude of the PAN, which they judge as very high, as well as the color, trajectory, and speed changes. The investigation provides the following explanations:

- The color change, from "black to chrome," can be attributed to the balloon's mixed composition, with a dark side and a bright side exposed to the sun. The perception of two distinct parts may be influenced by a change in perspective or the balloon's rotation during its movement.
- Estimating the speed, distance (and thus altitude), and dimensions of an unknown object observed in the sky is difficult to determine precisely without references. The witness compares the object to fighter jets they are familiar with, equating the characteristics of the PAN with those planes. However, it is plausible that the PAN is a smaller object at a lower altitude than a commercial or military airliner. Consequently, the speed perceived by the witness may seem very high because they assume the PAN is at a much higher altitude.
- The change in trajectory and speed can be explained by the balloon rising in an area where winds are stronger and more oriented towards west-northwest. This may give the witness the impression that the object is following a visually oriented final trajectory towards southeast. Conversely, the imprecise data regarding the angular size of the PAN and the angular distance it traveled do not allow for further data verification.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B," a very probable observation of a weather balloon.
