Summary: Long observations with the naked eye and binoculars of a bright point to the southeast on a very slow ascending trajectory: mistaken for the star Sirius.
--- seg-12ed4598da4b18c17084cecb [needs-review] ---
Description: On Monday, September 2, 2019, at approximately 5:40 AM, a witness on their terrace facing south noticed a sparkling light point located in the southeast of a clear sky. Viewed through binoculars, the witness described a disk with three distinct flickers in the form of white and orange points, and green/blue hues. The point gradually ascended into the sky and completely disappeared at 6:07 AM. Intrigued, the witness again scanned the sky at 5:00 AM the following Tuesday and Wednesday, both times facing southeast, but saw nothing. On Thursday at 5:29 AM, they observed the phenomenon again. They then woke their spouse who confirmed the presence of the PAN. Photographs and videos were taken. The point gradually ascended into the sky and completely disappeared at 7:15 AM. Only one testimony about this phenomenon was collected. Astronomical conditions confirmed by Stellarium indicate that on September 2, 2019, and the following days, SIRIUS was normally visible in the southeast from the observation location, with a very low elevation of around 10° between 5:00 and 6:00 AM (see attached Stellarium map). The first and last observations took place at 5:30 AM (see witness photos zoomed in on the light point, to the left of the streetlamp and above the houses' roofs): the witness initially thought they were seeing VENUS, but it was actually SIRIUS. Observations on September 3 and 4 were made earlier at 5:00 AM, and Stellarium confirms that SIRIUS was lower at that time. The witness's photos show a mask just below the observed point, formed by the houses (see the day map). This mask and the observation time of 5:00 AM (half an hour earlier than the previous day) explain why the witness could not see SIRIUS on September 3 and 4 as it was too early, and the star was not yet high enough in the sky to be visible due to the houses. The videos submitted show zoomed light points, but they also confirm the position of the PAN to the left of the streetlamp and the proximity of the mask formed by the houses. The peculiarity reported by the witness regarding the shapes seen through binoculars, namely a "disk with three distinct flickers in the form of white, orange, and green/blue points," and that "The binoculars allowed me to notice that the disk seemed to change orientation..." is likely due to effects related to the zoomed light point or the viewing issue reported by the witness themselves in an email: "I also need to tell you that I am astigmatic, and this astigmatism (uncorrected by wearing glasses in the morning when I have lunch) sometimes plays tricks on my perception of the shapes of certain objects." The residual peculiarity is therefore very low, and the consistency with the hypothesis of observing the star SIRIUS is demonstrated. The testimony's consistency is very good, with precise timing, two photos of the observation, two day photos of the location, three videos of the observation, and two witnesses (although only one questionnaire was completed by both spouses). The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: Misidentification with a SIRIUS star.
