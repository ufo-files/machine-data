--- seg-0df26bf5502fdd689e290242 [needs-review] ---
Description: On November 6, 2023, during a Monastir-Orly flight around 20:30, a commercial pilot, accompanied by his copilots, observed variable intensity light points, describing distinct trajectories and varying speeds, accompanied by brief flashes. The observation lasts approximately 1.5 hours, until the beginning of the descent towards Paris. This type of phenomenon recurs subsequently during other flights. Of weak strangeness and good consistency* (multiple witnesses, but unique testimony, no photo of PAN but fairly complete testimony), this case turns out to be an observation of Starlink® satellite flashes (see the investigation report). *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The description of the PAN consistently corresponds to this type of observation. An analysis conducted using the Stellarium software confirms that the PAN is located at an angle of 45° above the apparent position of the Sun, while the Sun is between 30° and 45° below the horizon. This configuration is observed for all the dates of observation reported. This type of observation, in a similar illumination configuration, was the subject of a detailed analysis in a dedicated news item on the GEIPAN website, specifically on the page: New Phenomenon Explained: Starlink Satellite Flashes. The case is classified "A", observation of Starlink® satellite flashes.
