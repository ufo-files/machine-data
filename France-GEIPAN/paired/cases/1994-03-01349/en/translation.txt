Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MESSINCOURT (08) 10.03.1994 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined (see the investigation report). On March 10, 1994, at approximately 8:55 PM, two witnesses observed colored lights that delineated a shape (circular and flat for T1, and triangular for T2) in the sky. The unusual trajectory intrigues the witnesses, as does the arrival of another PAN. No unusual noises were heard during the brief, intermittent observation by both witnesses. No other testimonies were collected. Only one hypothesis needs to be considered: a helicopter's passage.

There are two very strong elements in the testimonies that support the helicopter hypothesis and are consistent between the witnesses: the trajectory, and most importantly, a triangle of three navigation lights (white, red, and green).

The absence of radar detection can be explained by a low altitude (less than 200 or 300 meters), which is not confirmed but is consistent with other observed elements. Similarly, the absence of noise perception (perceived by T2, T1 does not comment) may seem surprising, but it is an experience shared by the GEIPAN in a few similar cases. It is also known that the focus on a visually "striking" observation can mask the accompanying auditory perception.

The other elements that contradict the hypothesis cannot invalidate it because their reliability is questionable due to lack of confirmation or inconsistency between witnesses, and even if they are assumed reliable, they can be explained in a way that does not reduce the probability of the hypothesis below 50%. This is the second PAN joining the first one, as seen by T1, with descending white lights (according to T2) or slowly turning (according to T1), inside the navigation triangle (according to T1) or outside (according to T2).

The testimonies would have justified an on-site investigation to clarify the information and understand the discrepancies. However, the consistency is sufficient to conclude, as the observation is quite long and detailed, consistent on the crucial points (both positive and negative). Details observed by T1 are not seen by T2 (which is not a contradiction), and the real contradictions (shape and positions of the white lights) are ultimately not decisive. Consequently, the GEIPAN considers the helicopter hypothesis to have a probability greater than 50%: classification B: helicopter.
