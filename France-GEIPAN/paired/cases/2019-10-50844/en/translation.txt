Summary: Observations of the rapid passage of a very bright point at high altitude and disappearance while changing direction: very strong likelihood of misperception with the passage of the International Space Station (ISS).
--- seg-f53b828375511676a69df0da [needs-review] ---
Description: On Saturday, October 5, 2019, in the evening, three individuals were walking their dogs along the Lake of Ganguise in the commune of Baraigne (11). One witness reported: "While inviting my two friends to look at the Big Bear, we detected a very bright, fast-moving point in its trajectory, heading straight from northwest to west while turning on itself. The PAN made a sudden 90-degree turn and disappeared from my field of vision (to the left)." This was a direct observation, under a clear sky without clouds, wind, or noise. The perceived strangeness by the three witnesses was primarily due to the intense brightness of the PAN, its speed, and its disappearance while changing direction (to the left). The consistency of the testimonies is very good:

- Three witnesses made the same observation and each submitted a questionnaire.
- The video of the end of the trajectory was sent to GEIPAN.
- It was filmed from 8:37 PM to 8:39 PM.
- Map photos were sent by the witnesses, confirming the location and orientation of the observation from northwest to east.

The hypothesis accepted is the observation of a pass by the International Space Station (ISS). The aspects corresponding to the ISS are:

- The intense brightness: In terms of visibility, the brightness of the ISS is remarkable (minimum magnitude of -3.6), easily observable in the sky.
- The observation time: The time range provided by the observers (8:36:00 - 8:38:15) is the one used to verify the passage of the object.
- The ISS trajectory from the CalSky.com website confirms the visibility and trajectory at the time of the observation (see the three CalSky image files);
- Image 1 confirms the intense visibility of the ISS;
- Image 2 confirms the passage of the ISS at the time of the observation;
- Image 3 confirms the positioning of the ISS above the Big Bear as indicated by the witnesses;
- The initial observation azimuth roughly coincides with the ISS trajectory;
- The final observation azimuth coincides well with the trajectory of the object, the ISS;
- The observed and actual movement directions roughly coincide;
- The object was high in the sky (elevation greater than 60°) and well visible during the observation duration.

The main strangeness is the rapid disappearance to the left. In fact, if one considers the observation plane of the witnesses, the "turning to the left" actually corresponds to the normal disappearance of the ISS at the end of its visibility, which is perceived as a receding due to the decrease in brightness and gives the impression of a turn. The transmitted video allows visualization of a brightly glowing point, but the zooms performed do not allow visualization of the shape of the ISS. The image of the small circular glowing spot, surrounded by concentric rings, is characteristic of the diffraction effect of a brightly glowing point by the optics of a camera lens. Consequently, the residual strangeness is low as it is explained by the hypothesis of a pass by the ISS. Given the very good consistency of the testimonies, GEIPAN classifies the phenomenon as A (very strongly misperceived) with the very high probability of a pass by the ISS.
