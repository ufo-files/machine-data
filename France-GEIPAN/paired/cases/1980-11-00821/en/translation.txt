Summary: Observation of the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid.
--- seg-d72fb3eda0870a441f05001d [needs-review] ---
Description: On November 11, 1980, around 6:30 PM, approximately one hundred people across the Southeast of France and Corsica witnessed the passage of a powerful light phenomenon in the sky. Many testimonies are recorded at the gendarmerie. Hundreds of witnesses in Italy (see the table of Italian cases) and Spain (Catalonia) will also report this extremely spectacular phenomenon. The press will also report the phenomenon (see the articles in Nice Matin). Upon receipt of a new testimony of an observation made on that day (see BONNIEUX (84)), the GEIPAN re-examined the case in order to be able to publish it. A unique chart compiles the essential elements of the multiple observations (see the investigation report and the table of French testimonies). Multiple witnesses describe a very powerful light phenomenon crossing the sky in less than a minute (for most) heading south or southwest. A wide variability can be observed in the elements described, both in terms of the color of the phenomenon (green, red, yellow, purple, white, pink, blue, etc.) and its shape (ball, cigar, saucer, triangle, and even parallelepiped), the estimated distance (30m to 2km), the direction of movement (mostly from North to South, or East to West, with some witnesses observing the opposite), and the duration of observation (primarily less than 1 minute, with a few cases lasting over 2 minutes). The experience shows that such variability is common in all massive observations; it is also necessary to take into account the fact that all witnesses, far from each other, did not see the phenomenon from the same angle and for the same duration: this induces a perfectly explicable variability in the accounts of the witnesses (see the investigation report). Nevertheless, the simultaneity of the phenomenon, and some common characteristics such as fragmentation into 3 elements, leave little doubt about the uniqueness of the origin of the phenomenon. In this type of case, it is the multitude of witnesses at the same time and their significant geographical separation that proves the phenomenon was very powerful and at high altitude: the hypothesis of atmospheric reentry becomes certain. The nature of the returning object is more difficult to prove: in this case, due to the southwest orientation of the phenomenon and its duration of less than a minute, which implies an initial speed clearly superior to 10 km/s, the hypothesis of the re-entry into the atmosphere of a satellite debris is overshadowed by the hypothesis of the re-entry of a natural meteoroid. Astronomers in Nice (see the article "The Light in the Azurean Sky: It Was Indeed a Meteorite") and Genoa have confirmed this hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: observation of an atmospheric reentry of a bolide, caused by a natural meteoroid.
