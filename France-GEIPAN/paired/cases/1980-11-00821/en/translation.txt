## GEIPAN Testimonies: November 11, 1980

Multiple reports of **unidentified flying objects (UFOs)** were recorded across France on November 11, 1980. Below is a list of locations and associated reports:

**Chamonix (73) to Grenoble (38)**

* **11/11/1980, T1-T3:**  Multiple witnesses reported seeing a bright, fast-moving object with a pulsating light.  (PV n°1133 (1980308411).pdf )

**Corsica (2A, 2B)**

* **11/11/1980, T1-T3:**  Similar reports of bright, fast-moving objects with pulsating lights. (PV n°1253 (1980308413).pdf )

**Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur (04, 06, 13)**

* **11/11/1980, T1-T12:**  Numerous reports of UFOs, often described as bright, disc-shaped objects with lights, moving at high speeds. Some witnesses reported multiple UFOs. (Multiple PV numbers, including167 (1980208579).pdf,177 (1980309804).pdf,367 (1980308423).pdf,599 (1980308437).pdf, and752 (1980308420).pdf)

**Rhône-Alpes (43, 38, 73)**

* **11/11/1980, T1-T12:** Similar reports to other regions, with witnesses describing bright, fast-moving objects with lights. (Multiple PV numbers)

Summary: Observation of the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid.
--- seg-d72fb3eda0870a441f05001d [needs-review] ---
Description: On November 11, 1980, around 6:30 PM, approximately one hundred people across the entire Southeast of France and Corsica witnessed the passage of a powerful light phenomenon in the sky. Numerous testimonies were recorded with the gendarmerie. Hundreds of witnesses in Italy (see the Italian cases table) and Spain (Catalonia) also reported this extremely spectacular phenomenon. The press covered the phenomenon as well (see Nice Matin articles). Following the receipt of a new testimony of an observation made on that day (see BONNIEUX (84)), the GEIPAN re-examined this case to be able to publish it. A unique table compiles the essential elements of the multiple observations (see the investigation report and the French testimonies table). The multiple witnesses described a very powerful light phenomenon that crossed the sky in less than a minute (for the majority) heading South or Southwest. A great variability can be observed in the described elements, both in the color of the phenomenon (green, red, yellow, purple, white, pink, blue...) and in its shape (ball, cigar, saucer, triangle, and even parallelepiped), the estimated distance (30m to 2km), the direction of movement (most from North to South, or East to West, see the opposite for some witnesses), and the duration of observation (mostly less than 1 minute, a few cases over 2 minutes). Experience shows that such variability is frequent in all massive observations; it is also important to take into account the fact that all witnesses, far from each other, did not see the phenomenon from the same angle and for the same duration: this leads to a perfectly explicable variability in the witnesses' accounts (see the investigation report). Nevertheless, the simultaneity of the phenomenon, and some common characteristics such as fragmentation into 3 elements, leave little doubt about the uniqueness of the phenomenon's origin. In this type of case, it is the multitude of witnesses at the same time and their significant geographical distance that proves the phenomenon was very powerful and at high altitude: the hypothesis of atmospheric re-entry becomes certain. The nature of the re-entering object is more difficult to prove: in this case, due to the South-West orientation of the phenomenon and its duration of less than a minute, which implies an initial speed clearly superior to 10 km/s, the hypothesis of re-entry into the atmosphere of a satellite debris is replaced by the hypothesis of a natural meteoroid re-entry. The astronomers of Nice (see the article "The glow in the Azurean sky: it was indeed a meteorite") and Genoa confirmed this hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: observation of an atmospheric re-entry of a bolide, caused by a natural meteoroid.
