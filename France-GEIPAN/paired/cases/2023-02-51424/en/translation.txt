Description: On February 13, 2023, at 6:46 PM, the sole witness of this observation was at his workplace in MEYREUIL (13), near a departmental road, when he observed a black, non-luminous object in the sky moving slowly and appearing to float. Its shape elongated and it spun on itself. This observation lasted between 3 and 4 minutes during which the witness took two photographs and two videos of the Unidentified Flying Object (PAN). According to Geipan's criteria, the consistency of this case is satisfactory, meaning that although this case relies solely on one testimony, it is supported by videos and photos that allow to determine the apparent trajectory of the UFO. The analysis shows that this observation is very likely that of an object carried by the wind, either a cluster of balloons or a light debris. The appearance and movement of the PAN as described by the witness seem to correspond to this type of object. We specifically note that the witness compared the movement of the PAN to that of a floating object without weight and that the appearance of the PAN in certain parts of the videos matches what is expected for this type of object. Since the observation took place during twilight, it is understandable that the PAN appears dark, as it is not illuminated by direct sunlight, which is consistent with what one could observe with balloons. It is also possible that the objects are naturally dark, whether they are balloons or light debris. It is also observed that the apparent trajectory of the PAN is in perfect agreement with the direction of the wind at low altitude, while its relatively moderate apparent speed is consistent with the low wind speed observed at the time of the incident, estimated at around 14 km/h at low altitude. However, the investigation did not allow to precisely identify the origin and provenance of this cluster of balloons or this light debris. Geipan classifies this case as A, very probable observation of an object carried by the wind, such as a cluster of balloons or light debris.
