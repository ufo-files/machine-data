Description: The witness is driving when their gaze is drawn to a light source, like a second sun. They stop their car and get out to photograph the phenomenon. The witness observed two types of phenomena (see commented photo): 
- From their eyes, they saw a "false" sun, with an irised outline: their testimony is typical of a parhelion located at 22° from the sun.
- On photos taken with their smartphone, the witness noticed, retrospectively, "light cylinders" opposite the sun: this phenomenon is typical of a "flare," which is a reflection produced inside the lens. One can verify that these light spots are symmetrical to the sun relative to the center of the image, which explains why they are not always located in the same place in the sky; their position depends on the sun's framing in the image. This case is classified "A": direct observation of a parhelion, and flares on photos taken retrospectively.
