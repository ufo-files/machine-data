**Description:** On November 10, 2014, at 10:00 PM, a witness looks out their window and sees a slightly flickering bright point in the sky. The PAN moves very slowly to the right while rapidly changing color. The witness attempts to photograph the PAN without success. The observation ends between 12:45 AM and 1:00 AM. As the witness begins filling out a GEIPAN Electronic Questionnaire (QE) around 1:40 AM, they look out the window again and see the PAN once more. They lose sight of it around 2:30 AM. Only one testimony will be collected. The duration of the observation (approximately 3 hours for the first phase and 45 minutes for the second) as well as the fact that the PAN moves very slowly to the right are entirely characteristic of an astronomical misperception. Additionally, the movement from west to north accompanied by a decrease in the angular height of the PAN is entirely characteristic of observing a star in the northwest. If this hypothesis is correct, the observation of the PAN would involve two different stars, assimilated into a single object by the witness, with the 45-minute absence of observation between 1:00 AM and 1:45 AM in the morning favoring this type of confusion. The rapid color changes of the PAN (red, green, blue) are also entirely characteristic of observing a bright star, whose light scintillates due to altitude winds. A Stellarium reconstruction for November 10, 2014, at 10:00 PM shows that a remarkably bright star, Vega (magnitude 0.0), is located at an azimuth perfectly consistent with that of the PAN (297° compared to 298° estimated by the witness) (see the investigation report). Of average strangeness but sufficient consistency, this case turns out to be a certain misperception with the stars Vega and Deneb, whose positions correspond exactly to that of the PAN at the indicated times. Furthermore, the arrival of a rainy disturbance during the observation led to altitude winds causing the stars to scintillate, corresponding to the rapid color changes of the PAN. The slight apparent movements perceived by the witness are the result of autocinetic illusion. This case is classified A, misperception with Vega and Deneb stars.
