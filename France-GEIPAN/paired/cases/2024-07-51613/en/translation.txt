Summary: Observation of the silent passage of a "fireball" whose intensity diminishes to become a dark object disappearing behind the urban landscape: observation of a celestial lantern carried by the wind.
--- seg-83439a798df8edc7081c8566 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the evening of July 28, 2024, from his apartment in Breuil-Magne (Charente-Maritime, 17), the witness observed a luminous phenomenon appearing as a glowing ball. The object was estimated to be at an altitude of 300 meters, at a distance between 500 meters and 1 kilometer. It moved quickly along a horizontal trajectory, without any perceptible sound emission. Its morphology corresponded to a luminous sphere surrounded by a slight thermal halo. The witness filmed the event with his smartphone, using a slight zoom to enhance detail capture. During its movement, the light intensity of the object gradually diminished, accompanied by the emission of black smoke. The object became completely dark while maintaining its trajectory. The observation ended when the object disappeared behind the roofs of the houses to the right of the witness. Of low strangeness and good consistency* (single witness but video of PAN), this case turns out to be the observation of a sky lantern. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The description and visual characteristics of the unidentified aerospatial phenomenon (PAN) perfectly match those of a sky lantern. This type of object typically has a burner that goes out after a few minutes of flight, which is manifested by a color change (from yellow to black) and the emission of a slight smoke. For comparable examples, via the website Misperies du ciel - Sky Lanterns Misperies du ciel-Lanternes célestes. The apparent trajectory of PAN is also consistent with the direction of the wind measured at the time of the facts. The investigation did not allow determining where this lantern had been released (see the investigation report). GEIPAN classified the case as "A", observation of a sky lantern carried by the wind.
