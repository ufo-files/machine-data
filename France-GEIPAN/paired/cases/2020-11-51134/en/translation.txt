Summary: Observation of the low-altitude movement of a dozen red-orange lights: probable misidentification with Thai lanterns.
--- seg-18b94c88cf31f0ec245c806e [needs-review] ---
Description: On Sunday, November 22, 2020, at approximately 6:00 PM (just after sunset), the witness observed a red-orange light at 20° to the east. The speed appears low at first, then higher as the witness gets closer. Other PAN appear, generally bright, roughly circular, about a dozen, following each other at regular distances. They are heading south / southwest at a relatively low altitude (below the clouds) and disappear from his field of vision. One of them veers towards the west at the last moment. The witness had time to take 8 photos (see the photographic dossier sent by the witness to GEIPAN with image reduction for transmission by email). No other testimony about this phenomenon was collected. The strangeness perceived by the witness is essentially due to the fact that one of the PAN suddenly changed course to the west with a strong acceleration. The case file is consistent, the witness's data is very precise, and the photos add value to the testimony. The witness considered many possible misidentifications, but did not mention the lantern hypothesis. Or the color "red orange," the shape "globally circular," the linear motion with PAN spaced at equal distances, and the fact that it was a weekend evening, point towards Thai lanterns. The observation is indeed compatible with Thai lanterns:

- **Appearance:** The reddish-orange color and shapes observed in the photos are quite typical of lanterns.
- **Trajectory:** The PAN move in a straight line, heading south-southwest, which is typical of lanterns being carried by the same wind current. The Bouguenais weather station, located 11 km from the observation location, reports an average wind speed of 4 km/h, with a direction of south (S) at 18:00, changing to south-southwest (SSO) at 17:00 and remaining SSO at 19:00 (refer to the weather document). The indicated direction is compatible with the direction of movement of the PAN. Average winds are light: 4 km/h, or lantern releases only occur in light winds, - Sunday evening: weekend evening (more conducive to lantern releases), - photographic analysis: according to the IPACO tool,  la chromaticité moyenne du PAN figurant sur la photographie DSC06315cadr.jpg is entirely consistent with that of a Thai lantern (see the IPACO document). The strangeness perceived by the witness mainly stems from observing the lateral acceleration at a dizzying speed, of one of the PAN that banked towards the west. This perception could be due to the following optical illusion: when the lantern goes out, the observer has the impression that the lantern is moving away extremely quickly, and depending on the respective positions of the observer and the lantern, this can lead to the impression of a change in direction, with movement to the west translating as a distance from the continuous PAN train heading south. The Loire-Atlantique Prefecture has been given no requests for authorization to release lanterns at this date. She also mentioned that the municipality of Nantes formally prohibits the release of balloons or Chinese lanterns above its territory. Requests to release balloons or Chinese lanterns in neighboring communes must, under all circumstances, be subject to the favorable opinion of the services of the Delegation of the Pays de la Loire of the Direction de la Sécurité de l'Aviation Civile Ouest. Unfortunately, this rule is not widely followed for lantern releases at private parties, making it very difficult to trace the origin of the release. A beam of very convergent evidence indicates an observation of the passage of Thai lanterns. The inability to define the origin of the release implies a classification B by the GEIPAN.
