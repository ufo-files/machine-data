Summary: Blinding light followed by the silent, slow passage of a cylindrical object with lights on its side: probable observation under unfavorable circumstances of a military transport aircraft, type Hercules C-130.
--- seg-1fb5719c10daa9b4f91771d3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On September 30, 2019, at 8:00 PM, a witness sees a flash of light and thinks it's a thunderstorm, but no clouds are in sight. Suddenly, a very powerful light appears, forcing the witness to close their eyes. When the witness opens their eyes again, a cylindrical object is seen performing a curve before being hidden by trees. This PAN (cylindrical) object, metallic in color, has lights on its side, but the witness does not see any wings and hears no noise. No other witness statements were collected about this phenomenon. The PAN is likely a military transport aircraft, type Hercules C-130, observed by the witness under particular conditions (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The flash of light initially observed by the witness resembles a flash emitted by an anti-collision light. As the witness immediately turned away, thinking it was a thunderstorm, and while searching for the source, they were unable to observe subsequent flashes. When their gaze returned to the initial direction, they were immediately blinded by a strong light, preventing them from distinguishing other flashes.

- This strong light is probably that emitted by one or more landing lights of the aircraft, directed towards the witness, which the witness compares to the sun (identical pale yellow color to the said lights). The witness mentions the presence of "lamps" on the side of the PAN, without specifying the number, color, or possible blinking. They could be navigation lights.

- The witness's blinding is such that they close their eyes, unable to observe the further development of the PAN, which, according to them, passes over the house, which cannot therefore be formally confirmed. This blinding also causes a phenomenon of retinal persistence; in addition to the observation conditions (during twilight), distance, and low angular elevation of the aircraft, when the witness sees it further west, they cannot observe its wings (or other details) and only distinguish a cylindrical shape, as the wings are visually in the same plane as the fuselage, which constitutes the main strangeness perceived by the witness.

- The movement is slow, and it follows a wide curve, perfectly in line with what such an aircraft flying at low altitude could do.

- The witness is not far (approximately 120 km) from the Istres Air Base, which houses such military aircraft.

- The other strangeness is the absence of noise perceived by the witness. This absence is relative, as although the wind is a carrier, it is very weak. Moreover, the distance between the aircraft and the witness may be sufficient for the noise not to be perceived. We can, however, wonder why the witness, since the aircraft seems to be flying low, did not hear any noise as it passed over their house. This last point is not formally confirmed, as the witness closed their eyes at the beginning of the observation, blinded by the strong light. They were therefore unable to see the aircraft pass over their house, which, in reality, could have easily passed further away. We were, however, unable to formally determine the presence of a Hercules C-130 in the area at the time of the observation.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation under unfavorable circumstances of a military transport aircraft, type Hercules C-130.
