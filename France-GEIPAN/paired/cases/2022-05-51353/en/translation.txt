Summary: Sequential observations by two witnesses in a vehicle on a winding, uphill road of the presence in the cloudy sky of an orange PAN with changing shape and on a descending trajectory: observation of the Moon.
--- seg-eea1e9a8b6f60f2ad4b31b84 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 8, 2022, two witnesses (T1 and T2) leave a nightclub where they have been spending the evening. It is around 02:00/02:15. They take the D34 and head towards VARENNE L'ARCONCE (71). As a passenger, T2 notices the presence in the sky of a PAN that he describes as "a thread of orange color." He alerts the driver (T1), who in turn observes the phenomenon. While driving, they realize that the phenomenon seems to be growing in size and descending towards the ground. During a stop, the PAN disappears only to reappear five minutes later. The witnesses get back in the vehicle to try to get closer but cannot find the PAN again. They smell tires, burnt oil. Thinking it might be a burning plane, T1 calls the firefighters who arrive around 2:40 a.m. The phenomenon is not seen again. The testimonies are consistent (two witnesses with precise testimonies, but no photo of the PAN).

According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The study of the file, based on the information contained in the Gendarmerie report, concludes that the observation was an astronomical observation of the first quarter of the Moon seen through clouds present that evening, or possibly ground obstacles, in the direction of observation, with a very low elevation (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Proven presence of the Moon in the observation azimuth. Reconstructed observation azimuths in three very close observation points to those of the Moon (1 to 4° difference).
- Possible changing shape depending on the configuration of the clouds or ground obstacles partially masking the Moon. Proven presence of clouds in the direction of observation.
- Orange color, consistent with that of the Moon low on the horizon.
- Precise unknown elevation, but a decrease in this elevation over the observation as the Moon sets.
- Impression of horizontal and vertical movement of the Moon due to the topography of the observation sites: a winding, ascending road.
- Temporary disappearance of the PAN due to a cloud passage totally masking the Moon.
- Final disappearance of the PAN due to the very low elevation of the Moon at the end of the observation, which could be definitively hidden by clouds and/or ground obstacles.

We should note that the Moon would have been observed by the witnesses in the same direction if it had not been for the PAN. The smell of tires and burnt oil reported by the witnesses during the observation is due to a totally independent, local cause. This could have contributed to the witnesses' strange perception. GEIPAN classifies as "A," Moon observation.
