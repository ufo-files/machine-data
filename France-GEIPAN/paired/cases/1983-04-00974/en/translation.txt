**Description:** The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed to draw more relevant conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The previous observation case classified as D and named NAY-BOURDETTES (64) 1983 is part of a recent batch of cases reassessed. On April 29, 1983, around 11:55 PM, a couple in a car observed from several locations a bright, square-then-round, orange-colored phenomenon in the sky. The witnesses were frightened as the UFO seemed close and followed their movements. They sought refuge with a third witness who noticed nothing unusual outside. The testimonies reported in the Gendarmerie protocol exhibit the characteristics of an astre (star) observation (see reconstruction). Well-known today characteristics of UFOs, with a growing number of explained cases of this type, especially over the last decade (see www.geipan.fr and search for cases with astre, Venus, moon, sun phenomena):

- Long observation duration of over 30 minutes, low altitude in the sky.
- The UFO's position remains stable when the witness stops and moves when the witness is in motion, giving the illusion of a "following ball." The direction of the astre is, of course, fixed absolutely. When the car follows a roughly constant course, the astre appears fixed relative to the vehicle and moves with the landscape. Since the witness had already been surprised by the UFO's appearance and considers it at a close distance, this movement along the landscape is interpreted as the UFO moving parallel to the vehicle, mirroring its speed, here 40 km/h above the houses. The astre disappearing behind obstacles and reappearing at the end of a street can be perceived as an intention on the part of the UFO to wait for the witnesses: "There, I spotted it above the CANCE establishments. It was hovering and seemed to be waiting for us. Out of fear..."

- Sensation of forward and backward movement of the UFO. The witness, having interpreted the astre as a close, moving object, logically interprets any variation in the astre's light intensity as a forward or backward movement of the object, which would maintain its own constant brightness. "We stopped again, and it was when we saw this phenomenon heading towards us that we backed up to escape." We verify that it is indeed an astre, specifically the Moon. The direction of the Moon (at 12:20, azimuth 128°, elevation 6°) is indeed above the CANCE buildings, which face the road when the witness is traveling on La Monjoie path, leaving Nay: "This time, we took La Monjoie path to try to get back to our parents' home. There, I spotted it above the CANCE establishments. It was hovering and seemed to be waiting for us. Out of fear..."

The point of misidentification, which leads to other aspects (following ball, forward and backward movement), lies in the failure to recognize the astre. The Moon is nearly full (phase 0.92) at sunrise, it is red in the color described for the UFO, but it does not have the classic shape of the Moon, neither round nor crescent. Misidentification is a common occurrence; the presence of cloud veils in front of the Moon only allows glimpses of it through cloud gaps of variable and irregular shapes, initially square ("suitcase," "window"), then becoming round but the misidentification is already in motion. The weather report for Lourdes-Ossun (30 km away) that night indicates cloud cover (Infoclimat), and the Gendarmerie protocol mentions a resident's call reporting having seen a glow behind a cloud in the Nay area around 11:00 PM that night, at least confirming that the cloud cover was not complete and could allow glimpses of the Moon's glow. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Red Moon observations.
