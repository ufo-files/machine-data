Summary: Observations of a changing light phenomenon with a bright orange color that seemed to follow witnesses as they moved: observations of the Rusty Moon.
--- seg-37e57f17c1045a1b0d990910 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case named NAY-BOURDETTES (64) 1983 is part of a set of cases recently reviewed. On April 29, 1983, around 11:55 PM, a couple in a car observed from several locations a bright, square-then-round, orange-colored phenomenon in the sky. The witnesses are frightened as the PAN appears close and follows their movements. They take refuge with a third witness who notices nothing unusual outside. The witness statements reported in the Gendarmerie protocol exhibit the characteristics of an observation of a star (see the reconstruction). Well-known characteristics of the GEIPAN with the accumulation of explained cases of this particular type over the past ten years (see on the website www.geipan.fr and case search with star phenomena, Venus, moon, sun):

- Long observation duration of more than 30 minutes, low altitude in the sky.
- The position of the PAN is stable when the witness stops and mobile when the witness moves, with the perception of being followed by the PAN. This is the illusion of the "following ball". The direction of the star is, of course, fixed absolutely. When the car follows a more or less constant heading, the object is fixed relative to the vehicle and moves with the landscape. As the witness had already been surprised by the appearance of the PAN and considers it at a close distance, this scanning along the landscape is understood as a movement of the PAN parallel to the vehicle, replicating its speed, here 40 km/h above the houses. The disappearance of the star behind obstacles and its reappearance at the end of a street can be perceived as an intention on its part to wait for witnesses: "There, I spotted it above the CANCE establishments." It was stationary in the air and seemed to be waiting for us. "In front of the fear... sensation of forward and backward movement of the PAN." The witness, having interpreted the star as a close object and, moreover, mobile, any variation in the star's light intensity is logically interpreted as a forward or backward movement of the object while maintaining its own constant intensity. "We stopped again and it was when seeing this phenomenon heading towards us that we made a U-turn to flee." We verify that it is indeed an astre, that is, the Moon. The direction of the Moon (at 00:20, azimuth 128°, elevation 6°) is indeed above the CANCE buildings, which face the road when the witness is traveling on the La Monjoie path leaving Nay: "This time we took the Lamonjoie path to try to get back to the parents' home." There, I caught a glimpse of it above the CANCE facilities. It was stationary in the air and seemed to be waiting for us. "In front of the fear..." The point of departure for the misperception, which leads to the others (trailing ball, forward-backward movement), lies in the non-recognition of the star. The Moon is nearly full (phase 0.92) at rise, it is red in the color described for the PAN but does not have the classic shape of the Moon, neither round nor crescent. It is a common misconception, the presence of cloudy veils in front of the Moon only allows one to see it through gaps in the clouds, whose shape is irregular and variable, here square at first ("suitcase", "window"), then becoming round later, but the misconception is already in motion. The weather recorded in Lourdes-Ossun (30 km) that night indicates cloud cover (Infoclimat) and the gendarmerie report mentions a call from a resident who observed a glow behind a cloud in the Nay area around 11 PM that night, which at least confirms that the cloud cover was not complete and could allow moonlight to pass through. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: red moon observations.
