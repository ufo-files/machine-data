**Report:** On April 13, 2023, at 10:55, the witness, traveling alone in her vehicle on the A16 highway between Montmorency (95) and Beauvais (60), her place of residence, reported observing a PAN. The object had an entirely unusual shape, white in color with nacre-like reflections. It moved slowly, following a slightly descending horizontal trajectory. The witness made a sketch illustrating the shape and characteristics of the PAN observed and provided photographs that precisely locate the observation site. The case consistency is average despite only one witness and the absence of photos or videos of the PAN, as it benefits from precise geographical positioning and a phenomenon sketch. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The radar map provided by CAPCODA (Center for Air Planning and Conduct of Operations and Air Defense) indicates the presence of a Cessna 152 aircraft, operating in the observation area at the exact time mentioned by the witness. The aircraft was performing a circular maneuver above the A16 highway, following a left-to-right trajectory corresponding to the one described in the testimony (see the investigation report). The hypothesis of a Cessna 152 is supported by several consistent elements, including: - The witness's description of the PAN: "white metallic object with a nacre-like finish," consistent with the visual of a CESSNA - The trajectory and speed, also compatible with the hypothesis, are characterized by a straight movement, at a slow and constant pace, and an altitude consistent with the flight profile of a Cessna 152. The aircraft's passage through cumulus cloud formations likely altered the witness's visual perception, particularly regarding the angular size and apparent shape of the object, which were already perceived as smaller. Additionally, the engine noise, potentially masked by ambient sounds inside the vehicle, may have gone unnoticed, especially as the witness's attention was heavily focused on the visual observation. The case is classified as "A": observation of a Cessna 152-type aircraft.
