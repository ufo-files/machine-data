Summary: Long observation of light formations circling in the night sky: observation of light displays produced by projectors called "skytracers" emitted from the Disneyland Park.
--- seg-bd4eba7075398776bf445d1d [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On January 6, 2025, at 02:49, the sole witness, in his apartment in Noisy-le-Grand (93), observed from his window lights moving in the sky in front of him. He describes the phenomenon as "multiple, with several lights of two different sizes, spinning in a circle and regularly spaced," white in color and of high brightness, accompanied by a white halo. The PAN was observed at an elevation between 30° and 45° in an easterly-northeasterly direction. The observation ended as the PAN was still present, with the witness intentionally stopping the observation. The witness photographed and filmed the phenomenon, clearly showing the PAN. The case is of good consistency*, thanks to the use of a photo and video of the PAN. The strangeness for the witness comes from the behavior and appearance of the PAN, which does not resemble the phenomenon already observed in the same direction, and which the witness identifies as lights produced on occasion of "festivities." *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation revealed that the observed white lights are a projection onto clouds of light animations produced by powerful projectors commonly known as "skytracers." These are animated with regular and repetitive movements (clearly visible on the witness's video), often circular, as described by the witness, but they can also move randomly at the operator's choice. The appearance of the light shapes can vary depending on the nature of the present overcast ceiling: a uniform and low overcast ceiling will offer better support for the lights, resulting in a more marked shape with greater brightness. If the overcast ceiling is higher or fragmented, the reflected light will be less intense and the shapes less distinct. We verified the overcast ceiling between 01:00 and 03:00 on the date of January 6, 2025 (see Annex 1). It is at an average altitude (around 2 km) at the time of observation, which is conducive to forming the light effects observed by the witness, but attenuated due to the cloud altitude. Lower clouds would have produced more perceptible effects. The cloud cover was also significant, ranging from 5-7 to 8/8 octas between 02:00 and 03:00 according to the two closest stations (Paris-Le Bourget and Charles de Gaulle airports), which is consistent for making skytracers visible. As for their use on the night of the observation, a search showed that the axis of observation is exactly in the direction of Disneyland Paris, at a distance of approximately 16 km and at an azimuth of 83° (see Annex 2). Regular light shows take place, but not at such a late hour. However, the observation occurred between two shows ("Disney Electrical Sky Parade" ending on the day of the observation around 21:00 and "Disney Tales of Magic" starting in the evening of January 10, 2025). A test of projectors is therefore entirely plausible, which could also explain the differences in appearance and behavior of the lights compared to what the witness is accustomed to observing in the same direction. GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": observation of skytracers emitted by Disneyland.
