Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MAUVEZIN (32) 1993 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On July 15, 1993, around 11 PM, and then on the 16th at 1 and 2 AM, three young campers, seasonal employees in Mauvezin (32), observed three times luminous phenomena of color moving in the sky. The witnesses perceived a deep rumble during certain observations. One of the witnesses attempted to take photos, but they turned out unusable. The hypothesis of an aeronautical confusion is the most plausible for this series of observations, which has identical characteristics to those of aircraft approaching or taking off from Toulouse Blagnac Airport. We will note in particular:

- The presence of powerful white/yellow lights, comparable to landing lights, lit under conditions of approach or takeoff.
- The presence of a red light described as central, comparable to one of the anti-collision road lights located under the fuselage; although the blinking is not mentioned in the testimonies.
- The shape described by the witnesses can be entirely comparable to that delineated by the landing lights of an airplane, either in an equilateral triangle when viewed from below, or in another geometric shape according to the angle of observation.
- The movement of the PAN is identical to the R17 air corridor at a 304° heading and connecting Toulouse-Blagnac to the point AGN114.8 Ch95X. Even if this route was not the same in 1993 as it is today, the relative proximity of Toulouse-Blagnac Airport does not prevent the overflight of the area where the witnesses were located or the presence nearby of commercial aircraft in approach or shortly after takeoff.
- The mention of the existence of a noise coming from the PAN in two out of the three phases, noise whose intensity and nature can vary significantly depending on the environment (weather and in particular wind, relief...) and the distance of this PAN from the witness. The fact that the witnesses are seasonal, working students living far from the observation site and unfamiliar with proximity to an air corridor, if not this one, can explain the confusion with airplanes and the absence of other testimonies. The consistency is average. Although all witnesses were heard at the Gendarmerie and the resulting protocol is quite complete, important data are missing, such as azimuth and elevation angle measurements of the PAN. An on-site investigation would have allowed many points to be clarified and possibly strengthen the hypothesis, if the flights suspected of causing the confusion had been identified at the time. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observations of commercial aircraft.
