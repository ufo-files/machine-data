Summary: Very long observation of two __PANs__ consisting of white balls and red points moving rapidly and erratically in the clear night sky: probable misidentification with stars.
--- seg-e8a6d6ab204c742e2c2617e3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 20, 2020, around 11 PM local time, at CHARTEVES (02), T1 was outside when they observed two PAN objects. One was moving west, composed of five white balls with erratic movements, from which red points were rapidly moving. The other was moving east, with similar characteristics. T1's wife (T2) and son were called to witness the phenomenon. The sky was clear, and no sounds were heard. T1 then went to bed.

The initial and main strangeness arises from observing five balls with erratic movements in two different parts of the sky (as detailed in the investigation report). The case's consistency is average (two witnesses), although the azimuths of the PAN's appearances remain unclear.

The most likely explanation is a confusion with two stars, Arcturus and Vega, located in roughly the directions indicated by T1. This hypothesis is supported by the long observation duration and the autocinetic effect, creating a well-known optical illusion for astronomers. T1's description is typical of a visual perception error, where a fixed bright point in darkness appears to move erratically.

However, this astronomical misperception hypothesis with a visual illusion (autocinetic effect) does not account for the strangeness of the red glowing points described by T1, which T2 did not witness. The GEIPAN is classified as B: probable misperception with celestial bodies.
