Description: On November 16, 2010, at 6:40 a.m., a driver observed for two to three minutes a stationary, luminous phenomenon in the sky at a great distance (several kilometers) that resembled a saucer. The saucer was surrounded by approximately thirty white and green lights. The driver got out of his vehicle to take a picture with his cell phone but the sensitivity was insufficient. He put his phone away and looked up at the PAN, which had disappeared. No other witnesses were interviewed. The peculiarity of this observation case largely depends on the witness's interpretation of the observed phenomenon: if one considers the description of the PAN as a saucer, the peculiarity becomes very significant; if one assumes that the object was too far away for this interpretation to be relevant, the peculiarity of the case diminishes and a simple hypothesis of confusion with a star remains plausible (such confusion is common with stars or planets observed low on the horizon). Furthermore, this case has average to low consistency due to the imprecision of the position and direction of observation, and the absence of a photograph. The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'C' due to a lack of corroborating evidence.
