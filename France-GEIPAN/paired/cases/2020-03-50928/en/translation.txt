Description: On March 8, 2020, around 3:00 PM, a witness, in a field, observed the passage of a dark, triangular but rounded-corner PAN. The witness attempted to take a photo, but the PAN was already distant. The witness then compared the PAN to "a dense palm tree head in its center, without color, with the fronds slowly moving with the wind around the perimeter." No unusual noises were heard. The PAN moved on a descending trajectory and was quickly out of sight. No other witness statements were collected regarding this phenomenon. Through analysis, we have shown that the PAN observed by the witness was very likely a drifting fantasy balloon (possibly made of Mylar) influenced by a light breeze present at the time and location of the observation (see investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Original, whimsical shape,
- Movement in the direction of the dominant light breeze.

In addition to the oddness of the shape, the apparent "heaviness" of the PAN is, in the absence of sunlight, induced by its massive appearance, its central rounded shape, and its dark color. Another oddity is more difficult to explain; it is the change in the shape of the PAN, which transitions from a triangular shape with rounded corners to a dense palm tree head in its center, without color, with fronds slowly moving with the wind around the perimeter (using the witness's analogy). This change could have been caused by the rotation of the PAN, which has an asymmetric shape. After leaving to retrieve his monocular, the witness noticed the new shape of the PAN. It could also be two separate objects, although an explanation would be needed as to why the witness did not observe both objects simultaneously at any point. A very small angular size could explain this, but we have no precise information on this.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of one or two fantasy balloons.
