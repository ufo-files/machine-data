Summary: Long observation of a colorful, static, and silent light phenomenon in the sky: likely astronomical observation of Sirius.
--- seg-8242f8b3bda381e9d06e6a12 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 23, 2011, around 4:45 a.m., a driver and their passenger were intrigued by the presence in the sky at low altitude of a luminous phenomenon with varied colors (blue, red) and seen with particular shapes. This phenomenon remained static, and no noise was heard during the observation that lasted 3/4 of an hour. The witness then decided to continue their journey. Only one testimony about this phenomenon was collected. The strangeness of the observed phenomenon, static for over 30 minutes, is low; it is the witness's description (triangles, glowing threads, etc.) that makes it strange. The consistency of the case is low since, unfortunately, only one witness was willing to contact us. Since the witness initially recognized a star, then saw details after observing it attentively, the GEIPAN favors the hypothesis of a star: Sirius low on the horizon is a good candidate; it is often the subject of this type of misidentification under these observation conditions that induce strong scintillation (changing multiple colors). However, a notable position difference between Sirius and the direction of observation noted by the investigator a posteriori, but 18 months later, is noted. This case is classified B: probable astronomical observation of Sirius.
