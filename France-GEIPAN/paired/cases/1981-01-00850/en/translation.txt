Summary: Observations of the silent movement of a elongated object of "fire" color during dawn: probable observation of high-altitude aircraft trails illuminated by the rising sun.
--- seg-ade86cf8eadf78b77dba98d7 [machine-unreviewed] ---
**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined to enhance the conclusions' relevance. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named CALAIS (62) 1981 is part of a recent batch of reexamined cases. On January 9, 1981, around 9:00 AM, three witnesses working in a nursing home observed in the sky the east-west movement of an elongated, yellow-red object. This object moved at a very high speed but no sound was heard. During the observation, which lasted 3 to 4 minutes, the object was obscured by clouds. Notably, two of the witnesses observed the same phenomenon the previous day. It should be known that the entire complex was razed starting in 2013, making it impossible to pinpoint the exact building where the witnesses were located (although two extreme positions were considered in the investigation report). The PAN observed by the witnesses exhibits all the characteristics of a condensation trail produced by an airplane flying at high altitude and illuminated by the rising sun (as detailed in the investigation report):

- A cigar-shaped or oval form similar to photographic examples of such trails.
- "Fire" color or typical red/orange/yellow of such trails when illuminated by the low sun on the horizon (at sunrise or sunset).
- Movement similar to that of a very distant object, "deeply far away," as noted by witness 1.

It is relatively common, under similar lighting conditions (sun near the horizon) and in high-altitude, very stable atmospheric conditions, for the condensation trail of a commercial jet to produce such visual effects, which are now well-documented with numerous photo testimonies of the GEIPAN. Common characteristics include that airplanes producing these short condensation trails are photographed near dawn or dusk, with the sun close to the horizon, resulting in a yellow-orange (sometimes even red) color on the trails, creating the "fire" impression noted by one of the witnesses. In 1981, the GEIPAN lacked the experience, now acquired and verified, of such testimony created by airplane trails. This led to the case's initial classification as unexplained. The airplane itself may or may not be visible, and when it is, it often appears as a very bright point preceding the condensation trails (see examples #7 and 9 of condensation trails). Most of the time, this bright point appears only briefly, depending on the geometry of the surface reflecting sunlight and the airplane's movement relative to the observers' position. The witnesses did not observe this phenomenon, but the PAN was only intermittently visible, occasionally obscured by clouds.

Regarding these trails, they can be more or less elongated, in the shape of a "V," a fuse, or an oval, which corresponds well to the witnesses' descriptions of the PAN's shape ("cigar," "circle," "oval"). When the implicated airplane made a turn, the perspective was likely more favorable, making the separation between the two condensation trails more visible, giving one witness the impression that at that moment, "it seemed cut in half." Finally, witness 1 notes the presence of "two white trails" before the PAN disappeared. This mention of white trails confirms the hypothesis of confusion with condensation trails of an airplane. It is impossible, 37 years later, to identify the specific airplane. However, given the low altitude observation (trails can indeed take on ochre colors at sunrise or sunset), the airplane could have been located between 50 and 150 kilometers east of Calais, encompassing a significant possibility of traffic in 1981 and leaving a reasonable chance of finding an airplane on a trajectory with a more or less pronounced turn. The consistency of the testimony is relatively weak. While there are indeed three dependent witnesses, they provide few quantifiable elements (no angular data, for example). The qualitative information given is sufficiently characteristic to validate the explanation.

**Conclusion:** The GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of airplane condensation trails flying at high altitude and illuminated by the rising sun.
