Description: A witness reports an observation made on July 17, 2012 at 4:03 a.m.: from his terrace, he observes two particularly bright points in the sky directed towards East-Southeast. He films his observation and, upon reviewing his video, discovers a third point. This ensemble appears to him as a triangle. He makes the same observation two days later. The observation of two fixed, very bright light points points towards an astronomical origin hypothesis: the sky map for that day indicates the simultaneous presence of Venus and Jupiter in the East/Northeast direction (see astro map). The witness's video is consistent with the arrangement of these stars in the sky. The third, less bright light point is Aldebaran, a very bright star (magnitude -0.6). The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A" for perfectly identified: observation of the planets Venus and Jupiter, and the star Aldebaran.
