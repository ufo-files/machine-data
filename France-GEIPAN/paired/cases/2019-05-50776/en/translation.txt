Summary: Observations of the slow passage from west to east of a white bright point in the deep sky: mistaken for the ISS (International Space Station).
--- seg-72d496a21620121d9bf4105f [needs-review] ---
Description: On May 31st around 11 PM, a witness on an outdoor terrace was intrigued by a bright white light in the sky. This PAN moved slowly from West to East. The witness called his friend, who also saw the light. The intensity of the light dimmed gradually until it disappeared. However, the witness was able to record a video. On June 1st at 10 PM, the witness saw the same phenomenon in the same direction and with the same trajectory. During his deposition at the Gendarmerie on June 2nd, 2019, the witness showed the gendarmes his short video: they indeed noticed a white circle on a black background. Unfortunately, the witness lost the video, which cannot be attached to the report. Only one testimony was collected on this phenomenon. Of low strangeness and good consistency (2 witnesses, but only one testimony; video capture of PAN, but video lost by T1), the case turns out to be a misidentification with the International Space Station (ISS) (see the investigation report). The description of the PAN (very bright white light) resembles the visual appearance of the ISS, which was passing overhead on the nights of May 31st and June 1st, 2019, at times consistent with those of the PAN. The ISS's trajectory matches well with that of the PAN, as it was moving from West to East, passing south of the location of the two successive observations. The determining factor for a misidentification with the ISS is the gradual disappearance of the PAN along its trajectory, which is characteristic of the ISS entering the Earth's shadow. Yet, the ISS did indeed enter the Earth's shadow during its passes on the indicated dates. The case is classified A, misidentification with the International Space Station.
