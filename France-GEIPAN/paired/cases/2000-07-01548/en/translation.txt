Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past many years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named SAINT-AFFRIQUE (12) 2000 is part of a recent set of cases that have been re-examined. On July 21, 2000, around 11:30 PM, a man and his son were observing the sky. They suddenly noticed, with the naked eye, five blinking white lights. The trajectories of these lights were short, with some turning around before disappearing. The observation lasted 5 minutes. The lights disappeared one after the other. The observation of blinking white lights moving linearly in the sky is indicative of an airplane. What alerted the witnesses here were the trajectories with turns and the sudden appearance and disappearance of these lights. Nevertheless, the hypothesis of an airplane, even if it cannot be a commercial airliner, remains possible and the most probable (military or general aviation) in conjunction with the presence of some clouds that could have obscured the planes. The data that would have allowed this hypothesis to be confirmed or refuted were not (or could not be) collected at the time of the testimony and are not available today. (We only have the Millau 30 km data from Météociel indicating a sky not entirely clear at 4 PM). This hypothesis has the advantage of completely explaining the observation while still being part of the possible or plausible. The lack of corroboration does not allow us to retain this as an explanation of the observation, but this same possibility (in light of the current methodology of the GEIPAN, see GEIPAN news) prevents us from considering this observation as strange and unexplained. We consider this observation as slightly strange and lacking in information to strengthen the airplane hypothesis, which is otherwise plausible. The GEIPAN classifies this case as C: lack of reliable information.
