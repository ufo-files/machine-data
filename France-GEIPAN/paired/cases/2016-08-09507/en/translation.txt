Description: On Saturday, August 6, 2016, at 11:05 PM, a couple in a garden observed the sky and saw four bright points flying silently in formation to the south-south-east above a nearby building. The PAN converged towards a single point, moved vertically upwards, then disappeared. The observation was very brief, and only one statement was collected. The description of the PAN does not allow any of the considered explanations to be retained with sufficient probability (see the investigation report). It could thus suggest a light show of a laser type or a flight of naturally (fireflies) or artificially (city lights) illuminated insects. However, the sky was perfectly clear, which does not align with the hypothesis of a laser light show. The hypothesis of a flight of insects does not fit with the disappearance of the PAN. The short duration of the observation (2 or 3 seconds) could also suggest a meteor, but the grouping of the bright points together and the absence of a glowing trail do not support this hypothesis. A meteor tends to fragment, not assemble. The case is somewhat strange but still moderate, as the considered explanations cannot be retained with a probability greater than 50% nor completely rejected. Note that the observation duration is very short (2 or 3 seconds) and the statement lacks precision (possibly due to the brief duration and the probable great distance of the PAN) especially regarding the angular size of the PAN. Finally, the statement is unique, while there was another witness (the witness's girlfriend) who only "confirmed" the observation (without a statement) and who is described by the main witness as "less intrigued" than him; and other friends were also present, looking at the sky but not in that direction. The lack of information and cross-checking does not allow the level of strangeness of the observation to be validated. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
