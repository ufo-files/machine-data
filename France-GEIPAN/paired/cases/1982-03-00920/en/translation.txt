--- seg-4333cb6c739739ab2cab9ca0 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. In the evening of February 27, 1982, at 8:15 PM, a witness is watching television when suddenly he sees through his living room window a luminous phenomenon described as a large ball and orange-colored flames. Twenty minutes later, the witness notices through the same window the presence of three balls about one meter from the ground, emitting lights of different colors (red, green, orange). No noise is emitted by this phenomenon. The witness observes these objects until 10:45 PM. He watches television again and does not see the phenomenon disappear. Only one testimony was collected. The witness remains shocked by his observation. This testimony is characterized by a strong strangeness as there is no solid hypothesis to explain the appearance and presence for several hours of 3 or 4 glowing balls, even though there are stranger things than simple fixed lights. It is also characterized by low consistency in terms of information: - The gendarmes made a site survey with photos to determine the observed position of the PAN. One may regret the absence of angular measurements of the balls, precision on movement or not. Above all, there is no observation by the witness of the disappearance of the phenomenon or, even more, in terms of reliability; - There is a strong inconsistency in the expression of the strangeness experienced by the witness. The witness takes 20 minutes to go to the window after being alerted by a glowing ball in the sky (with orange-colored flames). After noticing the three flickering balls one meter from the ground, the witness states that he continued to observe the objects for 2.5 hours but did not see their disappearance "because he was watching television." One may think that the symptoms perceived after this observation (and that the witness associated with this observation) could have amplified a strangeness (which was perceived much weaker at the time of the observation) to the point of possibly influencing memorization or narration; - The reliability of the information is tainted in several points: the witness no longer mentions the initial light in the sky that he observed at the window, while the gendarmes specify that it remained above the other three balls throughout. The witness states that he "never took an interest, either through reading or any other media, in studying this type of phenomenon," while, in addition to the fact that he knows how to contact the GEPAN in less than a few work hours (on Monday morning for a Saturday evening observation) during a period without the internet, he contradicts the gendarmes by adding that he has always wanted to be there when an OVNI appears. The consistency of this testimony and all that could be collected is insufficient in absolute terms and, even more, in view of the resulting strangeness. It is not possible to qualify such an observation as unexplained or inexplicable, which, in fact, is unusable. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
