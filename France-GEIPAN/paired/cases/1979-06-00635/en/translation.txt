Summary: Distant observations of a slow-moving red phenomenon: mistaking the Moon at sunset.
--- seg-35f2853b7e90d4ffb7818128 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time as Type 'A' cases, which are now being reassessed. This reassessment is not intended to reduce or increase any particular category of classification, but to make the conclusions more relevant. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or explicit changes in classification noted in the investigation reports. This observation case is part of Type 'A' cases classified in 1979 by GEPAN under the name COULLONS (45) 10.06.1979. On June 30, 1979, around 0:30 a.m., four people in a vehicle were traveling on D 54 between Saint-Florent (45) and Villemurlin (45) (see investigation notes). Upon reaching the path leading to the location known as Pierrefitte, they spotted in the distance a red phenomenon that seemed to descend from the sky at the level of the road. The shape described varies among witnesses (in the form of a banana with the tips pointing towards the sky or in the form of a drop). With the car stopped, they observed the PAN: no noise was heard, and no intermittent light signals were noticed on the PAN. The witnesses then saw the object move slowly. Afraid, they decided to turn around to return to Coullons to alert the gendarmerie. One of the witnesses saw the PAN disappear behind trees bordering the road. Received by the gendarmes at 0:55 a.m., they immediately set out to the indicated location without the witnesses. Initial observations that same night at 1:20 a.m. failed to find any traces. The next day, the gendarmes, accompanied by the witnesses, returned to the presumed location of the PAN and discovered a fire pit with a diameter of 1.60m. The ashes were still hot. No other traces on the ground or in the vegetation, and no other testimonies were collected. The investigation demonstrated that the night before (June 28) farmers had lit a brush fire. The gendarmes hypothesized that the witnesses likely saw a whirl of debris from the brush fire, which would explain the pear or drop shape of the phenomenon and its red color. Despite a spatial coincidence between the apparent location of the PAN and the location of the brush fire, the hypothesis of a mistake with the latter does not align well with the observation of the PAN. Today, the conceivable hypothesis of a mistake with the Moon is supported not only by the fact that the Moon was setting, which is perfectly consistent with the red color of the PAN, but also by the fact that its azimuth (281°) corresponds perfectly with the axis of D54. Furthermore, the visual aspect of the Moon at sunset is perfectly consistent with the description of the PAN provided by the witnesses, the crescent moon being positioned on the horizon. Although the witnesses do not mention the presence of the Moon, it was visible in the axis they indicate. The perfect consistency between the true position of the Moon, its location, and the descriptions provided by the witnesses leaves no doubt about the mistake. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, driving, fear). The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: mistake with the setting Moon.
