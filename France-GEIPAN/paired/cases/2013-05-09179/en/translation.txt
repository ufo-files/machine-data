Description: Testimony received in May 2015 regarding an observation made on May 25, 2013. On the evenings of May 25, 26, and 27, 2013 (uncertain time between 9:00 PM and 10:00 PM), witness (T1) and two other family members (T2 and T3) observed three low-altitude lights of yellow-orange color heading west. The phenomenon was stationary, then moved downward, and disappeared behind the urban landscape. No unusual noises were heard. The observation was made with the naked eye and binoculars, leading to different descriptions of the UFO (shape, colors, etc.). Only one questionnaire was collected. According to the investigation report (attached), the elements of strangeness are:

- Stars appearing abnormal: reported by T1 and T3.
- Light bar (observed with binoculars): reported by T1 and T3.
- Movement of lights: precisely described by T1, except for the time, which is certain to within an hour. Not part of T3's observation. Confusing for T2, beyond inconsistencies or contradictions, these movements were not spontaneously reported and thus do not seem to represent a strong perceived strangeness. T2's testimony does not confirm T1's regarding these movements of the UFO. Only two hypotheses are considered for this succession of three nights of the three fixed, then moving lights: helicopters or drones. However, neither can be reasonably retained (probability < 30%). The consistency of the testimony is low:

- T1 has been interested in UFOs since adolescence, satisfied to see observations on the first night and connect them to UFOs, reflecting on them in the days that follow, but did not think to take photos or videos, nor to call neighbors or friends, not even on the third night when the observation had already occurred twice.
- T1, though pre-sensitized to the strange, waited two years to trigger the testimony of his own strange observation. No fear or restraint created by horror or strangeness is found in him that could explain the delay in testifying, as he publicly discusses it on forums and in the press. This would tend to reduce the reality of the perceived strangeness at the time of the observation.
- Inconsistencies (within the testimonies) or contradictions (between the testimonies) on notable points. The observation does not find a plausible explanation. Nevertheless, the consistency of the testimony is too low to validate the strange and unexplained character of the observation. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
