Description: Witness account received in May 2015 concerning an observation made on May 25, 2013. On the evenings of May 25, 26 and 27, 2013 (uncertain time between 21:00 and 22:00), the witness (T1) and two other members of his family (T2 and T3) observe 3 coloured lights (yellow-orange) at low altitude towards the west. The phenomenon is stationary, then moves downwards and disappears behind the urban landscape. No particular noise is heard. The observation is made with the naked eye and with binoculars, producing different descriptions of the PAN (shape, colours, etc.). Only one questionnaire is collected. According to the attached investigation report, the unusual features are: - Stars whose appearance is not normal, for T1 and T3; - The bar of light (seen through binoculars), for T1 and T3; - Movement of the lights, described precisely by T1, except for the time, which is certain only to more than an hour. This is not part of T3's observation. For T2 it is confused: beyond the inconsistencies or contradictions, these movements are not reported spontaneously and therefore do not appear to represent a strongly perceived unusual feature. T2's account does not corroborate T1's regarding these movements of the PAN. Only two hypotheses are considered for this succession, on 3 consecutive evenings, of these 3 lights, initially fixed and then moving: helicopters or drones. However, neither can reasonably be retained (probability < 30%). The strength of the witness account is low: - T1 has been interested in OVNI since adolescence, is pleased from the first evening to make observations and be able to connect them to OVNI, and thinks about them again in the following days, but does not think of taking photographs or videos or calling neighbours or friends, not even on the 3rd evening, although the observation has already occurred on 2 consecutive evenings; - T1, despite already being sensitised to unusual phenomena, waited two years to report his own unusual observation. One does not find in him the fear or reserve caused by fright or strangeness that might explain the delay in reporting, since he talks about it publicly on forums and in the press. This would tend to reduce the reality of the strangeness perceived at the time of observation; - Inconsistencies within the accounts or contradictions between the accounts concerning significant points. The observation does not have a plausible explanation. Nevertheless, the strength of the account is too low to validate the strange and unexplained nature of the observation. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
