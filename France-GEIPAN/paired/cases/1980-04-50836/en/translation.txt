**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions' relevance. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The observation case previously named LE CHESNAY (78) 1980 and classified as D is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases. On April 5, 1980, around 10:45 PM, a witness in LE CHESNAY (78) observed a very bright white ball silently ascending in jumps into the sky, leaving a white trail behind. The object then moved horizontally at extremely high speed before disappearing. Intrigued, the witness discussed it with others, and another person reported seeing a similar phenomenon a little earlier in PARIS (75). Both witnesses were interviewed at the Gendarmerie. Two observation cases were created: CHESNAY (LE) (78) 05.04.1980 and PARIS (75) 05.04.1980.

The two phenomena observed by the witnesses were likely two meteoroid entries observed approximately one hour apart over the Paris region, moving along different trajectories (as detailed in the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following:

- PAN described as spherical, white, and bright;
- presence of a trail;
- very short observation duration, on the order of a few seconds;
- straight-line movement for the PAN observed by T2.

Some peculiarities were explained:

- For T2, the initial apparent immobility of the PAN for two seconds is consistent with certain bolide observations at the very beginning of their atmospheric entry before the trail forms;
- For T1, the first peculiarity, regarding the "jumps" in movement, is merely an appearance and is likely caused by variations in the bolide's brightness;
- The second peculiarity, more significant, is related to a trajectory that appears to be "at a right angle." We demonstrated that this "right angle" notion should be relativized for several reasons:

    - The geographic indications given by the witness regarding the initial and final azimuths of the PAN are imprecise and leave open possibilities for the PAN to move at an angle up to approximately 150°, suggesting a curved trajectory;
    - This type of curved trajectory, though rare, does exist for bolides, and we have found examples;
    - The witness stated they had to "bend their head back" to continue observing the PAN, which may simply mean they had to raise their head to follow it as it moved out of their field of vision;
    - A perspective effect is very likely present. The witness observes the PAN "ascending into the sky" and assumes it is a vertical-rising object. When they realize they need to raise their head to follow it, they perceive a change in trajectory for the PAN to move parallel to the Earth, giving the impression of an overall "perpendicular" trajectory.

In 1980, there were no current facilities for recording witness accounts of meteoroid passages. The absence of other records is not decisive. Modern recordings show that entries occur quite randomly and can happen within short intervals over nearby locations. The GEIPAN of that time lacked this experience and could rule out the meteoroid hypothesis by searching for a single phenomenon for both observations. The consistency is average, with a concise description of the PAN. Although the witnesses provide initial elevation angle data for both PAN, additional data would have been beneficial, especially for the PAN observed by T1, which lacks precision in the final azimuth and elevation. There is a strong probability of contamination between T1 and T2 witness accounts of distinct PAN observations, as the witnesses naturally tended to align their accounts after discussing them.

In conclusion, the GEIPAN classifies the case as:

- LE CHESNAY (78) 05.04.1980 as B: probable meteoroid entry observation for the T1 account.
- PARIS (75) 05.04.1980 as A: very probable meteoroid entry observation for the T2 account.
