Description: This recent testimony received in 2014 regarding an old observation (1986) does not mention a specific date (the first of August is given arbitrarily). The account of bright points crossing the sky from north to south in 12 minutes in the evening clearly evokes the passage of artificial satellites, as they could be easily observed at that time and even more so today. Of course, the zigzag trajectory described by the witness does not fit this hypothesis, but the autokinetic illusion (see Perception of the movement of a bright point in the sky) explains this impression, which is quite frequently described by witnesses under certain observation conditions. The impression of high-speed escape corresponds to the satellite entering the Earth's shadow. Since the witness did not provide a precise date, the GEIPAN was unable to verify which satellite might have passed at that time. This case therefore remains in category "C" due to the lack of a precise date.
