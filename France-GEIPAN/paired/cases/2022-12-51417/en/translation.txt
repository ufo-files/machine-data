Summary: Successive observations of dark shapes accompanied by a bright point: Observation of celestial lanterns.
--- seg-afa4c3f924ec070d2d37572a [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 13, 2022, around 5:50 PM, the main witness (T1), stationed at the control tower of Pau-Pyrénées Airport, observed from the tower's terrace, the presence in the sky of a PAN of approximately round shape and dark color with a glowing point. The object follows a straight and stable trajectory from east to west. The bright point disappears as the PAN passes north of the tower. A colleague (T2) also observes the PAN, which eventually disappears over the horizon, heading northwest. After informing the Marina "Detection and Control Center" (CDC) in Mont-de-Marsan, which confirms the absence of any low-flying aircraft, as well as the Aerial Transport Gendarmerie in Pau, a second unidentified aero-spatial phenomenon (PAN), identical to the first, appears at the same location and follows a similar trajectory. This event occurs simultaneously with the final approach of an Air France flight on runway 31 at the airport. Neither the crew of this flight, contacted by the control tower, nor the gendarmes present were able to observe this second PAN. A small, bright point, slightly more yellow in hue than the first PAN, is also visible and disappears under similar conditions as the PAN reaches the north position relative to the control tower. Both of the main witness's colleagues, T2 and T3, also observe this PAN, which eventually disappears in the same manner as the first one. According to the GEIPAN criteria, this observation case presents a satisfactory consistency. Despite the absence of photographs and videos of the PAN, it is corroborated by several detailed and precise witness accounts. *Consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation revealed that the characteristics of these two PAN as described by the witnesses were entirely compatible with objects carried by the wind, such as fancy balloons, possibly made of Mylar, or sky lanterns (see the investigation report). This first conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Movement in the direction of the dominant wind, from east to west
- Regular straight trajectory
- Roughly round shape for T1, rounded and slightly elongated upward for T2, and spherical for T3
- Calculations give a traveled distance of the order of 300 m at a distance of about 65 m closest to the witnesses for a speed of 5 m/s (that of the wind at 150 m altitude). We then established that these wind-carried objects were celestial lanterns, not balloons, based on the following elements:

- Presence of a white, then yellow, light positioned at the base of the PAN as indicated by T2 and T3
- Disappearance of this light, corresponding to the extinguishing of the candle
- T3: "In both cases, for me, it's Japanese lanterns"
- Oval shape The observation conditions at twilight did not allow T1 to clearly identify these lanterns, which is not the case for T3, for whom this hypothesis does not seem to be in doubt. The disappearance of the bright spots is explained by the extinguishing of the candle in the lanterns, which had reached the end of its life. The observation took place on a Tuesday evening, while lantern releases are generally conducted later in the week, during special celebrations (weddings, birthdays, etc.). However, near Christmas, the release of two lanterns by a private individual in preparation for the holidays is possible. The GEIPAN classifies the case as 'A': observation of two celestial lanterns.
