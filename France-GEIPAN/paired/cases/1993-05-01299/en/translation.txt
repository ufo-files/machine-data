Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named [AERO LOC] NORGES-LA-VILLE (21) 16.05.1993 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed (see the investigation report). On May 16, 1993, at 7:15 PM, a pilot of a ULM (Ultralight Motorized Aircraft) and his passenger observed a blue-reflected sphere spinning on itself while moving at high speed and quickly approaching the ULM. The pilot made a turn, and the PAN crossed his path. The pilot made another turn to better see and follow it, but the PAN had disappeared. The observation lasted 15 to 20 seconds. The weather and visibility were excellent that day. Only one testimony was filed with the Gendarmerie, but the second witness (passenger) confirmed by phone the pilot's statements. The PAN observed by the witnesses is likely a Mylar balloon carried by the wind at high altitude. The appearance is consistent: a spherical shape and metallic color reflecting sunlight. Such balloons have existed since the late 1970s, and especially at the beginning, they were often in a circular or oval shape. They can be inflated with air but are most often filled with helium, allowing them to rise to high altitudes and fly for between two weeks and two months. The witness's perception of a rapid movement contrary to his own is perfectly compatible given the speed of the witness. - The perception of the movement of a PAN results from a difficult perception of angular movement here due to the witness's own movement and an impossible distance perception for any witness who does not recognize the PAN. The witness himself says, "In fact, it all depends on the distance that separated me from the crossing of this device that I cannot estimate." - The wind speed at 2400 meters is unknown, but the witness estimates it as zero. Even at a standstill, the PAN can be perceived with a contrary speed, stronger or weaker, depending on the witness's distance crossing assessment. - Given the available elements, the witness's perception "of a contrary rapid flight opposite to his own movement" is compatible with a balloon flight. A perception "in the same direction or faster" would have created an incompatibility. The witness seems to have observed a rotation of the sphere on itself, but he is not certain, explaining it by a hypothetical protrusion that caught his attention. Furthermore, the fact that the witness could no longer observe the balloon once he made his turn is not surprising: with low angular dimensions, by the time this turn was made, the balloon had long since disappeared from his view. The consistency is average, with a fairly good description of the PAN. Angular data would have been useful, as it might help better determine the distance and the compatibility with the witness's given speed perception. We can also regret the absence of the second testimony. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Mylar balloon.
