Description: On October 7, 2013, around 2:15 PM, during his journey outside the village of Niedsoultzbach (67), a witness observed a large, clear, oblong shape stationary in the sky. This observation lasted approximately 3 minutes until the object became invisible due to the terrain. Two minutes later, the driver stopped, got out of his car, and attempted to locate the PAN, but to no avail. According to the witness' estimates, the object appeared to be less than 3 kilometers away, at an altitude of approximately 200 to 300 meters. The sky was covered by a low cloud, with a base below 400 meters. Only one witness statement was collected. By the end of October, the zeppelin was transferred for winter storage to Friedrichshafen, Germany, where it remained until March. The date (October) and location (Niedersoultzbach) fall along the direct trajectory between the Zeppelin's departure base at Pontoise-Corneilles in Vexin and Friedrichshafen, making it likely that the Zeppelin was overhead when witnessed by the observer. The visual characteristics of the zeppelin, particularly its shape and color, also correspond to the witness's description (white oblong shape). Furthermore, the dimensions of the zeppelin (75 meters long by 19 meters wide, with a cabin approximately 10 meters long) are consistent with the witness's estimates (10 centimeters at arm's length, corresponding to a distance of 750 meters for an object 75 meters long, without distinction of the nacelle). The Zeppelin is equipped with a helium-filled balloon with electric propulsion, explaining the absence of noise. Its maximum speed of 120 km/h can create the illusion of apparent immobility, especially as the automobilist is moving in the same direction and at the same speed as the Zeppelin. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B, probable observation of a Zeppelin.
