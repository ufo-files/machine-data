Summary: East-to-west (E-W) observation of a very bright white phenomenon with a red mark in front; sudden extinction in a clear sky: observation of a meteoroid re-entry.
--- seg-c28d6ab192306310663e7548 [needs-review] ---
Description: On February 17th around 8 PM, a witness in their garden observed an east-to-west passage of a very bright white, tube-shaped phenomenon with a red mark at the front. It abruptly disappears in the clear sky. The observation lasts between 5 to 6 seconds. No other testimony was collected. The bright and elongated visual aspect, as well as the linear and rapid trajectory in the sky, are characteristic of a meteor. The BOAM station in May-sur-Orne recorded a meteor at the same time according to a visible trajectory (from the station) located in an azimutal quadrant (150° to 160°) where the observation location is precisely situated, and a direction conferring a clear perception from EAST to WEST. The high elevation (45 to 60°) observed on both sides is consistent with a meteor at an altitude of 50 to 100 km moving from east to west, with the axis connecting the two points separated by 130 km. The witness was at the eastern limit of the station's visibility and was thus able to start seeing the meteoroid before and with a greater excursion in the sky (3/4 of the sky according to the witness). The witness very likely saw a meteor reentry. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: bolide observation.
