Description: The GEIPAN publishes its archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type "C" cases that may be subject to re-examination, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This case previously classified as "C" is being revisited. An investigation note refers only to the testimonies of the two witnesses of the observation on September 18, 1977. A third witness found during the investigation provides a testimony too vague (uncertain date, unknown time) to be taken into account in the investigation.

On September 18, 1977, between 10:30 PM and 10:35 PM, two witnesses in Malaucene (84) observed a luminous orange-fire phenomenon in the direction of the Bel-Air hill. The witnesses had time to see a rectangular shape that quickly disappeared behind the hill but illuminated the immediate surroundings. During the gendarmerie investigation, a third witness was found and stated that three weeks earlier he had seen a bright light behind another hill. Despite an appeal for witnesses in the press, no other testimonies were collected.

This case is consistent (two witnesses), the description of the PAN observed is fairly precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The phenomenon described has many common characteristics (shape, size, color, glow) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the setting Moon (see the investigation note). Moreover, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, while the witnesses made no mention of it. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, night observation).

In the current classification of GEIPAN, this case of average strangeness is classified as A: observation related to a misperception with the Moon.
