**Report on Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) Observation in Sainte-Foy-de-Pérollière, France (31) on July 8, 2018**

On July 8, 2018, at 11:30 PM local time, a witness contacted the GEIPAN (French UFO Investigation Body) by phone, shortly after witnessing a brief observation of a PAN in Sainte-Foy-de-Pérollière, France (31). The witness was unable to take any photos or videos of the phenomenon. Another witness present that evening did not see the UFO due to the rapid nature of the event, as described in the sole testimony collected.

Two months after the observation, the witness completed a technical questionnaire, providing a detailed description of their observation of moving bright lights in the night sky. The witness reported variations in the trajectory of the bright points and a dark object, movements at what seemed to be "blitzing speed," changes in the geometric shape of the lights, and fluctuations in brightness until the phenomenon disappeared. The entire observation lasted only about 20 seconds and was divided into five phases:

1. **Phase 1 (1 minute after a meteor shower):** Lasted 4-5 seconds. 5-6 very bright points appeared and descended over a short distance, creating a "cascading" effect, then their brightness dimmed.

2. **Phase 2 (5-6 seconds after Phase 1):** Four bright points formed a straight line, continuing the cascading effect observed earlier. This formation moved slowly downward, gradually closing in on a dark, rectangular shape, with the four bright points aligned geometrically on it. The witness noted dark orange hues at the end of this phase.

3. **Phase 3:** The movement changed from vertical to horizontal, still at what seemed to be a reduced speed. The rectangular shape transformed into a triangle.

4. **Phase 4 (the most unusual):** The object accelerated suddenly, without sound or trail, at what seemed like "blitzing speed." The witness followed it with their eyes for 1-2 seconds.

5. **Phase 5:** The shape returned to a rectangular form (like in Phase 2) with slow movement, then faded into the night and disappeared.

Only one detailed testimony was collected for this PAN. Despite the lack of photos or videos, the GEIPAN deemed the consistency of the testimony good. A field investigation allowed for a reconstruction, and a cognitive interview with the witness enhanced the investigator's understanding of the phenomenon.

While the interview took place after some time had passed since the observation, it also confirmed the coherence of the testimony. (Note: According to GEIPAN criteria, the strength of a testimony is evaluated based on the quantity and reliability of the information collected.)

This testimony includes a significant amount of very precise information about the key phase (Phase 4 described by the witness), with an estimated duration of 1-2 seconds. The observation was chaotic and discontinuous: the witness mentioned having to "leap," "pass a large tree (laurel)," and "continue visually following" the UFO, which they were able to do.

The witness found the experience very strange. The main elements of strangeness include:

- Absence of sound
- Perceived sudden acceleration of the UFO in Phase 4
- Bright points that could form dark geometric shapes (alternating between rectangular and triangular)

After investigation, the strangeness of the observation remains high, and the consistency of the testimony is not in question. This investigation was lengthy and rich in content and hypotheses (see the investigation report). The GEIPAN employed its most comprehensive investigation methodology (both remote and on-site) and consulted its expert committee twice.

Seven hypotheses were considered:

1. Passage of a satellite train
2. Fragmentation during atmospheric reentry
3. Fireworks
4. Aircraft
5. Formation flight of multiple/squadron aircraft
6. Retinal persistence phenomenon due to possible observation of bright lights illuminating the house facade before the UFO observation
7. Drone display

The hypothesis of formation flight of multiple/squadron military or civilian aircraft with lights on is the most consistent with the witnessed elements. However, the GEIPAN disqualifies this hypothesis based on two factors:

1. Angular distance traveled: Approximately 90-110 degrees in 4 seconds, implying a very high speed and thus proximity, but no sound was perceived despite a carrying wind.

2. Radar traces did not show as many aircraft, and their spatial distribution (altitude and direction) did not align with the witness's indications. Furthermore, an expert aeronaut from the GEIPAN considers night formation flight improbable, contributing to the hypothesis's disqualification.

Regarding radar returns, the GEIPAN regrets not having obtained the radar return of air traffic from the National Air Operations Center (CNOA) in time for this investigation. A request for the return was relanced too late regarding the data preservation deadline by the CNOA. However, the center for air traffic control in Blagnac provided radar data around Toulouse, but within a temporal range possibly too late in relation to the observation.

Based on the investigation and expertise of the expert committee, the GEIPAN classifies this observation as "D": observation of a phenomenon of unknown nature and origin.
