Summary: Observation of a straight-line passage across the sky of a white light: observation of the ISS (International Space Station).
--- seg-a2e9645382dd5833f2420695 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On March 26, 2025, between 8:15 and 8:20 p.m. local time, a witness was walking towards their home in the commune of Veyrier, in the canton of Geneva (Switzerland). Looking up at the sky, they spotted a bright white light with very high luminescence. The phenomenon presented a uniform white hue, with no perceptible chromatic variation, and an intensity significantly higher than that of visible stars at that time. The light source moved in a straight line at a rapid speed, without any apparent change in trajectory or speed. The observation, made with the naked eye outdoors without visual obstructions, lasted less than ten seconds. On March 30, 2025, the witness submitted a Technical Questionnaire (QT) to the GEIPAN detailing the circumstances and specific parameters of the observation. The information from the Technical Questionnaire indicates the observation began in the northwest direction with an estimated elevation of 60°, and ended towards the southeast marked by the light disappearing above Mont Salève. Meteorological analysis shows that the Bossey station (Haute-Savoie), located less than 5 km from the observation site, recorded a temperature of approximately 11°C, a weak wind of 2-3 km/h directed between southeast and northeast, and no precipitation between 8:00 and 8:30 p.m. Satellite images from Météociel confirm a generally clear sky over Veyrier, indicated by a red dot on the map (see Annex 1). Aeronautical analysis using the Flightradar24 tool reveals a commercial airliner passing through the observation window, following a trajectory consistent with the direction reported by the witness. This was flight EJU78LF operated by EasyJet, traveling from Paris to Naples. However, the aircraft was well to the west, behind Mont Salève, at a minimum distance of approximately 15 km from the witness (ground projection) and at an altitude of 37,000 feet (about 11.3 km). This configuration corresponds to an angular elevation of around 37°, significantly lower than that reported by the witness (60°) (see Annex 2). Astronautical analysis provided by COSE to the CNES highlights the International Space Station (ISS) passing through the considered time window, between 7:16 and 7:20 p.m. UTC, with consistent azimuths and elevations matching the description. The ISS trajectory extends from northwest (azimuth 296°) to southeast (azimuth 140°), with an elevation ranging between 10° and a maximum of 86° (see Annex 3). The case is considered to have average consistency: the information submitted in the Technical Questionnaire is accurate, but only one witness is recorded and no images (photos or videos) have been provided. The observed characteristics - intense brightness, rapid and regular movement, consistent direction and timing - fully correspond to the ISS passage. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": Observation of the International Space Station (ISS).

*COSE: Operational Center for Space Surveillance.
