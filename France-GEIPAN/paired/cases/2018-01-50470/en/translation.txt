Description: On Thursday, January 18, 2018, at 6:27 PM, two witnesses were intrigued by the movement in the sky of a rounded shape of very intense orange-red color that suddenly changed direction. No noise was heard during the observations. T1 takes a photograph. The round shape, bright orange color, and predominantly west-to-east movement consistent with a rising wind (see the Infoclimat site) are all elements that lead to considering the flight of a Thai lantern as a likely hypothesis. The lanterns are mostly launched on weekends, but a festive event can also take place on a Thursday evening (birthday). It is noted that the weather was rainy, but it had cleared up an hour earlier. The observed movement by T1 from full west elevation 50° to east elevation 30° corresponds to a PAN movement of 250 to 500 meters depending on its flight height of 100 to 200 meters (typical case of a Thai lantern); these movement values are compatible with a measured wind speed of the order of 20 km/h and an observation duration testified around one minute. Witness T1 describes an uneven glow with lights added below or above. This translates to:

This indicates that he sees the PAN from quite a close distance. In fact, it gives it an angular size of an airplane while it is 12km from an airport. A lantern seen at 100m has an angular size equivalent to that of the Moon and displays non-uniform colors, its color can vary depending on the angle of observation. Witness T2 only notes the bright orange, for his part. The photograph does not allow for any indication. Witnesses indicate a straight trajectory followed by a turn whose amplitude and direction are difficult to assess. It's T1 who is best placed to appreciate it as it seems the PAN flew almost vertically; The heading changes from SE to E. For T2, the impression seems to be a turn towards the South. In any case, we are located near a river, a situation conducive to local wind changes or currents. The GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a Thai Lantern.
