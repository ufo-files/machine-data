Description: On November 29th, at approximately 11:30 PM, a witness observed less than 10 seconds from their balcony, 6 bright points crossing the sky. The witness states that these points, gray-white in color, seemed to form a single, dark mass that obscured the stars. The object disappeared from the witness's field of vision. No noise was heard. Bright points in formation crossing a portion of the sky in less than a minute, on a Saturday night, suggests the passage of Thai lanterns. Let's check if the weather report indicates a local wind consistent with the observed movement. The weather report for Caen shows a light wind, shifting from southeast to north between 10:00 PM and 11:00 PM, then becoming very light and unstable in the middle of the night (weather report). Therefore, it is impossible to precisely know the wind conditions at 11:15 PM in Hérouville, very close to Caen, and thus confirm this hypothesis. Compared to other lantern witness reports, which most often mention orange balls, the witness here describes gray-white points. The flat, black mass described by the witness is very likely a so-called "contour illusion," common in cases of observation of groups of lanterns or satellite triples (but at this time, no satellites are visible). Given the uncertainty about the wind and the unusual gray-white color, the GEIPAN does not have enough elements to confirm this hypothesis of Thai lanterns, despite good similarities. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" due to a lack of information.
