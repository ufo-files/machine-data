Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On January 17, 1988, at 7:30 PM, a witness and his family observed a peculiar object above a hill facing their residence. The object was in the shape of an ellipse, illuminated by numerous intense and blinking lights placed under the object, which reflected in the trees. The object moved very slowly in the direction of the Saint-Jacques factory in Hayange. It then disappeared suddenly and brilliantly as a tiny light point. No other testimony could be collected. This case is characterized by its lack of consistency. There are no direct testimonies, only a report from a gendarme transcribing the witness's statements (with inherent risks of distortions or involuntary interpretations). According to the current standards of the GEIPAN, there cannot be an observation case without a direct and signed testimony from the witness. Furthermore, only one out of every four witnesses' observations is reported (the confirming information from other witnesses is therefore unusable), and what is reported lacks notable information. Indeed, angular data such as the angular size of PAN, initial and final elevations above the horizon, etc., are missing. It is mentioned that "around twenty lights located under the object..." without describing the object itself in terms of its shape, appearance, and color. Given the lack of information, what is described seems limited in its strangeness as it could be compatible with an aircraft or a reflection of a ground projector onto clouds (which were present that evening). The reported disappearance according to a sudden acceleration could result from two levels of interpretation by the gendarme and the witness. The witness seems to say that the craft accelerated very suddenly to disappear a second later as a tiny light point. The experience of GEIPAN teaches us that a witness typically interprets a decrease in the brightness and/or angular size of PAN as a retreat in distance from it, while these decreases could have come from the extinction (or change in orientation) of the lights or an increase in the atmospheric screen (fog, clouds), or other (canopies...) placed in front of the lights. Under these conditions, it is impossible to conclude. This observation case is unusable. GEIPAN concludes in C: lack of reliable information.
