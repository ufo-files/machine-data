Summary: Observation of a ball-sized orange object in the sky: likely observation of a balloon.
--- seg-f6df4102a6c30e9394b8988e [needs-review] ---
Description: On November 6, 2011, around 1:00 PM, a witness noticed in the sky a ball that he described as the size of a pinhead, orange but dull in color. It appeared stationary at a high altitude, but its distance was difficult to gauge. The witness briefly looked away and could not find the object in the sky when he looked back. The observation lasted for 10 seconds. No other witnesses were reported. The hypothesis of an orange balloon flying at approximately 500 meters seems to explain this observation. Its apparent immobility can be explained, on the one hand, by the fact that even if the balloon moves at the speed of the wind at ground level (30 km/h), at 500 meters it has an apparent angular velocity of movement of the order of 1°/s, a speed that is difficult for the human eye to appreciate on a uniform sky background near the zenith, and on the other hand, the wind at 500 meters is not always the same as at ground level. Since helium-filled balloons generally explode quite quickly as they ascend in altitude, the sudden disappearance of this balloon is perfectly normal. This case is therefore classified as "B": probable balloon.
