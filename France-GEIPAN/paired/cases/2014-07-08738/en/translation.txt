Description: The witness is in bed, turns their head towards the window, and sees a large, glowing red-orange sphere pass by, connected to a second, smaller one. The trajectory is slow, silent, and very close (above the roof). The witness observes two glowing red-orange spheres that they estimate are coming from the direction of Sainte Sévère sur Indre towards Thevet St Julien, that is, from southeast to northeast. Therefore, the objects were following a trajectory from south to north. The weather report for Châteauroux indicates a south wind at 1 a.m., weak and variable towards southwest or west (weather). The observed object could have been carried by the wind. The release of Thai lanterns is common in the summer during weekends, especially around July 14th. The witness's observation report is consistent with this hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B," a probable observation of Thai lanterns. The witness also refers to a similar observation, but with multiple spheres: it was likely from the same origin.
