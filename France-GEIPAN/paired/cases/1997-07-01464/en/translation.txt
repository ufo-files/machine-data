**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named CHOLET (49) 1997 is part of a recent batch of cases reexamined. On July 21, 1997, between 10:45 PM and 11:15 PM, two witnesses observed for 3 or 4 minutes the passage SO-NE above their home of a dark object with lights of different colors on each side and emitting a dull noise. We have shown through analysis that the witnesses likely observed, under difficult conditions (dusk and/or night), a small tourist plane preparing for landing, with landing lights on, after a VFR/IMC night flight, possibly at the Ancenis airport (see the investigation report). The perceived noise, similar to that of a "small plane" according to T1, and the total duration of observation perfectly align with the hypothesis. The precise shape of the plane is difficult to discern against a very dark sky (see the astronomical situation). The witnesses describe a circular object, elongated in its length and devoid of wings, but this is very likely a classic perceptual illusion, the brain unconsciously attempting to reconstruct a "solid" form from the plane's lights. The arrangement of the lights, as described by the witnesses, is also fully consistent with observing a plane approaching from the front, with the observation beginning when it is low enough on the horizon that the perspective "flattens" the triangular shape defined by the three landing lights (one in front and the other two further back) so they appear horizontally aligned or nearly so.

The testimonies diverge, however, on the color of the lights, described as white by T2 and red by T1. But T1 only observes during the final phase, when the landing lights can only be seen diagonally and at a much lower intensity, often perceived as reddish while still maintaining a white dominant. Another divergence is that, while T1 sees these red lights, T2 does not see any lights at all. An explanation, in addition to a potential memory lapse by one of the witnesses, could be that the difference in visual acuity did not allow T2 to distinguish (at least to the point of remembering) what T1 was still seeing. More difficult to explain is the absence of visibility of the anti-collision lights in this final phase as the plane passes over (or nearly) the witnesses. While at the beginning of the observation, these lights can be "overwhelmed" by the intense light of the landing lights, as the angle of observation increases as the plane moves towards the witnesses, the anti-collision lights should be visible, no longer obscured by the landing lights, which are less visible due to the angle of observation. Is this a deliberate (and dangerous) maneuver by the pilot who turned them off under poor weather conditions? The presence of a few clouds at 1500 meters altitude could, however, explain this temporary extinction of the anti-collision lights, within the context of an IMC flight of this plane. This needs to be qualified, however, as it seems surprising that the witnesses did not mention at any point observing the plane passing through a cloud, given that the full moon was shining and could have illuminated them, making them visible by contrast against the dark sky. This last point, however, does not seem sufficient to prevent the hypothesis from being considered, given that there is a possible explanation and considering the importance of other points in favor of the hypothesis. Consistency is average. We have two testimonies, one particularly detailed, but there are missing useful data for analysis, such as angular measurements (dimensions of the PAN, heights on the horizon during different phases, etc.). Especially, there are contradictions between witnesses that the gendarmerie investigation did not attempt to qualify and quantify. Consistency barely meets the requirements to validate the aircraft hypothesis with its residual strangeness. The aircraft hypothesis must be associated with particular aeronautical, atmospheric, or visibility circumstances. The case is classified as B, at the boundary of an exploitable case due to lack of consistency (level and reliability of information).
