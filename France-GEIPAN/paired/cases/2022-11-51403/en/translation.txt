Description: On the evening of November 12, 2022, two residents (T1 and T2) of BRUGUIERES (31) observed, for a few seconds, the rapid passage of a luminous phenomenon with a halo in the sky. Of average strangeness and quite consistent (two witnesses, but no photo of the PAN, no free narrative), the case appears to be a probable observation of a meteor (see the investigation report). The characteristics of the PAN align well with this hypothesis, in particular:

- Short observation
- Very high speed (T2)
- Shape, circular for T2, compatible. T1 is more precise in describing a shape "circular/hexagonal," the latter term being less compatible with the hypothesis, although the brevity of the observation may certainly not have allowed the witnesses to fully detail the PAN
- Color, orange, possible
- Low brightness, described by both witnesses as "soft and diffuse"
- Straight trajectory for T1. T2, like the shape, is more precise and diverges from T1: "[...] half-rotation in both directions, then slight zigzags (3) above my head then turning left while ascending"

No glowing trail was described by the witnesses, but only a halo, which is possible, under certain observation conditions, for meteors/shooting stars. No bolide recorded in the databases of the American Meteor Society (AMS) and the FRIPON (Fireball Recovery and InterPlanetary Observation Network) network corresponds to the PAN, but given its relatively low brightness, it may not have been observed and/or reported, and in any case, it could not have been recorded by the FRIPON network cameras, whose sensitivity is set to ignore events of the type meteor or shooting star, which occur too frequently.

The case is classified B, probable observation of a meteor.
