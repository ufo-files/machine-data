Description: On July 13, 2016, around 11:30 PM, a witness observed for 30 seconds a very bright white light stationary in the southwest of the clear night sky. Only one testimony was collected. The witness is the same as the one from the case of SAINTES (17) 03/21/2016 (misidentification with Jupiter close to the Moon). This time, the witness made another misidentification with a star, either Mars or Saturn, both located just to the left of the Moon. All three stars were at a low elevation that night. The location configuration (witness's drawing) is compatible with the visibility of only one of the three stars, in the indicated direction, with the other two hidden by obstacles (a tree to the right of PAN, a building to the left). If visible, the Moon would have been reported as a first quarter phase a priori. The extinction of PAN after 30 seconds could have been caused by the passage of a cloud. The initial impression of immense speed results from the witness's idea of an object turning off because it suddenly moves away. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Astronomical observation.
