Description: On October 28, 2020, at 7 PM, the witness observed a "white ball-shaped glow" among the clouds, stationary, accompanied by a narrow but very distinct beam of light directed towards the ground in a "cow pasture." The observation lasted approximately 5 minutes before the phenomenon abruptly disappeared, "like a lightning bolt." Only one testimony was collected. The consistency of this testimony, according to the criteria of the GEIPAN, is average, with a single witness and the absence of photos or videos of the PAN. The consistency, according to the GEIPAN criteria, refers to the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony. The investigation revealed that the witness likely observed the impact of a projector's light on the cloud layer (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following key elements: - The circular shape, identical to that of a ground-based projector,
- The white color,
- The immobility of the PAN,
- The absence of perceived noise.

Misidentifications of this type primarily occur when a uniform, low, or relatively low layer of clouds composed of stratocumulus or nimbostratus is present. These types of clouds offer sufficient density to reflect the light emitted by a projector, making it visible from the witness's position. In this specific case, the presence of a cloud layer at an altitude of approximately 1600 meters was confirmed. The observation of a light beam is possible when water droplets are suspended in the atmosphere. Although this is conceivable in this instance, it was not demonstrated, and weather data reporting excellent horizontal visibility at the time further supports this.

Since the observation took place during periods of restrictions associated with COVID-19 measures, with nightclubs closed at that time, it is plausible that the observed phenomenon was due to a temporary test of a projector (skyrose) by the nearest nightclub. However, this hypothesis could not be conclusively confirmed. The strangeness observed by the witness is primarily due to the perception that the beam seems to emanate from the PAN and head towards the ground, while in the case of a projector, the beam is projected from the ground and reflects off the clouds.

The case is classified as B: probable observation of the reflection of a projector's light on clouds.
