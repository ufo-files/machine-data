Summary: Nighttime observation of a stationary and silent light phenomenon followed by a rapid movement on a "L"-shaped trajectory: certain misidentification with the planet Jupiter.
--- seg-dee86b88e20507b95c8b5adc [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On June 18, 2019, around 01:00 AM, a witness was intrigued by the presence in the sky of a very bright phenomenon, first stationary, then moving very quickly on a "L" shaped trajectory. The witness took photographs. No particular noise was heard. The witness noted that the PAN was still present and closed their blinds to go to sleep. The following day, June 19, at 01:00 AM, this witness made a new observation of the PAN which had moved towards the West. This case presents a fairly strong consistency, as the provided testimony is quite precise, and the various photographs of the PAN allow for its azimuth to be determined. The presence of the Moon also allows for the PAN's position in the sky to be established (see the investigation report and photos of the PAN taken on June 18, 2019). It turns out that the internal clock of the witness's mobile phone was 13 minutes behind true time, the discrepancy having been measured thanks to the passage of a plane visible on a photo. The phenomenon was observed by two witnesses, but we only have one testimony, as the second witness did not complete the GEIPAN questionnaire. A comparison between the star map at the time of the observation and a photograph of the PAN shows that planet Jupiter corresponds perfectly to the PAN, both in terms of brightness and position. Only the witness's interpretation of their observation is incorrect, as the PAN is neither geostationary nor illuminated by the Moon. Furthermore, its sudden movement in a "L" shape is only an illusion caused by the phone's movement at the time of the picture. This case, of very low strangeness and good consistency (several photos), turns out to be a certain misidentification with planet Jupiter. The case is classified A, misidentification with Jupiter.
