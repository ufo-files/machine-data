Description: On March 23, 2020 at 23:08, a witness observed from the courtyard of his house a fixed luminous phenomenon in the direction of south, south/west. The witness is filming the PAN (camcorder). Only one testimony is collected. The witness will provide three images from the video showing the shape of PAN. Through a street view photo provided by the witness locating the position of the PAN and using Google Earth, we can deduce that it was approximately at an azimuth of 240°, with an elevation of around 30° at 23:08 (local time) on 23/03/2020. An analysis using the Stellarium application demonstrates the presence of the star Procyon at approximately these azimuth and elevation at that time. Procyon has an apparent brightness of 0.37, making it one of the brightest stars in the sky. The color and brightness described by the witness remain consistent with the observation of Procyon. The perceived strangeness by the witness results essentially from the images from his camcorder. Enlarging an image of a planet or star is impossible with this type of equipment due to defocusing. In fact, focusing cannot be achieved on an object that far away; a bright point filmed with the zoom causes optical and electronic artifacts. The various colors themselves are chromatic aberrations also related to defocusing. (See case: ETUPES (25) 08/22/2017). The GEIPAN classifies this case as A for a certain observation of Procyon.
