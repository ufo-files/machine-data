Description: On the night of April 10 to 11, 1997, a witness was awakened by a very bright white light followed by a firework-like noise. Standing at the window, the witness observed that the roof of their car, parked in their driveway, was on fire. The witness immediately called the police. On the scene, the police and firefighters collected samples. The University of Savoie laboratory, which analyzed the samples, identified basalt and suggested it was likely a meteorite. A specialist who arrived at the scene the next day expressed strong doubts about this hypothesis. Furthermore, a mineralogy laboratory manager at the Paris Museum of Natural History, who examined some debris, also ruled out the possibility of a meteorite fall. The SEPRA (presumably an investigation body or official) was alerted and collected three samples for analysis at the Tours Bioinformatics Laboratory (see attached document). Subsequent analyses conducted by the CNRS central laboratory in Solaize revealed that the samples contained components (sodium, magnesium, potassium, sulfur, and carbon) typically found in marine or terrestrial rockets (see AFP news dispatch of April 29, 1997, addressed to SEPRA). These final analyses definitively ruled out the meteorite fall hypothesis. The GEIPAN (likely a UFO investigation body) classified this case as A: vehicle fire due to a terrestrial rocket. The owner of the partially burned vehicle filed a complaint with the police. However, a log was not sent to SEPRA. Without direct testimony and access to the witness, the GEIPAN is not conducting the investigation. Nevertheless, we are publishing this case because significant work was done and led to a conclusion. This case also indicates that direct access to the witness would have very likely allowed for better coordination and optimization of investigation efforts.
