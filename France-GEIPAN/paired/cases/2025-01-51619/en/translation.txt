**Description:** On January 4, 2025, around 6:00 PM local time, a witness (T1) driving with his partner (T2) in Perpignan (66), France, reports observing a PAN consisting of a dozen flashing red lights moving across the sky. T1 pauses his drive and gets out of his vehicle to film the phenomenon with his smartphone. T1's parents, alerted at the same time, confirm they also observed the lights from a different location, providing partial corroboration of the sighting. The total duration of the event is estimated at approximately ten minutes. On February 25, 2025, T1 submits a Technical Questionnaire (QT) to GEIPAN along with a video of the sighting. To date, no other QT has been received, as the other witnesses mentioned have not formally come forward. The case exhibits strong consistency: even with only one formal testimony, multiple individuals observed the phenomenon. The primary witness's account is detailed and supported by a 2-minute, 8-second video.

The video clearly shows ten lights moving slowly upward in the sky. Distinctive ground markers help locate the scene, aiding analysis and understanding of the observation. Only one hypothesis is considered: the observation of celestial lanterns. The flickering, red lights moving in an upward trajectory influenced by the wind exhibit characteristics consistent with those typically observed during the deployment of celestial lanterns. The IPACO software, used by GEIPAN for photo/video analysis, authenticated the video and confirmed chromatic consistency with the lantern hypothesis (Annex 1). The perceived strangeness likely stems from the witness's viewing angle, but the lights' fluid motion remains typical of lantern releases:

- The initial source is located in the same place for all lights, pointing towards a single ground location. Rewinding the video, one can verify that all lights originate from the same point: they converge towards the ground, one after the other.
- The trajectory is consistently upward.
- The ascent occurs at a steady rate.
- The gap between each light remains constant, depending on the release order.
- No light doubles another or changes its trajectory.
- The apparent speed and orientation are consistent with a light wind from the north (4-6 km/h). See weather conditions (Annex 2).
- The only light appearing to move in the opposite direction in the video is a reflection (a pursuit flare).
- The highest lights dim in the order they appeared.

On March 9, the Prefecture was contacted to verify if a prior authorization request had been made for a lantern release. On March 13, the Bureau de l'Ordre Public et des Polices Administratives de Sécurité de Perpignan responded that no request for authorization to release balloons or lanterns had been received for that date. Further checks with municipal and national police services also indicated no reports of information regarding these lights. It is highly likely that this was an unauthorized "wild" lantern release conducted as part of a festive, sporting, or cultural event. It is worth noting that the observation occurred on a Saturday in the late afternoon, a time particularly conducive to such activities.

GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": Observation of celestial lanterns. According to GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.
