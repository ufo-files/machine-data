Description: On February 20, 2021, at approximately 07:10, while standing in front of his entrance door, the witness's attention was drawn to a bright white light that appeared in the overcast sky. This phenomenon moved silently towards the west, with limited ground lighting. The witness lost sight of the phenomenon, hidden by a row of hedges. The observation lasted 3 seconds. No other testimony corroborates this observation. The witness described precisely what he saw: the case's consistency is good. Upon reading the testimony, the case does not present any particularly strange characteristics. If not for the strangeness experienced and felt by the witness, which is always difficult to quantify. The strangeness is significantly reduced by the proposed explanation. The witness was surprised by the appearance of the PAN, as it was unusual, early in the morning in a slightly overcast sky. He immediately abandoned his first hypothesis of identifying a meteor, for three reasons, which he defined as follows: First, the low altitude of the PAN ("the light source is not very high, just in the cloud layer or just above" - PV). Second, the small area of ground lighting ("the lit areas remain limited" - PV). And third, the absence of noise ("no noise" - PV). Yet the observation remains compatible and consistent with a meteor. The phenomenon was at an apparently low angular height (but likely 20 km high) and, moreover, very far from the witness (several dozen kilometers). He indicated that as the cloud separated (becoming more "transparent"), he perceived the light and the light source better. It is frequent for a meteor to intensely light the ground, but due to the overcast sky and low angular height, it is normal that the lit ground area remains more restricted. Finally, the absence of noise is not disqualifying, as numerous accounts also testify to complete silence during the appearance of a meteor. The investigation concludes to a probable misperception with a sporadic meteor. The GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable appearance of a meteor.
