Summary: Observation of a fixed light with effects of movement and color changes: very probable observation of Jupiter.
--- seg-df0d3318fe4921734bdd4d92 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 3, 2018, at 10:30 PM, a witness is intrigued by the presence at low altitude in a clear sky of a fixed light. The witness then describes multiple movements (left to right, up and down) and color changes. No noise is heard. Deciding to film their observation, which lasts 3 to 4 minutes, only one testimony is collected. The witness very likely observed Jupiter. - There is a good position match in the sky. Jupiter is well in SE (123°) and at a height close (9°) to that observed (15°). - The described aspect (bright point, fixed light) corresponds well to a star or celestial body. - The color change from white or yellow, "with occasional bursts" corresponds to the atmospheric effect that alters colors and can create halo effects, especially in the presence of high humidity. - The perception of slight movement ("the bright point started moving strangely (left to right, up and down, moving away, coming closer") results from two classic effects in star observation: the ocular phenomenon of autokinetic (see the article on Reason and Psychology) that creates perceptions of angular motion, and one induced by intensity fluctuations (caused by the atmosphere) that the witness interprets as radial motion (toward or away from them). - The end of the observation is caused by a cloud passage. Indeed, it is verified that the sky was not completely covered at the end of the day according to the data from the Cap-Ferret station (see reconstruction). Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Very probable observation of Jupiter.
