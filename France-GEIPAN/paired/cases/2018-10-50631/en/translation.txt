Summary: Observation of the passage of a white glowing point in the sky: very likely misperception with a satellite.
--- seg-b8157c3774dc6d64dd2fc95c [needs-review] ---
Description: On Wednesday, October 10, 2018, at 5:30 a.m., the witness was smoking a cigarette on her doorstep when she observed a white glowing point in the night sky for several minutes. Having her smartphone at hand, she filmed the observation. She contacted the GEIPAN on the same day and submitted a questionnaire and a video two days later. Only one witness statement was collected regarding this phenomenon. The glowing point, described as larger and brighter than a star, appeared in the northwest and crossed the sky with a descending linear trajectory at a seemingly constant speed. After approximately 5 minutes, it disappeared in the southeast horizon (see investigation report). The aspect of PAN (white, larger than a star, constant light), its trajectory (linear, PAN disappeared at the horizon), and the duration of the observation (approximately 5 minutes) suggest the passage of an artificial satellite in the sky. The estimated angular velocity, calculated from the witness's description, remains consistent with this hypothesis. Data from Calsky indicates that the ISS (International Space Station) was not visible at that exact moment. However, several geostationary satellites were passing through the witness's sky. Two of them had trajectories close to the one described by the witness. These satellites are usually invisible to the naked eye as they are faintly lit. But there are periods in the year (periods preceding and following the equinoxes) when they can be temporarily illuminated by the sun. Due to their favorable trajectory, they are then sufficiently lit to be visible to the naked eye. Calsky states that the observation time - approximately 3 hours before sunrise - corresponds to one of these periods, a period favorable for this type of observation. Due to recording conditions and its low quality (smartphone held outstretched), the video provided by the witness is not exploitable. This case presents low strangeness and average consistency (single witness, fairly accurate description but unusable video). This case is very likely a misidentification with the passage of a geostationary satellite possibly and temporarily visible to the naked eye, given the time of year. This case is to be classified in category "A", very likely misidentification with a satellite.
