Description: At 4:30 AM, in the commune of Lavercantière, the witness observed, from their south-facing room, a bright point moving horizontally from west to east at an elevation estimated between 30 and 45 degrees above the horizon. This bright point was followed by a very bright, fluorescent green trail. The phenomenon suddenly disappeared in a sort of "explosion" after the initial bright point changed from orange to bright red. According to Geipan criteria, the consistency* of this case is rated as average due to the presence of only one witness and the absence of photographs or videos, as well as the briefness of the observation of PAN. *Consistency is the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony. The analysis of this observation case suggests that the witness likely observed a meteor (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The appearance of PAN (colors, high brightness, presence of a trail...) is consistent with that of a meteor
- Very short observation duration
- Straight trajectory
- "Explosion" at the end of the trajectory indicating disintegration and vaporization of all or part of the meteor in the atmosphere.

The only oddity for the witness is the horizontal trajectory of PAN, which can be explained by the presence of a meteor with its radiant near the horizon, or simply by a perspective effect. We searched through databases of testimony and automated video recordings for any possible presence of this meteor, without success, which is plausible given the very early observation time and the relative distance of the closest automated camera.

The case is classified as B, probable observation of a meteor entering the atmosphere.
