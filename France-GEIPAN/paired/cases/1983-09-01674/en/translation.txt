Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named COLMARS (04) 1983 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. On September 28, 1983, around 9:30 PM, four witnesses on a balcony spotted intense white and red lights "like flashes" above the "La Femme Couchée" mountain. The witnesses noticed that the UFO seemed initially immobile, then moved in a "zigzag" pattern. The UFO quickly crossed a narrow valley and disappeared towards the summit of "Chalufy." The briefness of the observation did not allow the witnesses to describe the object's shape precisely. Two hypotheses are explored to explain the witnesses' observation (see the investigation report). The first concerns a confusion with an atmospheric re-entry of an artificial object (satellite, debris...). Although some parameters of the testimonies (brief observation, flashing white and red lights...) could suggest such a phenomenon, the fact that no other testimonies exist in an urbanized area that could have been crossed by this phenomenon makes this hypothesis less credible. Moreover, in the catalogs listing all such atmospheric re-entries since 1958, none is identified for September 1983. The absence of a light trail is also a parameter that contradicts the hypothesis.

The second hypothesis is related to a simple confusion with a civilian or military aircraft observed at a relatively low altitude. This hypothesis better fits the testimonial data (flashing white and red lights, in particular, and the perception of a noise...), as well as the calculated data involving estimates of heights, speeds, and observation duration. There is no need to assume that the witnesses slightly underestimated the observation duration. Indeed, an appropriate combination of height/speed factors can accurately account for the observation, which remains consistent with the observation duration as reported by the witnesses and the angular distance traveled by this aircraft within the limited geographical and visual field of a valley. It is thus concluded that this aircraft would have flown both faster and lower than usual in the area. This conclusion is all the more valid as it is likely this peculiarity constituted the strangeness for the witnesses who did not recognize an aircraft. Although we have demonstrated that civilian aircraft today perform regular flights, use air corridors, and pass through a particular SID point, all located immediately near the witnesses and in the observed direction, we have not been able to determine if these data were already valid in 1983, but it is most likely they were. The same is true for flight restriction maps, particularly of altitudes and heights (regulated zones), which today indicate that the area is used by military aircraft; it is most likely that this was already the case at the time. It is not possible to prioritize between a military or civilian aircraft. In both cases, it is necessary to introduce a plausibility that is equally likely: for the military aircraft that generally flies low, it is the fact that there was an exercise that day with this particular flight direction, for the civilian aircraft that usually passes through, it is the fact that the altitude was lower. The consistency is correct, with a fairly thorough investigation by the gendarmes. However, it should be noted that angular measurements taken on site would have allowed for a better evaluation of the hypotheses.

GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a civilian or military aircraft.
