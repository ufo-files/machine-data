Summary: Observation of the silent movement of three bright red and highly luminous PANs (sightings of unidentified flying objects) and disappearance behind the landscape: probable observation of wind-carried sky lanterns.
--- seg-cebb89510d56555f3559b87e [needs-review] ---
Description: The main witness (T1) is on his terrace in Andernos-les-Bains (33) on June 23, 2018, around 11:15 PM to 11:30 PM, using his telescope to photograph the Moon and Jupiter, when his wife (T2) interrupts him, having just observed from the other side of the house two red-colored PANs (unidentified flying objects) moving from east to west. T1 arrives too late to witness their presence himself. Two to three minutes later, T2 contacts T1 again regarding the same phenomenon; this time, T1 has enough time to observe it. It consists of three PANs identical to each other and to those of the first passage, a bright red and highly luminous coming from the east and heading towards the south or south-west. These UAPs move relatively slowly, at a fairly low altitude, and do not flash. T1 does not perceive any noise emanating from these PANs which eventually disappear behind trees and houses after approximately 15 seconds of observation. Only one of the two witnesses submitted a report to the GEIPAN and no photos or videos of the PAN were taken, resulting in an average case consistency. *According to the GEIPAN's criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a report.* The investigation established that the witnesses very likely observed the passage of several celestial lanterns (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements: - A possible red color, although the most frequent color used for lanterns is orange - The number, the slow movement according to a horizontal trajectory with the wind, are also elements supporting the hypothesis. The observation, moreover, took place on a Saturday night, a typical day when sky lanterns are released to celebrate weddings, parties, birthdays, etc. The season, in June, and the weather with light wind, also provide ideal conditions for such launches. On the evening of the observation, festivities were held in Andernos-les-Bains (33), and although we were unable to find evidence of a lantern launch on that occasion, it could be a private launch. The case is classified "B", probable observations of wind-carried sky lanterns.
