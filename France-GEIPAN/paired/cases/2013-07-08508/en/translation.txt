Summary: Observation of three white bright points forming an equilateral triangle: probable observation of reflections of the sun on the triple satellite YAOGAN9.
--- seg-53686d26467fdbd5b32e963f [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 25, 2013 at 12:05 AM, a witness observed in the sky the silent movement of three white glowing points forming an equilateral triangle. A simulation of satellite passes with the Calsky.com site indicates that the YAOGAN9 satellite triplet is in view of the witness during their observation. These satellites appear as three bright points arranged in an equilateral triangle, spaced from each other by an angular distance of the order of 1°, that is, 2 times the apparent diameter of a full Moon. These satellites can reflect the sun for a few seconds (flare). See examples, with very different fields of view, on: Yaogan Satellite Triplet Flare and at the 40th second on the right of the video NOSS Yaogan Triplet Flare? The fact that the witness no longer sees stars at the center of the triangle is likely a classic perception illusion when observing grouped bright points. The witness states in their questionnaire that the apparent size of the triangle corresponds to 6 cm at arm's length (approximately 5 to 6 degrees), and in a separate message, they state that on 12/5/13 at 2:40 a.m., "the triangle formed by the middle star of the belt with the two feet is identical in proportion, at the same angle and height": This triangle has sides of approximately 8 degrees. The GEIPAN does not have the means to verify that the reflection of the sun on the satellite did indeed occur at this precise moment. The significant difference between the size of the Yaogan triplet (1°) and the size reported by the witness (between 5 and 8°) is substantial, and raises doubt about this hypothesis. Due to the near-perfect coincidence in time and position with the passage of these satellites, despite certain inconsistencies with the testimony, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of sun reflections on the YAOGAN9 satellite trio.
