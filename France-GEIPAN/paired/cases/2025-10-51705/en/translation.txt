Summary: Nighttime observation of the rapid passage on a curved trajectory of a glowing sphere surrounded by a halo of sparks: observation of the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid.
--- seg-02501bc5e3496285e8eb6a58 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The witness is near their home walking their dog on October 31, 2025 at 10:01 PM local time along a canal in Brienne-sur-Aisne (08) when they observe a bright sphere in the sky surrounded by a halo of sparks and followed by a long trail of light for 3 to 5 seconds. This PAN, emitting a strong light with a dominant color between white and gold, moves quickly and according to a curved trajectory, silently and without variation in trajectory or light, from northeast to northwest, at approximately 45° elevation. The witness estimates its length at 3 cm measured from the end of their arm. The disappearance is abrupt, the object appearing to fade away or quickly move away, leaving no residual light trace. The witness thinks of a "large meteorite" and specifies, "If that's the case, I must not be the only one to have observed it." The consistency* is average, with only one witness and no photos or videos of the PAN. *According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The only hypothesis explored, that of observing a bolide, is corroborated by the following elements: - spherical shape, typical of a bolide, - white/golden color, - high brightness, - rapid movement according to a curved trajectory, - brief duration, less than 5 seconds. No other testimony was collected by the GEIPAN or by the IMO/AMS (bolide observers) instead, the automatic cameras FRIPON* recorded two events exactly at the time of the observation, i.e. 21:01 UTC (22:01 LOC), one in Belgium from Libin, the other in the Netherlands from Oostkapelle (see Annex 1). The observation directions provided by the automatic image captures of these two events, particularly that of Oostkapelle, are consistent with the directions given by the witness. The deviation can be explained by the very brief duration of the observation and the difficulty in assessing the trajectory (see Annex 2). The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as 'A': observation of the atmospheric reentry of a bolide. AMS: American Meteor Society. IMO: International Meteorological Organization. FRIPON: Fireball Recovery and InterPlanetary Observation Network
