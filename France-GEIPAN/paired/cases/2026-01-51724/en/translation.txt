Summary: Sequential observations in the sky of the silent movement on an ascending trajectory of several bright yellow light formations with a combustion effect: observations of celestial lanterns (also known as Thai lanterns).
--- seg-ea38e59eafdc9b9afe58da41 [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 1, 2026, at 8:44 PM, from the terrace of his home in Rumilly (Haute-Savoie), the main witness (T1) observes to the east a first PAN, in the form of a point, yellow in color with a red halo, followed by a phenomenon described as a "combustion cone" and intense brightness. It moves silently and quickly along a straight upward trajectory before disappearing at high altitude. This observation is followed by the appearance of two other __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s with the same characteristics. One of them abruptly stops its light emission before performing a rotation movement and then disappearing. Witness T1 estimates the apparent size of the phenomena at approximately 5 millimeters, measured using a ruled held at arm's length. His companion (T2) also confirms having observed the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s. The first phenomenon is lost from view after 40 seconds of observation as it disappears behind trees. All three phenomena were observed for approximately 6 minutes. The main witness captured three distinct videos of the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s. The consistency* of this case is good, although only the main witness (T1) completed the GEIPAN questionnaire due to the availability of exploitable videos. The strangeness experienced by the witness is significant, as the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s exhibit characteristics that witness T1 cannot associate with conventional conventional aerial phenomena that he knows.

*According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The explored hypothesis, that of observing celestial lanterns, is corroborated by the following elements:

- The point-like shape described and spherical on the videos, with a reddish halo and overall orange color, typical of lanterns. The use by T1 of the word "combustion" is entirely compatible with the presence of a flame at the base of the lantern envelope.

- The changing brightness visible on the videos is typical of the flickering of a flame inside the lantern, open at the bottom.

- The silent, upward-trajectory movement, clearly visible on one of the videos. The wind at the Annecy station, located about 20/25 km away, is very low and variable: the movement of the lanterns is primarily governed by Archimedes' principle, resulting from the heating of the air by the combustion of the candle. The thus heated air, less dense than the ambient air, generates an upward force that causes a gradual elevation of the lanterns in altitude. The apparently rapid movement is caused by the relative proximity of the lanterns, clearly perceptible on the videos.

- New Year's celebrations are ideal for a calm release of lanterns with a very low wind.

- The short observation duration is consistent with the usual flight duration of lanterns linked to the extinction of the heating flame of the air volume in the envelope. This extinction was even filmed by T1. Examples of celestial lantern flights are given on this page: __celestial_lanterns__. The GEIPAN classification is "A": observation of celestial lanterns.
