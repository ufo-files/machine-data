Description: On Saturday, December 17, 2016, around 8:45 PM, a witness was intrigued by the silent movement of a very bright red-orange light in the sky. The light disappeared quickly, but the witness had time to take a picture. Only one testimony was collected. The witness likely observed a Thai lantern. The description he gives of what he saw with the naked eye is consistent. Moreover, it is a weekend evening, always more conducive to a festive event of releasing lanterns. The wind measured on Infoclimat is weak and consistent with the impression of little movement of the PAN.

The strangeness noted by the witness and likely the origin of his testimony (which, by the way, was thoughtful as it was late, more than a month after the event) comes from observing his picture, which shows an orange design. This is classic and results from a moving effect. The picture was taken with a long exposure (4 seconds), and the witness moved before the end. There is a bright central light point on the picture, corresponding to the stability time of the shot, followed by the moving effect, which generates a light intensity inversely proportional to the moving speed (the faster the lantern passes in front of the detector pixels, the less these pixels are "impressed").

The fact that this movement does not appear on the other light points in the picture (likely stars) is explained by the fact that these points are much less bright at the base and only "impress" the detector pixels during the stable period, passing too quickly in front of the other detector pixels during the moving phase.

The fact that the lantern is no longer present in the sky after the picture is a pure coincidence; the lantern went out. The fact that the witness describes the movement of the PAN in his viewfinder suggests that he then took a little time to look at the final picture on his viewfinder before looking back at the sky, the near simultaneity between the picture and the disappearance may not be as clear, although it is otherwise possible.

Therefore, GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Thai lantern.
