Summary: Observation of the movement of three bright points in the sky: observation of the Chinese satellite triplet Yaogan-31 A, B, and C.
--- seg-bd79354f25afcb06325aae17 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 18, 2020, as night fell, the witness (with his daughter) had his attention drawn to a very bright constellation composed of 3 points, forming a quasi-equilateral triangle. The phenomenon began to change in brightness and seemed to be moving. After a few seconds, less than a minute, each point eventually disappeared almost simultaneously. It looked like "3 ISS" forming a triangle. The consistency of this case is average, with only one witness report, though it is well-detailed, there is no photo or video of the PAN. The investigation revealed that the witnesses observed a trio of Chinese satellites, in a temporary alignment that made them visible to the naked eye; that is, the sun reflected off each satellite towards the witness. This conclusion is based on the following elements: - Number: three satellites moving in unison. This is typical of such satellites. - The appearance is entirely consistent with that of satellites (brightness, color, point-like appearance, straight-line motion), which the witness also noted independently. - The presence in the sky at the time of the observation of these satellites is confirmed (see data provided by the Operational Space Surveillance Center of CNES). - The elevations given by the witness correspond to the trajectory of the triplet within 1.5 minutes. - The estimated duration, of a few seconds, less than a minute, is consistent with the visibility duration of these flashes; it is also consistent with cases already studied by GEIPAN (for example, 30 to 50 seconds for the PETIT-QUEVILLY (LE) (76) 16.04.2014 case and 8 seconds for SOUSTONS (40) 01.12.2019). - The direction of observation was correctly estimated, as was the angular separation between each of the triplet components (5°). Classification as "A", certain observation of a trio of Chinese Yaogan-31 satellites A, B, and C. The witness observes the reflection of the sun on these 3 satellites.
