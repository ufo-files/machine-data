Summary: Observation of a crater in a field, then pieces of sheet metal found: probable explosion of a firebomb from the last war.
--- seg-70885bbaac15fe10c45b9f47 [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 11, 1990, the SEPRA was informed via media of the discovery of a significant crater that had appeared in a field. The crater is 14 meters in diameter, with a crown of collapsed earth approximately 2.5 meters high and a central mound 4.5 meters by 1.5 meters high. Upon examining the rocks and large blocks that had been brought up from the subsoil, two hypotheses were considered: - the fall of a large meteorite - an explosion underground A bulldozer was mobilized to explore the crater, proceeding gently in layers of 50 to 70 centimeters. Exploration continued down to a depth of 7 meters. As investigations progressed, metal fragments were found at various depths. Twenty-five metallic fragments were recovered, ruling out the hypothesis of a meteorite fall in favor of the probable underground explosion of a World War II bomb. The fragments were sent to the DGA Armament Laboratory, whose conclusions confirmed the proposed hypothesis. The reconstruction of the craft indeed identified a 250-kilogram incendiary bomb of British or American origin, and was likely manufactured in April 1942.
