**Description:** On August 6, 2016, at approximately 9:00 PM, a driver and her passenger observed two stationary light phenomena in the sky. The driver stopped to take two photographs with a camera. Only one statement was collected. The witness saw two PANs: a stationary light trail (IMG_0044) and a black spot to the right of an airplane trail (IMG_0045). The black spot was not visually observed and appears only in the photograph. It is very likely a close insect. The visual description and photo of PAN1 are characteristic of an airplane trail. Indeed, (Flightradar) perfectly identifies the plane in a typical configuration for producing very bright trails (plane facing west at sunset). The strangeness came from the testimony of a PAN "immobile in the sky for 11 minutes," which could not correspond to an airplane (over such a duration, an airplane would make at least a 70° to 90° variation in the sky). The witness, contacted by phone, clarifies things very differently from his written statement. He saw PAN1 while driving, then stopped and photographed PAN1 (IMG_0044), then took photo 2 (IMG_0045). He was looking for PAN1 in the viewfinder of photo 2 (it was not there as photo 2 turns out to be very zoomed in and framed elsewhere) and remembers his friend telling him at that moment that the PAN had disappeared from the sky. They continued to look at the sky without ever seeing PAN1 again, and the whole thing lasted 11 minutes on the watch. Thus, the actual observation time of PAN1 is much shorter, say the time to stop the vehicle ("400 m") and take the camera (the witness specifies that it was under her hand as she had just taken photos), perhaps 2 to 3 minutes. At the time of photo 1, the identified plane is at an altitude of around 8° or 9° (distance 70 km), 3 minutes earlier, it was at an altitude of 4° or 5° (distance 115 km), this angular variation is sufficiently low to be perceived as immobility, especially when the observation is not continuous. The disappearance of the PAN is perfectly explained by the entry of the latter into darkness (end of its direct illumination by the setting sun). The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: airplane condensation trail. This testimony illustrates the imprecision or exaggeration ("immobile for 11 minutes" "These 2 phenomena, we observed them exactly 11 minutes") that can taint the description of an observation, often unbeknownst to the witness, especially in a context of emotion or excitement ("heartbeats, excitement").
