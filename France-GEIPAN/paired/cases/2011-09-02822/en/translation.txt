Description: On September 30, 2011, around 10 PM, a witness observed a stationary bright light at a low point on the horizon. 45 minutes later, the witness saw the phenomenon move and head north, crossing half the sky in approximately one minute. The first part of the observation, concerning a static object, could be compatible with the star Arcturus in the constellation Boötes. The second part, where the blinking white and red light moves towards the north and disappears in about a minute, is harder to explain. With the hypothesis of an astronomical misidentification ruled out, and misidentification with a commercial airliner unlikely given its distinctive lights, the remaining possibility is an unlisted (military) satellite, which we were unable to verify. Of low strangeness and good consistency, this observation case can be classified as B for a probable misidentification with Arcturus for the first part, and C for the second observation. Overall, the case is classified as C due to lack of information and cross-references.
