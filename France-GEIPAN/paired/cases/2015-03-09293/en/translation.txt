Description: On March 6, 2015, the witness was driving on D112 near Lavalette (31) on their way to work. Around 8:30 a.m., while driving past "Grousset," the witness noticed a black, oval-shaped object in front of them in a clear sky, reflecting the sunlight and appearing to move slowly. The witness described the object as being "more or less above the road" with a small white trail extending horizontally from its base. A light orange coloration was also present on one edge of the PAN. After observing it for approximately two minutes, the witness reached for their mobile phone on the passenger seat, momentarily diverting their attention. When they looked back at the PAN, it had disappeared. The observation was made through the car's windshield. After the PAN vanished, the witness slowed down and searched for it by looking up at the sky all around, but to no avail. Only one testimony was collected. The PAN exhibited the appearance and movement of a solar captive balloon launched from a field to the right of the witness (refer to the investigation report). The peculiarity of the case is primarily due to the disappearance of the PAN while the witness was looking for their phone to take a picture. The balloon would not have disappeared; the witness would have recognized it (they would not have testified) and might have seen the operator on the ground while searching for and looking at the sky, as they likely did not see the operator because they were focused on searching for the PAN in the sky while driving. The peculiarity did not exist for following or crossing drivers who either did not see the balloon or saw it descending and/or landing (the witness mentions the presence of other users who noticed nothing). The testimony is consistent, with sufficient data provided by the witness and exploitable. There is also a photograph taken by the witness that recreates the scene, which was used to analyze the case. This allowed for the verification of the PAN's visual compliance with this type of balloon, the possibility of operating a balloon from the field, as well as the possibility of a witness not seeing the balloon operator, especially when they are surprised by the disappearance and scan the sky rather than the ground. Therefore, the main explanation here is the rapid descent of the balloon over a duration of one to a few seconds, considering that only the descent time until the top of the trees that subsequently hid it is taken into account. This descent can be explained by the balloon's disintegration or a ground-directed pull by effect spi (related to wind). Due to the strong visual similarity with a balloon and an explanation for the rapid descent of the latter, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a solar captive balloon.
