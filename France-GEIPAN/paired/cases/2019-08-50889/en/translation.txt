Summary: Long observations of changing movements in the Paris sky of a bright point with variable appearance: probable observation of a cluster of Mylar balloons.
--- seg-7dcbbff3869e8a2c63d90f00 [needs-review] ---
Description: On the morning of 25/08/2019 in Paris, a couple observed, from their terrace, for nearly 30 minutes a luminous phenomenon in the sky. The witnesses follow the movements of the PAN with a telescope, binoculars, and take photos and videos with their cell phone. The case is supported by these two witness statements (see the investigation report). According to the GEIPAN criteria, the case consistency is very good (two witnesses having meticulously described their observation, presence of photos and videos of PAN). *Consistency refers to the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony. The investigation leads us to the observation of an assembly of Mylar balloons inflated with helium and connected to each other. Its ascent is slow, regular in a circular arc. It is explained by a weak wind with a very variable direction between 250 and 2750 meters. Upon reaching a sufficient altitude, the pressure exerted by the expanded helium was able to burst the balloons, which may explain the sudden disappearance of PAN. The elements leading to this hypothesis are: - the shape of the PAN seen through the telescope, resembling an assembly of balloons, - the brightness of the PAN observed with the naked eye, consistent with that of highly reflective Mylar balloons reflecting the sun's luminosity, - the trajectory of the PAN, in line with weak and variable winds according to altitude, - the sudden disappearance could have been due to the bursting of balloons reaching a certain high altitude (theoretically) although no data supports this. The photos and videos are unfortunately little exploitable (fluctuating image clarity and loss of focus). Smartphones used to capture them are not suited to properly focus a small angular object, and the use of zoom only exacerbates the loss of sharpness. No corroboration of such a balloon release on that day has been identified, either in media sources or on social media platforms. This investigation thus allowed the characterization of the PAN, to propose a coherent hypothesis from the testimony, however without being able to provide formal proof. This case is classified by GEIPAN as category « B »: probable observation of a cluster of Mylar balloons.
