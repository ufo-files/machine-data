Summary: Observations of the movement of light phenomena in the sky in a descending trajectory: lack of information.
--- seg-28d44046bd1807d82fc74e13 [needs-review] ---
Description: On August 26, 2011, around 6 a.m., a witness and his spouse were intrigued by the slow movement of a bright phenomenon descending in the sky. The characteristics of this observation and its two-minute duration suggest the possibility of an atmospheric reentry of a satellite or space debris. However, such an event would have been observed by numerous witnesses across a wide area (north of France, Belgium, Netherlands, etc.), yet we find no record of this at the time, suggesting a very local phenomenon. The wind-compatible movement might suggest a group of aerostats, but with varying shapes, colors, and light intensities, making it difficult to identify the exact type of passive aircraft. Furthermore, not all observed objects seem to have the same speed or follow the same trajectory, particularly the last bright point crossing the witness's field of vision at a speed significantly higher than the others. Finally, the hypothesis of a civil or military aerial exercise taking place vertically above BA112, about forty kilometers northeast of the observation location (and thus at 20° from the observation direction), cannot be ruled out, but we were unable to verify it. This observation case initially appears somewhat strange, but lacks precision, preventing any definitive conclusion. A field investigation to better assess trajectories and orientations could provide more information. This case is provisionally classified as C, awaiting additional information.
