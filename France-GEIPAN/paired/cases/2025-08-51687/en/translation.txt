Description: On August 21, 2025, at 11:23 PM, the witness, outside their home in La Couronne (16), observes for a few seconds the passage of an unidentified aerial phenomenon (PAN). The object, spherical and very bright white, has a slightly cottony outline and is followed by a double trail. Its apparent size is compared to a ping-pong ball ("if the Moon appears like a tennis ball"). After reaching a peak of approximately 60° north, the PAN gradually disappears towards the northeast, tracing a perfect circular arc. Consistency* is average, with only one witness and no photos or videos of the PAN. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The strangeness for the witness is derived from the spectacular aspect of the phenomenon, caused by its intense brightness, its very regular movement, and its double trail. The only hypothesis explored, that of observing the re-entry into the atmosphere of a meteor called a "bolide," is supported by the following elements: - spherical shape, typical of a bolide,
- white color,
- intense brightness,
- rapid movement along a curved trajectory,
- presence of a trail, completely typical. The fact that it is double indicates that the meteor responsible for the observation is fragmenting due to its heating upon entering the atmosphere,
- brief duration, a few seconds. With a portion of the sky covered in 80° for a few seconds, the speed is high, consistent with that of a bolide. The angular dimensions of the PAN were calculated using the witness's indications, they are very small, on the order of 0.3°, thus suggesting that the celestial body responsible for this re-entry is likely of modest size (see Annex 1). No other testimony was collected by GEIPAN or by the IMO/AMS (International Meteor Organization/American Meteor Society), which allows observers to testify about bolide observations, nor by the automated camera system FRIPON (Fireball Recovery and InterPlanetary Observation Network www.fripon.org). GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a bolide.
