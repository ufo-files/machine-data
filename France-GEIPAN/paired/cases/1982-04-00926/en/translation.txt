Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as C and named THUMERIES (59) 04.04.1982 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined (see the investigation report). On April 4, 1982, around 10:30 PM, four people observed in the sky a bright, round, white PAN. According to T1, the PAN was stationary above THUMERIES before heading north at extremely high speed. As it moved away towards TOURMIGNIES (59), it left a red line in the sky that gradually faded (T2). The following elements: - T1: "a bright, round, white light object," - T1: "stationary for about two minutes," - T1: "three red points below," - T2: "It looked like a glowing balloon." ... "It seemed to be aureoled. It could be taken for a kind of fireball," might suggest a helicopter; the absence of perceived noise is not a counterargument since the distance is unknown and the wind is contrary to the propagation of sound to the witnesses. The case is characterized above all by its lack of consistency. Indeed: - Four witnesses observed the phenomenon, but only the testimonies of two of them have reached us. One witness did not respond to the gendarmes' summons; - These two testimonies are very brief and consist of a few descriptive lines; - No in-depth investigation by the gendarmes. No photographs taken on the spot, nor any reconstruction, no search for other witnesses; etc... - Many data are missing, such as the angular elevations during the different phases of observation, the angular dimensions of the PAN, the total duration of observation... - Other data are imprecise, such as, for example, the phase of immobility of the PAN described by T1 as well as the way it disappeared. The direction of the PAN's movement is also unclear (north for T1, northeast for T2); - Finally, some data are mentioned by one witness but not by the other, such as, for example, the presence of three red points below the PAN, only mentioned by T1. The observation is not strange since nothing is incompatible with the observation of a helicopter, but the totality of the testimonies lacks enough consistency to support this hypothesis, even though nothing can deny it. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
