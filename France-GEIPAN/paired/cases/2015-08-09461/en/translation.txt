--- seg-5b8381196c709b1e0121deb1 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: Recent testimony (April 2015) of an older observation (August 2014). The witness reports that on August 31, 2014, between 3 a.m. and 5:30 a.m., they observed, along with a friend, various light phenomena in the sky (shooting stars, planes). However, the particular sinusoidal movement of a light phenomenon and a "flash" particularly intrigue them. Due to its strangeness and weak consistency (late testimony with dating errors, imprecise timing), the case turns out to be a misidentification with one or more satellites. The most unusual observation, involving a flash, is characteristic of an Iridium-type flash phenomenon. Unfortunately, the testimony is too late and not precise enough to determine which satellite was responsible for the observation. Initially, the witness did not recognize the PAN as a satellite since it had a very unusual characteristic, namely a sinusoidal trajectory. However, the witness, contacted later by the GEIPAN, eventually recognized it as a satellite and that the observed oscillations were due to an optical illusion phenomenon (nystagmus). Thus, although the exact satellite could not be determined, the file is classified as a satellite misidentification. The case is classified A, satellite misidentification.
