Summary: Observations from a boat of three red lights aligned and tilted; sudden extinction: lack of information.
--- seg-938ea77fbd90bdbdf5b2e5e4 [needs-review] ---
Description: On the night of September 11, 2012, between midnight and 2 a.m., two individuals aboard a sailboat approaching the port of Île d'Yeu observed three red lights, aligned and tilted above the ocean, to the right of the green lighthouse at the entrance to the port. The phenomenon remained stationary for several minutes and did not emit any sound: it suddenly disappeared. The two witnesses then continued their maneuvers to enter the port. The observation of three red orbs at low altitude, nearly stationary for 3 to 4 minutes, suggests Thai lanterns. The local wind recorded in St Sauveur (see weather report) indicates a light southwest wind: the witnesses should have seen a slight movement to their right; however, the witnesses themselves were in motion, and the port of Joinville (on the northwest coast of the island) is sheltered from southwest winds. Thai lanterns are most often released after festivals on Saturday nights; the observation took place on a Tuesday around 1 a.m., but in a very touristy area. The simultaneous extinction of the three lights is highly unlikely for lanterns. Given these three unfavorable factors for the Thai lantern hypothesis, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "C," due to a lack of information and cross-references. The testimony was submitted too late to GEIPAN for a search to be conducted with air traffic control.
