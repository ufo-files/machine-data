Summary: Observation of white glowing points in the cloudy sky: observation of skytrackers (laser animation).
--- seg-352190f17b15503784b8d02d [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: While on his way to work in a vehicle, the witness is driving on D13, shortly after the village of Saint-Canadet (13), between Le Puy-Sainte-Réparade and Aix-en-Provence (Bouches-du-Rhône), when he notices, around 6:50 PM, the presence of several stationary bright lights in the sky, despite partial cloud cover. These lights appear practically in front of him, heading south. The witness decides to stop on the side of the road to observe the phenomenon more closely. He takes a photographic shot of the PAN at 6:54 PM. He then resumes his journey, continuing to observe the lights, now visible on his left, until they disappear visually around 7:00 PM. The precise location of the phenomenon, as well as the visual elements collected, are presented in Annexes 1 and 2 of this report. The consistency* is good, although the observation relies on a single testimony, which is precise and supported by a photograph* according to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The analysis of the photograph (Annex 3) and the examination of the testimony lead to the conclusion that the PAN corresponds to a set of terrestrial spotlights aimed at the sky, illuminating a low cloud layer between 800 and 1800 meters in altitude. This cloud layer, primarily composed of stratus clouds for the lowest levels, is clearly visible on the photograph and confirmed by the witness. The cloud data provided by Météo France, from stations at Bec de l'Aigle, Istres, Marignane, and Salon-de-Provence, located approximately 30 km from the observation site (Annex 4), corroborate this description of the cloud cover present that evening. These spotlights, also known as "skytrackers," are often used for entertainment or advertising purposes for an event. The investigation established that at the exact time of the observation and in the direction indicated by the witness, approximately 5.4 km away (Annex 5), the "biennale des imaginaires numériques" was taking place at the center of Aix-en-Provence (13), during which "monumental, immersive, and interactive" works were presented. One of these works consisted of a 16-beam laser installation arranged around the fountain of the Rotonde, located precisely in the observation axis. The appearance (shape, color, and number) of these laser beams impacting the low clouds (clearly visible in the event's illustration photographs) is very similar to that of the PAN (Annex 6). The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as "A": observation of skytrackers.
