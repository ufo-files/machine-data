Description: On September 24th around 0:45 a witness observes in the sky a set of luminous phenomena in the form of white flashes bursting behind a large white cloud. These phenomena come from the South and East, and the witness films these light flashes for 8 minutes using a "spy pen" camera. No noise is heard. When the witness reviews his video, he is surprised to see glowing orbs he did not observe in real-time. The witness's spouse also observes the phenomenon, but only one testimony is collected. Lightning strike records show intense storm activity over Northern France (see Météociel) in the middle of the night. The witness confirms having experienced storms after 2 a.m. The flashes observed behind the clouds are a result of this storm activity. The video, taken with a small "spy pen" camera, does not show these light flashes but rather fixed bright points. This type of camera is very insensitive in low light conditions, which may explain why distant lightning flashes behind the clouds were too dim to activate the sensor. The bright points that appear in the video are very likely fixed lights: street lamps or other man-made sources. The witness did not see them with the naked eye. Since these spy cameras do not have a viewfinder, the witness cannot compare what he is filming with what he is seeing. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "GEIPAN" type "A": observation of distant lightning before an storm. The lights filmed in the video appear to be urban lighting.
