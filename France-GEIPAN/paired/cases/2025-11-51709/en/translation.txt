--- seg-02f3a388e4d2b9d45049239e [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On November 10, 2025, around 6:45 PM, in Saint-Séverin (16), a witness observed two stationary light sources located to the southeast at an elevation of approximately 45°. These objects, which displayed a significantly brighter light than the surrounding stars, remained motionless before gradually disappearing after three minutes of observation, even though the sky was clear. No other witnesses were reported regarding this event. The peculiarity of the case lies in the unusual intensity of light from the two PAN, their relative proximity, the absence of their own motion, and their gradual disappearance in a perfectly clear sky. The consistency* of the case is average, with one unique witness and no photos or videos of the PAN. *According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Three hypotheses were examined (see the investigation report).

• The astronomical hypothesis was quickly dismissed as no star, let alone two, with such brightness would have been present in the southeast direction at the time in question.

• The hypothesis of observing particularly bright satellites was also quickly ruled out, as no orbiting object can maintain a stationary position for three minutes. The kinematics of low-orbiting satellites, which require a high speed of movement, are inherently incompatible with the immobility described by the witness.

• The hypothesis of aircraft, particularly helicopters, remains plausible: the orientation of their spotlights towards the witness would explain the strong light intensity perceived as well as the absence of audible noise. However, the analysis of aerial maps provided by CAPCODA does not formally identify the involved devices.

* Air Defense and Operations Planning and Control Center (Air and Space Force). We note the difficulty of precisely positioning the observed lights and confirming their immobility in a night sky without a reference point. The absence of photos/videos that could have aided the analysis of this case does not allow for further progress. Compared to two stars, the PAN does not present any particular strangeness for a night sky. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as "C": Unidentified Phenomenon due to a lack of data.
