Summary: Observation of a very bright, round object resembling a "ping pong ball" that appears stationary before suddenly moving at high speed in a clear sky: likely observation of an aircraft approaching Paris-Orly Airport, with its landing lights observed almost in line with the trajectory.

On July 30, 2016, at approximately 7:15 PM, a witness observed from their car at a red light on the RN7 in Athis-Mons, near Paris-Orly Airport, a bright light phenomenon in the sky to their left as they headed north towards the intersection leading to the gas station in the nearby shopping center. The phenomenon was described as a very bright round shape, similar to a ping-pong ball held at arm's length, remaining stationary for about a minute before slowly moving and disappearing very quickly in a straight line. The sky was clear, and the witness reported the presence of planes in the area during the observation. Given the witness's location, very close to Orly's runways, they were in a sector where inbound aircraft can be observed at a low angular distance.

Analysis of the questionnaire and sketch indicates the witness was heading north on the RN7 and observing to the west on their left, which corresponds to the area west of the runways. In this configuration, an aircraft approaching the airport from the west during the radar guidance phase preceding final alignment could appear almost directly in the observer's line of sight. The witness described an extremely bright object with no visible wings or flashing lights. This description is consistent with observing an airplane seen almost face-on, with powerful landing lights appearing as a very bright point in the sky and visually obscuring the aircraft's structure at a distance. In this type of configuration, wings and navigation lights can be difficult to discern, giving the impression of a solitary circular object.

The sequence described by the witness (apparent immobility followed by slow movement and then very rapid departure) is also consistent with a perspective effect related to the angular velocity of an aircraft observed almost in its trajectory axis. As the plane approaches along this axis, its apparent movement in the sky may seem very slight, giving the impression of a stationary phenomenon. As soon as the observation angle changes, for example, with a slight change in course or when the aircraft moves out of the frontal geometry relative to the observer, the angular velocity suddenly becomes more significant and can be perceived as a very rapid acceleration.

The available meteorological data for the time of observation indicate a dominant wind from the northwest at speeds of 9 to 15 km/h. Although this wind direction generally leads to the use of west-oriented runways (runway 25), the wind speed remains relatively low, and an airport's operating configuration can be maintained or adjusted for operational reasons. Under these conditions, the use of an east approach (runway 06), involving aircraft arriving from the west, remains compatible with the observed meteorological conditions.

Considering the immediate proximity of Orly Airport, the geometry of the observation, the described lighting characteristics, and the well-known perceptual effects associated with observing an airplane almost directly in its trajectory axis, the observed phenomenon appears consistent with landing lights of an aircraft in approach to Paris-Orly Airport. Consequently, the most plausible hypothesis to explain the observation is that of an aircraft in approach to the Paris-Orly Airport circuit, with its landing lights perceived as an intense bright point, giving the witness the impression of a stationary circular object then quickly moving away. However, due to the unavailability of radar data for analysis, it was not possible to precisely identify the aircraft. We note that the immediate vicinity of Orly Airport is under constant surveillance by air security. Any observation of an PAN would have been treated as a listed anomaly. The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as 'B': probable observation of an aircraft in approach to Paris-Orly Airport, with its landing lights observed almost directly in the trajectory axis.
