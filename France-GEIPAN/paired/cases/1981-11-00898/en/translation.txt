**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions' relevance. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The previously classified D case, named FEIGNIES (59) 06.11.1981 and observed on November 6, 1981, around 7:45 PM, is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases. A witness saw a very intense light phenomenon behind their residence and "near a power pole" in a field. The observation lasted about an hour, and when the light faded, the witness saw a cigar-shaped form surrounded by various colors. Subsequently, these lights faded, revealing an object resembling a rugby ball in gray metal. For 4-5 seconds, this object swayed back and forth before returning to its vertical position. The witness then noticed an orange corridor "coming from the location of this craft" and white traces in the sky. No noise was heard during the observation. The witness reported having previously observed similar light phenomena near their home the week before. The Gendarmerie was not notified of this observation for another 10 days, and no other witnesses were found for that date and observation area.

The analysis is based on:
- The statement's protocol (PV) of the witness's declarations;
- Aerial photos and mapping from both the past and present;
- A private investigation conducted in the days following the observation (reviewed in Lumières Dans La Nuit (LDLN) January-February 1984, issues 235-236), with a portion attached to the PV.

No elements from this private investigation are used to support the conclusion, which rests solely on the other evidence. Conversely, elements from this investigation that seemingly contradict the accepted hypothesis are considered to separate out factors strictly attributable to the witness's perception or implicit/explicitly derived conclusions from the investigation.

We conclude that the witness, newly installed (two months) and likely unfamiliar with the local area and agricultural practices, very probably observed one or more agricultural vehicles performing nocturnal work in the adjacent fields, specifically gathering and stacking beet piles along an accessible service road (as per the investigation report). This conclusion is supported by:

- Similar observations in the preceding days;
- The likelihood that nocturnal beet harvesting using specialized vehicles already existed in 1981, equipped with lights;
- The current beet harvesting season, with fields containing beet piles located near the witness's home in the observation direction;
- The completion of the harvesting work, already partially or fully done on nearby plots in the days preceding the observation, according to the private investigation;
- The PAN appearing "on the ground" per the witness;
- Bright lights, consistent with the vehicles' lights;
- Distance and dimension measurements, though necessarily imprecise due to observation conditions, aligning with the observation of such vehicles. The distance could be roughly estimated between 250 and 500 meters, with the service road approximately 400-450 meters from the witness in the observation axis;
- The long observation duration, consistent with the time needed for beet gathering;
- The imprecise shape of the PAN, changing due to a perceptual illusion;
- The agricultural vehicle's movements, as described by the witness, matching the horizontal displacement and swinging movements;
- The "orange corridor" observed by the witness after the vehicle left resembling what can be seen when a spotlight is directed towards the sky in favorable atmospheric conditions (fog, mist, humidity...). This could be one of the lighting spots for the work, left on for an unspecified time (explained by its orientation towards the sky). It's a plausible hypothesis with no confirming or denying evidence;

Two aspects of the observation (from the private investigation) seem less consistent with the hypothesis:

- Observing thin multicolored rays around the PAN. The hypothesis of a light diffraction phenomenon caused by steam on the window of the door-window combination was considered, but it can occur under certain conditions (significant temperature difference, high humidity, and close proximity), of which only the latter was unconfirmed due to lack of detail on the distance.
- Observing the PAN at a specific phase in front of the pole. The brief mention in the private investigation wasn't directly from the witness, and the drawing supporting this information wasn't made by the witness either. Furthermore, no explanation is given for what would have led the witness to believe the PAN passed in front of the pole. The factual data to evaluate this claim are missing: no comparative data on the PAN's tint/background, no data on the light power of the "lights" at that moment. The mention of the illuminated ground by the PAN not visible to the witness isn't contradictory with its position near the pole, but it's also not consistent with its position further away, near the service road, thus behind the pole. Additionally, doubts exist about the nature of the pole's "profiling" visible to the witness: it could be due to contrast with the landscape's background illuminated by the moon or contrast with the PAN's luminosity.

The consistency is poor, with a shallow exploration of the PAN and various observation phases' description. However, it's sufficient to conclude given the reasonable probability of the accepted hypothesis (resulting in low strangeness).

Therefore, the GEIPAN classifies it as: Very probable observation of one or more agricultural vehicles performing beet gathering work on a service road located in the observation axis.
