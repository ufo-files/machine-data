Description: On February 06, 2019 at 6:30 am, a driver noticed in his rearview mirror a cylindrical and milky-white luminescent phenomenon illuminating the road. The PAN passes in front of his vehicle and illuminates the interior of it. The surprised witness stops and gets out of the vehicle. The PAN has disappeared. No particular noise was heard. Only one testimony will be collected at GEIPAN. One hypothesis is favored: a misidentification with a meteor (see the investigation report). The description of the PAN does indeed strongly resemble a very bright meteor, called a bolide: cylindrical shape accompanied by a beam of light, very fast and silent movement, and an observation lasting several seconds. It is noted that the FRIPON network recorded no bolide in the northeast of France during the night of February 5 to 6, 2019. The only event concerns the south of the country. However, several bolides were reported on February 6, 2019, late at night. The BOAM network thus recorded 4 bolides between 05:00 UT and 06:00 UT, that is, between 06:00 and 07:00 . All of them were recorded by the LITIK 1 station, located in Fléville-devant-Nancy (54), that is, in a sector consistent with the PAN (Figure 9). This case has low consistency as the witness is unique and no photos or sketches of the PAN were provided. Additionally, the witness only provided a testimony to the Gendarmerie, without completing the Terrestrial Questionnaire (QT) for GEIPAN. As a result, many elements of the testimony are unknown. However, the description of the PAN strongly suggests a known astronomical phenomenon. With a fairly low strangeness and low consistency (single witness, no photo of PAN, no filled QT), this case appears to be a probable misperception with a meteor. The description of the PAN does indeed strongly correspond to this explanatory hypothesis. Several bolides were indeed recorded in northeast France and Germany on February 06, 2019, between 06:00 and 07:00 local time. However, none of them can be formally linked to the observation, as the witness is unique and there is a lack of elements in the testimony. The case is classified B, probable misidentification with a meteoroid.
