Summary: Brief nighttime observation of a point in the deep sky with increasing light intensity: misidentification with a flash from Satellite Iridium 33 P (debris).
--- seg-530e4d5eb442b2a4cb1c115c [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The witness is with a friend in the forest to take some astronomical photos of the sky. Suddenly, his attention is drawn to a bright point with a magnitude similar to Venus that appears near the Little Bear constellation, on the border of Cepheus. He observes it for a very short instant, at 23:08. The PAN seemed stationary. Only one testimony was collected. The location of the PAN's appearance and the available data in the testimony are not very strange (see the investigation report). The only strange aspect could be the stability of the PAN, but this is explained by the fact that the satellite gave the impression of approaching the witness, passing almost vertically above him. The witness is very precise in his observation, which allows for confirmation of the PAN's position and alignment with the preferred hypothesis of a reflection of sunlight off an object orbiting Earth. The file study concludes with a simple misperception of a satellite flash, which is very likely given the position of the identified satellite debris. The GEIPAN is classified as A: misperception with a flash from Satellite Iridium 33 P (debris).
