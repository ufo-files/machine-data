Summary: Observation of the silent west-to-east passage of a dark triangle bounded by three lights at each end: probable observation of a rare flare phenomenon of a Yaogan triple satellite.
--- seg-0b449ed72ba75f04c882bd29 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 1, 2019, at 6:45 a.m., a witness walking their dog was intrigued by 3 flashing lights they saw at low altitude heading west. After 3 or 4 seconds of observation, the witness notices that the phenomenon is moving slowly towards him/her. He then spots the outlines of a dark triangle with a light at each end. The witness runs towards his house to look for something to film or take a picture. Upon his return, everything had disappeared. The total duration of the observation is 8 seconds. No other testimony was collected. We have shown through analysis (see the investigation report) that among the two hypotheses explored, namely a confusion with an airplane in approach phase or a confusion with a triplet of Yaogan-type satellites in "flare" phase, only the latter seems capable of explaining the appearance and behavior of the PAN and resolving the oddities of the observation for the witness. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Individual point-like shape of each light,
- White color,
- High brightness, rarely and fleetingly observed for this type of satellite, due to a phenomenon of sunlight reflection on highly reflective components (antennas, solar panels, etc.). This phenomenon has already been observed and filmed. - Short observation duration, compatible with the known duration of this type of flare, approximately 8 to 10 seconds. The witness did not observe the dimming, having left to fetch something to photograph or film the phenomenon, probably just before that dimming, ultimately making the satellites invisible to the naked eye. The witness was unable to locate the phenomenon once they returned. - Slow movement along a straight trajectory from West to East, - Measurements and calculations of distances, dimensions, and elevations generally consistent. The witness did not observe any stars between the bright points forming the triplet, as he was able to cross areas of the sky with few stars and/or of sufficiently low magnitude to be visible to the naked eye, especially during such a short observation duration and low angular movement. A perceptual illusion phenomenon of shape is also present, occurring during a short observation of bright points moving in concert at night, covering a low angular extent, and traversing a non-populated area devoid of visible stars, giving the witness the impression that the three independent lights are linked to each other and delineate a solid black triangle object. Unfortunately, it was impossible to verify the actual presence at the time of observation of a Yaogan satellite triplet with our precise satellite positioning software. Inability to pinpoint the exact date of the observation. This prevents us from confirming the hypothesis in a consolidated manner, hence the classification as "B" with a probable hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a rare flare phenomenon from a triplet of Yaogan satellites.
