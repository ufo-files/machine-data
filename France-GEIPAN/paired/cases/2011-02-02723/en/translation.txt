Summary: Observation in the sky of the rapid movement of a white light with direction change: probable International Space Station.
--- seg-6f69593290ae19a77597aeab [needs-review] ---
Description: On February 3, 2011, between 7:20 and 7:24 a.m., a witness observed from a parking lot a bright, non-flashing white light moving in a straight line and changing direction in the sky. No sound was heard during the observation, and no trail was seen. The gendarmerie collected no other reports on this phenomenon. Initially classified as "D1" in 2011 by the GEIPAN, this observation case was re-examined in 2013 with a new hypothesis proposed by an internet user. Given the lack of witnesses (only one) and no recordings, the case is considered moderately consistent. The description of the PAN observed is very precise. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, trajectory) with a well-known astronautic object: the International Space Station (ISS), which was present in the observed area of the sky, despite the witness not mentioning it. In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of low strangeness is classified as PAN B, probable observation of the International Space Station.
