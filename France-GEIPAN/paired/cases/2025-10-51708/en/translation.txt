Summary: Observation of the passage of three bright points moving together at very high altitude: observation of a triplet of Chinese satellites Yaogan-40 02.
--- seg-e04f565ca23b9c2a9ee21192 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 9, 2025, at approximately 8:48 PM, a witness observed from the terrace of their home in Colomiers (31) three bright points at very high altitude, arranged in an equilateral triangle, with consistent size, moving in a straight and regular line along a south-north axis. Upon reaching the zenith, one of the bright points emitted a intense flash, described by the witness as "a satellite reflection." After about two minutes of observation, the brightness of the PAN dimmed, then it passed behind a roof. The witness then considered the possibilities of Starlink® satellites or planes, but their searches on dedicated apps yielded no results. The consistency is average, with a single witness and no photos or videos of the PAN. The strangeness, in the witness's opinion, arises from the presence of three bright points moving in unison.

*According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The only hypothesis explored, that of observing a Chinese Yaogan triple satellite, is supported by the following elements:

- Three white points arranged in an equilateral triangle, which maintains its stability and moves in a straight and regular line in the sky, corresponding to a formation flight.
- Analysis of astronautic data via a dedicated site (inthesky.com) confirms the passage at the exact time of observation (between 8:48 PM and 8:50 PM) of the Yaogan-40 02 (A/B/C) triple, launched from China on May 11, 2025. This satellite moves on an orbit from south to north.
- The witness estimated one side of the triangle at 15 cm at arm's length, which corresponds approximately to an angular separation of 12 to 13°. In GEIPAN files concerning similar triple observations, the angular separation between two components is generally around 5°. This value is also confirmed by angular measurements taken from the maps on the inthesky.com site (see annex). It is therefore likely that the witness overestimated the apparent separation.
- One component of the triple experienced a brighter flash (satellite flare): this behavior has already been captured on video for the Yaogan 16 triple (see video Yaogan satellite triplets in real-time).

The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of a triple satellite.
