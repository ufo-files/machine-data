Summary: Long observation in a car and at home of a very bright and static light in the night sky: observation of the planet Venus.
--- seg-a4c6d1ea77f22967e97566d1 [needs-review] ---
Description: On February 6, 2017, around 8:45 PM, a driver notices a very intense light in the sky. Upon arriving home, the witness continues to see this light, which is stationary. They observe it with binoculars. The observation lasts for 45 minutes, after which the witness goes to a friend's house. The following evening, around 7:45 PM, the witness makes another observation of the same phenomenon, located slightly higher in the sky than before. Only one testimony was collected. The description of this observation is characteristic of an observation of a star:

- The observation duration is long (45 minutes) and the PAN does not move or only moves slightly in the sky;
- The PAN appears for two consecutive evenings and appears sufficiently identical in aspect and position for the witness to identify it as the same phenomenon: "I noticed in the sky the same very bright object observed yesterday." This observation turns out to be a misidentification with the planet Venus, which is indeed present, but not in the northwest as the witness states, but rather slightly south of west.

The analysis of the witness's photograph, which provides the position of the PAN relative to the terrain, allows for a precise determination of this position and, consequently, its correspondence with Venus's position. Additionally, the witness's indication regarding the position of the PAN the following evening, one hour earlier, is also consistent with Venus's position (higher and further south). This is illustrated in the attached document.

Venus is brighter than the stars. Its colors can vary and change due to atmospheric effects, as Venus is often near the horizon and thus viewed through a thick layer of atmosphere. This case is classified as A: Observation of the planet Venus.
