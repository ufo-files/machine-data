Description: On August 12, 2010, between 5:00 and 6:00 AM, a witness in their garden observed, in a cloudy sky, a bright point in the northeast direction. The witness went inside, and upon exiting, they saw the point in the southeast. The point's trajectory appeared irregular. Only one witness statement was collected on this phenomenon. In the investigation, we demonstrated that the witness observed, in a very cloudy sky, two stars in succession: the star Capella and the planet Mars (see the investigation report). This observation was accompanied by a physiological effect of autocinetic (micro-eye mobility). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The PAN's punctual shape, similar to the two stars
- The white-yellow color, identical to Capella, slightly different for Mars, more reddish
- The high brightness, comparable to the beginning to Venus, slightly less intense for Mars
- The initial and final azimuths consistent with both stars
- The initial and final elevations consistent with both stars
- The irregular movement is only apparent, caused by a physiological phenomenon of micro-eye mobility (autocinetic)

The witness could not realize they were observing two different stars, as they were very similar in color and brightness. They also stepped away for a few moments shortly after the observation of Capella began, which was subsequently obscured by clouds. Upon returning, they were able to observe Mars in the southeast, not obscured by clouds at that time. Since the two stars were likely the only ones visible in succession in the sky, the witness logically thought it was the same phenomenon.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": successive observation of the star Capella then the planet Mars, accompanied by a physiological phenomenon of micro-eye mobility.
