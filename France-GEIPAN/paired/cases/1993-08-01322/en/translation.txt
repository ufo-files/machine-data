**Case Description and Re-evaluation: Saint-Gaudens (31) - 14 August 1993**

The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions' relevance. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-evaluation sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification.

A previously classified D case, named SAINT-GAUDENS (31) 14.08.1993, is part of a recent batch of cases under review (see investigation report). On August 14, 1993, around 10:00 PM, multiple individuals in two separate locations observed an unusual phenomenon heading towards the Pyrenees. Three witnesses reported their observations to the local Gendarmerie. The described phenomenon was a circular red light, stationary to the right of Mont Valier (for T1 and T3) or heading towards Cagire (for T2). One witness (T3) used binoculars and noticed five red-orange lights within the mass. The observation lasted about 10 minutes, then the light disappeared in a few seconds.

T2's entire oral statement is consistent with a phenomenon solely on the ground. Indeed, it was initially perceived "in the woods" and then "descending into the Pyrenees range, in a horizontal movement." It's worth questioning if the other witnesses' accounts also suggest this, as the explanation might exceed GEIPAN's sole competence.

T1 and T3, whose observation points are close, place the UFO at the height of Mont Valier, slightly to the right, based on their statements or drawings. Only T3 mentions the sky at the end of his observation with the phrase "the strange phenomenon disappeared horizontally in the sky." This doesn't mean the UFO was at high altitude. Mont Valier is not an isolated peak but extends to the right with a visible ridge line at a slightly lower angular height from the observation point (Saint-Gaudens). Moreover, in front of Mont Valier, there are several reliefs also visible at the same angular height on the right, notably the mountain range at the summit of Cournudère (1563m). T1 and T3's drawings could depict one of these relief lines but not the horizon, which they may not have even distinguished.

T2 and T3 describe the phenomenon's disappearance horizontally ("disappeared as it descended into the Pyrenees range, in a horizontal movement"). However, T1 and T2's drawings, taken alone without the mention of "horizontally," could suggest an upward elevation in the sky. The oral mention of "horizontally" cannot apply to a UFO observed at increasing angular elevation. Thus, the witnesses T2 and T3 intended to depict a horizontal displacement primarily involving a distance evolution. This evolution cannot be directly translated into a flat drawing, so both witnesses represent it with a cone, with sizes progressively reducing, of the UFO. They intended to depict a horizontal escape, not an upward elevation. This horizontal escape might only be an illusion. Indeed, witnesses perceiving a dimming of an UFO's light, without being able to judge its actual distance, might interpret this as a genuine decrease in light emission from a stationary or a horizontal escape of a constantly-lit UFO.

GEIPAN's experience shows that the strange context often leads witnesses to interpret a phenomenon as a horizontal escape when it's actually stationary. T1's testimony introduces a form of inconsistency more difficult to resolve. T1 describes no movement of the UFO, including during its disappearance, in his oral account. The "bending forward" associated with the "appearance of three beams of light" can only be understood as a perceived change in orientation of the UFO since it was already observed with an oriented shape ("crushed oval"). The main drawing aligns with the account with a stationary point (no angular elevation). Conversely, the secondary drawing mentions a "course change" with an upward arrow. In this context, it's possible T1 also intended to depict a horizontal escape in perspective but the inconsistency prevents a conclusion.

Therefore, the explanation exceeds GEIPAN's sole competence as the observation, once the unusable part of T1's testimony is set aside, is compatible with a phenomenon entirely on the ground. The UFO at the time should not have been classified as D because nothing indicates it was an aerospatial phenomenon.

Despite this, GEIPAN can provide some clues for investigating a ground phenomenon, without claiming to narrow down the search (see investigation report). However, this search clashes with the fact that T2's observation direction, as indicated by the Pic de Cagire, does not align with T1 and T3's directions, signaled as to the right of Mont Valier, except if T2 had designated Pic de Cagire as a reference point instead of Mont Valier. There's no confirmation or denial of such a confusion. The angular difference between the two peaks is indeed small (20°) but for a familiar witness, the two references are rather distinct, even at night. A more in-depth investigation at the time would have allowed identifying this difference, seeking clarifications from witnesses' indications, and either confirming or denying this difference.

At this stage, this difference adds to the strangeness as the correspondence of appearance, time, and duration suggests a phenomenon of the same nature occurring in two different places. The absence of an explanation after a search utilizing all ground phenomenon investigation skills (not just aerospatial) would create a level of consistency demand on the testimonies (level of information and assessment of its reliability) that's unlikely to be met 25 years later, leading to exploitable rather than unexplained phenomena.

Consequently, the case is classified as C: exploitable because it exceeds GEIPAN's sole competence.
