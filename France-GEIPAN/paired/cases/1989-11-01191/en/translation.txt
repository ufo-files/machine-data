Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MIRECOURT (88) 22.11.1989 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On November 22, 1989, between 2:00 and 2:15 a.m., a woman (T1) observed through her window the rapid passage on a slightly descending trajectory of a brightly red and well-lit phenomenon. The witness woke up her husband (T2) who also spotted the phenomenon. T1 described a shape resembling two superimposed cylinders separated by a black band, with the lower cylinder being longer (see the drawing in the statement). T1 described sparks and flames around the object as well as a spark tail in fog, then the phenomenon was lost from view by T1, hidden by three large pines near the house. No noise was heard during the short observation. T1 contacted the gendarmerie and the press. With the experience gained by the GEIPAN from this type of observation, the witnesses' description leaves no doubt today. It is a re-entry atmospheric phenomenon (see the investigation report). Only the exact nature of this re-entry remains to be explored. There is no other witness (which is understandable at 2 a.m.) which does not allow to determine the actual duration of the phenomenon. According to the description of PAN, the hypothesis of an artificial re-entry is favored, even more so as the trajectory and passage time of the Soviet satellite Cosmos 2047 are very consistent with those of PAN. Although the re-entry time is referenced to November 21, 1989, it is known that this is done with a margin of error of a few hours, making it entirely possible for a re-entry on November 22, 1989, at 1 a.m. (UTC). The duration of the phenomenon (20 seconds) is a priori too short for an artificial re-entry of a large artificial object, and would rather suggest the hypothesis of observing a natural re-entry (bolide), but on the other hand, a much longer duration clearly favors the hypothesis of an artificial re-entry. It is obvious that the witnesses only observed a part of the actual duration of the phenomenon: PAN was obscured by trees at the end of the observation, and the witnesses only discovered PAN (at their window) from their bedroom. The case is classified as A, almost certain misperception with the atmospheric re-entry of satellite Cosmos 2047.
