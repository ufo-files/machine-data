Summary: Observation of the movements of a bright PAN in the cloudy night sky: probable observation of a drone.
--- seg-6127beb3f9027998f5ed41b7 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On November 11, 2024, around 5:30 PM local time in Épernay, a witness observed from his living room a stationary bright point in the sky. Intrigued, he paused a phone call to photograph and then video the object. The unidentified airborne phenomenon (PAN) rapidly departed and eventually faded out. The witness continued recording, noting rapid movements. The object reappeared shortly after in a vertical position above his home. Stepping onto his terrace, the observer described it as a round shape emitting intermittent light. Eventually, the PAN resumed motion and disappeared into the clouds. The consistency* of this observation case is deemed satisfactory: although the case file relies on a single witness, he captured usable photos and videos for analysis (see the investigation report). *According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The peculiarity of the case, for the witness, lies in the perception of remarkable and unusual light coupled with a complete absence of noise. A remote investigation, followed by an on-site one, helped to reduce this peculiarity, notably by exploring the drone hypothesis - a track considered by the witness himself and supported by the following elements gathered during the inquiries:

- The speed of a drone, highly variable, is not incompatible with the apparent speed of the PAN observed during the event.
- Light outages are explained by remote controlled extinction or temporary masking via the drone's structural elements.
- The observed white color, although not strictly aeronautical (colors should be white, as well as green and red), could correspond to a (or two) customized light(s) by the drone user, who needs to keep it in view, especially if the light's brightness is significant, which is the case here.
- The perceived absence of noise is likely due to the distance between the witness and the drone, which also produces a moderate sound coupled with a wind that would only be a carrier at the very beginning of the observation.

Regarding the photos and videos, their analysis reveals the following, supporting the hypothesis:

- Estimated distances on photos using the IPACO* software for common drone dimensions are consistent.
- Trajectories observed on multiple videos (ascents and descents), as well as periods of immobility, are compatible with those achievable by a drone.
- Zoom causes defocusing, which can explain the effects observed on the videos (irregularities or various shapes present in the defocusing task), as the actual shape of the light emitted by the PAN is well punctual.

Note that the user of this drone, who remains unidentified, is in violation of several regulations. The observation started at the flight authorization limit time, prohibited during the night (30 minutes after sunset), and the area is strictly forbidden for drone overflight. The GEIPAN classifies this case as a "B" observation, likely a drone.
