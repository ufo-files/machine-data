Summary: Short flight observations by a flight instructor and two students of a circular glowing phenomenon above their aircraft and rapid disappearance: probable observation of a commercial aircraft reflection.
--- seg-70efc57550c3db18a1b2637d [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named GRAVELINES (59) 2002 is part of a recent set of cases that have been re-examined. On December 9, 2002, around 10:45 a.m., a flight instructor is in flight with two students. During the takeoff phase from Calais-Dunkerque Airport (Marck commune, 62), they are surprised to see, for less than 10 seconds, a circular luminescent phenomenon that approaches them before changing direction and disappearing from their view. The color of the PAN is initially described as white-yellow and then gray metal. The flight instructor is surprised because this object made no maneuver to move away. The Lille-Lesquin control tower, contacted immediately, does not notice any trace of an aircraft on the radar screens. The flight instructor contacts the Gendarmerie around 5:00 p.m. to see if anyone else has seen the phenomenon: besides the flight instructor and his two students, no other testimony is collected. The witnesses likely observed a reflection of the sun on a commercial jet flying at about twenty kilometers (probably the Brussels-London route). Indeed (as detailed in the investigation report):

- The described aspect (color, shape) is compatible, and the flight instructor considers this hypothesis and only refutes it due to his understanding of the conditions of production that he does not consider met (sun position);
- The respective positions of the witnesses and the sun allow for explaining a specular reflection on the fuselage of an aircraft correctly oriented in mirror on the London-Brussels line crossing at 20 km from the witnesses;
- The condition of specular reflection (and thus sensitive to geometry) explains the brief duration of the PAN;
- The witnesses perceive the disappearance of the PAN according to a distance fade-out, which corresponds to a classic illusion. Indeed, when the light intensity of a PAN decreases, it is logical for a witness to interpret this as a distance fade-out of a PAN that would have maintained the same light intensity. The only uncertainty about the hypothesis lies in the following points:

- The phenomenon of specular reflection on an aircraft fuselage is known in nature but is not the subject of any modeling allowing for a quantitative validation of the level and profile of the light intensity evolution;
- There is no evidence of the aircraft's presence, but this is non-determinant as the records are not accessible 17 years later. The preceding elements only affect the probability of the hypothesis, which remains very plausible. The consistency is good (several witnesses, sketches, indications of positions). Consequently, the GEIPAN concludes in B: probable observation of a commercial jet (reflection).
