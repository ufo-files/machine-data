Summary: Observation during a flight of a very bright light halo in the sky: observation of rocket elements in deorbiting maneuver, more precisely the second stage of the SpaceX® Falcon 9 launcher.
--- seg-a43c7cb2690dbf97cddadba2 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 15, 2024, at 08:00 UTC, the witness, from the cockpit of an airplane crossing the ocean, observed a luminous phenomenon. The aircraft was then near the coordinates 50°30'N, 52°W, at an altitude of approximately 30,000 feet (approximately 9,000 meters). By scanning the sky at an angle of about 60 degrees above the horizon, he detected a very bright luminous halo with a diameter comparable to that of the Moon. This halo moved towards the horizon, generally towards the east, for an estimated duration of 2 to 3 minutes, with constant brightness throughout the movement. The phenomenon then disappeared behind the horizon line. Three photographs of PAN were transmitted; one of them appears to show a dark mass associated with the luminous halo. Of low strangeness and good consistency* (single witness but several photos of PAN), this case turns out to be the observation of an orbital maneuver of a space launcher (see the investigation report). *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The description and appearance of PAN strongly evoke the degazing of a rocket stage in space vacuum illuminated by the sun (so-called stage passivation step). A reconstruction of the trajectory of the Starlink® 10-10 group, launched on a Falcon 9 rocket, approximately 1 hour and 40 minutes before the observation, shows that its trajectory matches exactly that of PAN. The Falcon 9 rocket's second stage was beginning its second orbit, a privileged moment for a "deorbit burn" maneuver, intended to deorbit it for a controlled reentry over the Indian Ocean. This process is described in the note GEIPAN FALCON 9 and STARLINK®. The case is classified "A", observation of rocket elements in deorbitation maneuver, more precisely the second stage of the SpaceX® Falcon 9 launcher.
