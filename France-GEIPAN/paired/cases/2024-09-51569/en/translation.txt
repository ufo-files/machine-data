Summary: Observation of the presence of a "vibrant" but stationary blue halo in the sky prior to a sudden disappearance to the east: probable observation of a sky-tracer.
--- seg-941c1384839616a686f94cb4 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: In Paris, a witness descends into the courtyard of his residence and observes in the sky, at 9:16 PM, a stationary blue halo oriented towards the northeast of the capital. The observation lasts for a few seconds, less than a minute in total. The witness captures the scene using his smartphone by taking two identical photos, one of which includes a timestamp. Although visible at high altitude, the halo does not allow for precise determination of its shape or dimensions. The light exhibits slight instability, describing a vibrant movement while remaining fixed, before executing a very rapid lateral displacement to the right and disappearing almost instantly to the east. Just before this eclipse, the witness manages to take one last photo. The investigation relies on this unique testimony. The consistency* of the case is considered good with the receipt of a complete Technical Questionnaire accompanied by 2 photos of PAN.

*CONSISTANCE: According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The hypothesis accepted is that of a sky-tracer (a powerful projector aimed at the sky). It is based on:

- The blue color, common for a sky-tracer.
- The confirmed presence of a cloud ceiling (photograph provided by the witness + 7/8 octa cloudiness reported in the MétéoCiel database), providing a surface to reflect the powerful light beam.
- The trajectory, initially fixed followed by a sudden acceleration in a direction without apparent noise, which seems to correspond to a maneuver of the ground-based projector. This hypothesis reduces the perceived strangeness.

However, we found no public data or information record when we visited the observation location, allowing us to locate a sky-tracer at the time of the observation, despite the presence in the observation axis of "L'Atelier des Lumières" (interrogation of an employee).

GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a sky-tracer.
