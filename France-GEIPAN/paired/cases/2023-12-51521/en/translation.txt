--- seg-c4a0446d9053e0fed5b74d84 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 21, 2022, two witnesses in Lachaux (63) observed, on the surveillance camera recordings, the presence of an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon (PAN) characterized by powerful distant light flashes, usually oriented towards the north. These flashes are observed on multiple occasions: on November 8, 2022, between 6:15 PM and 8:15 PM, on March 8, 2023, between 9:45 PM and 10:30 PM, on May 9, 2023, between 9:30 PM and 10:00 PM, and on December 9, 2023, between 7:30 PM and 9:30 PM. The observed luminous phenomena present constant characteristics: a white color according to witness (T2) and a rather orange hue according to witness (T1), visible in the direction of northwest, north, or northeast. The PAN is also observed with the naked eye and recorded with T2's smartphone on May 9, 2023, and December 9, 2023. No noise is detected by either the witnesses or the surveillance video system. After consulting the meteorological archives regarding lightning strikes in France at the dates and times of the observations, it is noted that in each case, a thunderstorm is present, usually located to the north of the witnesses, about a hundred kilometers away (see the investigation report). This naturally leads us to the hypothesis of distant lightning observations associated with these storms, whose distance prevents the thunder from being heard by witnesses or recorded by the video system. The GEIPAN lightning and thunderstorm expert is consulted and states that:

- Thunder can be heard up to an average distance of 30 to 40 km, and occasionally up to 70 km for the most powerful lightning, which is less than the distance separating the witnesses from the observed storms.

- A high-altitude observer with an unobstructed view (which is the case for witnesses facing north) can see distant landscape elements illuminated by these lightning bolts, including the treetops of the high hills located at the boundary between the departments of Puy-de-Dôme and Allier, and possibly the lightning bolts themselves at low altitude, up to a distance of 150 to 200 km. The effect produced is that of a powerful flash on the horizon, similar to what is visible in PAN videos. - Some observers, in the presence of such flashes, will speak of "heat flashes", an incorrect term as it is simply lightning too far away for thunder to be audible, just as in the case of witnesses. - Note, however, that lightning occurring between clouds is not logged by the lightning impact recording system, which only logs lightning between the ground and clouds. However, an uncertainty point remains regarding the observation of March 8, 2023, where the storms were respectively to the west (therefore not in the right direction) for one, and too far away, at approximately 200 km distance, for the other. Similarly, for the observation of May 09, 2023, on the other hand, the storms were close to the witnesses, at a distance of less than 10 km. They would have therefore normally been able to recognize them as such. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": observation of distant lightning.
