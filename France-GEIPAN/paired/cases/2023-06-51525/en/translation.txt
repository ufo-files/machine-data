Description: On June 13, 2023, around 3:00 PM, a witness located in their garden in Digne-les-Bains (04) observed in the sky an unidentified aerial phenomenon (PAN). The silent object had a shape resembling a gas tank, a white color, and appeared stationary. However, it was slowly moving from southeast to east. The observation lasted between 10 and 20 seconds until the PAN disappeared behind a layer of clouds. The testimony was collected on March 5, 2024. The consistency* of the case is assessed as average due to a single testimony without visual support (photo or video) of the PAN. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation explored three hypotheses (see the investigation report): 1. A large weather balloon, whose appearance could correspond to what was observed, provided it had both lost altitude and suffered a partial partial deflation. 2. A Zeppelin-type airship, possibly en route to participate in the 24 Hours of Le Mans; this lead was ruled out after verification, as no aircraft of this type was present in the region at the time of the observation. 3. That of a plane, possibly military. The latter hypothesis appears most plausible for the following reasons: 
- The described shape is compatible with that of the fuselage of an aircraft. The apparent absence of wings can be explained by atmospheric diffusion effects limiting the perception of lateral elements. 
- White color: a frequent characteristic of commercial airliners, this shade is less common on military aircraft, pointing the analysis towards a civilian craft. 
- The estimates provided by the witness, combined with the calculations made (apparent dimensions, altitude, distance, and speed), are consistent with those of an aircraft in flight. The perceived sense of slowness can be attributed to a moderate to high altitude speed, amplified by perspective effects. 
- Absence of condensation trail: at the estimated altitudes, atmospheric conditions may not have been favorable for the formation of trails, which remains consistent with the observation. The delay between the date of the observation and the receipt of the questionnaire by GEIPAN did not allow for the National Center for Air Operations (CNOA) to be contacted to verify the possible presence of an aircraft corresponding to the described characteristics. However, a consultation of collaborative air traffic tracking platforms was carried out. No trace of an aircraft compatible with the description was identified. This absence may be explained by the possible deactivation of the transponder of the affected aircraft, or by the inherent limitations of the coverage and comprehensiveness of the data provided by these free-access services. The case is classified as "B", probable observation of an aircraft.
