Summary: Observations of the passage of a polygonal and sparkling object in the sky; rotation on its transverse axis: probable observations of a light debris (unknown origin).
--- seg-8e368bf03f41319f1144d0c4 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On April 18, 2016, around 5:40 PM, two witnesses (T1 and T2) at a bus stop observed a spinning, sparkling object in the sky. Both witnesses described the PAN as square in shape. T1 estimates the object's trajectory as straight, while T2 describes it as descending. T1 filmed the observation, which lasted approximately two minutes for T1 and 13 minutes for T2. Both witnesses initially thought it might be debris from a plane. No other testimonies were collected. The strangeness of this case is low, mainly due to the regular rotation of the PAN on its transverse axis (as detailed in the investigation report). The witnesses observed two objects. The information provided by the witnesses is detailed but of limited reliability, as they acknowledged difficult observation conditions ("we were in a bad position to judge," "the sun preventing us from clearly distinguishing the PAN, which was only visible through the occasional white light flash it emitted in our direction"). This could also be due to discrepancies or contradictions in the descriptions of the trajectories. Fortunately, we have a video of object #2, on which we focus our analysis, considering that the statements do not contradict the possibility that object #1 has the same explanation. The physical and dynamic characteristics of the PAN observed and filmed (for object #2) by the witnesses resemble those of light debris, subject to the combined effects of gravity and an initial unknown/created rotation by the general wind or its local turbulences (in an urban environment), combined with a shape effect favorable to the object (asymmetry, helical effect, etc.). The object's descent itself creates a relative wind that can create or contribute to this rotation, as the witness's drawing indicates a horizontal rotation axis. This same rotation and the induced lift and drag effects by the wind can cause a deviation of 20 to 40 degrees from the wind's trajectory. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as 'B', a probable observation of light debris (unknown origin).
