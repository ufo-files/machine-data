Summary: Observation of the movements in the night sky of a swarm of orange lights: probable misidentification with birds.
--- seg-85d90d14dd694dba8e540a2d [needs-review] ---
**Description:** On the evening of Saturday, October 10, 2020, around 8:20 PM – 8:25 PM, a witness, outside with their spouse, observed a swarm of small orange lights that darted and spun around in stationary circular patterns, resembling phosphorescent birds. A few isolated individuals (2-3 possibly) approached the swarm from the southwest and then departed. The well-lit group began moving towards the north, northeast/east, decreasing in intensity and disappearing into the few clouds in the distance. No photos, videos, or sketches are associated with the testimony. Only one testimony about this phenomenon was collected. Two hypotheses were considered: Thai lanterns or birds. The observation is indeed consistent with a movement of Thai lanterns:

- **Appearance:** Small lights of orange, white, and phosphorescent colors.
- **Motion:** "Darted and spun around."
- **Duration and Disappearance Mode:** The observation duration (2 minutes) is consistent with the flight time of lanterns, and the disappearance of these lanterns could be caused by the gradual extinction of burners. For a witness, this extinction might mistakenly be attributed to UFOs entering the clouds, while these clouds are usually located higher than the maximum altitude of lantern flight.
- **Observation on a Saturday evening.**

However, three elements contradict this hypothesis:

- No request for authorization to release lanterns was made at the prefecture level.
- No other reports (neither to the GEIPAN nor online) despite a significant number of lanterns in a densely populated area, which should have been observed by others.
- The direction of movement towards the north, northeast/east.

An important element in characterizing a lantern phenomenon is wind. Indeed, lanterns are only released in light winds and they follow the direction and wind speed. Data from three weather stations (St Germain des Près, Jussieu, and Montsouris), located 5-6 km east of the observation site, report average winds of 6-11 km/h, which is consistent with light winds (see weather document). The PANs moved towards the northeast/east, while the three weather stations west of the observation point reported winds directed respectively towards the east, southeast, and south, which might seem contradictory (see Google Earth document). Given that the winds are light, and we are in an urban area, the wind direction and speed can vary significantly. It could be argued that the apparent inconsistency between the weather data and the observation is not significant, as it is not relevant to refer to the wind direction given the light wind speed and the urban location.

Regarding birds, the observation is indeed compatible:

- **Appearance:** Small lights of orange, white, and phosphorescent colors.
- **Trajectory:** "Darted and spun around" and moved together towards the northeast/east.
- **Second observation by the witness at the same time the following week:** This second observation, very similar, was identified as birds (see witness letter).
- **Highly populated urban area** with numerous public lights that could explain the witness's appearance.
- **Disappearance could be due to changes in public lighting related to the areas crossed.**
- **The League for the Protection of Birds of Île-de-France, contacted, suggests it is a group of starlings (or even ring-necked parakeets) that were chased by scaring operations.** However, the mayor's office of Charenton reported that no scaring operations had been conducted. It is still possible to assume that it is a gathering of birds present in the region, outside of any scaring operation, considering the proximity to the Bois de Vincennes. The GEIPAN classifies this observation as B, a probable misidentification with birds.
