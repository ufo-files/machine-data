Summary: Long observation of a luminous phenomenon with colored lights in the night sky: mistaken for Capella.
--- seg-603cc1a0f9d27d6cfa7d2eb6 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 25, 2017, at 10:40 PM, a witness on their terrace was intrigued by the presence of a white, yellow, and blue light in the sky at a low altitude. No noise or trail was observed. The witness filmed their observation with a camcorder and, upon reviewing the footage, discovered an object with peculiar shapes. The witness ended their observation around 1:10 AM, with the object still in the sky to the north. No other testimonies were collected regarding this phenomenon. The witness observed the star Capella, which was indeed at the indicated positions in the sky at the beginning of the observation (10:40 PM, 15 degrees altitude, and 320-degree azimuth, or N.O.) and then moved downward, as described by the witness, towards the north at the end of the observation (1:00 AM, 4 degrees altitude, and 345-degree azimuth, nearly north). Notably, the majority of the witness's perceived strangeness, and what likely led them to provide this testimony, resulted from what they saw through the camcorder's viewfinder or on the camera recording. Clearly, the trajectory and the zero-star effect (the color and flickering effect is classic for a star seen at a low elevation through atmospheric turbulence) indicate that the object being filmed with the camera is indeed Capella. Furthermore, the witness does not report the presence of a star next to the PAN while the sky was clear, and Capella could only be visible with its nearly identical location to that given for the PAN. Under these circumstances, the GEIPAN does not dedicate additional resources to explaining the strangeness generated by the camera (as it was not seen with the eyes). Indeed, experience shows that one always arrives at an explanation, but with efforts that would be unjustified in this case. For the same reason, the GEIPAN no longer considers testimonies that only pertain to the perceived strangeness through a photo or video without direct eye contact. This case is intermediate because it started with a classic, perceived strangeness with the naked eye that needed to be investigated. In particular, strong streetlight lights in the video can already cause mysterious reflections in the dark parts of the image. It is also known that a strong zoom on a star can produce all sorts of shapes due to focusing malfunction, geometric and radiometric quality of the device, with a probable accenting effect from a camera (of lower quality than a regular camera) aimed at a double star (Capella). Finally, it also happens that a witness zooms in on a light other than the one that initially intrigued them, as a strong zoom framing is not easy for anyone. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Misidentification with Capella.
