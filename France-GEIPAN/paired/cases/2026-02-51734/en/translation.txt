Summary: In-flight observations of the appearance and disappearance of intense lights: observation of Starlink® flares.
--- seg-13cca8dafcef9d39296fec34 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On March 3, 2026, at 22:10 UTC, following a reconnaissance mission, the crews of two Mirage 2000D reported a luminous phenomenon (PAN). The signal is characterized by three sources of extreme brightness, presenting an intensity comparable to high-powered landing lights. These sources manifest at irregular intervals, every minute, for 15 to 20 seconds, and persist overall for 30 minutes, traversing the trajectory between the initial zone near Clermont-Ferrand (63) and the arrival zone near Nancy (54). A single testimony is recorded. Only one testimony will be collected. Of moderate strangeness and good consistency* (4 witnesses, but a single testimony (T1), no visual of PAN), this case turns out to be an observation of Starlink® flares (see the investigation report). The witness (T1) description details an observation of this type: numerous appearances at regular intervals of 15 to 20 seconds, intense brightness comparable to aircraft headlights, a consistent appearance angle throughout the observation, close to the horizon. A recreation of the celestial situation using Stellarium® reveals that the configuration enabling this brief illumination by the setting sun for the observer is confirmed at the time of the PAN observation (the sun is set at -45° below the horizon, localized flares at 2 to 5 degrees of elevation). More details are provided in the investigation note GEIPAN "FLARES" OF STARLINK® SATELLITES (OR "FLARING SATELLITES"). The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'A', observation of Starlink® flares. *CONSISTENCY: According to the GEIPAN's criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.
