Description: On Wednesday, April 4, 2018 at 9:00 PM, a witness is intrigued by the presence in the night sky of a very bright and stationary PAN. The witness describes a flickering, circular object with what appear to be long filaments. They observe the phenomenon for 40 minutes and record a video. The object disappears, hidden by a cloud. Three family members witness the event, but only one account is collected. The testimony is clearly an observation of Venus. The witness themselves refers to Venus. They note that its intensity seems higher and they can distinguish filaments around it. Verification shows that in the direction of the given observation, "towards the Montchat neighborhood," or approximately 280°/290° azimuth, Venus is indeed present at around 280° and a height of ~10° (the witness indicates 10° to 15°). This proximity is undeniable. If the PAN was not Venus, it should have been noticed by the witness in close proximity to the PAN at some point during this lengthy observation, as the clouds in the direction of the PAN are transient (as shown in the account and photo). It is common for intense observations of Venus to create the impression of ray jets, here filaments. Consequently, the GEIPAN case is classified as A: Venus.
