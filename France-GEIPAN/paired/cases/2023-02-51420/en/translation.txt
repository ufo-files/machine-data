Description: On February 14, 2023, the witness is at his home. While closing his shutters at 01:30 AM, he spots in the sky a bright, sparkling light that appears larger than a star. He initially thinks it's an airplane, but after 10 minutes, and especially upon returning to his observation point around 03:00 AM, he realizes the phenomenon is still present in the same location. The sparkling is intense, clear, and regular. The witness notes rapid color changes, first red, then white, and finally green. The witness films and photographs the PAN. On the video and photos, colors do not appear, and the PAN remains blurry, likely due to strong zoom lenses. Having resided in this commune for over 50 years, the witness accurately identified the position and orientation of the phenomenon. Only one testimony will be collected. The case is well-documented by the testimony, video, and two pictures. The initial strangeness largely stems from the PAN randomly changing colors and appearing to be a single element, which is contradicted by the investigation (see the investigation report). The color change effects are due to the atmosphere and temperature, generating various phenomena (diffraction, turbulence, refraction, absorption, diffusion). The intrinsic strangeness, what the witness felt, is not quantifiable, but we understand the reasons for his questioning during the observation and, especially, upon watching the recorded images. The file analysis leads to the conclusion that the witness observed two different stars: Betelgeuse and Procyon, in two distinct phases. The observation timeframes, the astronomical positions of Betelgeuse and Procyon during these two phases, and the nearby building that obscured Betelgeuse's setting, all legitimately questioned and challenged the witness who thought he had observed a single fixed point in the clear sky. The characteristics of the PAN support this conclusion, particularly: - The strong sparkling causing rapid color changes - Its punctual shape, similar to that of a star - Its immobility. The GEIPAN is classified as A: Observation of stars Betelgeuse and Procyon.
