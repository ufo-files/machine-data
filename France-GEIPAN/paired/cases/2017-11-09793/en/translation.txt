Description: On the evening of November 9, 2017, around 10:25 PM, a witness from their home noticed a peculiar luminescent phenomenon in the night sky. The witness describes "a glowing cloud followed by a 'beam' of light." The observation lasted until 11:00 PM, with the "glowing cloud" gradually fading out. The witness had time to take photographs. No other testimonies were collected. The witness observed a rare optical phenomenon called a light pillar, or solar pillar, or luminary pillar. This natural phenomenon occurs when sunlight or moonlight reflects off ice crystals suspended in the atmosphere. It's visible when the Sun or Moon is near the horizon. It also occurs with artificial lights such as street lamps. Indeed, the witness mentions "the reflection of city lights." However, the ground-based light source must be quite distant, as it's not directly visible. Street lighting provides light sources that can reflect off suspended ice crystals in the air on cold days (the Carpentras weather station reported 1.7°C that day) or in clouds, creating light pillars. In this case, it's the combination of a natural phenomenon (flat ice crystals located lower than usual due to cold weather at high altitude) and artificial with the presence of strong, point-source ground lights that produced this photometeor. The light sources could be from the city of Salon-de-Provence, located in the southwest direction, as the phenomenon. For more information, consult the Light Pillar article. Consequently, the GEIPAN is classified as A, as an observation of an atmospheric light pillar.
