Summary: Observations of a bright, fixed light in the sky, then in motion: very likely observations of an airplane seen from the front, approaching Le Havre-Octeville Airport.
--- seg-0e7f0868c51fc6b887c59456 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On November 22, 2012, around 9:15 PM, a witness is intrigued by the presence in the overcast sky of a bright, fixed light. Then around 9:24 PM he realizes that the light is moving slowly and horizontally towards Fontenay. The witness's mother also saw the fixed light "like a projector" but did not see any flashing lights. The light disappears, hidden by the roofs of the houses. No further testimony will be collected. The gendarmerie conducted an in-depth investigation with the military air traffic control, nearby police stations, the Octeville airport, METEOFRANCE... and opts for the possibility of observing an aircraft coming from Lyon to land at Le Havre-Octeville by approaching the runway from the northeast, 3/4 into the south wind. The radar traces do not indicate this aircraft, but these traces are only given for an area less than 10 km west of Montivilliers; the aircraft coming from the southeast must pass through the aeronautical marker LHO, located in Mannevillette (LHO IAF 49°35’51.1” N - 000°10’51.8” E), well to the north of this trace area: it will have bypassed, to the east and then to the north, the selected area for the radar trace (see attached map). A plane seen from the front at low altitude is visible from 20 km, or even 30 km and more, depending on weather conditions; it appears as a single bright point because the position lights and strobe lights, much less powerful than the narrow-beam landing lights, are not visible at that distance. It is noted on that day a great horizontal visibility of 60km, despite the dense cloud cover as seen on Météociel. These rare weather conditions can explain why witnesses typically do not notice this regular commercial aircraft. It is also possible that the pilot chose that day a particular route or altitude. The same aircraft, when viewed from the side, then shows its position lights (red or green depending on the side) and its strobe lights (red and/or white depending on the flight phase), and the landing lights, oriented towards the front of the aircraft, are almost no longer visible. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": very probable observation of an airplane seen face-on, approaching the Le Havre-Octeville airport.
