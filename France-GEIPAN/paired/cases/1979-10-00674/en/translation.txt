Summary: Observations of a red, phosphorescent object above a forest that changes shape before disappearing: mistaken for the rising Sun in a cloudy sky.

Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case previously classified as D and named THIVARS (28) 25.10.1979 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On October 25, 1979, around 7:30 a.m., a driver and her two passengers were traveling on D137 towards Chartres. Near the entrance to the town of Fresnay-le-Comte, they spotted a glowing object above a forest that intrigued them. This object was red in color, not blinding but rather phosphorescent (T2). According to the witnesses, it had the shape of an isosceles trapezoid (T1) or a bowl (T2). No particular noise was heard during the observation, which lasted between 15 and 20 seconds. The object rose, changing its shape (from rectangular to a simple line) before completely disappearing. Two statements will be filed with the gendarmerie. The details provided by the witnesses suggest a misidentification with the rising sun, as the PAN is described as phosphorescent and compared to a sunset: "like when the sun is very red in the evening" (T2), "this shape had the color of the sun when it's red and not blinding, like when it rains the next day" (T1). It has been verified that the sun was perfectly aligned with the direction of the observation and was rising (see investigation notes). The presence of cloud cover very likely prevented the witnesses from identifying the sun. The same cloud cover, with the probable presence of small gaps or clearings, all in motion due to the wind (greater than 15 km/h), can indeed perfectly explain the change in shape of the PAN as well as its disappearance, as the sun gradually disappeared behind a cloud. It is also worth noting that the PAN "was emitting a clarity that lit up the forest," which is consistent with the sun. The witnesses do not mention seeing the sun at any point, even though it was rising on the horizon, exactly in the direction they indicate. The perfect consistency between the true position of the sun and the location and description of the PAN leaves no doubt about the misidentification. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (driving, surprise, poor weather conditions). Consequently, this case of low strangeness is classified by the GEIPAN as PAN A: observation related to a misidentification with the sun at sunrise.
