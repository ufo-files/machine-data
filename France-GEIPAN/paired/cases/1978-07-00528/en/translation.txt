Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish all its archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) and now being re-examined in order to be more relevant in their conclusions. This observation case previously classified as D and named PRECY SOUS THIL (21) 18.07.1978 is part of a recent set of cases re-examined. Around 4 a.m. in the night of July 17th to 18th, 1978, a camper observed a stationary luminous phenomenon in the sky at a low altitude. The witness described an orange ball shaped like a saucer or an upside-down plate. The witness took three photographs before the phenomenon slowly and then quickly disappeared towards the south. No noise was heard during the observation. The gendarmerie did not collect any other testimony in the area about this phenomenon, and the development of the photographs yielded nothing. The color of the PAN (fire, hence orange) and the fact that the PAN was low on the horizon, towards the southwest, are quite characteristic of a setting star (see investigation notes). Setting aside for a moment the final movement description towards the south, a solid explanation for the PAN can be given. The Moon is present (verified with Stellarium) at an azimuth of 240°, which is precisely the direction of Dompierre-en-Morvan (viewed by the witness from Lucenay ~240°), a direction attributed to the initial phase of the PAN. The hypothesis of a mistake with the Moon would be strengthened by the fact that its angular height of 2° is perfectly consistent with a description of a low-altitude, fire-colored PAN (the color of a setting red moon). Additionally, cloud formations were present at that time. A portion of the Moon through cloud gaps can take on any shape, and thus the shape described by the witness. The final movement towards the south is very poorly documented. It remains compatible with the Moon mistake in the sense that the cloud gap may have moved towards the south or, at least, had a perceived movement "towards the south" when observed from a 240° bearing. However, the wind was generally towards the east that day (no available indication at 4 a.m.), and it could indeed break down for the witness into a radial movement of closure from the 240° bearing (not perceptible for the witness) and an orthogonal movement to the 240° line of sight, which the witness perceives as going towards the south. The very strong consistency between the true position of the Moon and the position and description of the PAN gives a good probability to the Moon explanation, and the little known about the final mobility of the PAN remains compatible with this Moon explanation. It is not the witness's visual perception that is in question, but the interpretation the witness makes of his observation through his feeling (fatigue, surprise). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of low strangeness is classified as PAN B, a probable observation related to a mistake with the Moon.
