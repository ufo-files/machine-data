Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This previously classified observation case is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On October 10, 1997, around 9:15 PM, a witness (T1) observed a static, spherical glow in the sky to the East outside their residence. The witness then describes the various movements of this PAN. The witness's spouse (T2) approaches and observes the unusual movements of the glowing PAN accompanied by a deep rumble. The witnesses lose sight of the PAN as it moves behind the roof of their home. No other testimony was collected by the Gendarmerie. With an average strangeness and consistency (two witnesses, but a contradiction in their testimonies, no photos), the case appears to be a probable misidentification with a Hercules C-130 transport aircraft taking off from a nearby air base (see the investigation report). The points supporting this conclusion are as follows: - Initial observation of a strong, yellowish-orange spherical light corresponding to landing lights, visually confused because observed at a very great distance; - Alternating periods of apparent stops and movements corresponding to the various aircraft movements: stair-step ascents and heading corrections; - Variations in the apparent size of the light, caused by a less or more perfect alignment of the aircraft's movement axis with the witness's line of sight; - Apparent disappearance when these two axes form a certain angle, as the aircraft turns; - Anti-collision lights are not illuminated, which is possible on C-130 and explains why the witnesses did not mention any flickering of the observed lights; - The standard navigation lights at the wingtips, red and green, are not visible as they are obscured by other nearby lights of the same color as the landing lights; - The noise, although described differently by the two witnesses, is peculiar in the case of the C-130 and does not resemble what they are accustomed to hearing from fighter and commercial aircraft regularly overflying the area. It is described as "muffled" by both witnesses, which corresponds well to the noise produced by a C-130; - A second observation of a ramp consisting of several lights, described by the witnesses as orange-pink or reddish, corresponding to what is possible to observe for the various lights equipping the C-130, which can appear more or less aligned depending on the observation angle and distance; - Finally, the area and more broadly the entire Eure department are regularly overflown by all types of military aircraft, with the proximity of the Evreux BA105 (nearby) and the Orleans base (100 km away). An aircraft taking off from one of these bases (rather from the Orleans base as the Evreux base had been consulted and did not list any flights) could have been a different type and therefore stranger (e.g., Hercules C-130) for the witnesses while still being plausible. The case is classified as "B": probable misidentification with a military transport aircraft.
