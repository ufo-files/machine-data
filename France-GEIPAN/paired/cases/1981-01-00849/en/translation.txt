Summary: Observation of the landing and takeoff of an object that left traces on the ground.
--- seg-1fac5eee3fc2e6d5ae04ab3d [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 8, 1981, the witness was on the upper terrace of his property, constructing a small masonry building to protect a pump. Suddenly, he heard a whistling sound and saw a craft descend towards the ground, land a few dozen meters away from him, and take off again after a few seconds on a vertical trajectory before disappearing at high speed in the witness's eyes. Below the craft, the witness saw prominent circular shapes that he identified as hatches with feet. The gray, zinc-like craft did not emit flames or smoke and measured approximately 2.5 meters in diameter by 1.70 meters in height, it was circular with a thicker band along its diameter; the "feet" were the size of a mason's bucket. The entire observation lasted between 30 and 40 seconds. When the witness approached the landing site, he found a trace about 2 meters in diameter on the ground with "scrape" marks at certain points along its circumference. The Gendarmerie intervened on January 9, interviewed the witness, observed the trace on the ground, and collected soil samples from within the trace as well as wild alfalfa plants progressively from the trace to about 10 meters away. A GEPAN investigation took place on February 17. The traces were still visible, and a new series of alfalfa and soil samples was collected. Soil analyses were conducted by four different laboratories, each using different techniques and providing a complementary perspective. The synthesis of these analyses revealed three main results:

• A significant compaction of the soil indicating the presence of a heavy object that left a light deposit of iron and iron oxide, as well as phosphates and zinc, which could be from friction against the soil of a paint coating;

• The black, reddish-brown residue found on the trace could be combustion residue;

• A significant (but below 600°) heating was also detected.

Biochemical analyses of the alfalfa samples were performed by a biochemistry laboratory at INRA. They showed multiple degradations of the plants correlated with their distance from the trace. The causes of these degradations were not determined, but the hypothesis of an intense electric field can be considered. The analyses conducted did not allow for a quantitative estimation of the phenomenon or provide proof of the witness's account, which remains unique, although his behavior does not cast doubt on his credibility. An important, unusual phenomenon occurred that day, and the investigation did not determine its origin.
