**Case Description:**

On October 6, 2010, at 8:17 PM, two individuals observed from the exterior of a building at the Golfech nuclear power plant a phenomenon flying overhead. It was described as a black triangle with flickering white lights at its ends and a flickering red light at its center. The phenomenon flew at low altitude in a dark, cloudy sky. No noise was heard. One of the witnesses (T2) recorded a video using his cell phone before the object gradually disappeared in the direction of Saint Loup. A preliminary investigation by the GEIPAN on November 22, 2012, concluded the likely observation of an aircraft passing. A new investigation was conducted in October 2013 due to the possibility of accessing the Golfech power plant site. Several hypotheses (EDF maintenance equipment, plane, drone, hoax, PAN D) were considered (see investigation report). This complex observation case posed a methodological challenge for the GEIPAN due to several factors significantly weakening the case:

- The two witnesses interpreted the phenomenon very differently.
- T2 made a statement unfavorable to the witness in his report to the gendarmes.
- The video, considered evidence by T1, turned out to be a streetlight video. Besides being obviously not evidence, this video may have influenced the witness's testimony retroactively if the witness unconsciously aligned their account with the video's depiction instead of their own memory of the phenomenon.
- T2 appears easily influenced when comparing their gendarmerie statement to their interview with C Albiero.

Given these conditions, the value of their statements is uncertain, while their testimony cannot be ignored. Therefore, the GEIPAN assigned a consistency value of 0.6 to this case, lower than the 0.7 typically given to a single witness.

Additionally, the aircraft hypothesis initially proposed by the GEIPAN has several inconsistencies (see investigation report); it would only be acceptable if significant distortions of the testimonies and the existence of an undetected low-altitude aircraft by radar or central surveillance were considered.

The GEIPAN considers certain the passage of three flickering white lights arranged in a triangle around a flickering red light. The black triangle reported by the witnesses is likely the result of a common optical illusion known as the "contour" or "shape" illusion: the actual object could be a set of four independent light sources or a much larger craft than the triangle described by the witnesses (aircraft or other hypothesis).

The altitude is undeterminable, as in all cases of night-time light point observations: the witnesses estimated very different altitudes, which is entirely normal; it is very likely that the true altitude of the luminous phenomenon is even different.

The strangeness of the phenomenon was judged as average to high (0.55) because the closest hypothesis (aircraft) has several elements inconsistent with the observation.

This case presents average consistency and strangeness, at the boundary of categories B, C, and D: a slight deviation in the strangeness or consistency rating could place it in neighboring classes. Due to this sensitivity, the GEIPAN sought the advice of the GEIPAN expert panel at a meeting in Paris on April 12, 2016. The opinions were divided:

- Four experts voted for a category D1 classification, considering the luminous phenomenon overhead the power plant was unexplained.
- Eight experts voted for a category C classification, considering the case file contained too many inconsistencies to provide a consolidated opinion.

Therefore, the GEIPAN classified this case as category C due to insufficiently reliable information.
