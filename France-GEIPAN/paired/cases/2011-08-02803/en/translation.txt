Description: In its publications on the GEIPAN site, it includes old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined for the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D1 and named TOULOUSE (31) 24.08.2011 is part of a recent set of cases re-examined (see the investigation report). On Wednesday, August 24, 2011, around 3:32 a.m., a witness was looking at the stars behind a window. He suddenly observed the silent passage to the south of Toulouse of a dark triangular shape. The witness was surprised by the lighting under the PAN, consisting of "several orange glowing rectangles forming ramps all around the craft." The observation duration was 5 seconds according to the witness, and the phenomenon was quickly obscured by the urban landscape. No other testimony was collected. The observation does not allow for a sufficiently probable hypothesis. The shape and trajectory of the PAN could correspond to a military or business aircraft, but the lights do not match a known configuration, and the absence of radar trace is difficult to explain by a very low altitude as the witness would have then perceived a noise, even behind windows. The new investigation brought nothing in terms of hypotheses since the witness refused the investigator's request for contact. The initial consistency of the case was barely sufficient to support the level of strangeness and the unexplained nature of the case: - average information based mainly on a sketch, no photo, only one witness; - correct reliability (despite the fact that the witness did not follow up on the initial request to give a statement at the gendarmerie). The witness's refusal, with knowledge of the case's unexplained nature, to respond to GEIPAN's requests can certainly have legitimate causes, but in the absence of details, it's an element that alters the perception of reliability. Consistency no longer supports the level of strangeness and the unexplained nature of the case. GEIPAN classifies the case as C: unexploitable due to a lack of reliable information.
