**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions. Through new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This previously classified observation (D) named CHABEUIL (26) 01.01.1977, which was initially reviewed in 2011, included an attached sky map indicating the presence of Venus in the observed sky sector. The new conclusion emphasizes elements that contributed to the oddity of the case at the time and confirms the astronomical observation.

On January 1st, 1977, between 7:30 PM and 7:45 PM, two individuals were driving on a communal path in the Serre neighborhood of Valence (26). During their round trip on this road, they were intrigued by a bright white light phenomenon at low altitude. The stationary PAN with a broad shape first rose rapidly and then disappeared in the south. No noise was heard during the lengthy observation. A third witness, found by gendarmes during the investigation, reported having seen nothing unusual that night and in his field, but described observing a PAN one year earlier. The observation is well-documented through photos and gendarmes' reconstructions. However, there are inconsistencies in the descriptions of the shape or movement of the PAN. Its quasi-stationary position for an extended period (at least 10 minutes separated by at least 15 minutes) suggests the observation of a star.

Regarding its appearance, some points in the testimonies describing movement or a broad shape may have seemed strange, leading to the rejection of this hypothesis during the initial investigation 40 years ago. However, the GEIPAN's experience allows these perceptions to be explained. The variation in light intensity, caused by the atmospheric veil shifting in front of the star, is logically interpreted by the witness as a (radial) change in distance (in their direction) of a PAN that would maintain a constant intensity and often be described as "lightning-fast," especially during its final escape and disappearance.

- Witness T1 states, "At that moment, the object moved at a dizzying speed towards the south."
- Witness T2 says, "After about a minute, this craft started to rise and decrease in size. This maneuver lasted about five seconds, and at that point, the fireball moved towards the south like a shooting star. I must add that when the craft took off, I had the impression that our car was moving backward."

It's notable that the south direction of movement matches the direction witnesses attribute to the PAN, supporting a perception of radial movement. Only T1 describes a movement that could suggest a non-radial trajectory with a position change in the sky, saying, "It moved at a dizzying speed towards the south while describing a curve of about 45 degrees." T2 indicates a size greater than that of a star, with projected shapes and rays. This also occurs during the observed observation of very bright stars like Venus or Sirius near the horizon, with the most common element (also observed here) being variable colors and twinkling.

It's worth noting that the observation was made in the presence or proximity of fog banks, which enhance atmospheric dispersion effects on lights from the star. Finally, T1 does not distinguish any shape effect beyond a broad glowing point that is generally round, and both witnesses often refer to a star to describe the PAN.

When we find (see the attached reconstruction based on gendarmerie photos) that Venus is exactly (within one or two degrees) in the direction of the PAN (234 degrees, not straight south as thought by the witnesses), Venus has a descending trajectory until it's a few degrees above the top of the hill at the end of the observation. The strong brightness with ray dispersion due to the fog has likely led T2 to believe the PAN was on the hill, as T1 always placed the PAN above the ground. The final disappearance of the PAN is due to the passage of a thicker fog or cloud.

Perceptions of intelligence in the PAN's (takeoff) reaction to the witnesses' headlights are purely coincidental. Indeed, T2 specifies that the "reaction" of the PAN took two minutes ("I think our car's headlights bothered it because two minutes after the headlights, the craft took off"). Venus is exactly in the direction of the PAN. The deviations from the standard star appearance are consistent with those recorded during numerous observed observations of Venus or Sirius near the horizon, and what wouldn't be (non-radial movement) is noted by only one witness (T1), not the other.

In conclusion, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Observation of Venus.
