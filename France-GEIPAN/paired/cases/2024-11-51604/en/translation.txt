Description: On November 24, 2024, early evening, from the first floor of his home in the commune of BURNHAUPT-LE-BAS (68), the witness observed an atypical luminous phenomenon in a sky with near-total darkness. The observation focused on the eastern horizon, towards the Black Forest mountain range (Germany). Initially, four aligned light sources were detected. One of them went out as the observer briefly left to get recording equipment. Upon his return, the configuration had changed: two new sources appeared to the north (to the left of the initial field). One presented a strong intensity and apparent stationarity, while two other mobile lights quickly converged towards this fixed source before going out synchronously. Attention then shifted to the residual static element located in the background of a copse. Analysis of the video document produced by the witness allowed for precise kinematics to be determined: the white light source was not stationary; it performed a slow horizontal translation towards the north (left), before initiating a vector inversion towards the south (right). After passing the azimuth of the copse, the object maintained a nearly horizontal constant trajectory until its definitive disappearance. Although the observation relies on a single witness, the speed with which the report was transmitted to GEIPAN and the exploitable quality of the video are decisive factors. The active cooperation of the observer allowed for precise information gathering. While the preliminary phases of recording are less documented, plausible explanatory hypotheses have been advanced in light of the context. Technical analysis of the visual support unambiguously identified the phenomenon appearing in the images, giving the case very good consistency. According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information collected for a testimony. The privileged hypothesis for the main phenomenon recorded at the end of the observation is aeronautical, despite the witness's knowledge of the usual traffic related to Basel-Mulhouse International Airport, located several dozen kilometers away. Indeed, meticulous comparison of the video images with trajectory data from Flightradar24 software reveals a perfect correlation. An airplane in approach phase to this airport was following a trajectory that, from the witness's perspective, faithfully reproduces the movements of the strong white light source. This correspondence is confirmed both spatially and temporally, the synchronized clock of the phone allowing for precise comparison with air traffic records. Two other planes present in the area also explain the two mobile lights that suddenly went out during the second phase of the observation. As for the four aligned light points observed on the horizon during the first phase, they likely correspond to the movements of snowplows on the slopes of the Windeck-Hinterzarten ski area. This station, located near the Feldberg, which is the Black Forest's highest peak at 1,493 meters, had in fact inaugurated its 2024-2025 season on Friday, November 22, 2024, only two days before the event. The observation of phase 3 was retained for the case classification. GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": observation of an airplane in approach to Basel-Mulhouse Airport.
