Description: On October 4, 1954, between 6:40 and 7:00 p.m., a 10-year-old girl (T1) riding her bicycle saw at a distance of 20 meters an "object" she initially mistook for a camping tent. According to the child, the oval-shaped object was 2 meters wide and 2.5-3 meters tall, with a conical top, and rested on a rectangular base. As she approached, a hatch (1 x 0.6 meters) opened. A man leapt from the object, closed the hatch behind him, and stood motionless. Of normal size, he had a "square-shaped" object on his head, piercing eyes, a long garment with long brown-red hair. As the man advanced, the frightened child ran away without looking back. Two other individuals reported at the same time and in the direction where the girl was located, one seeing "a bright red light object" that he didn't pay attention to, the other hearing a sound like an ice cream freezer that made her leave her home. The child's father and a neighbor went to the observation site the same day at 9:00 p.m. and noted that the grass was "slightly crushed in a circular manner." The gendarmerie visited the site on October 6 but was unable to make any observations. No further details were gathered during the investigation. In summary, for this case, there are four distinct facts:

- A single witness who saw an object and "a human being" near her path. She did not see the object take off.

- Simultaneously, a farmer saw in the sky a bright red light object.

- A third person heard for a long time a sound similar to that of an ice cream freezer.

- The child's father and a farmer found at 9:00 p.m. the same evening, at the observation site of T1, grass crushed in a circular manner.

No conclusive evidence links these four facts. Assuming they are linked, one can consider a hypothesis:

- Several elements seem compatible with a helicopter, perhaps a Bell 47, seen from the front (oval shape): the rotor mast, the hatch, the noise, the crushed grass, the pilot. The location ("on a grassy path leading to a small clearing") could be suitable for a landing. However, the rectangular base, the position of the hatch (would the witness have noted its width if it had been on the side?), and the bright red light of the object are less easily interpreted. This case appears of average strangeness as a simple hypothesis can more or less fit. Its consistency is weak, based mainly on a single witness (the child) and a brief observation.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as C due to lack of information and cross-checking.
