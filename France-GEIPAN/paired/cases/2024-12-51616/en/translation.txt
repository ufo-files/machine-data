Description: On the evening of December 23, 2024, around 8:30 PM, the witness exits his kitchen to call his dog, who is barking intensely and refusing to come inside, unlike other dogs already inside. Upon raising his eyes, he notices two red shapes in the sky, moving slowly towards him. One shape (A) hovers above a field, while the other (B) gently moves away. After some time, B returns to A, and both shapes remain stationary. The witness manages to calm his dog and observes the objects, which remain silent and motionless, before turning off his external lights to take two pictures. At this moment, B moves away, followed by A; the two shapes draw closer, merge, then quickly disappear into the sky. Greatly affected by the event, the witness recounts these facts, specifying the cold weather conditions and the absence of starlight visibility, his attention remaining focused on these silent, slow-moving, luminous objects. The consistency of the testimony is rated as average, with only one witness. The addition of two photos could have enhanced consistency and improved the quality of the information, but as these are not the original pictures (with technical data), their analysis was limited by the tools of the GEIPAN. According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Four hypotheses were explored (see the investigation report), but only one turned out, after the investigation, to be sufficiently probable with both testimonial and photographic data: the observation of celestial lanterns. This hypothesis is based on the following elements:

- Round shape, compatible.
- Color described as red, consistent with that of lanterns, although most often described by observers as more orange.
- Movements consistent with the light breeze.
- Observation date close to Christmas Eve: a release attempt is possible, followed by a larger release on the night of Christmas Eve.
- Observation duration compatible with the lifespan of lanterns.
- "Fusion" of PAN might only be apparent, the two lanterns potentially visually merged on the same observation axis.
- Disappearance of these lanterns as they are caught in a stronger air current.

An image processing analysis was conducted on the photographs taken by the witness. It was not successful on these non-original pictures. The witness, when asked to provide the originals, did not respond. The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as "B": probable observation of celestial lanterns.
