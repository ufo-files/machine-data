Description: Recent testimony (11/10/2012) of an old observation. A witness observed with binoculars on October 9, 2010 at 1:20 a.m. a luminous object in the sky. The witness is surprised by the intensity of the light and the observation of glowing points of color. The astronomical chart, oriented according to the witness's observation direction (S-SO), shows the presence of the planet Jupiter, of magnitude -2.9, that is, brighter than all the stars in the sky. From 1:20 a.m. to 2:20 a.m., it moves westward from azimuth 193° to 220° and descends from 40° to 36° in elevation. The witness indicates the object in the SO (South + 45° West: 225°), very bright, especially in comparison to the stars that were only barely visible. A southeast wind, quite humid, clears the background of the sky, masks the least bright stars, and causes intense twinkling: this probably explains the changes in color from green to white to red observed by the witness. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B," a probable observation of Jupiter. The deviations of around 20° between the observer's indications and the astronomical position leave uncertainty that does not allow this case to be classified as A.
