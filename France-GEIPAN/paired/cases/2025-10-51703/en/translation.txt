Summary: Observation in the night sky of a luminous phenomenon composed of several parts and moving in a straight line before a gradual extinction: observation of the degassing of a stage of a Falcon 9 rocket.
--- seg-d24aef91c471bc331641936f [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Wednesday, October 29, 2025 at 7:28 PM, the witness was driving on D764 between Sizun and Landerneau, in Finistère. Under a perfectly clear sky with no nearby sound sources, he notices an unusual bright point of light whose brightness surpasses that of a star. To deepen his observation and take pictures, he stops at the intersection of D764 and Impasse Kernévez La Cage, in the commune of Ploudiry. The map provided by the witness, specifying his exact position and the trajectory of PAN, is available in Annex 1. The witness describes: • a white, slightly blue-tinted glowing point surrounded by a diffuse halo; • Two less intense beams oriented respectively upward and downward; • Within the upper beam, a grayish, elongated "cigar" shape reveals darker areas; • a straight-line movement from northwest to southeast, at approximately 45° elevation; • stable brightness, then gradual fading, over an estimated total duration of two minutes. The phenomenon disappears after crossing the Moon's azimuth. A third-party witness, located in the immediate environment of the main observer, reports having seen the same phenomenon at the same time from his home, thus confirming the observation. The consistency* of the testimony is considered good: the main witness shows precision and provides a photographic snapshot whose technical exploitation, however, proved impossible. His account is nevertheless corroborated by a second eyewitness who did not testify before GEIPAN. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The perceived strangeness by the witness is strong. The residual strangeness, after investigation, appears weak for the GEIPAN. The main hypothesis under consideration is that of a fuel dump (degazage) from the second stage of a Falcon 9 rocket launched on the same day. Timeline and orbital dynamics: On October 29, 2025, SpaceX launched a Falcon 9 rocket from Florida at 4:35 pm UTC (5:35 pm French local time), carrying 29 Starlink® satellites into an inclined orbit at 53°. The satellite deployment occurs approximately 55 minutes after launch, around 17:30 UTC. The second stage then begins its second revolution around the Earth. The observation made by the witness from the Sizun (29450) sector occurs around 6:30 PM UTC, which is 1:55 hours after the rocket launch. This schedule corresponds to the period during which passivation operations, consisting of the final evacuation of residual fuel, are typically carried out. These degasifications produce very bright plumes at high altitude, visible from a great distance when the object is still illuminated by the Sun while the ground is in shadow, a condition met that evening. Concordance with other observations: The same phenomenon was reported in Ireland between 18:25 and 18:35 UTC, with very similar descriptions: bright point, diffuse halo, slow movement from west to east, no sound. Irish media (Irish Examiner, WeathÉire, Ireland Live) confirmed it was the second stage of the Falcon 9's debris, providing timelines consistent with the Breton observation. The observation sites in Brittany and Ireland are separated by approximately 600 km. At the speed of a rocket stage in low Earth orbit (~7.5 km/s), the passage over these 2 regions takes 1 to 2 minutes: it is thus the same orbital passage, seen successively from the 2 locations. Geometric concordance (visual aspect and direction): From Sizun, the phenomenon appears in the northwest, moves towards the north/northeast, then moves away towards the southeast. This apparent trajectory is precisely what is expected when an object in a 53° inclined orbit passes north of the witness: the trajectory is generally west to east, but its projection in the sky from Brittany translates into a NO to SE arc. In Ireland, located closer to the ground track, the object is described as moving from west to east, which is consistent. Luminosity aspect and morphology: The observed characteristics are perfectly compatible with a cryogenic gas plume illuminated by the Sun:

• white/bluish color: solar light scattering by crystals or droplets of ejected fuel;
• diffuse halo: cloud of crystals or droplets expanding;
• upper and lower beams: areas of higher concentration of the plume, aligned with the ejection direction;
• no noise: the orbital maneuver altitude being 150 kilometers or more;
• no flickering: the object is not an aircraft, but an unlit rocket stage, with only its plume visible;
• perceived dark areas: shadow cast or internal contrast within the plume, the stage itself being invisible at this distance. The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'A': observation of the degassing of a stage of a Falcon 9 rocket. This phenomenon has already been the subject of numerous testimonies at GEIPAN and a FALCON 9 and STALINK® news item.
