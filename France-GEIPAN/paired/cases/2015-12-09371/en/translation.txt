Description: On December 12, 2015, at 6:10 PM, two residents of VENDAYS-MONTALIVET (33) went outside to observe a flash of the Iridium satellite. At 6:14 PM, they saw a bright point in motion that dimmed and then reignited two or three times in succession. The observation of the PAN lasted thirty seconds. Only one testimony was collected. The description of the PAN as a glowing point similar to a star in motion, dimming and reigniting several times, is characteristic of observing an artificial satellite rotating very quickly on itself. Reconstructions on Calsky of satellite passes show that one of them can correspond both in timing and trajectory to SL-23 R/B, numbered according to international designations 41107 and 2015-074-C. Notably, its trajectory from southwest to east peaked at approximately forty degrees angular elevation, which is consistent with the witness's estimated altitude for the PAN (see the investigation report). Of average strangeness but sufficient consistency, this case turns out to be a rare misidentification with a Zenit rocket stage passing through an orbit change shortly after launch. The trajectory of the PAN as well as its description perfectly match those of the SL-23 R/B object, which was very well visible at the time of observation. Furthermore, a visual observation of this object confirmed its variable and spectacular nature. This case is classified A, misidentification with a satellite, involving the SL-23 R/B rocket stage numbered 2015-074-C.
