Description: On July 21, 2024, at 12:10 AM, from the terrace of his home in Saint-Martin-en-Bière (77), a witness (T1) observed for approximately five minutes a unique phenomenon described as a slightly blue-white glowing point, with a brightness comparable to a bright star or Venus, located to the southwest at an estimated height of around 45°. According to T1, this point made several rapid vertical movements, with intermediate stops, a partial ascent, a lateral shift of about 10°, and then a return before disappearing. The context was that of an approaching storm, with wind, lightning, and a few raindrops. His companion (T2) also saw the phenomenon but did not wish to report their observation to GEIPAN. The case consistency is judged to be average. The testimony is detailed, accompanied by a sketch, with the precise time, duration, direction, and dynamic description. However, although a video was made by T1, it was not provided as it was deemed unusable. The strangeness experienced is based on the very bright and punctual nature of PAN, its apparently sudden movements with successive stops, its intermittent blinking, and the absence of perceived noise. The witness found the observation singular and striking. The drone hypothesis appears to be the most plausible. The reduced appearance to a simple glowing point, the alternation between stationary phases and brief and rapid movements, as well as the intermittent blinking observed, are consistent with a nocturnal drone seen at a distance that it was impossible for us to estimate. This area, allegedly overflown, implies a maximum regulated flight height of 50 meters during the day. Nocturnal drone flights remain prohibited, except in special cases. Note that in complementary exchanges with the investigator, the witness himself indicates that the elements gathered to date mostly favor this hypothesis, without us being able to be sufficiently affirmative in light of surprising contextual elements (prohibited night flight, windy and stormy atmosphere, no perceived noise, etc.). The residual strangeness of this observation remains, however, moderate if we retain this hypothesis, and as a consequence, GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of the flight of a drone in the nocturnal sky.
