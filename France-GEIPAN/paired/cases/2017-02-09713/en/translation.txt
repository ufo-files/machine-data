Summary: Observation of a stationary white light phenomenon in the sky: probable observation of Venus.
--- seg-cb3983fedc1590a3deb32d41 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On February 14, 2017, at 7:56 PM, a witness in their car on the Paris ring road observed a stationary white glowing point in the sky. The witness took a photograph of the PAN with their smartphone. The observation lasted 2 to 3 minutes. As traffic allowed them to move forward, the witness lost sight of the PAN. Only one testimony was collected on this phenomenon. With low strangeness and average consistency (single witness, photo of the PAN, but testimony after several months), the case appears to be a very probable misidentification with Venus (see the investigation report). The description and photograph of the PAN strongly suggest this type of misidentification: the observed object is white and bright, stationary for several minutes, and the direction of observation corresponds very well to that of Venus, particularly bright on the evening of the observation. Only one doubt persists regarding the actual angular height of the PAN, which was anyway average, as the witness was inside their vehicle and thus had a reduced visual field in height. The absence of other urban elements besides a single streetlamp does not allow for the exact location of the witness, but it appears that the photograph of the PAN is likely tilted to the side. It is worth noting that the witness only mentioned the presence of a single white and bright object facing them, while they would logically have described two roughly identical objects (the PAN and Venus) and somewhat closer in the sky if Venus had not been the origin of the observation. However, the doubt about the actual angular height of the PAN does not allow for a formal validation of the explanatory hypothesis. The case is classified B, probable misidentification with Venus.
