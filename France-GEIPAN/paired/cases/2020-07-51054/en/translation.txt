Summary: Evening observation of the silent passage of a bright white cigar-shaped object: observation of an airplane.
--- seg-a54eedd07f3a526ff06cd88a [machine-unreviewed] ---
**Description:** On July 30, 2020, at approximately 9:15 PM, a group of friends was heading towards the banks of the Loire River to admire the river. Suddenly, their attention was drawn to the presence in the sky of an object in the shape of a cigar, very bright white. The object moved in a southerly direction and was lost from sight. No noise was heard. Perplexed, the three friends began to ask questions. Another person in the village, outside of this group, was able to film the phenomenon. Only one testimony was collected. The video is attached to the documents sent by the main witness. We studied the hypothesis of an observation of a commercial airliner (see the investigation report). After analysis, this hypothesis is confirmed and strengthened by the following elements:

- The witness observed only one object in their field of vision with the presence of an airplane confirmed (Flightradar24 map).
- Shape consistent with that of an airplane fuselage.
- White color, common for an airplane fuselage, and very bright due to solar radiation reflection on the fuselage surface.
- Movement (straight trajectory and constant speed) consistent with that of a commercial airliner.
- The Flightradar24 application indicated that an Airbus A320 airplane, originating from Roissy CDG and destined for Monastir (Tunisia), was at the exact time of the observation in the correct azimuth, at an altitude and an orthogonal projection distance from the witness's position, giving an observation angle very close to that reported by the witness.

At the altitude at which this airplane was flying (8200 meters), a stable, dry air, with a temperature not sufficiently cold (-40 to -45°C), could have been enough to prevent condensation trails from forming.

Several peculiarities noted by the witness can be explained by the following reasons:

- The visible absence of wings can be due to a combination of factors: observation angle, atmospheric diffusion effects, observation distance, relative positions of the witness, the sun, and the airplane, and the reflectivity of the involved surfaces, which is more pronounced on the cylindrical fuselage of the airplane than on its wings.
- The "crab-like" movement of the airplane, as perceived by the witness, is likely caused by a perspective effect, as this movement was not observed on the video.
- The observation of "luminescent balls" at the end, probably due to a brief, intense reflection of the sun on the airplane's window rows, creating a "rapid scrolling" effect, although only one movement was possible while the witness mentions at least two.
- The absence of noise: a significant distance between the witness and the airplane.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": observation of an airplane, specifically an A320 performing the Roissy CDG / Monastir (Tunisia) route.
