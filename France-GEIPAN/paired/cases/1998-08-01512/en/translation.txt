Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified observation case C is part of a recent set of cases re-examined (see the investigation report). On August 14, 1998 at 6:00 AM, a witness from their home observed a very bright large point in the sky. The PAN is generally stationary but appears to move very slowly: it seems quite low in the sky and heading east. The witness films their observation. During their deposition with the Gendarmerie, the witness states they had already observed the same phenomenon a few days prior. Only one testimony is collected, but the witness's spouse also saw the PAN. This phenomenon does not present any strangeness as the witness's description perfectly aligns with the hypothesis, where the planet in question, VENUS, is indeed located at the position of the PAN without the witness noticing or reporting it. The witness accurately describes the phenomenon (the stimulus being very real) and there is a second witness (but not interviewed). Additionally, there is a video supporting the statement. Current tools allow for a good reconstruction of the astronomical and meteorological situation, thus partially compensating for the weaknesses of the interrogation by retrieving essential and discriminating data. However, the video is not available, the second witness was not heard, and the gendarmes' interrogation is very brief and incomplete. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": very probable observation of the planet VENUS.
