Description: On October 13, 2015 at 12:40 a.m., a witness was disturbed by noise in the sky (jet aircraft overhead). He opens his blinds to see what is happening, that's when he sees above his building a "craft" with several triangular shapes and white dots. He immediately films the phenomenon. The wind is strong and cold that evening, the witness cannot stay outside for long. By watching his video, he discovers several craft, including one point that is more or less fixed. The witness provides his testimony regarding a GEIPAN in May 2019. No further testimony will be collected. The witness transmitted a video showing several bright points and an orange oval shape. The witness states that he only saw one PAN with his own eyes; the other bright points are only observed on the video. He reports clear, starry skies, but the weather station records from NÎMES GARONS on 13/10/2015 in the morning (see the weather document) indicate a very cloudy, humid sky with north/south winds of 20km/h with gusts up to 35km/h and limited visibility to 13 km. Thus, the very dense cloudiness can explain the appearance and disappearance of the PAN, which reduces the strangeness of the observation. It is normal for a late testimony to have this type of inconsistency, but it does not facilitate the identification of the phenomenon and thus reduces the consistency of the testimony. By the way, the witness states in the questionnaire that he does not remember the astronomical conditions, but he does specify "a completely clear, starry sky..." in the weather conditions of the questionnaire. Several hypotheses are being studied: - lantern hypothesis for the bright points: the period of October on weekdays, in the middle of the night with a wind between 20 km/h and 35 km/h does not support the lantern hypothesis, which could correspond to the bright points (but only seen on the film..). The North/South wind is not conducive to observing East/Southeast. - Military aircraft hypothesis: Before the observation, the witness heard aircraft noises, but not at the exact moment of the observation. This does not rule out the airplane hypothesis, as with the distance and according to the direction and altitude of an airplane, it is possible that there may be no noise at the observer's level. The short observation duration does not allow confirmation of the east/southeast trajectory reported by the witness for the sole PAN seen with their eyes. Given the late reporting of the observation, it is not possible to obtain confirmations of civilian or military aircraft passes through radar tracks. This hypothesis seems the most probable, but it cannot be confirmed. The strangeness of the observation cannot be confirmed, as the weather can explain the disappearances, and the airplane hypothesis is not incompatible, given that it was airplane noises that drew the witness to the window to observe the sky. Despite the video transmitted, the consistency of the case is not good, as the witness only saw the bright points on the video and not in real time; they could correspond to other optical phenomena or reflections in the lens. The antiquity of the testimony does not allow for additional information to confirm the airplane hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies this case as C: lack of reliable information.
