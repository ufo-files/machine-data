Summary: Low-altitude observation of a colorful glowing point that appears to blink: observation of Jupiter.
--- seg-36819464c83c8a7ae5134b4a [needs-review] ---
Description: On April 1, 2017, at 10:30 PM, a witness and two others were intrigued by the presence of a very bright point at low altitude in the night sky. This point seems to blink in different colors. Only one testimony is collected. The observation is consistent with that of a star; in fact, the entire testimony often refers to a star ("like a star but.."). What surprises the witness is the size and brighter light (or "very slightly brighter") and especially the change in color. In fact, that evening Jupiter was well in SE "above the CHU building" (as indicated for the PAN). Jupiter is very bright (Magnitude = -2.45, only Venus would be brighter in the sky) and at low elevation (between 10° and 20°), which offers possibilities for color variations due to increased atmospheric diffusion and turbulence at low elevation. Many witness reports GEIPAN result from this misidentification, see site GEIPAN. The fog (seen by the witness) or the clouds present that evening caused the disappearance of PAN. Note that the request for a testimony GEIPAN made to the other two people accompanying the witness did not result, which is often the case when the feeling of strangeness is not shared. As a result, the case is classified A as the observation of the planet Jupiter.
