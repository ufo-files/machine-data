Description: On August 17, 2024, witness (T1) and his partner, residing in the commune of Novalaise (73), were lying in bed, recently retired in the darkness (only the television was on), when they observed around 11:10 PM through the window a PAN in the sky. The sky was overcast with a light rain. T1 describes this PAN as silent and consisting of "a glowing disk separating into several rectangles" that swept across the sky in a regular, repetitive pattern. Its speed of movement was high, and its color white, brighter than the moon. After 2 to 3 minutes of observation and a movement towards the west, the PAN disappeared. T1 estimated its elevation at 45/50° and the angular distance traveled at approximately 40 to 50° from the sky. The consistency* of this case is good: although only one of the two witnesses completed a GEIPAN questionnaire, a video of the PAN was provided and could be analyzed (see investigation report).

CONSISTENCY *According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The hypothesis of observing a sky-tracer (a projector or laser aimed at the sky) proved probable for the following reasons:

- Overall round shape, common. Upon watching the video, individual shapes appear rather round despite T1's description (possibly obscured by perspective) of "rectangular" shapes.
- White color, typical.
- Absence of noise.
- Repetitive and regular movements, quite typical of such a device that automatically scans the sky.
- Variation in scanning speed, sometimes very fast, also typical of a sky-tracer.
- Observation on a Saturday night in summer, a time when sky-tracers are often used in a festive setting.

The investigation work did not identify the user of this device, which could be an individual or a more distant shopping center. Note that T1 did consider this hypothesis but rejected it due to the absence of a visible beam. Indeed, because of the rain, one might expect a beam to be visible, but T1's elevation estimate for the PAN was probably high: it is quite low, at the limit of landscape elements (trees, etc.) that could have obscured the beam. Other factors may contribute to the absence of a beam, such as distance and the light power of the sky-tracer source, which remain unknown.

The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as "B": probable observation of a sky-tracer.
