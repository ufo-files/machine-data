Summary: Multiple witness observations of a low-flying massive object: observation of the passage of a military C130 aircraft.
--- seg-b8ae5e8522ca8086d1fe78ac [needs-review] ---
Description: On the night of February 23 to 24, 2016, around midnight, several people in a car were intrigued by the passage at very low altitude of a large object with fixed or flashing lights of different colors. Witnesses describe a circular or triangular object moving slowly in the sky at the level of the treetops. Four witnesses give statements at the gendarmerie. It appears that witnesses T1, T2, and T3, who observed the same vehicle, and the isolated witness, observed a perfectly identified C130 aircraft (air traffic) that took off from Base 105 in Evreux heading southwest and then flew south of Conches-en-Ouche at a low altitude with a heading of approximately 210° (see the attached investigation report). Here we have a perfect illustration of what an airplane, in conjunction with other factors, can instill as fear, emotion, and contempt, especially when: - this airplane is singular (large military aircraft), causing fear in the witness from Conches-en-Ouche. The plane was too large and flew too slowly for the witness. Indeed, it is already known that Airbus 380 and 320 aircraft, during takeoff or landing, do not give the same impression of speed and altitude, even though everything is identical. - There is an illusion of stationary flight (for witnesses on RD830) resulting from a "pivot effect" when both the witness and the phenomenon are in motion, and the axis joining them pivots around a fixed point or area that is then perceived as the stationary point of the phenomenon. The impression of rotation is enhanced here by the presence of a fairly continuous first-plane of trees obscuring the identification. - The lighting is very particular, here the full Moon which is not seen directly but which, by reflection on the aircraft or diffusion in the tops of the trees, creates disk, cone, and halo light effects that can indeed contribute to the strangeness. The more comprehensive analysis is detailed in the attached document, including: - The radar trajectory of the C130 aircraft. - The cartographic reconstruction of the observations with the aircraft's trajectory, the positions of the witnesses, and their direction of observation. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: misidentification with an aircraft. More generally, the case illustrates the richness and fragility of human testimony in the face of an unrecognized phenomenon, under far from ideal observation conditions, and moreover, in a situation of emotion caused by the strangeness. Witnesses of the same vehicle estimate very different distances (300m to 10km), they see the aircraft in a primarily circular or round shape but with significant variations, while the witness in Conches-en-Ouche sees a triangle. The red lights can be either fixed or flashing according to witnesses. The driver of the car is certain he stopped the vehicle while the passengers claim otherwise. One can also question a possible influence effect between witnesses or "priming of strangeness" when a witness detects the phenomenon and shows it to others by qualifying it as OVNI.
