Summary: Observation of long threads moving in the sky and balls from which small glowing animals emerge: observation of ballooning (aerial locomotion method of some animals).
--- seg-432e6dac9e906394c2bc7196 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 23, 2015, at approximately 1:50 PM, a couple observed in the sky a peculiar phenomenon that intrigued them: three round balls from which burst out tufts of threads several dozen meters long. Balls emerged from thousands of tiny spiders that spun and were lit up like diodes. The witness took photographs. Only one testimony was collected. The white threads photographed by the witness have all the characteristics of spider silk threads known as "Virgin's silk" or "angel hair." These threads are the vehicles for minute young spiders that are thus carried by the wind. This phenomenon is called ballooning. The month of October is particularly conducive to this observation. It also requires fair and dry weather and a light breeze, which was the case that day: see the weather report. These extremely fine threads are only visible if they are charged with moisture and/or seen against the light. It is normal for them to become invisible when approached, either because the observer changes angle relative to the sun or because they dry up quickly. The provided photographs accurately depict this phenomenon. The multiple balls observed by the witness may be aggregations of spiders (see photo). In conclusion, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of migratory young spiders and their silk threads, commonly known as "Virgin's silk."
