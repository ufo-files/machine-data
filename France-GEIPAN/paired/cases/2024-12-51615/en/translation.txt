Summary: Sequential observations of the appearance and disappearance of bright points in the night sky: observation of Starlink® satellites on their definitive orbit (phenomenon of "satellite flaring").
--- seg-b3786c40c8efc2ecda64ee0a [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The witness, residing in Petite-Île, Réunion (974), observes from December 15, 2024, between 7:15 and 7:30 PM UTC, an unusual bright phenomenon in the sky heading south, over the ocean. Over four consecutive evenings, he reports the appearance of yellow to white light balls. On January 31, 2025, he submits a duly completed Technical Questionnaire (QT) along with several video recordings to the GEIPAN. The witness describes the phenomenon as follows: "A first ball generally appears above the sea, as if it's coming from far away, then grows as it systematically moves from west to east. Sometimes it's followed by a second ball of similar color and size, whose trajectory is generally more random." These lights make brief and rapid passes, disappearing towards southeast before reappearing a few moments later, up to twenty times during a single evening. The description and visual aspect of the phenomenon precisely match the observation of light flashes emitted by Starlink® satellites ("satellite flaring"). This phenomenon results from a brief sunlight illumination when satellites are in a stable position on their final orbit. The reflection of solar rays on their surface, in a specific illumination configuration, can be seen with the naked eye from Earth or an aircraft. The observation of these flashes emitted by Starlink® satellites is detailed in the GEIPAN investigation note, accessible via the following link: Starlink® satellite flashes. These observations are made under specific conditions: vertically aligned with the Sun, slightly above the horizon, with the Sun between 30 and 45 degrees below the horizon. These conditions are met during this observation.

• In this observation case, the PAN are visible towards the South and close to the horizon.

• The 30-degree elevation reported by the witness is overestimated as the analysis with the GEIPAN's image processing tool, IPACO®, of one of the videos showing 2 PANs, quantifies the elevations at 3.1 and 4 degrees (see Annex 1). The witness's overestimation seems partly due to the use of the zoom (x5).

• A check using Stellarium confirms that at the time of the observation, the Sun is also located towards the South, between 30 and 45 degrees below the horizon (see Annex 2). The consistency* of the testimony is considered very good, with observations over several days and videos.

*According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The case is classified as "A", observation of Starlink® satellites on their final orbit (satellite flaring phenomenon).
