**Description:** On June 5, 2015, at 9:10 PM, a witness observed from their 4th-floor balcony two round, dark-colored phenomena in a clear sky. The first PAN (unidentified flying object) appeared relatively stable, while the second moved slowly in a circular arc, centered around the first object. The witness filmed the phenomenon for a long time, then decided to drive closer but lost sight of the objects (the witness's videos will be uploaded to the site later due to a temporary technical issue). Only one testimony was collected. The discernible shapes in the videos strongly resemble two hot air balloons (or gas balloons) whose gondola does not appear optically separated from the balloon due to the distance. The dual-body aspect (balloon + gondola) is confirmed by a radiometric analysis (specialized software IPACO). The two partially reconstructed trajectories (from the video and ground references visible in it) are quite similar, except for a loss of angular height of one of the two shapes. These trajectories can at times give the illusion (the witness's perception) that one shape is circling around the other. These trajectories resemble those of objects carried by the wind, with a constant drift to the left of the witness. The decrease in altitude of both shapes is consistent with the need to land before nightfall (according to regulations). See the investigation report. It was not possible to finalize the exact identification of the devices responsible, however, a vast majority of the descriptive parameters derived from the visual observation and from the videos perfectly converge towards this very simple and plausible explanation. The probability of a flight originating from Kehl (or Offenbourg) is high. Indeed, there are tourist flight companies in this sector located in the direction of the observation and at a distance compatible with the size of the hot air balloons observed in the video. The strangeness is therefore very low, and even with only one witness, the consistency remains quite strong, as the videos confirm the observation and allow for a very objective approach to the observed phenomenon. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies this observation as A: observation of hot air balloons.
