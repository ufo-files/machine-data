Description: The gendarmerie reports an observation made on February 1, 1981, at 2:40 a.m. by a night watchman. During his patrol, he spotted three large pink and dull orbs forming an isosceles triangle above the sea. No flashing lights or sounds were observed during their movement. No other testimonies were collected. The case was initially classified as unexplained when it was reported in 1981. Unexplained cases hold potential scientific knowledge, but their status must be consistently validated over time. These cases are therefore reviewed. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this review sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This review does not allow us to maintain the case's "unexplained" status. We do not have the written and signed testimony of the witness who refused to provide one. It has been a long-standing rule in GEIPAN to work with direct and signed testimonies. Any other situation compromises the reliability of the testimony.

The testimony is vague (no written description from the witness, no questions and answers). Size, distance, and speed information are always subjective and difficult to interpret. Knowing the initial and final positions in the sky and the duration of the observation would have been helpful.

Given the limited information, nothing rules out the possibility of a balloon or children illuminated by city lights. It was a weekend night, likely conducive to a festive event. The PAN are described as dull and non-emitting lights, which could indicate ambient lighting in the middle of the night. The wind was light and coming from the north at 1 a.m. (as reported on the Infoclimat website). A witness observing the PAN over the sea (not directly above him) would primarily perceive a lateral movement from left to right, either because the PAN are carried south by the wind from La Valette or because they are moving from La Valette to La Seyne-Sur-Mer, as the witness described (as noted in the report). Furthermore, since the wind was light, it could have slightly changed between 1 a.m. and 2:40 a.m. The low consistency of the testimony does not allow us to validate an inexplicable strangeness, especially since the available information could be consistent with a balloon or children's flight.

Therefore, GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Lack of reliable information.
