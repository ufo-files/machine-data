**Report:** On October 20, 2024, around 8:15 PM, from his home in Villars-les-Dombes (01), the witness observed the sky as part of his follow-up of the Tsuchinshan-Atlas comet. He noticed a dark shape composed of several identical and blurry bright points. The whole scene, reminiscent of a "starry murky sky," moved without emitting any sound. The trajectory was straight, stable, and regular, oriented from northwest to southeast. The witness used a monocular telescope to better observe the phenomenon but did not distinguish any additional details. The observation lasted about 30 seconds before the phenomenon disappeared behind the roof of a nearby house. The consistency* was judged as average to low: one unique witness and no photos or videos of the UFOs.

*According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Three hypotheses were examined: observation of a group of LED balloons, a formation of Starlink satellites, or a flight of birds. The Starlink satellite hypothesis was quickly dismissed as the trajectory of potentially visible units at the time of the incident did not match the reported phenomenon. Similarly, the hypothesis of glowing balloons was rejected as the wind direction, both on the ground and in the air, presented a significant divergence from the witness's reported trajectory.

In contrast, the hypothesis of a group flight of birds with clear ventral plumage, whose plumage reflects the glow of urban lighting, appears, for the following reasons, as the most solid:

- Angular distance calculations confirm that at a cruising speed of 20 km/h, the subjects are at a distance where the human eye cannot distinguish their morphology, thus obscuring any perceptible movement of wing beats.
- The significant presence of light pollution along the flight path is likely to produce a diffuse reflection on clear plumage, explaining the appearance of faintly glowing and vaporous points.
- The stable, regular, and straight movement observed is in perfect accordance with the transit flight of migratory birds or specimens returning to a resting site.
- The observation area has a remarkable ornithological density. Several local species, with white plumage, are active at twilight during their movements to secure roosts.
- The direction of movement of the UFOs directly points to a major ornithological park housing over 3,000 individuals representing a diversity of 600 distinct species.

Although the consistency of this case is evaluated as average to low, due to, among other things, the imprecision of the observation time slot, the explanatory hypothesis appears sufficiently supported by physical and environmental correlations. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies this case in category "B": a phenomenon identified with a high probability as a transit flight observation of birds.
