Description: On August 14, 2015, around 5:00 PM, a witness spotted in the sky a triangular shape moving slowly with a swinging motion. Using binoculars, he distinguished a purple-blue color when the object flew overhead. The object remained below the clouds and moved silently from north-northwest (NNO) to south-southeast (SSE). Only one testimony was collected. The weather conditions recorded in Lanvéoc, a few kilometers away, indicated a strong north wind with a overcast sky. In Brest, the aeronautical weather report was as follows: METAR LFRB 141500Z 31010KT 280V340 9999 SCT017 BKN030 16/12; fragmented cloud base at 1700 feet (500m), under a thick layer at 3000 feet (1000m). The observed object, flying from NNO to SSE, therefore exhibits characteristics of an object carried by the wind. The described shape could be mistaken for a hot air balloon, but the flight time is unusual for this type of flight (aerospheres prefer to fly early in the morning or at sunset to avoid thermal currents). Furthermore, the area is prohibited from overflight due to numerous military sites. Finally, since the cloud ceiling is low, and the object appears almost point-like to the naked eye, it must be an object of relatively small size (if the object has an apparent size of the order of a tenth of the size of the Moon and is flying at 500m altitude, it measures less than 50 cm). A child's balloon, perhaps a shiny mylar balloon released nearby, is much more likely (very common models in various shapes). There was a celebration in Roscanvel (to the northwest of the observation location) on the evening of August 14. Fairs stands may have been set up that day. Given these information, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "B" for a probable observation of a child's helium-filled balloon.
