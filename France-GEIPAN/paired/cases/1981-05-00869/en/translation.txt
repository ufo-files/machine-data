**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined to enhance the conclusions' relevance. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The observation case previously classified as D and named TERNAY (41) 22.05.1981 is part of a recent reexamination of several cases (also known as MONTOIRE (41) 1980). On May 23, 1981, around 11:40 PM, four family members are driving on CD4 (in the commune of TERNAY (41)). They spot (hardly visible due to very cloudy weather) a highly luminous phenomenon at low altitude. The driver stops, intrigued by the PAN now near a copse. The observation, lasting about 15 minutes, allows the witnesses to see an oval-shaped object with lights of various colors (white and blue-green) and, at times, red-orange lights at the ends. The object, which seems to emit beams of light, remains stationary. No noise is heard. The PAN disappears behind the copse. The driver continues on to take two family members to SAINT-MARTIN-DES-BOIS (41) and drops them off. Two of the four witnesses then return to the site via a different route. Upon reaching the copse, a thick fog suddenly envelops the woods. The witnesses turn back. A final glance in the direction of the copse reveals small red-orange lights near the ground. The witnesses leave the site and return home. The following morning's investigation finds no traces on the ground or in the surrounding vegetation.

Through analysis (see investigation report), we demonstrated that the witnesses, under unfavorable observation conditions (very dark night environment), could not identify an agricultural vehicle or, more likely, a forestry vehicle working at night in a field or small wood about 2 kilometers away from their position. This observation distance, coupled with the impossibility of distinguishing sky from earth under these conditions, led the witnesses to believe the unidentified phenomenon was in the sky at low altitude, while we were able to show that it could actually be on the ground in a sloping area with an elevation higher than that of the witnesses.

Thus, we explored two hypotheses: an agricultural vehicle working in the fields or a forestry vehicle performing felling or maintenance work at night. Although both hypotheses can account for the PAN's appearance (particularly its bright lights), the latter is favored because it better explains the witnesses' observations, especially given that a small wood, located along the observation axis, was cut between May 1978 and July 1981, as shown by aerial photos. There is every reason to believe that logging operations on this wood had already begun by the time of the observation.

Various maneuvers by the object causing confusion were particularly observed by T1, who notes: "At times, a light emanated from each end, in a red-orange color. Occasionally, this bright light seemed to flicker, and this observation was likely due to the object's different positions." The "flicker" noted by several witnesses is probably an effect caused by the lights of the object temporarily being obscured by trees or tree branches during its movements.

The key reference point for the witnesses' observation is a small copse. The PAN may appear mobile relative to this copse and eventually disappear behind it. We have shown that this is a parallax illusion: the copse is much closer to the witnesses than the PAN, the witnesses' vehicle changes position, so the PAN appears to move in relation to the copse, eventually disappearing behind it.

The final apparent oddity, regarding the non-observation of the PAN when T1 and T2 came to stand on CD116 behind the copse and searched for the PAN, is primarily explained by the fact that the witnesses logically looked behind the copse to their right and did not think to look in the opposite direction to their left, towards the south.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a forestry vehicle performing nighttime maintenance work in a small wood located about 2 kilometers south of the witnesses.
