Description: On September 29, 2009, at 3:00 PM, a witness observed through a window a small, high-altitude, white, flickering light. After one minute, the light stopped and then resumed its trajectory without flickering. According to information gathered by phone from the witness, who was looking south, the object was moving east (see map). Ground weather reports mention a northwest wind at 2:00 PM, shifting to north at 3:00 PM. Therefore, the observation does not correspond to an object carried by the wind at low altitude. However, Météo-France releases daily weather balloons from Trappes around 12:00 PM local time (2:00 PM UTC), which is one hour before the observation (see the report indicating that the ground wind at 2:00 PM was northwest, weak (5 kt), and then shifted to west at 25 kt from 2800 meters upward). Since the winds at altitude were blowing east, this balloon could explain the observation, approximately one hour after its release. The flickering corresponds to the rotation of the balloon or reflectors. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A" (identified) due to the observation of a Météo France weather balloon launched from Trappes at 2:00 PM.
