Summary: Observations of the unusual movement of a bright point in the sky: observations from the International Space Station (ISS).
--- seg-2614f4c8b6b0e897cff89185 [needs-review] ---
Description: On May 24, 2017, after an evening spent with a group of astronomers observing Jupiter, two witnesses were intrigued by the unusual SSE-ENE movement of a very bright point in the sky that receded and disappeared. The witnesses very likely observed the passage of the International Space Station (ISS). The ISS is in the directions observed at the beginning (SSE high in the sky) as well as at the end of the observation (ENE on the horizon). The time indicated at the end of the observation 23:45 is off by 8 minutes (end of ISS passage at 23:37) and this offset is likely due to a later look at the watch than the end of the observation and/or a fast watch. Given the brightness of the ISS in the sky (much brighter than any other star) and its greater mobility, it is not possible that the witnesses, who came to observe a starry, cloudless sky, did not see it. The description appears to deviate from a satellite movement in the sky, as it mentions a curved trajectory. However, the description reflects the classic witness error of interpreting a change in light intensity as a radial approach or recession (towards the witness) of the PAN. Thus, the curvature of the trajectory is not seen in the sky (rapid changes in azimuth and altitude) but imagined in the direction of the witness. The expressions accurately describe this phenomenon: 
- Witness 1: "it was coming down towards us while getting bigger", "the ball was moving very fast towards north/northeast, rising high in the sky until it became the size of a star, then a barely discernible bright point, then nothing." 
- Witness 2: "a bright, moving object heading towards us", "a steep descent", "it moved straight in front of us without descending or rising to disappear." The trajectory reconstruction drawing provided by Witness 2 (see questionnaire) shows a PAN coming from ESE and moving straight towards the witnesses, then turning and moving straight back towards ENE. A witness cannot detect a radial movement of an unknown object; they can only interpret it, often incorrectly, based on the concurrent change in its size or brightness. Here, the brightness of the ISS could have increased very strongly as it reached its maximum brightness in the east-southeast position high in the sky, creating the "steep descent phase of the observation" before decreasing in intensity, probably more slowly than during its increase. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of the International Space Station (ISS).
