Summary: Nighttime observations of the silent passage of an orange-colored light phenomenon: observation of festive glowing balloons with integrated LED or a group of flying lanterns.
--- seg-aedffb542638af46d9a49474 [needs-review] ---
Description: On Saturday, June 02, 2018, at 10:05 PM, a witness (T1) observed from their home "an orange-colored glow in the shape of a circle coming from Chambéry." This luminous phenomenon of color moves slowly in the stormy sky. The witness then takes a pair of binoculars, allowing him to see not one but three "lights" that appear to form a PAN in an "orange fog around." Two other witnesses (T2 and T3) will also observe the PAN. The hypothesis of a confusion with a group of Thai lanterns has been studied. A second hypothesis of festive glowing balloons with integrated LEDs is considered. These two hypotheses are quite close. The 40-minute observation duration reported by T1 (verified and consolidated) and the 4-minute observation of "four fixed blue lights of variable intensity from medium to strong" lead us to favor the hypothesis of festive light balloons with LEDs. We maintain as a secondary hypothesis the possible observation of lanterns. T1's perception of a structure underlying PAN, in the moment of a flash, remains a perceived oddity. Emails were exchanged with T1 to deepen the understanding of the observation. These exchanges brought complementary information, but they did not allow everything to be explained. They instead brought elements of strangeness that also turned out to be more or less incompatible with our hypotheses. The PAN object of this investigation is very similar to that of the CHAMBERY (73) case study on 03.06.2018. It took place the following day (at approximately the same times and with a similarly prolonged observation duration). Our analysis shows that the hypothesis of festive glowing balloons with integrated LEDs or flying lanterns is probable. These two hypotheses remain quite close. Ballons or lanterns could have been launched during these two consecutive weekend nights. The majority of observation parameters conform to the hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": observation of festive glowing balloons with integrated LED or a group of flying lanterns.
