Description: On November 11, 2016, around 10:00 PM, a witness observed the sky from outside their home located in Villeneuve-les-Bordes (Seine-et-Marne). In the northwest direction, to the left of the Great Bear constellation, and at an estimated elevation of approximately 60°, the witness spotted a fixed bright light object, similar to a particularly bright star. Upon closer attention to this light point, they noticed it exhibited slight zigzag movements, with a low angular amplitude corresponding to about 10 to 15 cm at arm's length, alternating from left to right, then inversely, before returning to a position close to the initial one, albeit slightly offset. By then shifting their gaze towards the east, the witness identified a second phenomenon with similar characteristics: altitude position, stellar appearance, and slight and irregular movements. The observation ended while both PAN were still visible. The total duration of the observation could not be precisely established. The witness also reported other similar observations occurring on dates prior to and subsequent to November 11, 2016. The observation analyzed within this investigation specifically concerns the one on November 11; explanatory elements regarding the other occurrences are presented in the annex. The case has average consistency: one witness but numerous elements provided and photos targeting subsequent observations. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of considered reliable and objectified information gathered for a testimony. The investigation concluded that on November 11, 2016, the witness observed two stars, Vega and Capella (see the investigation report). The angular height discrepancy of about 30° for Vega (between the witness' estimate and the actual position of the celestial body) does not seem prohibitive for an angular height estimation reported during the day, while the phenomena were observed at night. The perceived oddity relies on the impression of the PANs moving (very fast and abrupt movements both to the right and to the left, zigzags, and returning to their initial places within a few "centimeters," all within a visual space of 10 or 15 cm), which can be explained by an autocinetic effect. The case is classified as "A" observation of Vega and Capella.
