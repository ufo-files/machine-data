Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined to be more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named CHANAZ (73) 22.08.1980 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On August 22, 1980, at approximately 9:45 PM, a witness was drawn outside their home by a bright light. They observed in the sky a bundle of red-orange colored lightning-like flashes with a tail. The phenomenon moved quickly and silently on a straight trajectory. No smell was detected. Only one testimony was collected on August 24, 1980. By its visual aspect and trajectory, the PAN described is perfectly compatible with the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid or a satellite craft (see the investigation report). The duration of observation is not described, but it can be indirectly estimated between a minimum of 10 seconds and a maximum of 1 or 2 minutes. It is compatible with the reentry of a long-duration meteoroid, as it followed a trajectory tangent to the atmosphere (a rare phenomenon but already observed for durations reaching two minutes), or with the reentry of a satellite craft (which always follows a tangent trajectory). What reduces the likelihood of the hypothesis, without invalidating it, is the lack of other testimonies, even though such a PAN had time to overfly a large geographical area, it was large, bright, and visible for at least 10 seconds, and this at a time of night, though not too late in the summer vacation period. The consistency is poor. It could have been improved by questioning the witness to obtain a more precise angular measurement in azimuth and elevation, and especially to obtain a much more precise indication of the duration of observation than the one that the investigation was able to estimate indirectly. This consistency is just sufficient to support the hypothesis of atmospheric reentry despite the residual strangeness linked to the absence of observation by other witnesses of such a long and bright reentry. In conclusion, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: observation of the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid or a satellite craft.
