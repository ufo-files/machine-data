Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named PITHIVIERS (45) 1979 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. On December 2, 1979, around 6:30 PM, a couple of witnesses, along with their children, were walking in Pithiviers. They were intrigued by the presence in the sky of numerous lights of different colors, some of which were flashing. These lights seemed arranged in a triangle. A whistling noise was heard. The PAN appeared stationary before moving across the sky in a line and then disappeared from view. Only these two testimonies were collected by the Gendarmerie. After reassessing the case, it turns out that one hypothesis can explain the testimonies (see the investigation report). This is the hypothesis of a line of military helicopters convoying to the Air Base 123 in Orléans-Bricy. This is based on the following points: - Moving in a "line" towards Air Base 123 in Orléans-Bricy, which is typical for a military convoy;
- Possible stop times noted by the witnesses;
- The colors of the observed lights, green, red, and yellow, which are consistent with the regulatory lighting signaling of aircraft (navigation lights and landing lights);
- Flashing lights can be explained by the possible proximity of navigation lights, which are fixed, and anti-collision lights, which flash, or by the spatial geometry between the witnesses' position and that of the aircraft.

Other aspects of the testimonies, which present a bit stranger elements, were noted and, for the most part, explained:

- Presence of blue or purple lights. These lights are not naturally part of the mandatory lighting signaling of aircraft. However, these lights can take on a bluish or purplish hue depending on the observation conditions or the observer's visual acuity.
- The triangular shape perceived by T1. T2 does not observe this shape and instead states that he sees "three lights arranged in a triangle," which is closer to reality. Indeed, at night and without fixed references, it is common for witnesses to equate these lights with a solid object of a delimited shape by these same lights, even though nothing factual allows us to affirm that the observed object indeed has this shape, or even that a shape exists (it could be individual, scattered objects that are not connected). It's a perceptual shape illusion.
- The perceived noise: the witnesses only hear a whistling sound. There are numerous and undetermined conditions for hearing noise from the sky 40 years later: altitude, wind (strength and direction), the witnesses' sound environment, hearing abilities of the witnesses... At best, we can say that the sole perception of a "whistling" sound is not impossible, but it remains unproven.

The case's consistency is poor with few concrete elements available. We can particularly note the absence of angular measurements (altitudes over the horizon, dimensions of the PAN...) and the Gendarmerie's succinct investigation, which only retraced the witnesses' route on foot. No sketches of the PAN were made by the witnesses, and no photos or videos were taken. However, this low level of consistency is sufficient to validate the hypothesis, given the limited level of uncertainty resulting from it. The case is not far from being unusable due to a lack of reliable information.

Classification: "B": Probable observation of helicopters.
