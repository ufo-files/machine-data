Description: The GEIPAN was informed on October 1st by the UFO66 website of several observations made on September 30th at approximately 11:50 PM. Witnesses reported simultaneous observations of very bright light balls quickly crossing a portion of the sky, which appears to be a bolide, a type of meteor (see explanation of the phenomenon in the article "Bolide?"). "The meteor that crossed the sky of Gers yesterday Wednesday, September 30, 2015 at 10:53 PM was visible from Les-Vans (07), Campome (66), Canet (66), L'Yssandon (19), Saint-Victor-en-Marche, Brissac (34), Chemillé-Melay in Maine-et-Loire (49), Toulouse (31), and as far as Lyon (69)." (source UFO66). The GEIPAN received a direct formal testimony of an observation at 10:53 PM in BONNES (86) near Poitiers. Numerous witnesses came forward in Toulouse with the newspaper "La Dépêche du midi". A member of the GEIPAN also observed it from the corner of their eye. At 10:53 PM, the GEIPAN's automatic "Allsky" camera also recorded the phenomenon, as did the cameras at the Pic du Midi Observatory and Rochechouart (87) between Angoulême and Limoges (see camera images). 3D modeling produced from camera images allows for a perfect characterization of the light phenomenon's trajectory at very high altitude, around 60 km. This was caused by the atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid, fragments of which may have reached the ground. (see Google Earth modeling: 2015-30-09 2254.kmz) The brief duration of the phenomenon implies an initial velocity greater than 8 km/s, pointing towards a natural origin: a small mineral object likely from the asteroid belt. A space debris would have produced a longer phenomenon. The GEIPAN verified on the http://www.calsky.com website that no listed space debris had fallen that day within France's visibility. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A", perfectly identified: observation of a natural bolide.
