Summary: Very brief observation of the rapid passage of a luminous phenomenon in the night sky: observation of a meteor.
--- seg-d532d75d5097d4980a611e2b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 6, 2019, at approximately 04:25, the witness observed in the starry sky and for about 2 seconds a white/yellow light moving very quickly on a horizontal trajectory from south to north. Only one testimony was collected. This case is of good consistency (single witness, but testimony is quite precise and complete). The investigation revealed that the witness very likely observed a meteor. The description of the PAN is quite characteristic of this type of observation, lasting only two seconds and the PAN moving very quickly and in a straight line. No bolide was recorded by dedicated surveillance networks at the time of the observation, but the brightness of the PAN is rather modest, compared to the brightness of the stars in Ursa Major, and would not have allowed for such a detection. The absence of a visible trail, in the case of a shooting star or meteor, is not a discriminating factor for the hypothesis. Indeed, in this case, the meteor, observed under the described conditions (elevation of 30°, horizontal trajectory, brightness similar to that of the stars in Ursa Major) very likely has a thin trail that the witness was unable to observe for the following reason: this modest elevation and this horizontal trajectory indicate a considerable distance between the meteor and the witness, and therefore a thicker atmospheric layer than if it had been observed at the zenith. The light rays emitted by the trail, originally thin, are affected by atmospheric diffusion, ultimately preventing the witness from distinguishing the trail. The case is classified as A: observation of a meteor.
