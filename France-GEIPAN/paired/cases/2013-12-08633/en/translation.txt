Description: The witness, looking towards the north-northeast at 5:30 PM, observed "from a moving vehicle an orange light above the horizon. The light appeared stationary, it was neither a star, nor the Moon, nor a streetlamp." Indeed, there are no very bright stars in that direction, nor the Moon, and it's too high in the sky to be a streetlamp. Given that the observation occurs shortly after sunset, one could consider the temporary reflection of the sun on an airplane or satellite. The witness states that the light appeared stationary, but as he was in motion, this claim cannot be considered rigorous. The GEIPAN found no commercial aircraft on "Flightradar24" that could have caused this reflection, but a military aircraft cannot be ruled out. The Calsky sky chart provides several satellite candidates: the NOSS satellites passing in the witness's observation direction at that time, in particular the rocket debris: 3-4 Rocket. These objects, generally dim (magnitude 4-6), are known to sometimes "flash." While these hypotheses are plausible, they could not be sufficiently solidified for the GEIPAN to adopt them. The GEIPAN classifies this unusual case in category "C": lack of information.
