--- seg-649683cdaa348ca3caee5d74 [needs-review] ---
Description: On Sunday, October 24, 2021, at 7:00 a.m., from the terrace of a village house in Villautou (11), at the border between Aude and Ariège, a witness observed a faintly glowing point moving in a straight line at a very low speed towards them. Initially thinking it was a satellite, the witness actually distinguished several glowing points. They decided to go get their glasses. Upon their return, they saw that the phenomenon had taken the form of a black triangle with three white, slightly yellowish lights at each corner. At approximately 75° elevation, the object performed a turn by pivoting on itself to head northeast. The observation lasted about a minute. After a preliminary remote investigation, a cognitive interview was conducted with the witness. This interview allowed for the precise reconstruction of the technical observation parameters (see investigation report). According to GEIPAN criteria, the consistency of this case (amount of reliable information) is good, with complete and exploitable data, and the conduct of a cognitive interview. The aeronautical hypothesis (presence of an aircraft) is ruled out by the air traffic radar map. The hypothesis of an observation of the International Space Station (ISS) is explored further. Several peculiarities perceived by the witness are explained, although some characteristics of the observed object seem, at first glance, incompatible with those of the ISS. In terms of space and time, the positions and movements of the ISS and the observed object coincide relatively well. At the beginning of the observation, there is an azimuthal difference of approximately 30° between the two, which gradually decreases to around 10° during the passage to apogee, with the final trajectories of the two objects converging towards an azimuth of 55°. This initial difference can be explained by an approximation in the witness's reproduction of the initial position of the object; a 30° difference is not disqualifying. A minimal temporal offset (a few seconds or about a minute) is also noted, but it is not significant enough to be disqualifying. The perceived change in trajectory is due to a change in the witness's perspective after the station passes its apogee, as demonstrated by the study of the final directions taken by the object and the ISS, which are consistent with each other. The witness's initial perception—a brightly glowing white point moving slowly along a straight trajectory—exhibits all the characteristics of the ISS. The main peculiarities appear after the witness retrieves their glasses: the triangular shape, apparent size, and presence of three glowing points. Regarding the dark triangular shape, two elements can provide answers: For the clearly observed dark triangular shape, two elements can provide answers: The very small size of the object (1°) makes it difficult to distinguish clearly. The observation conditions at dawn, during the transition from night to day, degrade color perception and visual acuity, corresponding to mesopic vision conditions. Myopic individuals are even more affected by their visual acuity deficit. As for the apparent size, estimated at 15 mm on a rule held at arm's length by the witness, the ISS, viewed from the ground, appears as a point. It is known that some witnesses overestimate the apparent size of the ISS due to its intense brightness, giving a false impression of great dimensions. Regarding the three identical and unchanged white-yellow glowing points, it is confirmed that at the time of the observation, no resupply cargo was located near the ISS. Some eye conditions cause double vision, but tripling this vision is unlikely. This point remains difficult to explain, without invalidating the hypothesis, especially in light of the other corroborating elements. A similar file from the GEIPAN, dated 2018, reports that a witness also perceived several glowing points during a confirmed observation of the ISS, without a satisfactory explanation (see MOULEZAN (30) 26.12.2018). Finally, it seems unlikely that the witness did not mention another glowing point nearby corresponding to the ISS if the observed object was not the ISS. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of the ISS.
