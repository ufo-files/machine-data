Summary: Observation of the silent movement and maneuvers of a white-yellow cigar: lack of corroboration.
--- seg-053c929f4b93b97dc8317f90 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 27, 1954, at approximately 11:15 PM, a witness walking down a street (R.N. 40) saw a glowing, opalescent light about ten meters ahead of him. It was a "craft" in the shape of a cigar, with a front end that appeared wider than the rear, measuring 8 meters in length and 0.80-1 meter in diameter, without any windows or doors. It moved silently at about 30 km/h, at an altitude of 20 meters above the road, following its curves. It emitted a white-yellow light confined to the contours of the craft, not illuminating the roofs of the houses it flew over. It disappeared around a bend in the road, reappeared further along, then suddenly veered sharply to the left (towards southwest) at the entrance to the commune of Marck, 5 kilometers away. It then appeared motionless, in the shape of a sphere, with a more intense, yellower glow. The witness returned home, prepared his meal, and still observed the object at the same location, but after his meal, it had disappeared. Despite the witness's efforts to have someone else witness the phenomenon, he was the only one to make the observation. This observation is of average consistency, with only one a priori honest witness. What he describes is not very strange, as it merely involves a glowing point observed from a considerable distance. The object's movements are only noted when the witness moves; upon reaching his home, he perceives the object as motionless. Several hypotheses are possible: - a very distant, low light on the horizon that, as a result, appears to follow the witness's movements; an astronomical check reveals only the planet Mars in the direction of the witness's observation, but it sets at 11:00 PM. A light from the Calais port could also be considered. - A helicopter's beacon could have produced the same effect. The GEIPAN classifies this case as C due to a lack of corroboration.
