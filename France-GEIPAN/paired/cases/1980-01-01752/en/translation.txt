Summary: Observation of a stationary bright point in the sky: mistaken for Venus.

Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type "C" cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as "C" in 1980 by the GEPAN. On January 1st, 1980, between 6:30 PM and 7:00 PM, a driver on the A8 highway in the east-west direction observed a fixed bright light towards the west in a clear sky. Using binoculars, the witness was able to describe this very bright point as a string ball that showed black squares between its strands. The witness took photographs of the phenomenon, but the negatives later given to the gendarmerie were transparent and devoid of any image. No other testimony was collected on this phenomenon. The described phenomenon has many common characteristics (observation duration, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: Venus. Moreover, Venus was well present in the observed sky area, while the witness does not mention it. It is not the witness's visual perception that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feeling (surprise, driving at night). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this weak strangeness case is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception with Venus.
