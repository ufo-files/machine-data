**Description:** On Sunday, December 2, 2018, at approximately 7:05 PM, a driver was intrigued by the presence in the overcast sky of two bright red lights pointing straight south. Upon arriving at his destination, the witness noticed the lights were still visible; he then took two photos and a video with his smartphone. He observed the two objects gaining altitude along the same trajectory and disappearing into the clouds. No noise was heard during the observation. Only one testimony was collected. The witness likely observed Thai lanterns. The observation is consistent in the following ways:

- **Appearance:** Two bright red or orange lights, as depicted and translated in the video.
- **Trajectory:** The two lights followed each other, a typical behavior of lanterns caught in the same wind current.

The observation of the movement is very imprecise; it can be inferred that the objects moved towards the west (the initially more eastern PAN joined the first one) and north (as there was an increase in altitude for the observer looking south), but the magnitudes of these movements are unknown. There is also a significant unknown regarding the wind direction carrying the lanterns; the wind direction measured at ground level varies greatly according to nearby weather stations (Istres, Cap-Couronne, Marignane, etc.) and around 7 PM. Under these conditions, the compatibility of the observation with wind cannot be assessed:

- **Duration and Disappearance Mode:** The observation duration (10 minutes) is consistent with the flying duration of lanterns, and the disappearance of these objects is likely due to the gradual extinction of the burners, which a witness might misinterpret as the lanterns entering the clouds, while these are generally located higher than the maximum flying altitude of lanterns. It was an evening during the weekend, which is conducive to such festive events.

Therefore, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
