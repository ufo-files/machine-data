Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case, named BESSINES SUR GARTEMPE (87) 1993, is part of a set of cases recently reviewed. On October 5, 1993, around 6:20 PM, several people in a garden near a forest observed the appearance of colored lights in a clearing. After a few moments, the witnesses heard a thunder-like noise and saw a flash of light. The luminous phenomenon in the clearing disappears. The re-examination of this case establishes the following: - The witness descriptions use terms commonly associated with thunderstorm events: lightning, thunder, detonation... - The distinctions from a thunderstorm explanation are unconvincing. "It was not a storm because... there would not have been a single detonation," "the lightning was going in one direction and the detonation or noise in the other." - the element that seemed the strangest, the lights in the wood, did not allow for verification by the witnesses that they were indeed coming from the wood, as the witnesses declined to go and see. So it could very well have been a series of lightning flashes from a storm seen through the wood. Indeed, the observation location (near the vegetable garden) is on the south side of a dome (Puy de Sarran), the lights are seen at the "northernmost clearing" which is near the top of Puy de Sarran, the wooded areas are mainly on the sides of this dome (the descending elevation is 10 by 70 meters) so that flashes from the north or northwest horizon of the dome (no trees on the east side of the dome) can be seen through the trees without crossing much tree thickness (the dome shape of the forest cover) to appear on the clearing surface (the latter is seen by a witness and thus without tree obstruction). The fact that the lights were seen at 4 meters above ground level in the clearing further reduces the thickness of the trees traversed upstream of the clearing by the lightning's light path. - The Limoges weather service, contacted by mail by the brigade regarding the situation at 6 PM and without any contextual elements, indicates nothing unusual at 6 PM without expanding the time frame. On the other hand, the response from the radio station in Sauvagnac mentions heavy rain, distant thunderstorms, and lightning in multiple directions, starting at 8:00 PM, and does not rule out the possibility of activity between 6:30 PM and 7:00 PM. This information strongly suggests the occurrence of thunderstorms (with some lightning and a single thunderclap) 30 minutes or an hour earlier, 12 km to the north. - The witnesses are friends, apparently in a lively or animated state (each having consumed 3 beers and a pastis in the afternoon before the observation, as noted in the police report), which may have facilitated communication or even amplified their sense of strangeness. - One might wonder why the gendarmes did not perceive the storm situation from their premises, located 2 km from the observation site. Lightning could have been obscured by the dome's relief, as the hypothesis is of distant lightning beyond the dome, and anyway, lightning is not always perceived indoors. On the other hand, the sound of detonation ("like an explosion or a thunderclap") was indeed heard from T4's residence, but it is located closer to the garden (1km) and to the north of the dome, thus closer to the supposed source of the thunder and without any potential sound-screening effect from the dome. It is not possible to affirm that the origin of the PAN is the storm, but the totality of the elements gives this hypothesis a probability superior to 50%. Furthermore, the consistency of the testimonies is weakened by the complete absence of angular measurements of the light sources, as well as the durations of the lights. The totality of the testimonies is at the limit of being unusable. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: storm.
