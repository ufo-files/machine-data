**Description:** The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the relevance of conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The previously classified case D (BESSINES SUR GARTEMPE (87) 1993) and named BESSINES SUR GARTEMPE (87) 1993 is part of a recent batch of cases re-examined.

On October 5, 1993, around 6:20 PM, several people in a garden near a forest observed colored lights appearing in a clearing. After a few moments, witnesses heard a thunder-like noise and saw a flash. The luminous phenomenon in the clearing disappeared. Re-examining this case reveals the following:

- Witness descriptions use terms commonly associated with thunderstorms: flashes, thunder, detonation, etc.
- Explanations ruling out a thunderstorm are unconvincing. Statements like, "It wasn't a thunderstorm because... there wouldn't have been a single detonation," or "Flashes moved in one direction, and the detonation or noise in the other," are noted.
- The most unusual element, lights in the forest, did not undergo verification by witnesses as to whether they originated from the forest because the witnesses declined to go check. Therefore, it could have been a series of thunderstorm flashes seen through the trees. The observation location (near a vegetable garden) is on the south flank of a dome (Puy de Sarran). The lights were perceived at the "northernmost clearing" near the top of Puy de Sarran. For the most part, forested areas are on the flanks of this dome (a descending slope of 10 by 70 meters). Thus, flashes from the north or northwest horizon of the dome (no trees on the dome's east side) can be seen through the trees without traversing much tree thickness (the dome's forested cover is in the shape of a dome) and appear on the clearing's surface (this clearing is observed by one witness and thus without tree obstruction). The fact that the lights were seen at 4 meters above ground level at the clearing reduces even more the tree thickness traversed upstream by the light path of the flashes.

- The Limoges weather service, contacted by mail by the brigade about the situation at 6:00 PM without contextual elements, indicates nothing abnormal at 6:00 PM without expanding the time frame. In contrast, the radio station in Sauvagnac reports heavy rain, distant thunderstorms, and flashes in multiple directions, starting at 8:00 PM, without ruling on the 6:30 PM to 7:00 PM time frame. This information is highly compatible with the onset of thunderstorm alerts (a few flashes and a single thunder roll) 30 minutes or an hour earlier at 12 kilometers to the north.

- The witnesses are friends, presumably in a rather lively or animated state (each having consumed 3 beers and a pastis in the afternoon prior to observation, as noted in the statement), which may have facilitated communication or amplified the feeling of strangeness.

- One might wonder why the gendarmes did not perceive the thunderstorm situation from their premises located 2 kilometers from the observation site. Flashes could have been obscured by the dome's relief, as the hypothesis is of distant flashes beyond the dome. In any case, flashes are not always perceived indoors. However, the sound of detonation ("like an explosion or a thunder roll") was clearly heard from the T4 residence, but this residence is closer to the garden (1 kilometer) and to the north of the dome, thus closer to the presumed source of the thunder and without any potential sound-screening effect from the dome. It is not possible to affirm that the lights' origin was a thunderstorm, but the totality of elements gives this hypothesis a probability greater than 50%. Furthermore, the consistency of testimonies is weakened by the complete absence of angular measurements of the lights' origins, of flash durations. The totality of testimonies is on the edge of being unusable. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as B: thunderstorm.
