Summary: Nighttime observation of the silent low-altitude passage of a triangle bounded by three fixed lights: very probable observation of a military A400M aircraft in exercise.
--- seg-ed16014a22fba78e45bdc8be [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Tuesday, November 9, 2021, around 7:00 PM, on the road from Sierentz to Habsheim (68), the witness observed through their windshield three fixed white/blue lights delineating a triangular shape passing vertically below them for less than 30 seconds. No noise was perceived by the witness, and the PAN moved away towards north-northeast while slightly changing direction. Upon returning home, the witness was able to see and hear two fighter jets heading "towards the last assumed direction of the object." The consistency of this case is average: a precise testimony, but unique, without photos or videos. The investigation revealed that the witness very likely observed a military A400M aircraft from Air Base 123 Orléans-Bricy, conducting a Very Low Altitude exercise to the south of Air Base 132 Colmar (see investigation notes). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- A confirmed presence of an A400M on the scene at the time of the observation, within the context of significant Air Force exercises.
- For the needs of this exercise, the A400M flew at low altitude in "stealth mode," meaning at a reduced speed with minimal lights on. The observed lights could correspond to the two landing lights and one of the runway lighting lights located on one side of the fuselage.
- The apparent slow movement; the A400M moved relatively slowly during the low-altitude exercise. The actual speed was difficult for the witness to gauge, as they themselves were in motion.
- The A400M is a large, imposing aircraft that gives an impression of proximity when flying at low altitude. The perceived oddity by the witness is due to several factors:

- The absence of noise
- The triangular shape

Regarding the absence of noise: the A400M is a noisy aircraft when flying at full speed and when close to the observer. The perception of the noise it emits depends on its speed, but also other factors, particularly observation conditions, the distance separating the witness from the aircraft, and wind direction, whether carrying or not. In this case, the wind was light and variable, making this element non-determinant for the perception of noise. In contrast, the observation conditions are important: the witness observed from a moving vehicle with initially closed windows, and the aircraft was flying at a reduced speed. These factors may be sufficient to explain why the witness did not perceive any noise, at least initially.

As for the perceived triangular shape, it was determined by the three visible lights alone. It was nighttime, and the aircraft's color was inherently dark. That's why the witness could not perceive details (propellers, tail, etc.).

Classification: A: Very probable observation of an A400M conducting an exercise.
