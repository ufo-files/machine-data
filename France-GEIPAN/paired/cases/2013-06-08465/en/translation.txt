Description: On June 5, 2013, at 12:34 AM, a witness observed in a clear sky the passage of a very bright, yellow-orange phenomenon. He filmed his observation with his smartphone. This observation of a very bright light point moving from West to Northeast in a few minutes is explained by the passage of the International Space Station (ISS): the Calsky record (see document) is perfectly consistent with the observation: time and direction. The ISS appeared in the South-West at 12:32 AM. The culmination was at a height of 32° in the North-North-West at 12:37 AM. The station gradually disappeared at 12:42 AM in the North-North-East. The changes in direction described by the witness are explained by the fact that the apparent trajectory of the station in the sky is a curve, and without a reference point in the dark sky, the witness adjusts the perceived trajectory into a succession of straight lines. The station's brightness decreases progressively from the culmination to the horizon; this decrease in brightness is interpreted as the station moving away from most observers. The witness took a video with his smartphone without stabilizing it during the recording, making the video document unusable. The witness was looking in the right direction to see the ISS, which was bright enough to be noticed by any observer: if another phenomenon had appeared, he would have described both phenomena simultaneously, as they were perfectly visible in the clear sky at that time. This case is classified as "A": observation of the International Space Station.
