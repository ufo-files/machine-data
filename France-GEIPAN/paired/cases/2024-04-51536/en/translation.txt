Summary: Observation of the movement of a PAN in the shape of a rocket: observation of a commercial aircraft (Cessna Citation 700).
--- seg-058273e981302a48a9f04c8b [machine-unreviewed] ---
**Report:** On April 29, 2024, at 6:18 PM local time, a witness on vacation in the commune of Les Trois-Ilets, Martinique, observed from their apartment balcony a PAN resembling a rocket or missile traveling on a nearly vertical trajectory. The witness was able to capture video of the phenomenon. Their companion, also present and a witness to the observation, saw the PAN, but did not provide a formal statement to GEIPAN. The case exhibits good consistency (one witness with a detailed description and video of the PAN). According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Two hypotheses are considered: observation of Starlink® satellites and that of an aircraft (see investigation report). The analysis concludes that the observed phenomenon corresponds to an aircraft, a Cessna Citation 700. This identification is based on the following elements:

- Aircraft N806QS, identified through the FlightRadar24 app, was overhead in the witness's location at the time of observation.
- Its direction and movement are roughly identical to those described by the witness.
- The elongated shape of the PAN is typical of an aircraft fuselage with a condensation trail formed at 12,000 meters altitude, partially illuminated by the setting sun.
- The straight, steady movement is also a decisive factor. The aircraft's altitude explains both the absence of audible perception and the poor visibility of the wings. Additionally, the configuration of the two engines, located at the rear of the fuselage, causes a condensation trail. Illuminated by the setting sun's light beneath the flight path, this trail creates the appearance of a continuous and homogeneous structure observed from the ground.

The analysis of the hypothesis involving Starlink® satellites reveals low concordance with the observed phenomenon due to the typical characteristics of these objects (bright points without trail) and their azimuth and elevation offset. This hypothesis was therefore dismissed. The case is classified as "A," observation of a commercial aircraft.
