Summary: Observation of a round glowing object moving in a straight line and blinking in the night sky: probable observation of a satellite rotating rapidly on itself.

Description: On August 11, 2018, around 10:25 PM, in Limoux (11), while observing shooting stars from his terrace, the witness spots a PAN almost vertical. The object moves linearly from southeast to northwest, appearing to fly at a low altitude. Bright, abnormally large, and blinking, it moves at a steady speed without emitting any noise, faster than a high-altitude plane. Gradually, its brightness and blinking fade until it disappears into the night. The observation lasts approximately 3 minutes. With a low strangeness and average consistency (single witness, no photo of the PAN, despite a fairly complete and precise testimony), this case appears to be a probable observation of a rapidly spinning satellite, the satellite ERS-2 (see the investigation report). *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Indeed, the description of the PAN, particularly its rapid changes in brightness, strongly suggests it could be a satellite spinning rapidly on itself. The analysis of satellite trajectories confirms that two satellites, ERS-2 and AZUR, correspond to the observed passage in terms of trajectory and timing reported by the witness. These two objects, being uncontrolled space debris, spin rapidly. The ERS-2 satellite appears to correspond more coherently to the PAN due to its higher apparent speed and rapid spinning, being particularly known for spinning quickly on itself. The case is classified as B, probable observation of a rapidly spinning satellite on itself.
