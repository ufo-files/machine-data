Summary: Sequential observations of the presence and movement of lights in the Paris sky: aircraft observations.
--- seg-866373ac0883285bb9b6fe13 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 21 and 22, 2017, between 9:00 PM and 11:00 PM, a witness on their balcony on the 8th floor was intrigued by stationary and moving lights that they filmed and photographed. Only one testimony was collected. The testimony was somewhat strange, as it suggested that individual lights were stationary for an extended period. The witness, contacted by phone, clarified that each light, taken individually, lasted no more than one or two minutes. The same contact provided the orientation of the observation, which was generally south from Paris 10th (contradictory indications in the questionnaire; the witness indicated the PAN in the northeast). These two elements naturally lead to the hypothesis of aircraft landing lights at Orly. Indeed, the traffic shows numerous configurations of aircraft approaching Orly from the north, and thus with their lights facing Paris. The flight altitude is then between 1000 meters and 3000 meters, and the distance to Paris is 20 to 60 kilometers. With a speed of 400 K/H, an aircraft can remain in the vicinity of a single heading towards Paris (and thus with a quasi-stationary effect from Paris) for 20 kilometers, which is already 3 minutes. The altitudes correspond to a height in the sky of a few degrees for a witness in Paris with a clear view above the rooftops, a height of PAN consistent with photos and videos of the observation. The aircraft hypothesis is beyond doubt, even more so when the witness specifies over the phone that at least for one of the lights, this movement led to perceiving and recognizing the green and red lights of an aircraft. One might wonder why the mistake occurred one evening for a witness who has lived in the same apartment for over 10 years. But one can equally wonder why the witness did not mention in their testimony the presence of aircraft lights associated with one of the PAN and why they located the PANs in the north-northeast for an observation from a balcony facing south. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: aircraft.
