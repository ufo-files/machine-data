Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type "C" cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as "C" in 1981 by GEPAN. On January 24, 1981, at 9:10 AM, while driving her vehicle, a witness saw, despite the fog, a bright yellow ball. It moves in a north-south direction. High in the sky, the object appears to be rotating on itself. This ball disappears into the landscape. This case is moderately consistent (one witness only), and the description of PAN observed is precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witness have never been questioned. The phenomenon described has many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the Sun, seen through the fog. Moreover, the Sun was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, as well as the fog. It is not the visual perception of the witness that is in question, but the interpretation the witness makes of his observation through his feeling (surprise, driving through the fog). In the current classification of GEIPAN, this weak strangeness case is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception of the Sun seen through the fog.
