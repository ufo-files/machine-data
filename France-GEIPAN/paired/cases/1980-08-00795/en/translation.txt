Summary: Observation of the movement of a luminous phenomenon in the shape of a crescent that appears to follow the witnesses: astronomical misperception of the Moon.
--- seg-8cece377da1931c3d184c094 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Sunday, August 17, 1980, around 10 PM, in a forest house, two witnesses were intrigued by a very bright phenomenon heading west. Observed through binoculars, an object in the shape of a large crescent projected intense beams of light. Deciding to get closer to investigate the luminous phenomenon, the witnesses took a vehicle and continued to see the crescent, which seemed to move rapidly and follow their vehicle. Feeling afraid, the witnesses decided to turn around and go back. The luminous phenomenon, after stopping, disappeared. No traces were found on the ground or vegetation, and no other witnesses could provide additional information. This case, previously classified as D, recently underwent a review (see investigation notes). The case is consistent (2 witnesses), and the description of the observation location and the PAN observed is fairly precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, etc.) with a known astronomical object: the Moon at sunset. Moreover, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, while the witnesses made no mention of it. It is not the witnesses' visual perception that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, anxiety, and fear). In the current classification of GEIPAN, this case of weak strangeness is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception of the Moon.
