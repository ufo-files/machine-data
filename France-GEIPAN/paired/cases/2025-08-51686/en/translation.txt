Summary: Two successive observations of two groups of glowing points in motion across the night sky: likely observations of Starlink® satellites in transfer orbit towards the operational network.
--- seg-253e90053900810c5c8821b5 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 30, 2025, around 9:30 PM, witness (T1), accompanied by their family, was by the waterbody in Metz (57) to watch the fireworks display for the Mirabelle Festival. Looking north, slightly east of the Big Bear constellation, they spotted a cluster of over 15 white glowing points resembling satellites moving from west to east on parallel but not aligned trajectories. The phenomenon lasted approximately 15 seconds, with the objects gradually dimming until disappearing without any flickering, trail, or sound. Around 10:00 PM, witnesses observed a second phenomenon: about 7 glowing points appeared at the zenith and quickly moved towards the south. Their trajectories seemed independent, with no fixed formation. The observation lasted about 20 seconds until they disappeared. The entire family confirmed observing the same phenomena. Consistency was average, with only one witness reporting their observation to GEIPAN and no photos or videos of PAN taken. 

*According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. For T1, the strangeness stemmed from the spectacular nature of the phenomenon, mainly related to the number of PAN and their clustering. The only hypothesis explored, that of observing multiple Starlink® satellites, was corroborated by the following elements:

- The appearance of __UFO_PROTECTED_001__s resembling satellites: punctual, white in color,
- Movement along a straight trajectory,
- Disappearance "as if satellites were no longer illuminated by the sun," according to T1, for one of the groups,
- Three Starlink® constellation launches had occurred shortly before the observation (on August 27, 28, and 30 for a total of 80 satellites) (see Annex 1). The time gap between these launches and the observation explained the appearance of all satellites, which were no longer aligned but presented more as clusters, as described by T1, with some dispersion. The August 27 and 28 launches were conducted from Florida on non-polar orbits heading east, thus with a trajectory oriented from west to east, as seen from Metropolitan France. The August 30 launch was conducted at 1:59 PM local time in France from Vandenberg Air Force Base in California for a polar orbit resulting in a passage over France according to a trajectory that could be oriented from north to south. 

The witnesses, intrigued by the first passage of numerous satellites likely from the first two launches, focused their attention more intently on the sky. By chance, they were able to spot others about 30 minutes later, this time from the August 30 launch, closer together but having had time to disperse, losing their classic linear appearance known to T1 "not at all aligned like Starlink satellites."

GEIPAN classified the case as "B": probable observations of Starlink® satellites in transfer orbit to the operational network.
