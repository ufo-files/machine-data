Description: On Sunday, December 11, 2016, around 6:30 PM - 6:45 PM, a driver noticed in the clear sky a red-orange phenomenon shaped like two trails surmounted by some sort of rectangle. The PAN seemed stationary, then rose and slowly faded away until it completely disappeared. The observation lasted between 3 and 5 minutes. Only one testimony was collected. The witness's initial impression ("I think it's an atmospheric phenomenon, but I don't have enough knowledge to verify it") appears to be correct. It's likely sunlit clouds. Indeed, in the direction indicated ("West or southwest from my position"), the sun was setting (setting at 6:45 PM, azimuth 250°) and may not have been visible to the witness (who was driving). The witness reported a clear sky, but some cloud traces could be strongly lit (in mirror) by the setting sun and form orange geometric configurations, sometimes unusual (here, trails surmounted by a rectangle) that eventually "faded away." Venus and Mercury were shining in the sky in the direction indicated for the PAN, and especially Venus (very bright at magnitude -4.2) at 35 degrees elevation. Either the witness didn't notice it (which can happen) or Venus was hidden behind a cloud. In either case, this means the phenomenon wasn't seen at 40 degrees elevation as indicated. Absolute elevation measurements (without reference to a local geographic landmark) are very common errors. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies this case as B: cloud observation.
