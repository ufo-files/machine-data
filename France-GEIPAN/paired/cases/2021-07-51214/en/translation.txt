Description: On July 17, 2021, around 8:45 PM, while enjoying their home garden under a clear sky, the main witness (T1) spots a glowing object appearing to come from the direction of Fort Napoléon and moving silently across the sky. They alert their spouse (T2), stand up, and approach to better examine the phenomenon. The PAN is described as a bright, smooth sphere resembling a bocce ball, descending on a straight trajectory towards the sea. The object remains visible for approximately 15 seconds before disappearing behind nearby trees. Only one direct testimony of this observation was collected. The consistency* is average, with only one of the two witnesses reporting their observation to GEIPAN and no photos or videos of the PAN available. *According to GEIPAN's criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Based on the available elements (see the investigation report), the description and behavior of the PAN, the wind direction compatible with its movement, and the relative speed, it is estimated that the observation is likely that of a Mylar-type balloon, possibly released or lost from Fort Napoléon or its surroundings, perhaps by a child in a highly touristy area during the summer. GEIPAN classifies the observation as A: observation of a Mylar balloon.
