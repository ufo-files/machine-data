Summary: Observation of a glowing sphere and a broken poplar tree on the ground: likely a natural phenomenon due to parasites and tree fall.
--- seg-d211f59db539e8ee284b6cf8 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 31, 1983, at approximately 6:45 PM, a witness, alerted by the unusual barking of his dog, saw through a thick fog, despite the night, a bright light "as large as a tractor wheel" moving slowly and disappearing very quickly behind a curtain of poplars located at the edge of the river. The following morning, based on the witness's directions, a 75-foot tall poplar was found broken at a height of 26 feet: the upper portion was found on the ground among wild vegetation, and the break appeared in two distinct ways. The tree had a first clean break through the center of the trunk, followed by a wood tear approximately 7.9 feet long. The analysis results indicate that the tree had likely been attacked for a long time by fungi that gradually degraded it without changing its external appearance. When the degradation reached the middle of the tree, its balance was compromised, and it could have fallen due to minimal pressure. This phenomenon was enhanced by the effect of dominant winds, which caused general fatigue in the plant between 13 and 16.5 feet high, facilitating the appearance of destructive fungi. The brown area and scarred bark layer found at the break are consistent with this hypothesis. Regarding the bright light, one of the laboratories consulted offered an explanation. In nature, there are many species of luminescent organisms, including numerous fungi. These organisms can emit intense light to illuminate a room or navigate in the dark. The proposed hypothesis is that the tree trunk, colonized by these organisms, may have, at the moment of its break, revealed these organisms, causing a bright glow for a certain period, which the witness saw without being able to interpret it.
