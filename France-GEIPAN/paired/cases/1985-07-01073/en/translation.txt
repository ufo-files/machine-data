**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed to ensure more relevant conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation previously classified as D and named PLOERMEL (56) 1985 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been re-examined (see the investigation report).

On the night of July 22 to 23, 1985, at 11:15 PM, a witness observed from their home an unusual light phenomenon. Until 3:30 AM, the witness and others observed the phenomenon from various locations in the Ploërmel (56) area. The phenomenon was described as an oval shape emitting a pale white light accompanied by a rotating light source. The witness described a light phenomenon that seemed to sweep across the sky from side to side. No noise was heard. The phenomenon suddenly disappeared. Only one testimony was collected. This observation, which initially seemed unusual, appears to be the spatial and temporal coincidence of an unusual, particular phenomenon occurring in an equally unusual context.

Two hypotheses were explored and are ultimately quite similar, as they involve the observation of light beams from projectors impacting a low cloud layer:

- Beams from a nightclub device (Skytracer).
- Beams from the event "The Triumph of Saint-Cyr in the Coëtquidan Military Camp."

While there are some discrepancies, particularly regarding the directions of the PAN during observation phases from the Gendarmerie station, there are many correspondences between these hypotheses and the physical and dynamic characteristics of the PAN as well as the observation conditions:

- General observation direction towards the east (except from point D for the Skytracer hypothesis);
- Visibility as provided by Météo-France data, compatible with observation distances;
- Presence of a low cloud layer;
- Low observation angle possible because clear horizons from at least two observation points and compatible with considered distances and cloud altitude;
- Main shape of the PAN is oval;
- Presence of "beams" of light;
- Proven movement of the PAN, like a mobile projector ensemble;
- Low brightness;
- White color.

Other aspects of the phenomenon do not align well with either or both hypotheses, particularly:

- The rectangular shape observed to the left of the main shape in phases 2, 3, and 5;
- The position of the PAN in phase 4 too far north as deduced from the "Bretagne-Magazine" reproduction of the observation location (outside the investigation perimeter GEIPAN). Is this reproduction accurate? (It included houses in the landscape, and the conclusion on the observation direction would have been discordant). Is it another phenomenon?
- The use of projectors for the nightclub hypothesis is unlikely;
- The date of Monday evening implies that the nightclub was open outside the weekend, which is not excluded during summer, and that, regarding the "Triumph" of Coëtquidan, which takes place during the night from Saturday to Sunday after July 14th, it could have been a test or adjustment of the event or at least of the projectors.

However, most of these points remain weak in comparison to the many and concordant aspects that make the hypotheses plausible, with a higher possibility for the projectors of the "Triumph" in the Coëtquidan military camp. Indeed, their existence is proven, and they could have been used a few days before (or after) the ceremony to prepare (or replay) anomalies encountered during the event. Such an hypothesis cannot be verified 33 years later, but nothing rules it out or considers it improbable.

Therefore, a classification as "B" is appropriate: confusion with a set of projectors, so the impact of the beams is projected onto the low cloud layer.
