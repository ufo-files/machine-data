--- seg-3f0d2a489f453e96a810469a [needs-review] ---
Description: On Tuesday, October 19, 1954, between 1:30 and 1:40 a.m., several military personnel (including a second class, a corporal chief, and a sergeant) from a radar station in Doullens (80) (the radar was not functioning that evening) observed an unusual aerial phenomenon located below a continuous layer of stratocumulus clouds (estimated base at 1000 meters) during a dark night. The observation consisted of three phases:

**Phase 1:** At 1:34 a.m., a green glowing phenomenon appeared above a farm in the northeast (direction Mondicourt) emerging from the clouds at a 45-degree angle with the vertical, then moved horizontally to the east at an angle of 45° to 90° (text) or 120° (diagram) in half a second, without sound, changing color (green to orange then to a dull red). This phase was apparently only seen by the sentinel in their guard post on the embankment in front of the radar station.

**Phase 2:** The phenomenon abruptly stopped above La Bellevue (to the east) at an angular height of 60° (estimated height at 800 meters). This stationary period lasted 2 to 3 minutes, allowing the sentinel to alert their superiors. The object stood out clearly against the cloud layer with an angular size of approximately 1°. For the sergeant, it had a disk shape with the bottom visible, tracing an inclined ellipse at 25-30° to the horizontal. It emitted light that changed color approximately every 5 seconds: "pallid green" turning to orange then yellow (sergeant) while maintaining a verdant background (second class). It oscillated (sergeant) or moved horizontally and vertically (second class).

**Phase 3:** The phenomenon moved in the opposite direction of its arrival at high speed and disappeared into the clouds for 30 seconds, reemerging a few seconds (sergeant) or 2 minutes (second class) before disappearing again. The moon, in this cloudy sky, was to the east (azimuth 84°) and could have been a source of confusion: it was in the direction of observation given by the witnesses (northeast). Furthermore, it was very close to Jupiter that evening (distance of approximately 2.5°) and particularly bright (magnitude -1.78). Additionally, the witnesses did not mention the moon in their field of vision. However, the perceived color changes and movements are difficult to explain for this hypothesis. The flight of an airplane does not seem compatible with a stop of several minutes at a constant angular size. A helicopter could also be considered, with color variations attributed to the visibility of left and right position lights (red and green). However, the very high speeds observed do not fit this hypothesis. If the altitude of 800 meters (inferred from the estimated cloud ceiling of 1000 meters) is correct, the distance of the phenomenon during Phase 2 is of the order of a kilometer, and its size is 15-20 meters (1°). This case of average consistency, though strange and remote, is classified as D: Strange phenomenon / Very strange phenomenon of average to strong consistency.
