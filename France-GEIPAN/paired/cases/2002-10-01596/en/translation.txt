Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named ANNECY-LE-VIEUX (74) 20.10.2002 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On October 20, 2002, around 6:40 PM, a couple observed from a terrace the silent movements of a group of 7 to 9 very small dark or black objects in the sky. These objects displayed different movements relative to each other. The observation, first with the naked eye and then with binoculars, lasted about ten minutes. The entire group moved in a NNE direction at an estimated very low speed and at a constant altitude. Only one testimony was collected. Air traffic controls in the area were contacted, but no unusual activity was reported. We explored two explanatory hypotheses for the phenomenon observed by the witnesses (see the investigation report). The first concerns a confusion with a group of black birds moving at high altitude in a migratory corridor. Although the geographical and temporal context of the witnesses, located in a migratory corridor and making their observation at the end of October, during peak migration, supports this hypothesis, it remains fragile, for two main reasons: - the known and calculated altitude data, observation duration, and speed do not match the characteristics of the birds known to pass through that day; - and most importantly, the trajectory is in the opposite direction of the typical migratory path.

The second hypothesis, that of a confusion with a cluster of black balloons attached by strings, fits very well with the data. A light southwest wind was blowing on the day of the observation, in the same direction as the movement of the PAN. The movement was very slow. The known and calculated data regarding this speed, altitude, and observation duration align well with this hypothesis. Thus, with an estimated altitude of 500 meters, we have a total angular displacement of approximately 67 degrees, which is plausible given the geographical configuration of the site. Furthermore, the movements of individuals within the group (approaches, distances, "rotations", etc.) can be explained by the fact that they are connected to each other, more or less loosely, by strings. Carried by the local wind, they move as a single unit, but they are still subject to minor local influences that cause them to move randomly and independently of each other, within the limits of the string connecting them. This variant of a playful balloon release does indeed exist and produces such effects, as shown in this video where the five balloons constantly change their relative positions while remaining confined in a restricted area, all connected by strings. The GEIPAN did not have enough experience with this type of balloon release during the observation and initial investigation. The observation took place on a Sunday evening in the late afternoon, which is conducive to such a festive or recreational event. The consistency is poor, with only one testimony collected, while several witnesses were present, and certain data, particularly angular, are missing. No photo or video recordings were made. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of playful balloons.
