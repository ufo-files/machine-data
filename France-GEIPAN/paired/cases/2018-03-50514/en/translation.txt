Description: On Friday, March 02, 2018 at 2:32 am, a witness is on the balcony of a cruise ship cabin in the Mediterranean. The sky is illuminated by the full Moon and dotted with clouds. While trying to take souvenir photos of the moonlight, the witness is surprised and intrigued by an extremely bright and white phenomenon. The witness takes two photographs just before the PAN suddenly disappears. Upon entering their cabin, this person is surprised to have been outside and in the cold much longer than they thought. Only one testimony will be collected. The witness observed and photographed Jupiter. The PAN depicted in the photo is located precisely at the position of Jupiter: - this PAN is at a little less than 90° to the left of the Moon, since the Moon is described as dead ahead of the ship and the PAN is described almost directly behind (a little to port as seen in the photo) the ship. The witness's drawing also shows a nearly 90° offset between the Moon and the PAN; - using the IPACO analysis software, it is easy to determine the height of the PAN above the horizon and also to measure the height of the Moon. This one allows to find the exact time of the photo; - we check that at the time and place of the observation, Jupiter is like the PAN at a height of 19° on the horizon and offset by 85° to the left of the Moon. Due to a long-exposure photograph (1 second), Jupiter appears with significant motion, drawing variable arabesques from one shot to the next. The photo greatly contributed to the strangeness perceived by the witness, far beyond what he was able to see with his own eyes. He considers, for example, that the two successive photos correspond to different faces of the PAN in evolution (he says "what is very well determined on the 2nd photo taken 4 seconds apart and which shows another very different face"). The witness speaks of a "clear sky with occasional cloud residue," which perfectly explains his gaze being drawn to Jupiter, a very bright star that evening, as well as the sudden disappearance of the PAN behind a cloud that came to cover Jupiter. This GEIPAN testimony illustrates what happens to many witnesses, across all sociologies:

- **The level of strangeness** that a star, and particularly the strangeness component created by a camera, can generate: this witness loses their sense of duration (1 hour 40 minutes in reality occupied by the PAN compared to 15 minutes perceived), perceiving "an immense flying object extremely bright and of a very surprising shape appearing to be much larger than a large airplane";

- **The satisfaction** a witness can feel from the sensation and the luck of seeing and photographing a OVNI;

- **The need**, in the face of a genuinely observed strangeness, to find an explanation for being the only one to notice the strangeness while others were in a position to do so. Here, our witness believes that the crew of the ship "very likely decided not to disclose the information, despite their interest in this phenomenon, which is certainly too strange, out of concern for destabilizing some passengers or crew, especially since it is a cruise..." The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Jupiter observation.
