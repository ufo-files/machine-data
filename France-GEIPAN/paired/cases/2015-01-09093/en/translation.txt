Description: On January 30, 2015, around 7 PM, a driver on the Bordeaux ring road, near the commune of Carbon-Blanc (33), was suddenly startled by a very intense blue light that "covered the entire environment in his field of vision" (see the witness's drawing). No unusual noises were heard. Thinking it was the light from a gyrophare, the driver looked in his rearview mirror for a specific vehicle but saw nothing. No information was given on the radio. The driver continued his journey in a daze. No other witnesses were found for this phenomenon. The violent and sudden lighting of the sky for 2 seconds could have two possible causes: a particularly intense lightning bolt or a bolide (meteorite fall). Lightning strike records indicate that day there were strikes near the observation area, but only between 1:13 PM and 2:14 PM (see Météociel 1 and Météociel 2 records). In Bordeaux-Mérignac, a cloudy weather with a light west wind of 4-15 km/h, not exactly compatible with the witness's account (rain and strong wind), was reported by the Météociel record. The witness's account would lean more towards the first hypothesis, but it is not confirmed by weather records; it remains, however, possible. No bolide was recorded by automatic cameras that day, but cloud coverage was almost total over France at that time. The GEIPAN classified this case as "C" due to the lack of information to validate either hypothesis.
