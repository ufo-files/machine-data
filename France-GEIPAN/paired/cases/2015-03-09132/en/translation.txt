Description: On March 5, 2015, two witnesses driving observed successively in the sky several phenomena of different shapes that they could not explain. Only one testimony is collected. The witness actually observes 3 successive phenomena: 1- a elongated object, black and matte mass, composed of several white lights at different heights. A wing is visible. A check of civil air traffic shows a low-flying aircraft approaching Lille-Lesquin airport in the direction of the witness's observation: flight EZY693F, whose yellow-orange drift is also well noted by the witness (see Flightradar log). This GEIPAN phenomenon is therefore perfectly identified and classified "A": observation of an airplane. 2- The witness then sees a fairly high yellow light in the sky, in a direction close to that of the Moon. An astronautical verification (see Calsky log) indicates that this is the time of year when sun reflections on geostationary satellites are most likely, precisely in this direction. However, it is difficult to confirm this hypothesis. This GEIPAN phenomenon is likely identified as a satellite reflection and classified "B." 3- Its last observation concerns an object in cigar-shape, similar to the first, but without fins. A check of civil air traffic shows no aircraft at low altitude in the observed direction. Given the similarity to the first phenomenon, which was an airplane, the hypothesis of an airplane is favored: the fact that it does not appear on civil air traffic records suggests it could be a military aircraft, therefore less lit up than a civil aircraft. Given the low strangeness of the case, the GEIPAN did not trigger military air traffic research.
