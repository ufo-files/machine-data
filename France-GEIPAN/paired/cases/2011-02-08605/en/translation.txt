Summary: An automobilist observed a glowing ball in motion at the horizon with color changes: observation of Venus.

Description: On Sunday, February 13, 2011 at 6:07 a.m., a driver on D940 between Lamarque-Pontarcq (65) and Lourdes (65) was intrigued by the slow movements of an orange ball at the horizon. The witness noticed irregular lighting. Upon arriving home, he used the zoom on his camera to continue observing. The observation lasted approximately thirty minutes in total. Only one testimony was collected. Calculation of the observation directions of the phenomenon shows that, according to the road profile taken by the witness (see map 1 and 2), the observation direction of the phenomenon is consistently around 130° azimuth. Astronomical verification shows that the planet Venus is visible in this direction, extremely bright. This confusion is common: as the planet is low on the horizon, witnesses perceive it as close, flying over the landscape. Color and detail changes are the result of twinkling caused by the atmosphere; these effects are very strong and variable at the horizon (similar to sunset). The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the planet Venus.
