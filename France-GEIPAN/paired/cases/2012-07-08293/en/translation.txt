Summary: Observation of the movement in the sky of a first light followed by a second on the same trajectory and at constant speed and distance: rare observation of the passage of the ISS and the Japanese space cargo HTV.
--- seg-9c35acaf51fdbc7f807eeb79 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 27, 2012, between 4:20 and 4:25 AM, a witness was intrigued by the silent passage across the sky of a milky white, oval-shaped light. The observation lasted approximately 45 seconds before a second orange-colored light phenomenon appeared, perfectly tracking the trajectory of the first light phenomenon. These lights moved at constant speed and deviation on a straight trajectory. The phenomenon disappeared, hidden by the landscape. The witness had the luck to observe a passage of the International Space Station (ISS) with a magnitude of -3 (like Venus) and the Japanese space cargo HTV with a magnitude of 3, a few hours before its docking with the ISS (see Calsky log and Canadian Space Agency website), on the same orbit, a few degrees apart. This case is classified "A" as perfectly identified observation of a very rare ISS + Japanese space cargo HTV passage. Note that the witness described the object as large as the Moon, and for an observation duration of 45 seconds: this type of estimation error is not uncommon for surprising phenomena.
