Description: On March 26, 2016 (not September 26 as indicated in the free text) between 9:50 and 9:55 AM, a witness from a parking lot observed a bright, spherical object passing over the city. He described a sphere joining two other "balls." The entire group then flew in formation, before a fourth "ball" joined them. They disappeared together out of the witness's view, hidden by nearby buildings. Only one testimony was collected. The witness's description of the observation case initially suggests helicopters. South of the city of GRASSE, approximately 12 km away, in the direction indicated by the witness, there is a helicopter pilot training center located near the CANNES-MANDELIEU-LA NAPOULE airport runways. A phone call to AZUR HELICO confirmed this hypothesis: indeed, the manager stated to the investigator after quick research that "at least 3 aircraft were in flight" with instructors on that Saturday morning, March 26, 2016, at the time of the observation, between 9:30/10:00 AM. The peculiarity perceived by the witness lies in the spherical shape of the observed elements. It should be known that for training purposes, AZUR HELICO has several R 22 and/or CABRI G2 type helicopters in its fleet. These two types of small training helicopters, when seen from the front, take on the appearance of spheres, and perhaps even from the side, given the fineness of their tail. Their color is quite dark, and the sun illuminating them can give them the "shiny" appearance described by the witness. If the movement speed seems fast to the witness, it should be noted that these craft fly at 180 km/h. The absence of noise is also an element that seems to have intrigued: however, the witness was located near the Grasse hospital, a not particularly noisy place ("traffic noises from vehicles"), but undoubtedly loud enough to not perceive the fineness of some sounds. Moreover, the helicopters were a priori well to the south, so their sound could be much more attenuated (unlike a witness located directly below), and not carried by the wind. Additionally, noise pollution limits are in place in this densely populated area, and R 22 or CABRI G2 helicopters are not particularly loud. Furthermore, if there had been something else in the sky at that time, it would not have been missing from mention by other people (populated area) and especially by the flight instructors. However, this is not the case. Finally, the observation time of 15 seconds is really very short to properly grasp the phenomenon, like a flight of helicopters moving away in the direction of Mandelieu-la-Napoule and disappearing from view, hidden by nearby buildings. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: certain misperception with a helicopter flight.
