Description: On February 22, 2014, around 7:45 PM, a resident of MARQUETTE-LEZ-LILLE (59) returns home from grocery shopping. While parking in front of her home, she spots a bright point of light moving to the left in the sky. After approximately two minutes of observation, the PAN disappears from the witness's field of vision, who then goes towards her garden. She sees the PAN again and notices a second PAN moving in the opposite direction. The two PAN cross paths. While the first continues to move away, the second stops and remains stationary in the sky until clouds cover the sky, between 10 minutes and an hour after the observation began. No other testimonies were collected. Of average strangeness but sufficient consistency (many coherent and precise details allowing the case to be credible), this case turns out to be a misidentification with a perfectly known astronautic object, the International Space Station (ISS), crossing the trajectory of a particularly notable star, Sirius. The ISS was visible at an hour very close to the one given by the witness, on a trajectory perfectly consistent with that of the PAN. The close proximity during its trajectory to the star Sirius could have created the illusion, for a short period of time, of a reverse movement of that star. This is not the visual perception of the witness that is in question, but the interpretation the witness makes of her observation through her feeling (fatigue, surprise, observation in several phases). This case is classified A, misidentification with the ISS crossing the trajectory of the star Sirius.
