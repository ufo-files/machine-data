Summary: Observations of the movement of two dark PAN in the sky: probable observations of balloons.
--- seg-0ec8075f1d44c42f9fab1d5d [needs-review] ---
Description: On May 28, 2023, around 4:00 PM, two witnesses (T1 and T2) observed two PAN in the sky over the commune of Mâron (36). One of the PAN is round-oval in shape and dark in color, and the other is long, thin, and black. The PAN at low altitude are moving on a descending trajectory, slightly "rocking" on themselves. The observations are discontinuous, the witnesses going inside their homes to retrieve their mobile phones in an attempt to take photographs. The observation lasts approximately 1 minute 30 during which a photo will be taken before the PAN disappear from the witnesses' view. The case is of good consistency* for the following reasons:

- Presence of a photograph that could be analyzed by IPACO on several parameters.
- Two dependent testimonies.
- Availability of very cooperative witnesses throughout the investigation.
- Investigation conducted remotely and on-site to obtain all useful data (angular sizes, elevations, azimuths, etc.).

*According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. We explored two main hypotheses (see the investigation report): - Gliders, visible on the radar traces of the National Center for Air Operations of the Air and Space Force, - Balloons. The glider hypothesis quickly proved to be unlikely given the reported position and altitude of the object. - The distance of the gliders from the witness's position means their different angular heights do not correspond to those of the PAN - The area of flight is not within the observation zone. The other hypothesis, that of two balloons drifting with the wind, presents more parameters corresponding to those of PAN:

- The observed and described behavior as a "swinging" or "oscillating" motion,
- Their appearance, which can be compared to two types of balloons, one rather round and the other elongated, dark in color, is available in commerce,
- Their movement is in the direction of the wind,
- The speed of movement is plausible for the distances considered,
- The measurements and calculations made with the image processing software IPACO® are consistent with two balloons of a standard medium size between 60 and 90 cm located at approximately 300/500 meters distance at the end of the observation for altitudes between 120 and 180 meters. The rapid disappearance into a cloud would only be an illusion since the balloons are only perceived in the photograph due to a strong 6.45 zoom. It is therefore logical that the naked eye does not perceive them later. Despite thorough investigations, it was not possible to determine with certainty if the observation corresponded to that of lost or released balloons near the witnesses, for example from a room located in the azimuth from where the PAN appeared, and which had been decorated with balloons for a festive event that day. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable balloon observations.
