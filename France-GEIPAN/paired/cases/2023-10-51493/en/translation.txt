Description: The only witness to this observation case was behind his house on the evening of October 19, 2023, to bring in his chickens when at 8:08 PM he observed bright round objects moving in all directions in the sky. He interrupts his observation and, after returning a few moments later, he notices the presence of three lights on the ground, side by side, and a little further to the right, closer, what appears to be a glowing silhouette, motionless. The witness then observes that the silhouette disappears and the three lights "pass over" him before also disappearing. Two photographs of the last two PANs were taken by the witness. In addition, other photographs taken later in 2023 and at the end of 2024 were later provided to the investigator, on which one or more of these PANs are also visible. No other testimony was collected at GEIPAN (see the investigation report). The consistency* of the case is good. Although the observation had only one witness, several photos of PANs were taken and could be used for the investigation. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The first observed PANs could be satellites, although the lack of consolidated data does not allow for a more formal statement. Regarding the three aligned lights forming PAN 1, we were able to determine with certainty through a geographical and photographic analysis that they were the lights of wind turbines located about fifteen kilometers from the witness. The persistence of these lights in the same place and over time in photographs taken at different times, as well as their appearance (color, flash) are also elements that support the hypothesis. Finally, regarding the last ground phenomenon observed by the witness and systematically photographed, resembling a "silhouette" ( PAN 2), although we were unable to determine its exact nature, everything indicates that it is probably a luminous object (the nature of which remains to be defined) or a person (using a flashlight?), having no connection with an aerospace object. It was impossible during the investigation to characterize the phenomenon in a certain way. The GEIPAN classifies these multiple observations as follows:

- Observation of the first PANs moving in all directions (PANs not photographed): "C", the lack of data does not allow us to confirm or deny the hypothesis of satellites.

- Observation of three PANs aligned on the ground (PAN 1 photographed): "A", observation of wind turbine lights.

- Observation of the phenomenon on the ground, in the form of a luminous silhouette (PAN 2 photographed): "C", although some elements suggest a probable illuminated object on the ground, it was impossible to properly characterize this phenomenon.
