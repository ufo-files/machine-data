Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more accurate in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named HERSERANGE (54) 1979 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been re-examined. On December 18, 1979, starting at 2:14 a.m., five family members observed for 15 minutes three glowing disks (the color varied among witnesses from white to orange-red) vertically above a school. They would dim and brighten. For some witnesses, these disks had an ascending movement. This observation is characterized, on the one hand, by a strong perceived strangeness among the witnesses, as indicated by the repeated use of terms like OVNI and saucers, and, on the other hand, by the extreme sparsity of the testimonies. It involved three glowing disks that dimmed and brightened (in a direction passing through the vertical of the school). This is the essential (and almost one-sentence) and common (the sentence is nearly repeated) information in the five statements from the same family, the parents and the three children aged 13 to 10. The little additional information varies among witnesses. The father and mother (T1 and T2) indicate (or feel, in the case of T2) that the disks were ascending into the sky. Two children, T3 and T5, do not specify any movement, while T4 is the only one to mention that one of the disks separated from the others. The color of the disks can also vary among witnesses, ranging from orange to white or unspecified. Nothing is known about their position in the sky, angular size, orientation, or the clear or precise outline of these lights (which would be an indication of their materiality or lack thereof), etc... With so few elements, it is not possible to seek an explanation. One might regret that the Gendarmerie or the GEIPAN did not conduct additional interrogations or reconstructions, as it is now illusory to do so 40 years later. On the other hand, this observation stands out for its inherent lack of consistency that did not encourage further investigation. When strangeness is strong, testimonies are rarely concise, and even less duplicated. The most singular aspect, compared to the body of GEIPAN testimonies, is the absence of any indication regarding the conditions under which the PAN disappeared. The mother and the three children went back to bed while the PAN were still there! All this raises doubts about the reality of the perceived strangeness. It is also noted that T1 (the family father) had previously made a large number of observations of saucers. These observations and their repetitions may have generated in him a strong emotional charge that explains why he woke up the other witnesses, including the young children, by shouting "saucers." These other witnesses may thus have been more impressed (even if it was not the first time) by T1's feeling than by the observation itself, to the point of becoming solidary with that feeling. This would explain the repetition of minimal descriptions... and that everyone went to bed leaving the father alone with the observation. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
