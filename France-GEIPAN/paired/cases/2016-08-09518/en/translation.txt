--- seg-4737a243e98d272872e858b7 [needs-review] ---
Description: On August 03, 2016 at 23:06, a witness and his partner observed the slow and silent movement of two glowing objects (sunflower yellow) following each other. One of the witnesses takes a photograph with his camera. The objects are moving away towards the South. They fade away, leaving only a red dot visible. The photograph transferred to the computer intrigues them. Only one testimony is collected. The witness experienced two oddities: a first one with a PAN seen in the sky with their own eyes, and a second one even stranger with the photo. The visual observation clearly indicates a flight of Thai lanterns: the characteristics such as color, trajectory (corresponding to the wind direction measured that evening: 300° while the witness locates the arrival of PAN at 330°), the final extinction mode, the appearance that can be cylindrical (like a "tibia"), as well as the date on an summer night. The surprise generated by the photo (depicting glowing flagella around PAN) results from a photo artifact. The operator left everything in automatic mode (flash "on" and 1-second exposure duration), and triggered his device which immediately flashed. The flash stopped very quickly as soon as the light threshold necessary to capture the dark parts of the field (tree branches) was reached, but the image capture continued for the entire second for the brighter elements of the field (here the lantern) that did not need a flash to be captured. Thinking that the exposure was over (confusion is easy between the quasi-instantaneous duration of the flash and the longer exposure duration), the operator stopped properly framing to move, the shutter still open, resulting in these light zigzags or flagella. In this long exposure complement after the flash stop, the dark parts no longer impress the sensor and remain fixed in state at the end of the flash, that's why the witness may think that his device did not move since the tree branches are clear, while it did move for the capture of the lantern. This testimony illustrates the potentially deceptive nature of Thai lanterns and even more so of photographs. It is also illustrative of the chain of mistaken interpretations that surprise or emotion can trigger. Here the camera's blocking and its dating error are experienced and described as related to the strange phenomenon in the sky. The origin of the PAN is a Thai lantern, but the key element of the strangeness experienced by the witness is rather the photograph, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: photographic artifact.
