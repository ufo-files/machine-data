Summary: Long observation of two colored light phenomena in the sky: probable observation of stars.

Description: On April 13, 2015, from 10:10 PM to 11:30 PM, a witness observed two luminous, colored phenomena in the sky with the naked eye and then with binoculars. The observation for over an hour of luminous objects in the sky, multicolored, moving slowly, according to the rotation of the Earth (15°/hour) is typical of star observation. In this case, the directions given by the witness are not very precise, but they seem to point to the stars Procyon and Capella, which are, however, less bright than Sirius, or more specifically, less bright than the planets Venus and Jupiter (see the sky maps created with the "Stellarium" software for Albi at 10:10 PM and 11:10 PM). The unusual aspect of the observation lies in the description of changing colors, and the shape of a sea urchin test (shell). The Meteociel report for today indicates high temperatures for the season (27°): see the weather. These significant temperature differences induce turbulence in the atmosphere, causing intense twinkling, particularly visible through binoculars. It is these somewhat peculiar atmospheric conditions that gave the observed stars this appearance. This type of scintillating occurs also in strong winds, especially if it is hot. It should also be noted that the witness observed with 10x25 hiking binoculars, with a high magnification of 10, but a narrow aperture of 25mm, which are not well-suited for astronomical observation (one prefers lower magnification of 7 or 8, and a larger aperture of 50mm for that). These twins can induce shape and color aberrations in these particular conditions; the high magnification makes a stable, hand-held view very difficult. The GEIPAN categorizes this case as B: star observation. A more precise testimony, positioning the stars among themselves, would have allowed the identified stars to be more accurately determined.
