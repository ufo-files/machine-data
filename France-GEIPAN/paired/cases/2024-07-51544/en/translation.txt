Description: On July 14, 2024, around 04:00, the witness, on the terrace of their residence in Maule (78), observes what they describe as two "balls the size of a star" flying in parallel from the northeast. The objects appear less white than the surrounding stars and slightly reddish. After a few moments, the movements of the PANs (presumed Unidentified Flying Objects) become erratic: they move away and quickly approach, regroup into one, split into four, then regroup again into two, spinning around each other and making sharp turns. The objects also stop for approximately 2 minutes. The observation lasts over 8 minutes until the two PANs are lost from view. The case consistency is low, with only one witness who was unable to take photos of the PANs. According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Several hypotheses were explored in this investigation, one of which particularly caught our attention: the possibility of multiple drones (as detailed in the investigation report). Indeed, the described movements are typical of drones. The reported speed, very fast at times, and the prolonged immobility are also consistent with this hypothesis. However, it would take several highly experienced pilots to perform the described maneuvers, which are quite risky. Several clear areas are located nearby the witness's position, which could have served as take-off and landing points for the drone users. Due to darkness and the observation distance, the shape of these drones is not visible, only the white lights equipping them are, which are typically rather red, green, or even blue. It should be noted, however, that in addition to the rarity of simultaneously using four drones, their users would be breaking two prohibitions: night-time use and flying over an urban area. We contacted the municipalities of Maule (78) and Achères (78), the latter having organized a drone show the previous evening instead of the traditional fireworks display. We also reached out to several drone-operating companies in the area, but received no response. Despite the general appearance and behavior of the PANs resembling drones, their number (4), the complexity of the maneuvers, and the late observation time make this hypothesis difficult to sustain. The absence of data regarding the dimensions, distance, and speed of the PANs prevents further analysis. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "C": Unidentified Phenomenon due to lack of information.
