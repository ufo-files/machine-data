Description: Recent (07/03/2013) testimony of an old observation that the witness cannot remember the exact date (an arbitrary date is given). The observation occurred on a road between La Rochelle (17) and Niort (79) at the entrance to Niort then to Echiré (79). One Saturday night around midnight in late August 1997, a motorist and their passengers were surprised to see in the cloudy sky a white oval mass around which smaller oval shapes were spinning. No particular noise was heard. The observation lasted for several hours from various locations, including the witness's home, who took several photographs with a disposable camera. The witness eventually went to sleep. All the photographs turned out black. Only one testimony has been collected to date. This case of observation of "spinning saucers" in a cloudy sky on a Saturday night for a long duration exhibits all the characteristics of skytracers, these projectors used by nightclubs to signal:

- A fairly typical shape (a set of ovoid masses arranged in a circle or ellipses).
- Typical behavior (continuous rotation around a central point and overall movement by slow scanning or total or partial rotation, with possible stops). A central beam around which various smaller projectors spin.
- Cloudy sky: an ideal medium for projectors that draw shapes that are more or less rounded depending on the angle of incidence and the relief of the clouds.
- And if there is no fog, which is likely in this summer season, the ascending beams remain invisible.
- It's a Saturday night (the most frequent day for sky tracers) and summer vacation period, so more festive than usual.
- The duration of several hours: the discotheques turn on the sky tracers around 10 PM, until the early morning.
- Sky tracers were very trendy at that time.
- No noise was heard.
- A white, oval color: the most common for sky tracers.
- A large angular size of the whole.
- The phenomenon visible from several dozen kilometers and the halos' movements over several kilometers. Compatible with the witnesses' movements.
- Failed photography: quite normal as the phenomenon is mobile and not very bright; it would have taken a camera with a large lens and a very sensitive film to obtain a good image of the phenomenon.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": very probable observation of a "skytracer". The impreciseness about the date and the age of the facts do not allow the GEIPAN to determine the precise origin of this projector. See: light animation and light animation.
