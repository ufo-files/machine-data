Summary: Sunset observation of a "steam trail" white-grey in the sky: observation of a condensation trail from a commercial aircraft.
--- seg-28157044ad2e9de1cdd30bbd [needs-review] ---
Description: On November 26, 2020, at sunset, a resident of CERGY (95) takes photos from the window of her apartment (5th floor). She sees "clouds" of strange, vertical shapes. She zooms in on these shapes to try to determine what they are. She first sees a large "spot," then one minute later she sees a second one, and finally, two minutes after that, a third "spot" becomes visible. The witness thinks of an atmospheric reentry of a "meteorite". Only one testimony will be collected on this phenomenon. Of low strangeness and good consistency (single witness but several photos of PAN), this case is the observation of the condensation trail of a commercial aircraft (see the investigation report). The witness favors the hypothesis of a meteorite fall. Several elements allow us to eliminate this hypothesis. First, the duration of observation, since the witness was able to see the PAN for at least 3 minutes. Or, a natural atmospheric reentry, involving the passage of a meteoroid, followed by the fall of a meteorite, lasts only a few seconds. In addition, no meteorite fall or bolide passage was reported in France at the time of the observation. The visual aspect of the PAN in the photos is perfectly typical of a condensation trail from an aircraft, particularly a bi-engine one since it is double and of a similar color to sunlight diffusion. A reconstruction on Flightradar24 shows that flight AF718, involving a Boeing 777 (twin-engine) en route from Paris to Dakar, having just reached its cruising altitude, was heading away to the southwest, a direction consistent with that of the PAN. The case is classified A: observation of a condensation trail from a commercial aircraft.
