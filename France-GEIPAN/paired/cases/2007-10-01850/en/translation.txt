Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D1 observation case named LILLE (59) 01.10.2007 is part of a recent set of cases reviewed (see investigation report). On October 1, 2007, at 07:40, a driver on the A27, near the Belgian border, observed to his right and above flat fields a group of eight white lights arranged four by four, which he perceived as "a delta wing." It continues to roll, then parks on the emergency lane. The PAN then appears stationary. The witness made a bad manipulation on his phone when he tried to take a photo of the PAN. After a few seconds, the PAN inclines and then moves away, disappearing. Only one testimony will be collected. The PAN observed by the witness is very likely the result of powerful lights mounted on two poles illuminating a stadium belonging to the Luchin domain. The accidental alignment and position near a row of trees led the witness to believe they were seeing a single object with lights in stationary flight at low altitude. The renovation of the Luchin domain having been inaugurated about two weeks earlier, it is likely that lighting tests took place before the first matches or training sessions on the concerned field. The poor observation conditions and the intense brightness of the lights contributed to the witness not being able to observe the structure of the pylons supporting these bright lights. This conclusion is based on the following elements: - A complete re-examination of the 2008 initial investigation of GEIPAN, which contained errors in the witness's placement and observation direction, leading to inaccurate estimates of the azimuths and dimensions of PAN. The presence of a line of trees in the distance, in the direction where the lights are visible, also likely contributed to this confusion. These erroneous witness statements may be due to the time lag between the observation and the first encounter with the witness (4 months) and between that encounter and the writing of the investigation report (5 months); delays that are conducive to creating false memories; - after corrections and verifications, it appears that the entire observation actually took place on the straight of the highway, according to an angle of approximately 30° relative to the direction of the witness's movement. Given the limited visibility due to natural obstacles (vegetation), we were able to deduce two probable positions: one near kilometer 4 (from the Belgian border) where the observation began, and a second near kilometer 3, where the witness stopped; - observation conditions were poor as it was raining during the observation, horizontal visibility was poor (around 3 to 4 km), and we were at twilight, just before sunrise. These conditions meant that the sky was likely still very dark and the witness's visibility on the highway was poor, with distant landscape elements not or barely visible; - the Luchin domain, purchased by LOSC (Lille football club) in 2003, was in the final phase of renovation in the autumn of 2007. However, the lighting poles were already in place, and it is likely that operational tests or maintenance had been conducted prior to the first matches or training sessions after the inauguration; - from kilometer 4, the view is completely unobstructed and flies over fields without obstacles until the domain. This visibility is permanent until around kilometer 3. - The witness begins his observation around kilometer 4, from the moment the view is clear to the right. At this moment, it happens that two pylons are visually aligned from this location; - the white color of the lights matches the witness's description; - the pylons in the field are 25 meters tall and support lights arranged horizontally in rows of 3, 4, 6, or 8. The poles observed aligned at the beginning of the observation support 7 and 3 lights, and their visibility from the witness's position is left to right respectively 4 and 3, in number and alignment exactly as the witness represented the lights of PAN in his drawing; - the lights in question are visible just above a tree line, just as PAN is; - the witness then stops to observe better, about one kilometer further. The gap between the pylons is visually larger at this moment, but it does not concern the witness who still cannot distinguish the pylons supporting the lights, probably due to their intense brightness preventing the distinction of details, and the poor observation conditions. The PAN appears larger to him, which leads him to believe it has not moved from its position as he himself approached the area and thinks he is dealing with a single object. He was still able to discern the metallic structure of the spotlights, which he described as being "the dark gray or black device"; - the flashing of one of the PAN lights could be explained by a malfunction of one of the spotlights, as the stadium lighting was in testing phase; - the PAN was described as too large. But the estimate is highly questionable as it was reconstructed (on-site investigation) several months after the observation and based on an initial incorrect location by the witness. On the other hand, we know that witnesses naturally tend to overestimate apparent dimensions; - the movement during the loss of sight by the witness is difficult to explain but it is a sequence lacking in precision and coherence. If the movement axis is purely one of receding (as implied by statements such as "perpendicular to the axis of the planes"), it could be a classic illusion created by the gradual dimming of lights that the witness logically interprets as the PAN moving away. On the other hand, if we consider the sketch with lateral displacement (which is no longer perpendicular to the aircraft trajectories!), there is no explanation; - the description of the final inclination of the lights is less ambiguous and ultimately constitutes the only source of strangeness within the framework of this hypothesis. The consistency is average: one single witness, a first investigation marred by incorrect position measurements, points lacking in precision especially regarding how the PAN disappears, no photographs. Given the exact correspondence on many crucial points, the only divergence, regarding the inclination of the lights, which only appeared during the disappearance, is not sufficient to change the very probable nature of the explanation. This divergence can therefore be attributed to a poor perception by the witness (optical illusion, poor observation conditions, etc.). As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: very probable observation of a set of light spots observed at a distance under poor conditions.
