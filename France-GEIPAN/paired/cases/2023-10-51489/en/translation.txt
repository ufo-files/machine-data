**Description:** On October 29, 2023, at 4:55 PM, the witness, at their home in Fontainebleau (77), observed a "ballet of four bright lights" resembling four white and bright stars in motion. This observation lasted approximately three minutes during which the bright points moved erratically, without any apparent logic. At the end of these three minutes, the lights suddenly disappeared in an instant. Accompanying their testimony, the witness provided a reconstructed photo, taken from the observation location, where they reproduced the PAN observed. The absence of additional witnesses, such as photos or videos of the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s, makes the case consistency average according to the criteria of GEIPAN, which defines consistency as the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.

A remote investigation, complemented by a telephone interview with the witness, allowed several hypotheses to be explored, with only one being retained: a flight of herons (see the investigation report). Although difficult to formally demonstrate, this hypothesis offers a plausible explanation for most of the observed characteristics of the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s:

- The color and apparent erratic trajectory of the birds can correspond to the witness's description of "four moving large stars."
- The particular lighting conditions of the evening can also explain this observation: the weather was cloudy with breaks in the clouds. The witness specified that the phenomenon occurred within one of these breaks. White birds, illuminated by the sun's rays, may have appeared striking in contrast with the dark gray background.
- The sudden disappearance of the phenomenon can be attributed to the birds passing through a shaded area, causing a loss of visibility due to distance.
- Finally, in the direction indicated by the witness, there are two wet areas housing colonies of waders, such as herons.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as category B: probable flight of herons within a light beam between two clouds.
