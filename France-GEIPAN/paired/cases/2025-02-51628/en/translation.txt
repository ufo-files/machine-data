Description: On February 25, 2025, at 7:40 PM, the witness and his daughter observed from the terrace of an apartment building in Saint-Pierre (974) a luminous phenomenon in a clear night sky, in a northwest direction. The observation concerns a group of 5 to 6 white bright points located in a restricted area of the sky, presenting an intensity comparable to that of the brightest stars. These points flash in what appears to be a random manner. The group follows an evolving trajectory, moving successively to the right, then down, and finally to the left, at a moderate but clearly perceptible speed. Using binoculars does not provide additional information: the bright points appear slightly enlarged, with no discernible structure between them, suggesting a distant origin rather than a nearby phenomenon. No noise is reported during the observation. As the witness was preparing to record the event, all the points suddenly disappear, except for one that continues to blink. As soon as the video recording begins, it stops moving, flickers four times at regular intervals, and then disappears completely. The analysis leads the witness to search for similar cases on the GEIPAN site. He consults a report from July 23, 2024, but finds no record that precisely matches his observation. He therefore decides to testify and provides precise geolocation and orientation data to allow for the verification of possible known object presences in the area at the time of the events. The case consistency is good with 2 witnesses (although only one filled out a GEIPAN questionnaire), clear indications, and a video. The weather conditions at Pierrefonds Airport in Saint-Pierre were good during the observation, with a calm wind (approximately 14 km/h from the northwest at 19:00 and decreasing subsequently as it shifted to the north) and a completely clear sky (see Annex 1). After verifying night aeronautic activities, the 2nd RPIMA (2nd Marine Parachute Infantry Regiment) confirmed conducting night drops between 7 PM and 8 PM with 3 drops of paratroopers under canopy, for a total of 10 military personnel: "On 2/25, we jumped at night in a Pilatus over the regiment's stadium. There were 03 planes between 7:00 PM and 8:00 PM. - 01st plane 04 skydivers - 02nd plane 03 skydivers - 03rd plane 03 skydivers. The jumpers are operational droppers with GPS and night vision binoculars. They turn on the strobe (flash) lights in the plane and turn them off on the ground. The Pilatus is not equipped with ADS-B transponder*, so it is not visible on ADS-B aircraft tracking tools. The jumps were made over Saint-Pierre (therefore in the direction of observation of the witness, orientation to the north) and in the direction of the stadium of the 2nd RPIMA located to the northwest of the witness's position and near the Pierrefonds airport, hence the leftward movement at the end of the observation. The disappearance of the lights can be explained by loss of visibility behind landscape elements or a different orientation of the parachutists. The correspondence between the observation and the parachute activity carried out during the same time slot, for several "leapers" forming the flickering "cluster" described by the witness and also seen by his daughter, leads to classifying this phenomenon as identified. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": parachute jumper observation. *Consistency: According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. *ADS-B (Automatic-dependent surveillance-broadcast): surveillance system for air traffic control.
