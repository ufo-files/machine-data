Summary: Observation of the appearance and movement of two non-flashing light points in the night sky: observation of two fancy Mylar balloons equipped with LED lights.
--- seg-c677c331b0fcd028437db440 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On January 31, 2022, around 8:45 PM, the witness observed a first bright point in the sky at a relatively high altitude, moving faster than a "classic" plane and not blinking. They observed it for nearly 3-4 minutes until it dimmed as if gaining altitude and then disappeared. About two minutes later, they observed another point exhibiting the same behavior. The consistency of this case is average, with a single witness and no photos or videos of the PAN. In the investigation report (see attached document), we showed that the witness likely observed two illuminated party balloons moved by the wind. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Generally moving in a straight line with the wind, but the orientation differs slightly from the data provided by the witness. At night without references, the initial and final azimuths of the PAN's movement are difficult for the witness to estimate, explaining this difference.
- White color, common for such balloons, and non-varying brightness, unlike celestial lanterns.
- Point-like shape, the distance of observation not allowing the real shape of the balloons to be distinguished, which are modest in size.
- Balloons are often released in small groups. Observing two individuals a few minutes apart is entirely plausible. The witness's perceived strangeness is due to the perception of the PAN's ascent and high speed. We have shown that the perception of the PAN's disappearance can give this impression of gaining altitude, and it is also very difficult to estimate speed at night without references. Although this type of balloon is usually released on weekends in spring or summer outdoors, a single release remains possible during the week, on the occasion of a local or family celebration, for example.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": observation of two fantasy helium balloons equipped with an LED light.
