Description: On March 8, 2013, around 7 PM, a driver was intrigued by an intense light in the sky coming from a round shape moving towards the north and disappearing in a fraction of a second. No other testimonies were collected. Of average strangeness and weak consistency (single witness, short-duration observation), this case could be a possible misperception with a balloon flying with the wind, or a car headlight reflection on an electric line (see Investigation Report). However, the low quality of the testimony and some contradictory details (weather, differences between the two sketches of PAN) prevent formal identification of PAN. It is not the visual perception of the witness that is in question, but the interpretation the witness makes of his observation through his feeling (surprise, short-duration observation). This case is classified C, due to lack of data, although hypotheses are plausible.
