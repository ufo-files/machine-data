Description: On September 2, 2016 at 5:20 AM, a witness observed a very white and very bright stationary ball in the sky. The observation lasts 5 to 6 minutes in the direction of Saint-Martin-d'Août. The witness goes to look for another person and the phenomenon seems to fade out spontaneously. Only one testimony is collected. The witness was surprised by Sirius, which frequently happens because it is a very bright white star, usually observed near the horizon (when visible), which can give the impression of a glowing ball (optical effect and/or halo conferred by the atmosphere). Here, the witness was looking in the direction of the star (Saint-Martin-d'Aout is in the southeast direction like Sirius) and was looking a little above the horizon (Sirius is at 5° elevation). The time it took for the witness to go and find his friend, Sirius could have effectively disappeared (angular displacement of 3° in 10m) behind a cloud (the witness describes a black night) or landscape features on the horizon. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of Sirius.
