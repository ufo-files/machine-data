Description: On January 5, 2018 at 10:35 PM, a resident of CONTAY (80) is on the phone when he sees through the living room windows, two round lights of orange color flying in the clear sky in an east/west direction. The witness goes to the first floor and takes a pair of binoculars to observe the PAN. The total duration of the observation is 90 seconds. No further testimony will be collected on this phenomenon. Of strange and weak consistency (single witness, no photo), this case turns out to be a very probable misperception with 2 Thai lanterns (see the investigation report). The description of the PAN is perfectly typical of this type of misidentification, even more so as the witness compares the observed objects to 2 round, orange gas lamps. The trajectory of the PAN is consistent with that of the wind, whose speed explains the short duration of the observation. The authors of the probable release could not be found. The witness refutes the explanatory hypothesis, very likely because of the overestimation of the actual distance separating him from the observed objects. It should be noted that it is impossible to visually estimate the distance of an object in the sky, especially at night and if the observed object is unknown. The witness very likely estimated the distance to the PAN based on his own aeronautical references, interpreting the observed objects as being the size of aircraft. However, Thai lanterns are much smaller than aircraft, and the witness could have greatly overestimated the actual distance between them. The case is classified B: probable misidentification with two Thai lanterns.
