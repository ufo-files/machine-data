Summary: Observation of the silent movement of 8 orange-colored objects in the night sky: probable observation of the passage of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-40122ae5e8d5c8ff63b4ea11 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On September 13, 2015, from 1:15 to 1:30 AM, a witness observed from their balcony the silent passage of 8 bright (successively appearing) orange lights moving from southeast to northwest across a clear sky. The objects disappeared one after the other. The witness took photographs. Only one report of this phenomenon was collected. The observation of orange balls crossing the sky in a few minutes, on a weekend night, strongly suggests Thai lanterns, likely released nearby after a local or private party. The witness took several photos; they turned out out of focus (focus blur, very common in night photos under these conditions) and only show a blurry orange circle. However, they confirm the reality of the phenomenon, and the color of the phenomenon is entirely consistent with that of Thai lanterns. The movement was observed from southeast to northwest, which is compatible with an east wind, shifting to southeast during the night, as recorded at Toulouse Blagnac (Météociel record). The very regular movement of the objects in the sky is perfectly normal for an object carried by a light breeze; if the objects fly over industrial areas with strong ventilation or large heat sources, the trajectory will of course be irregular. Since lanterns are often released one after the other, after the same delay corresponding to the time of the burner ignition, the result is a very organized line of flying lights. Due to their low intrinsic brightness, Thai lanterns are often perceived as farther away than they actually are, and thus faster. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of Thai lanterns.
