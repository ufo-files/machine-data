Summary: Observation of the passage of three objects, primarily spherical in shape, moving in a triangular formation and appearing to reflect sunlight: probable observation of Mylar envelope balloons.

Description: A witness (T1) and his spouse, in their garden located in the commune of Puilboreau (17), observed the presence of a light comparable to Venus, located to the northeast at an elevation of 45°. Initially static, it then moved very slowly to the left. A few moments later, T1 observed in the same area of the sky the presence of three other unidentified airborne phenomena (PAN), spherical in shape, appearing metallic and reflecting sunlight. These moved in a triangular formation, traveling east to west at a speed comparable to an aircraft at altitude. No position lights, flashing or condensation trails were visible. Consistency* is average according to the criteria of GEIPAN, with only one of the two witnesses having completed the QT and no photos or videos of the PANs. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Among the hypotheses explored (see the investigation report), only the hypothesis of Mylar envelope balloons was retained, due to their characteristics compatible with the description of the PANs given by T1: - Spherical shape and highly reflective metallic color, - Straight-line movement with the wind. These balloons could have been released or lost from the Puilboreau rugby stadium during the celebration of their victory, following a match that ended around 4:55 PM (total duration of 1 hour 40 minutes), although the observation started nearly two hours later. It is also possible they were released earlier during a private celebration. The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as "B": probable Mylar envelope balloons.
