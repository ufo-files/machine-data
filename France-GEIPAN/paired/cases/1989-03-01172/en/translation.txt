Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type "B" cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case (previously named Bonnières-Sur-Seine (78) 13.03.1989) is part of cases classified as "B" in 1989 by the SEPRA as "spectacular natural phenomenon of light propagation." On March 13, 1989, between 10:30 PM and 11:28 PM, several witnesses in Morbihan (56) and Yvelines (78) observed a large-scale light phenomenon in cloud layers. The observed colors were very varied, ranging from yellow, green, to red. The gendarmes who conducted the investigations drew parallels between this phenomenon and a solar eruption that occurred at the same time and was reported by the media. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical phenomenon: the aurora borealis. Furthermore, an aurora borealis was indeed observed exceptionally in France on March 13, 1989 (see investigation notes). It is perfectly normal that the witnesses did not immediately recognize an aurora borealis, as this astronomical phenomenon is rarely observed from Metropolitan France (with an occurrence rate of about once every ten years). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of low strangeness is classified as PAN A, observation of an aurora borealis.
