Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. The observation case previously classified as D and named SAINT-MARTIN-DE-CRAU (13) 24.08.1980 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined (see the investigation report). On August 24, 1980, around 8:00 PM, two witnesses in their garden were intrigued by the presence of an object in the air. The witnesses describe an object in the shape of a saucer, of a "polished aluminum" color. The upper part of the object seems to oscillate, while the lower part appears fixed. The object descends vertically then slowly and heads towards a field, crossing RN113. The object changes direction towards the North on a horizontal trajectory, and the witness loses sight of it. No noise or smoke was noticed. No other testimony was collected by the gendarmerie. During the on-site investigation by the gendarmerie, no particular findings were made, as the object never landed on the ground. The movement of the PAN in line with the wind direction could suggest a type of balloon, possibly of Mylar type, which could explain the "aluminum polished" color effect. Mylar balloons existed at that time. However, the description given by the witnesses and the sketches made by the gendarmes (not by the witnesses) do not allow us to determine what type of balloon it could be. The shape is indeed relatively complex, with original elements. In order to have a better understanding, confirm or refute the balloon hypothesis or any other, we would have liked to have had more concrete elements that are not included in the gendarmes' investigation, such as: - Angular measurements of the PAN. - A drawing made by the witnesses, not by the gendarmes. - Estimates of angular heights at the beginning, middle, and end of the observation. - Color measurements using, for example, a Pantone color chart. Although a balloon hypothesis can be formulated, the lack of consistency in the testimony, despite the on-site investigation by the gendarmes, does not allow us to draw a conclusion. Consequently, Geipan classifies the case as "C": lack of reliable information.
