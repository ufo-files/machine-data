Description: On September 1, 2025, at 04:50, the witness, in their garden, observed in the clear, starry sky to the east an unidentified airborne phenomenon (PAN) that was very bright, spherical in shape, and white in color. The object appeared to grow in size before disappearing after approximately 2 minutes and 20 seconds, still heading east but at a lower altitude. The case has average consistency, with a single witness and no photos or videos of the PAN. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The strangeness is formed for the witness by the strong brightness of the PAN and its rapid disappearance. A radar request was made to the Air Center for Planning and Conducting Air Defense Operations (CAPCODA) on September 15, 2025, the response was provided on September 16. No radar trace corresponds to the observation, that is, in the east sector in relation to the witness. The hypothesis of observing a "satellite flare" (a brief flash of light on a satellite caused by the Sun) was studied. It is corroborated by the following elements:

- Spherical shape, white color, and high brightness, all compatible with such a flare,
- Observation duration of 2 minutes 20 seconds: the brightness of a flare can increase progressively, reach a maximum before suddenly decreasing to disappear,
- The Operational Center for Space Surveillance (COSE) was consulted on this case to provide satellite tracks that could be in the sky at the relevant azimuths and elevations around 04:50. Two Starlink satellites were indeed present at exactly these times and locations with corresponding final visibility, and a descending trajectory towards the horizon, as observed by the witness (annex 1). It is, however, impossible to determine which of the two could have produced this flare, given their spatial and temporal proximity. The GEIPAN classifies the case as 'B': probable observation of a 'flare' (briefly lit satellite by the Sun) from a satellite.
