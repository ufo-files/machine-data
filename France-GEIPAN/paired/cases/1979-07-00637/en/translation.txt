Summary: Observation of a red-orange mass in the sky: astronomical observation of the Moon at sunset.
--- seg-3a9e1be58629416c93b22cb6 [needs-review] ---
Description: This observation case was classified as "C" in 2008 by the GEIPAN and has undergone a new review (see document: Investigation Notes). On July 4, 1979, at 2:00 a.m., a person returning home noticed a large, orange-red mass shaped like a shell. The frightened witness returned home and observed the phenomenon for 2 minutes, hearing no sounds. The mass then disappeared. No other witnesses were found for this phenomenon. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the Moon at sunset. Additionally, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky. The issue is not the witness's visual perception but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, fear). In the current GEIPAN classification, this case of weak strangeness is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception of the Moon.
