Summary: Observation of the silent movement of a dozen white points in the early morning sky: likely observation of birds.
--- seg-31c8610c864c9dd0e596ab29 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 23, 2013 at 7:30 a.m., a witness was surprised by the sight of a dozen glowing points moving silently across the sky, in three successive waves. He totally excludes the possibility that it could be Thai lanterns, fighter planes, or any reflection of light. No other testimony was collected. The probable explanation is that of a flock of migratory birds. At this time of year, many birds migrate from the North to the South by using the main migration corridors. One of these corridors passes to the East of Callac. In general, migratory birds fly at altitudes between 100m and 400m. However, against a headwind – as is the case here – birds reduce their flight altitude, making them more visible at dusk or dawn. In particular, the Barnacle Geese (oies) migrate at this time, from the North to the Gulf of Morbihan. Their plumage is essentially brown, black, and white. At night, illuminated by the Moon, only the white part remains visible, and the yellowish color described by the witness corresponds to the color of the light reflected by the Moon. The dark wings are not visible in the darkness. The observation is therefore not strange. The only slight oddity comes from the last light, more intense than the others (but it is noted that this point was not present at the time of the observation and questionnaire and was mentioned by the witness during the investigation, which could also result from a false memory phenomenon 3 years later). This observation is of average consistency at first (one witness, no photos or videos) and strengthened by a cognitive interview. The GEIPAN therefore classifies the case as B, probable bird observation (several groups of Canada geese or other migratory birds).
