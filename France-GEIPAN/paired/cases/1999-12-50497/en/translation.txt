**Description:** The GEIPAN is reviewing old cases to improve the accuracy of conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this review sometimes results in a change in classification. This review focuses on two observation cases previously classified as D and named BROYES (51) on 10.01.2000 and SOMMESOUS (51) on 31.12.1999.

On January 10, 2000, between 9:00 and 9:05 PM, a driver on D39 near the water tower in Broyes (51) was suddenly totally blinded by a very bright light that entered the car through the sunroof. The engine and car radio abruptly stopped, but the ignition remained on. According to the witness, after a minute, the phenomenon dissipated, the car radio restarted, and he was able to drive home, terrified. Following a phone call from the Operational Center of the Gendarmerie, officers immediately visited the witness's home, who was severely shocked, then the observation site. They noted that the sky was clear and starry (shooting stars) and that air traffic was significant and visible (flaring lights). The temperature was winter-like but without freezing. No power line disruptions were observed along the road. Additionally, at this location, public lighting is non-existent. No flights were reported that night, according to military sources.

On-site investigation by SEPRA and a car reconstruction revealed that the observation lasted between 15 and 30 seconds, not a minute as initially reported by the witness. The Gendarmerie also found another witness who observed a similar phenomenon but several days earlier.

On December 31, 1999, between 7:30 and 8:00 PM, a driver on N4 near Sommesous (51) was blinded by a white flash-like light. No noise was heard. All dashboard indicators lit up, and the car's engine abruptly stopped, putting it into neutral. The engine started again quickly on its own. A mechanic's check on the battery terminals found nothing unusual.

We have two observation cases with strong spatial (a few kilometers) and temporal (10 days) proximity, appearing to be highly similar (see the single investigation report for both cases):

- A non-localized light (or flash) perceived by a car driver;
- Engine stop and dashboard disruptions.

The BROYES (51) case on 10.01.2000 is stranger as the light is blinding and enters the vehicle. No physical hypothesis can be considered given the unusual description. We can only speculate about induced or amplified strangeness by the witness himself, but we have no evidence to support this:

- The witness had watched the TV series *The X-Files* shortly before the incident, during the broadcast of season 4, which includes a scene where a UFO flies over a vehicle and illuminates it with a dazzling white light. The witness may have experienced genuine strangeness that night and been influenced, consciously or unconsciously, by science fiction during the memory or narration of the events.
- The young witness, living with his parents, may have been traumatized by an event unrelated to the UFO and, for reasons related to his occasional drug use (admitted by him) or otherwise, was unable to justify his state to his parents upon returning home without inventing an experience directly inspired by *The X-Files*.

For the SOMMESOUS (51) case on 31.12.1999, physical hypotheses (meteor, flash photograph, or firework spark a few hours before the turn of the millennium) lack supporting evidence. The consistency of these observation cases is POOR.

**Case of BROYES (51) on 10.01.2000:**

- In terms of information: While an on-site investigation was conducted by the Gendarmerie and SEPRA (the GEIPAN at the time) shortly after the event, we only have a brief report of this investigation. No audio, video, or written records of various interviews and reconstructions during this investigation are available in the file.
- In terms of reliability: One can question the influence of science fiction given the similarity of the narrative to a scene from *The X-Files* (as mentioned above). One can also question the impact of occasional drug use (as noted above).

**Case of SOMMESOUS (51) on 31.12.1999:**

- In terms of information: The Gendarmerie conducted no investigation beyond taking the witness's statement because SEPRA did not request or investigate further, and the Gendarmerie did not perceive sufficient interest from SEPRA to investigate on its own.
- In terms of reliability: The statement was not spontaneous. The witness, who had previously heard the narrative of the BROYES (51) case, may have been naturally impressed by the story and made a connection with his experience, enhancing its strangeness and influencing (unknowingly) certain aspects of his statement. Only a thorough on-site investigation could have:

- Explored and possibly validated one of the physical hypotheses (as mentioned above) specific to this case;
- Alternatively, confirmed a sufficiently extensive range of similarities between the two cases to consider them as arising from the same phenomenon and, from there, cross-referencing information:

- To potentially propose hypotheses to explain this phenomenon;
- At a minimum, increasing the consistency of this observation to two reliable testimonies.

The strangeness of both cases, though unequal, is significant. The consistency is too low to support an unexplained character for either case or both combined. Therefore, GEIPAN classifies both cases as C: Lack of reliable information.
