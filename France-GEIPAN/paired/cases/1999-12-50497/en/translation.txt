Description: The GEIPAN is re-examining old cases to be more relevant in conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes results in a change in classification. This present review concerns two observation cases previously classified D and named BROYES (51) 10.01.2000 and SOMMESOUS (51) on 31.12.1999. On January 10, 2000, between 9:00 and 9:05 PM, a driver on D39 near the water tower in Broyes (51) was suddenly totally blinded by a very bright light that entered the car through the sunroof. The engine and the car stereo abruptly stop, but the ignition remains on. According to the witness, after one minute, the phenomenon dissipates, the car radio comes back on, and he is able to start his car and drive home, terrified. Following a telephone call from the Operational Center of the Gendarmerie, the gendarmes immediately go to the home of the greatly shocked witness and then to the site of the observation. They observe that the sky is clear and heavily star-studded (meteors) and that air traffic is heavy and visible (flashing lights). The temperature is winter-like but without freezing. No disruption of the power line along the road was observed. Moreover, at this location, public lighting is non-existent. From the military source, no flight is reported that evening. The on-site investigation of SEPRA and the car reconstruction will establish that the observation lasted between 15 and 30 seconds at most, rather than a minute as in the witness's initial report. During the investigation, the gendarmerie will find another witness who observed a similar phenomenon but a few days earlier. On December 31, 1999, between 7:30 PM and 8:00 PM, a driver traveling on the N4 highway at Sommesous (51) was blinded by a white-hot flash-like glow. No noise is heard. All dashboard indicators light up and the car's engine abruptly stops, putting the car in neutral. The engine restarts by itself quickly. A mechanic's check on the battery terminals, initiated by the witness, revealed nothing unusual. We have two observation cases with high spatial (a few km) and temporal (10 days) proximity that appear to have strong similarities (see the single investigation report for both cases):

- Undetermined (or flash) light perceived by the driver of a car;
- Engine shutdown, dashboard disturbances. The BROYES (51) case (10.01.2000) is stranger in that the light is blinding and enters the vehicle. No physical hypothesis can be considered given how strange the description is. We can only formulate the hypothesis of induced or enhanced strangeness by the witness himself, but we have no elements to weigh this: - The witness watched the TV series X-Files shortly before the events, at a time when M6 was broadcasting season 4, which includes a scene where a PAN flies over a vehicle and illuminates it with a dazzling white light. The witness may have encountered a genuine oddity that evening and been influenced, consciously or unconsciously, by science fiction during the memorization or recounting of the events; - the young witness, living with his parents, may have been traumatized by an event unrelated to the PAN and, for a reason related to his occasional drug use (admitted by himself) or for any other reason, be unable to justify his state to his parents upon his return home other than by inventing an experience directly inspired by The X-Files. For the case of SOMMESOUS (51) on 12/31/1999, physical hypotheses (meteor, photographic flash, or firework spark a few hours before the turn of the millennium) have no elements to weigh them. The consistency of these observation cases is POOR. BROYES (51) case on 10.01.2000: - In terms of information, although an investigation was conducted on-site by the gendarmerie and the SEPRA (GEIPAN at the time) shortly after the events, we only have a brief report of this investigation. No audio, video, or written records of the various interviews and reconstructions that took place during this investigation are included in the file; in terms of reliability, one may question the possible influence of science fiction given the similarity of the account to a scene from the X-Files series (see above). One can also question the influence of occasional drug use (see above). In the case of SOMMESOUS (51) on 12/31/1999:

- In terms of information: The gendarmerie conducted no investigation beyond interviewing the witness because the SEPRA made no requests or inquiries, and the gendarmerie did not perceive enough interest from the SEPRA to investigate independently.

- In terms of reliability: The statement was not spontaneous. The witness, who prior to his testimony had heard the account of the BROYES (51) case, could naturally have been impressed by the narrative and drawn parallels with his own experience, intensifying its strangeness and (unknowingly) influencing certain aspects of his deposition. Only a thorough on-site investigation would have allowed: - to explore and possibly validate one of the physical hypotheses (see above) specific to this case; - or, conversely, to confirm a sufficiently broad field of similarity between the two cases to consider them as stemming from the same phenomenon, and from there to cross-reference information: o to potentially formulate hypotheses to explain this phenomenon; o at a minimum, to increase the consistency of this observation with two witnesses. The strangeness of the two cases, though unequal, is important. The consistency is too weak to support an unexplained character in either case or in both of them. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies both cases as C: lack of reliable information.
