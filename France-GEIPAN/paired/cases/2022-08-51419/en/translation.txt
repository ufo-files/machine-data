Description: On August 21, 2022, at 9:30 PM, four witnesses reported observing from the terrace of a rural house in the commune of Pierrefort (15), several oval-shaped, white-colored light phenomena. These objects moved regularly, traveling in close pairs along a straight, slightly ascending trajectory from northwest to southeast. The observation lasted approximately five minutes during which a video was recorded. No associated sounds were perceived by the witnesses, and the light phenomena disappeared spontaneously without any particular event. The case consistency is good, with multiple witnesses and a video of the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s (the published video is anonymized). However, only one witness provided a statement to GEIPAN. According to GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The description of the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s (oval shape, white color, regular and repetitive movements along the same trajectory) strongly suggests the observation of a light display produced by a skytracer (or skyrose), a projector usually used by nightclubs or to signal festive events (as detailed in the investigation report). The overcast sky at middle altitude is also a favorable factor for the observation, as the clouds serve as a support for the light projected into the sky. Similarly, the absence of a visible beam between the ground projectors and the clouds can be explained by the distance (11 km from the witnesses' location) and excellent horizontal visibility, with few particles suspended in the air. Although we do not have precise information about the model used that evening, the theoretical range of such outdoor projectors is significant (up to over 10 km). An investigation determined that a party was taking place in Paulhac (15), a commune only a dozen kilometers from the observation location, on the evening of August 21, 2022. Contacted by phone, the company that organized the closing dance confirmed the use of a particular type of skytrackers, "lyres," capable of projecting powerful light beams into the sky. The use of these lyres would have started after the observation time of the PAN but an earlier use for testing purposes is entirely plausible. The case is classified as "A," observation of skytracers.
