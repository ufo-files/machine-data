Description: On Monday, October 7, 2013, around 6:30 PM, a driver on the A 63 was intrigued by the movement of a white point in the sky. The witness sees it moving away to the south, disappear, then seems to climb back towards the north. No color flashing indicating an airplane is visible. At 6:30 PM, the setting sun is at an Azimuth of 251° and a Height of 9° 55 '. It illuminates planes laterally; the wings, not illuminated from above or below, appear invisible. Not all planes leave condensation trails in the sky: those that fly below 7000 meters (approximately) do not. According to the Flightradar24 website, there are numerous flights on this axis, most at altitudes of over 10,000 meters, but some at lower altitudes (see attached log). At 6:52 PM (civil time) two planes cross in the observation sector. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of two planes, flying at a medium altitude and illuminated by the setting sun.
