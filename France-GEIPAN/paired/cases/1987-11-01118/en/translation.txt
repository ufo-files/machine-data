**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MONTFORT-SUR-MEU (35) 27.11.1987 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been re-examined. On November 25, 1987, at approximately 8:20 PM (T1), and on November 27, at around 11:30 PM (T2, T3, T4, T5), multiple witnesses observed a bright light phenomenon heading west for an extended period. This bright red object was described as a ball or banana by some witnesses. The object appeared stationary or moved very slowly. No noise was perceived. According to the gendarmerie investigation, several witnesses were interviewed. No suspicious traces were found on the ground in the relevant area by the gendarmerie. No weather balloon was launched in the specified slot from the Rennes Saint-Jacques (35) meteorological station. No helicopter flights were conducted at night during the indicated days. This case is consistent (multiple witnesses), and the descriptions of the PAN observed are very precise (see investigation notes). One hypothesis is considered: astronomical observation. The duration of the observation (up to about a quarter of an hour for both cases), as well as the fact that the PAN is relatively stable or moves very slowly, are initial indicators of this type of misperception. The clear sky also supports this hypothesis. However, other details provided by the observations allow for a definitive explanation of the PAN as a misperception with the Moon:

- The Moon was exactly at sunset in the directions of observation (the Moon is at 222° for the first observation and 245° for the second, while both observations are located near the pylon around 235°).
- The actual movement of the Moon (westward and toward the ground) is also consistent with the descriptions.
- The PAN observed on November 25 and 27 are described as having a large apparent diameter and were quite bright, and (especially for November 27) according to descriptions that are quite typical of the Moon, in the phase near the First Quarter, at sunset.
- The colors of the two PAN are perfectly consistent with the colors of the Moon at sunset.
- All five witnesses stated that what they saw was not the Moon. None of them mentioned the Moon's position relative to the PAN, nor did they even confirm if they had actually seen the Moon (for example, T2 says, "I'm positive it wasn't the moon. There was only one object"). This despite the fact that the Moon was present in the indicated direction and not hidden by clouds, as at least on November 27, the witnesses (and the Rennes weather station) reported the sky as completely clear and starry.

For the first observation (T1 on November 25), the misperception with the Moon was likely enhanced by the presence of cloud veils (or scattered clouds) that partially or totally covered the Moon at times (consistent with weather data). It is thus possible to explain the almost round or oval shape (not a crescent), the disappearance and reappearance, and the forward-backward movements (optical illusion caused by the variation in intensity of the visible part of the Moon according to the covering and/or transparency of the veil).

On November 27, T2, T3, T4, and T5 describe an object in the shape of a crescent, banana, or half-moon with yellow or red colors... yet they do not recognize the Moon in a sky otherwise described as cloudless. This is an intriguing element; other unreported elements may have misled the witnesses, there may have been a minimum of deceptive cloud veils, but the conclusion of a misperception with the Moon remains incontestable in light of the numerous other elements.

Therefore, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: misperception with the Moon at sunset.
