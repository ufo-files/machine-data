Description: On several occasions in January 2019 and then in January 2020, a witness observed in the night sky of Guadeloupe a luminous object with changing colors in motion, capturing several videos of it. Of low strangeness and average consistency (unique testimony lacking in precision but with several videos of PAN), the case turns out to be an observation of the star Sirius. The object's appearance in the videos strongly resembles a bright star with intense twinkling. The air instability at altitude classically causes twinkling (intensity variations), apparent motions (geometric variations), and color changes due to atmospheric dispersion, phenomena particularly pronounced for objects at low altitude. Furthermore, the witness's recurring observations over multiple nights are characteristic of an astronomical misperception. Cross-referencing indicates that Sirius was precisely in the observed direction, with the witness also overestimating the object's elevation in the sky. The distinctive colored flashes of this star perfectly match the reported chromatic variations. The case is classified as 'A': observation of the star Sirius. *CONSISTANCE: According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.
