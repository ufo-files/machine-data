Description: On June 29, 2019, at 8:45 PM, a witness on their balcony observed a very bright object passing through the sky. The object seemed to change shape and/or position, and the witness at times observed glowing points. The PAN disappeared from view, hidden by the environment. The witness offered several hypotheses but remained skeptical. Only one testimony was collected. With average strangeness and consistency (single witness, video of PAN but of poor quality and unusable), the case appears to be the probable observation of a Mylar fantasy balloon. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Some descriptive elements of PAN, upon initial reading, could correspond to three hypotheses: a bird, a drone, or a balloon. Of these three hypotheses, the balloon is prioritized as it best correlates with the elements provided by the witness (see investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following:

- The changing shape of PAN: a fantasy balloon (character, logo, etc.), which could even be partially deflated, can explain this polymorphic aspect, which changes with the wind.
- The chrome-like color is entirely consistent with this type of balloon, due to light reflection on its highly reflective surface. What the witness described as lights is typical of areas placed optimally in relation to the sun, inducing maximum reflectivity. However, we cannot confirm the actual lighting of this balloon by the sun, which was located very low on the horizon at the time of observation.
- The straight, north-south trajectory is consistent with that of a passive object, such as a balloon carried by the wind, oriented in the same direction. This wind is weak, which explains the relative long duration of observation. The strangeness felt by the witness during and after the observation remains significant, despite all their attempts to correlate their observation with a known phenomenon.

The investigation conclusion, which directs the observation towards that of a metallic Mylar fantasy balloon, whose release origin could not be found, leads us to classify this case as "B", probable observation of a Mylar fantasy balloon.
