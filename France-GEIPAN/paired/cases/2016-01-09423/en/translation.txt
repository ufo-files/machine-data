Description: On Sunday, January 24, 2016, at approximately 12:45, a witness was intrigued by the O-E passage of a luminous flying object with an oval shape. The Pan disappeared as a gray point on the horizon. No particular noise was heard during the observation. The phenomenon described by the witness resembles an aircraft, helicopter, or plane, despite an oval shape that is seemingly incompatible. A check of civil air traffic on the Flightradar site, based on so-called "secondary" radar information, reveals two unusual aircraft in the area (see available logs in the annex):

- A Beech 200 aircraft, flight referenced OOLET, flying at an altitude of approximately 8,000 meters, making multiple circles over the Braus pass.
- An Ecureuil helicopter, flight 3AMAX, which the radar only detects intermittently as it flies at an altitude of about 1,000 meters, approximately 10 kilometers from the witness. The log at 11:57 UTC (12:57 local time) shows the two aircraft very close together, as indicated by the witness. The aircraft then made a distinct change in direction. This type of aeronautical activity in this sparsely populated area is unusual: a regional event search reveals that at this time, the 16th special stage (ES16) of the Monte-Carlo Automobile Rally was taking place (see web page copies in the annex):

- ES16: Departure at 12:08 from Col de l’Orme – Arrival at Saint-Laurent (Power Stage) (Live TV) 12:07. This stage was broadcast live on television. It is highly likely that the helicopter was capturing the footage, transmitting it to the aircraft, which then relayed it to the central TV production. The GEIPAN did not conduct investigations to identify the various operators of the system. This type of operation is standard for major, highly mobile sporting events (see this intriguing aircraft, for example).

The Nice Météociel log: Météociel reports a light northwesterly wind at 12:00, shifting to full south at 13:00. In both cases, the wind is unfavorable for the propagation of the helicopter's noise to the witness. The helicopter is approximately 10 kilometers from the witness, a distance sufficient to prevent it from being heard under these conditions. The witness, who only discerns an oval shape, is likely only observing the reflection of sunlight off the aircraft, without being able to make out details.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: observation of a helicopter overflying a stage of the Monte-Carlo Automobile Rally.
