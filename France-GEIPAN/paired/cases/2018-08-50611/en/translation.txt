Description: On August 23, 2018 at 23:44, a witness was surprised to observe a blue patch, then yellow-orange, above the clouds. The witness has time to take a photo and a video that intrigue him further. The phenomenon is fixed, then in motion. No particular noise is heard during the observation, which lasts for about thirty seconds. His wife also observed the phenomenon, but for a shorter period of time. Only one testimony will be collected on this phenomenon. Based on the investigation report (see attached document), we can assert that the hypothesis explored, namely the confusion with bright light spots from ground-based projectors ("Skyrose") located on a low cloud layer, aligns with the characteristics of PAN as reported by the witness. This conclusion is based on the following elements: - the shape of the PAN, elongated, composed of several grouped elements, is typical of such light impact tasks; - the blue color is equally typical, the witness's observation of a yellow-orange color being explained by the probable use of colored filters at the output of projectors, which can be interchanged at will by the user; - the absence of noise is also consistent with the hypothesis, as well as the "sweeping" movements, which are typical of the movements of the "Skyroses." The date is less certain (a Thursday), but we cannot rule out the possibility of its use in the context of a private party or for testing purposes. All such confusions occur in the vast majority of cases in the presence of a uniform low to moderately low cloud layer, composed of stratocumulus or nimbostratus clouds, which offer sufficient density to reflect the light emitted from the beacon, making it visible from the witnesses' position. In the present case, the presence of a low cloud layer (at approximately 200m altitude) is confirmed. The main strangeness reported by the witness, namely that the PAN appear to be behind the clouds, can be explained by the fact that it is likely the witness had never previously had the opportunity to observe such beacon-like patterns on the basis of clouds; as a result, he may have formed an incorrect perception of the appearance these patterns could take (probably imagining them clearer and brighter if they had been located "under" the clouds, since they would not have been affected by their presence), while in reality, the patterns are not "under" the clouds, but "on" their base, only visible due to the impact of light beams on the water droplets that make them up, and to a greater or lesser extent depending on the density of the cloud. We were finally able to determine a plausible location for the spotlights that generated these PAN, namely the Manoir du Paris Country Club complex, located directly in the axis of the beam highlighted in the photograph taken by the witness, although we were unable to confirm the presence of these spotlights. The GEIPAN classifies this observation as B, indicating a fairly high probability that the lights are reflections from ground-based impactor task lights onto the cloud layer (Skyrose-type projectors).
