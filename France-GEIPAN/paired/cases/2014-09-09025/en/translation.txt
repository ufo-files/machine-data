Summary: Observation of several colored lights at the horizon: certain observation of nocturnal lighting signaling from two distinct groups of wind turbines and a flare from an Iridium satellite.
--- seg-e06b34f9942539448e5d0c43 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On September 21, 2014, around 9 PM, a driver and their passengers were intrigued by an unknown light phenomenon in the sky. Only one testimony was collected. Based on the objective elements gathered (see investigation report), namely: - the color, regular flickering, and immobility, identical to the characteristics of the regulated nighttime lighting of wind turbines. - excellent visibility allowing observation from a great distance. - azimuth and elevation measurements allowing visibility of wind turbine lights from successive witness positions. - low angular height. - the illusion of movement amplified by the absence of references, preventing the witness from accurately assessing the azimuths - and, on the other hand: - Calsky data confirming the existence of a powerful -7.0 magnitude reflection on an Iridium satellite, visible from position 1 of the witnesses, around the time of observation - the rapid disappearance, consistent with the short visibility of flares.

The GEIPAN concludes that the case initially concerns the simultaneous observation towards the West of the nighttime lighting of a first group of wind turbines and a flare produced by an Iridium satellite, and subsequently, towards the southwest, of the nighttime lighting of a second group of wind turbines. This case is classified as "A" for a certain observation of the nighttime lighting of two distinct groups of wind turbines and an Iridium flare.
