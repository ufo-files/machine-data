Description: In Bailleul (59) on June 15, 2022, at 7:08 PM, the witness, sitting in their garden, observed a bright object in the sky. Not recognizing this form, he films and zooms with his phone. The PAN is moving slowly. The witness observes it for 4 to 5 minutes and has the impression of a change in trajectory at the moment of its distant disappearance. He does not think it is a plane, a helicopter, a weather balloon, or a drone, and he wants to know what he saw. Only one testimony is collected. The investigation revealed that the witness very likely observed a mylar balloon, possibly a lost children's balloon (see investigation report). The main elements supporting this hypothesis are as follows: - Description of shape and color - Direction of motion consistent with wind direction. The shiny aspect that caught the witness' attention is entirely compatible with a mylar balloon when the sun reflects off its aluminum surface. The change in direction, perceived at the end of the trajectory, can be explained by the presence of strong wind gusts. The GEIPAN is classified as A: observation of a Mylar balloon carried by the wind and illuminated by the sun.
