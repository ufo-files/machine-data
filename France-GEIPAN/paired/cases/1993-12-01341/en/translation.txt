**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the accuracy of conclusions. Through new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named ROQUEMAURE (30) 1993 is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases.

On December 25, 1993, at 00:25, a witness in a car spotted above the canal connecting ROQUEMAURE (30) to the Rhône two circular, fiery red lights, but not blinding, "offset in depth but on the same line," remaining stationary then moving in two different directions. The witness lost sight of the first and followed the second, moving more slowly. They saw the shape one last time above CADEROUSSE (84), pulled out a camcorder to film it, and lost sight of it. No noise was heard during the observation. The witness (T1) immediately went to the nearest gendarmerie station to be interviewed. The gendarmes then visited the scene to conduct routine investigations but found no evidence of the phenomenon. Notably, the video recording on cassette is missing from the GEIPAN's archives. Additionally, the gendarmes found a second witness who observed from their vehicle on the Ile de la Barthelasse in Avignon (84) two white lights moving earlier that evening, on December 24, 1993, around 19:00. This witness (T2) was interviewed at the gendarmerie on January 26, 1994.

The totality of the witness and environmental data supports the hypothesis of confusion with a set of projectors used in a festive context (see the investigation report). These projectors created mobile light spots on low clouds, which the witnesses were unable to recognize. This conclusion is based on the following:

- The two PAN observed have either a defined shape (circular for T1) or are indecipherable (lights for T2). This difference could be due to T2 being farther from the impact of the beams on the clouds (higher clouds) and/or the nature of the clouds themselves (more diffuse).

- The circular shape observed by T1 is classic of projector beam impacts on low clouds.

- The white color noted by T2 is typical of these projectors. Red is rarer but does exist. The color's evolution over time (white at 19:00 and red at 00:25) is explained by certain models of skytrackers allowing the installation of colored filters in front of the beam at will.

- The movements and immobility of the PAN noted by the witnesses are also typical of automated motor systems or manual manipulations of these skytrackers, which can move independently in all directions.

- The impact on the clouds, themselves invisible, is well described by T1, who notes their presence in perspective on the same line. These clouds, stratocumulus between 2 and 3 octas at 8, at altitudes varying between 1500m and 19:00 and 1000m at 00:25 the following day, provide an ideal surface for the formation of skytracker beam impacts.

- Excellent visibility at the time of the event indicates the absence of fine particles in the lower atmosphere, preventing beam formation.

- No noise is produced by the skytrackers, obviously.

- The witnesses' locations, between Orange and Avignon in a suburban area with a maximum radius of 20km, align well with the known range of skytrackers. Furthermore, the witnesses are themselves approximately 9km apart, which is enough for the beam impacts on the clouds to be visible from their respective positions.

- Finally, the observation period, a Christmas night, is ideal for the use of such projectors in festive settings. The consistency is poor, with a very brief second testimony, a quick gendarmerie investigation, and a lack of angular data. Additionally, a video document was created by T1 but is missing from the GEIPAN's archives, though given to the gendarmes by the witness. Today, easily recognizable laser animations had led to very few misunderstandings and witness observations before 1993, and the strangeness that could be created was not yet known to the GEIPAN of that time. It is impossible today to find the origin of this animation given that it occurred over 25 years ago, and this search must be conducted within a radius of several dozen kilometers from the observation location. The absence of a trace is not decisive in either direction. We have observations from this period where the investigation led to the discovery of the animation, highlighting that equipment producing such effects was sold to discothèques in France (see the gendarmerie report for the observation case ILE BOUCHARD (37) 11.12.1993). The perfect alignment of appearance with the meteorological situation, the occurrence on a Christmas night near a large city, and the period with already heavy use of skytrackers in discothèques or animations are the elements allowing us to retain this hypothesis as very probable. The GEIPAN concludes with a "A": very probable observation of beams from two skytrackers, used during a festive period, projecting their beams onto the cloud layer, creating two impact spots.
