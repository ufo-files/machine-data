Summary: A loud, strong rumble preceding the observation of a dark mass with many lights: likely observation of the French Air Force Patrol practicing at night.
--- seg-bb90e81cb9a56c400deb991f [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 3, 2015, around 10 PM, during a family gathering, a witness and his son go to the back of the family garden. A deep, profound, dull noise is then heard, followed by the witness observing a moving mass above the trees of the neighboring house. Many non-flashing lights (one hundred) are seen below the mass. The witness wants to alert other people, but the loud noise prevents him. No one in the group saw the phenomenon, but they all heard the muffled noise. Only one testimony will be collected. This observation of multiple multicolored lights under a dark mass is entirely atypical. The engine noise reported by the witness points towards an aeronautical hypothesis. A cartographic verification (see attached map) indicates that the witness is on the axis of Salon-de-Provence's runways, from which the Patrouille de France takes off, particularly active at this time of year, to prepare for end-of-promotion and July 14 demonstrations. A check with the Patrouille de France indicates a takeoff at 21:49 for a flight of approximately one hour. (See the video "France Patrol Night Training." ) A southeast wind is noted at that time in Marignane on Météociel. The patrol therefore had to take off towards the southeast, into the wind, flying almost directly over our witness. It is therefore very likely that what the witness observed was either immediately after takeoff, which occurs 4 by 4 (as seen in the video), or after the formation: focused on the entire navigation lights (at least 4 per plane, meaning at least 32 lights), the witness had the illusion of a single dark object carrying all the lights (a classic illusion known as the "shape" or "contour" illusion). It is noted, however, that the times do not match exactly (22:00 indicated by the witness, for a takeoff at 21:49): is it a second pass after regrouping, or an uncertainty in the time reported by the witness? The witness reports a hundred lights, versus 32 on the 8 planes: this difference is notable. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": observation of the French Patrol soon after its takeoff. The precise scenario of the observation could not be established due to the lack of the precise flight path of the planes minute by minute. Only a very strong coincidence between the events will be noted.
