Description: On May 30, 2024, at 11:55 PM, the witness, on his terrace in Elsenheim (67), observed in the sky five lights of a white/green hue never seen before. Intrigued, he immediately contacted his father, who also confirmed the observation. Among these five bright points, three displayed independent flickering. The witness also noted a beam of light apparently connecting the ground to the brightest point. The phenomenon occurred at the level of an orange cloud and was visible for approximately 15 minutes before suddenly disappearing. The witness completed a Technical Questionnaire (QT) on May 31, 2024, and submitted it to GEIPAN, along with a sketch, photos, and a video. Despite only one of the two witnesses reporting the observation, the case is considered consistent (presence of a photo and a video) and is determined to be light animation tests conducted during the "Azariste" festival from May 29 to June 2, 2024, at the Colmar Nautic Base, approximately 10 kilometers from the witnesses. Tests were indeed carried out on the evening of May 30 in preparation for a sound and light show that began the following evening and continued for three consecutive evenings from the beach at the nautic base. Various event photographs are available on the festival website, including one showing six green beams projected into the sky (see Annex 1 and photos of the Azariste show and the journey of the senses). A newspaper article in "l’Alsace" describes the preparations on the evening of the observation, including the six projectors used, which can emit different colors (the article shows green, red, and white). We were able to verify the observation azimuth using landmarks on the photograph from PAN, which is 230°, virtually pointing towards the Colmar Nautic Base (which has an azimuth of 240°). The 10° difference can be explained by the fact that the beams are not necessarily projected vertically but at a certain angle. The presence of low clouds and precipitation over Colmar, as attested by the witness ("cloudy, rain before the phenomenon") and the "l’Alsace" article, allowed the projectors' light to impact these clouds, creating light patches visible to the witnesses, and also made at least one beam visible, intermittently. The case is classified as A: observation of sky roses. *According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony.
