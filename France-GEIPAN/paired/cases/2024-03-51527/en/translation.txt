Description: On March 12, 2024, at approximately 1:43 PM, the main witness (T1), present in an open-plan office of a building located on the heights of Villeneuve-Loubet (06), observed through a closed window, along with three colleagues, a PAN described as a "O"-shaped, silvery, highly reflective object. Initially stationary to the north, the phenomenon gradually gained altitude before slowly moving towards the northeast, disappearing from view after about three minutes of observation. The case consistency is deemed satisfactory: although only one of the four witnesses reported their observation to GEIPAN, a high-quality video of PAN was provided and analyzed as part of the investigation (see the investigation report). According to GEIPAN's criteria, consistency corresponds to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The analysis indicates that this PAN is very likely a helium-filled balloon in the shape of a "O" or "0", consisting of a reflective silver Mylar envelope. Its behavior, with an initial phase of immobility followed by a slow ascent and straight-line movement, is consistent with that of a passive object carried by the wind blowing in the same direction. The balloon is located in a hilly area subject to micro-aerological variations, which explain the initial phase of immobility followed by its slow movement while "waving" itself, clearly visible on the video. This type of festive balloon is commonly used for birthdays; it could have been released or lost from a residence located below, for example in the heights of Antibes or Vaugreniers. GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": observation of a Mylar balloon carried by the wind.
