Summary: Unique witness report of a giant white glowing object floating above a field. Inclination and rapid, silent departure towards clouds: Unidentified phenomenon.
--- seg-1599f297cef2d32cc2654bf0 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On June 29, 2012, at approximately 6:06 a.m. (time precision as per the investigation report), a walker, alerted by his dog, was intrigued by the presence of a white, stationary, glowing object at low altitude above a field bordering a forest. The observation that lasts less than twenty seconds still allows the witness to state that the object had great dimensions, a flat shape below, and a concave shape above. As it begins to rain, the object rises slightly, tilts, and departs at high speed towards the cloudy sky. No particular noise or smoke was observed during the frightened witness's observation. No other testimony was collected about this phenomenon. This case has the particularity of being extremely similar and synchronous with a well-classical natural phenomenon: the observation of the sun rising between two cloud layers. However, several important elements do not align:

- Primarily, the direction of observation: a 54° deviation from the sun, such a deviation is impossible to attribute to uncertainty.

- The inclination of the phenomenon before its disappearance: this second element could be explained by a perception illusion due to the phenomenon's light intensity, or by analogy to media-reported observations of PAN.

- The apparent size of the phenomenon: almost 10 times larger than the sun's image: overestimating the apparent diameter of the Moon or the Sun at sunrise is very common, but rarely to this extent. The GEIPAN investigators went to the scene a first time to verify the location and observation directions indicated by the witness and ruled out the witness's unintentional error in estimating the direction. A second time to study the possibility of complex reflection of the sun allowing observation in another direction. They were unable to put forward a satisfactory hypothesis; however, it should also be noted that the weather conditions on that morning were very particular and, of course, not reproducible: intense heat and heavy rain the previous day, and a thick cloud cover on the morning in question. Its strangeness is average, as the phenomenon is similar to a well-classical natural phenomenon, but with at least one important non-conforming characteristic. The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'D1' - unexplained and of average consistency.
