Description: The GEIPAN was contacted by phone on August 24, 2009, regarding an observation of several lights balls seen the previous night (in the night of September 22-23, 2009). Recontacted a month later to obtain the Terrestrial Questionnaire related to this observation, the witness withdrew, having since discovered that it was Thai lanterns. However, he took the opportunity to report a strange phenomenon observed by his 7-year-old son on Thursday, September 24, 2009, around 7 PM. A questionnaire and a drawing were faxed to the GEIPAN in October 2009. That evening, as the child played in the garden, his attention was drawn to the sound of a plane coming from the south. The child was then intrigued by a white, opaque, rectangular object behind the plane. The object tilted to pass under the plane and then suddenly fell to stabilize below a high-tension line. It then progressed towards the neighbor's TV antenna (10 meters away), rose at very high speed, suddenly stopped, and then took off vertically, disappearing into the sky. The observation, which lasted between 20 and 30 seconds, took place on a clear, cloudless night. No other testimony was reported to the GEIPAN. A steady wind of 17 km/h was blowing from the north-northeast, and the object, in the form of a white, opaque parallelepiped, appeared at an altitude from the southwest of the observation location, against the wind. It suddenly fell to stabilize at roof level, silently progressed under the high-tension line, still against the wind, before taking off vertically and disappearing into the sky. No usual hypothesis can explain these criteria. This case results from the testimony of a 7-year-old child, an indirect report as it was relayed by his father. The GEIPAN did not consider it desirable to delve deeper into the testimony of a child of this age. The sincerity of the testimony is not particularly in question, but children of this age can be more easily influenced, even unconsciously, by discussions or situations in their immediate environment, which could lead to exaggerating or misinterpreting observed elements that may have been mundane at the time. Note that the father had already given a first testimony to the GEIPAN a month earlier, and this other observation was quickly explained (Thai lanterns), but it may have created in the child this factor of influence or concern related to the possible strangeness of the sky. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Lack of reliable information.
