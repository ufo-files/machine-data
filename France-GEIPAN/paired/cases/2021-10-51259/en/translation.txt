Summary: Nighttime observation by a driver of a slow-moving then rapid green light: observation of a military transport aircraft A400M.
--- seg-ad2c7c923e1dcd1369b43ba2 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 21, 2021, at approximately 8:30 PM, the witness is in their vehicle driving on D6 from Chevilly (45) to Sougy (45). Their attention is drawn by a small green light on the left, moving slowly and quite low, before suddenly accelerating. The witness immediately stopped, got out of their vehicle, and used their phone to photograph and record the craft. They then observed the phenomenon crossing the sky. Only one testimony was collected; only the photograph is attached to the hearing protocol. The consistency is good: one witness, one photo. The investigation concluded that it was very likely an A400M military transport aircraft from Air Base 123 in Orléans-Bricy, conducting a Very Low Altitude (VLA) flight to the south of Air Base 132 in Colmar (see investigation notes). This conclusion is based on the following elements: - Lights with colors typically used in aeronautics. - Known presence near the base, on approach. - Apparent slow movement: the A400M moves relatively slowly during VLA flights. The actual speed is difficult for the witness to estimate, partly because they were themselves in motion during the initial observation phase, and partly because the aircraft could have moved non-transversely to the witness's position. The periods when it seemed to accelerate were when it turned and took a more transverse position. This last point is one of the three peculiarities noted by the witness, the others being the shape and the absence of noise. Regarding the absence of noise, even though the A400M is a very noisy aircraft, the perception of the noise it emits depends on other factors, particularly the distance separating it from the witness and the wind direction, whether carrying or not. We have seen that it was likely the aircraft was about 3600 meters away, not 30 meters as initially estimated by the witness, who, under unfavorable observation conditions (nighttime and in motion, at least during the first observation phase), was unable to accurately estimate the distance. We have also noted that the wind was blowing from the north, opposite the direction of observation; therefore, it was not carrying. These two elements can suffice to explain why the witness, especially during the second observation phase when they stopped, did not perceive any noise. Regarding the perceived triangular shape, the aircraft was low on the horizon and observed at a low angle. At night, and due to the inherently dark color of the aircraft, these details (propellers, tail….) could not be perceived by the witness. The simplest shape the brain deduces under these conditions is a triangle. This angle of observation can also explain the initial unvisibility of the other lights, obscured by the aircraft itself. As the angle of observation changes with the movement of both the aircraft and the witness, they eventually become visible and are captured on the photograph. Classification: A - Observation of a military transport aircraft A400M.
