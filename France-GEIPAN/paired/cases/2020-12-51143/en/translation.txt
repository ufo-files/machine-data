Description: On December 12, 2020, at 17:27 local time, the witness' attention was drawn to a "strange" trail in the sky, in a southerly direction (SSO) from their position. The witness quickly takes out his camera and manages to take several photos of this speeding object towards the ocean. The witness wonders, thinking of a human-made space debris, a small meteorite entering the atmosphere, or a link to the SpaceShipTwo (spaceplane prototype). The witness specifies that his camera remained on daylight saving time. Therefore, the actual time is 3:27 PM Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), which is 4:27 PM local winter time. In order to verify the time of the observation, it was requested that the witness take a photo of the talking clock. This allowed the highlighting of a positive drift of 15 minutes in the camera's time. Assuming a constant drift between December 2020 and April 2021, the observation can then be timed at 15:12 UTC. The witness mentions an observation direction of 225° and an elevation of 20°. Analysis of the photos and use of Google Maps/Geoportal allows for the definition of an observation direction of approximately 220° (see document). At 4:12 p.m. local time, the Sun was at an azimuth of 222° and a height of 8°. Its azimuth places it in the (horizontal) field of the photo. Since it is not visible, it can be assumed that it is hidden by the thick cloud layer visible above the horizon, a cloud layer confirmed by meteorological sites (6/8 octas of cloud cover, indicating a very cloudy sky). The photos provided by the witness (shape, color) point towards an aeronautical misperception: condensation trail. The Flightradar24 website allows for the identification of a potential aircraft, an Airbus A321, TP1359, on a London-Lisbon route, which at 3:12 PM was at an altitude of 35,000 feet, approximately 150 km from the observation point, and in a south/southwestern direction (see document). The photos sent by the witness show that the object is moving from left to right, so the trail must be located south of the observation point. Or the identified aircraft has a trajectory that passes to the north. An analysis of the winds at altitude (see document) indicates a wind of 216 km/h at 315° at the suspected location of the aircraft, which could lead to a drift of several kilometers in the wake, causing it to move from a northern position to a southern position relative to the witness. The elongated shape seen in the early photos appears to correspond well to the condensation trail of an airplane (the zoom even seems to suggest a bi-engine). The aircraft's azimuth is close to that of the Sun, so the reflection is excellent. It is important to note that observing condensation trails from airplanes, under certain meteorological and astronomical conditions, can be particularly spectacular and lead to speculations about their origin. When air masses at high altitude are very humid, it promotes the persistence of these streaks, which spread out and disperse randomly with the altitude winds (see the following article: condensation trail). The UFO GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": mock with a condensation trail.
