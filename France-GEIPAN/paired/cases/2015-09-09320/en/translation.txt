Summary: Three appearances and disappearances of a pale yellow spherical light phenomenon in the sky: likely observation of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-35fb52ec7be5cb5dcabea606 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Sunday, September 13, 2015, between 9:05 PM and 9:15 PM, a witness was intrigued by the repeated appearance and disappearance of a pale yellow spherical light phenomenon in a clear sky. The phenomenon made erratic movements and no particular noise was heard. A 1 minute and 34 second video was recorded by the witness. Although accompanied by another person during the observation, only one testimony will be accepted. Based on the evidence gathered in the investigation report (see attached document), namely:

- The identical yellow and red-yellow color to that of lanterns.
- Brightness comparable to that of a very bright star.
- Shape described as spherical.
- Behavior identical to that of objects subject to no or weak wind: movement in the direction of the dominant wind and ascent caused by warm air.
- Observation duration consistent with the lifespan of lanterns.
- Date: a Sunday evening, consistent with those conducive to the organization of lantern releases for celebrations.
- Number compatible with the quantity of lanterns launched successively.
- Disappearance caused by the extinction of lanterns.
- Absence of noise.
- Proximity of installations conducive to lantern releases in the direction of the witnesses' observation.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B" for a probable observation of Thai lanterns. The witness, informed of this hypothesis, refuted it: as he has already launched lanterns, he knows their appearance. However, one must realize that the appearance of a Thai lantern seen from a distance is entirely different from the view one has when launching them. The GEIPAN did not conduct a local investigation to identify potential perpetrators of this release. This type of investigation is only launched for releases of many lanterns on important occasions, which are relatively easy to identify. Smaller releases can be done for minor private events (birthdays or trials) which are very difficult to detect in an urban environment.
