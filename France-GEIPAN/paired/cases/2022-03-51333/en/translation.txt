Summary: Observations, with the naked eye and then with binoculars, of a moving luminous phenomenon in the sky: observation of the star Sirius during the first observation and the star Arcturus during the second.
--- seg-e7643edac28593c0f9c78e14 [needs-review] ---
Description: The witness reports to the gendarmerie about an observation of PAN made from their home in Thieulin (28), on March 21st at 23:15. They describe a particularly bright star compared to the others, moving in an irregular manner, with small jumps, "a bit in all directions." He adds that he made a new observation on March 23, 2022 at 6:02 a.m., still in the same direction but higher on the horizon. The investigation concluded that the first observation involved the observation of the star Sirius, and the second observation involved the star Arcturus (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The appearance is entirely consistent with that of a star (brightness, color, aspect, no noticeable real movement) which the witness also noted himself, by comparing the PAN to a star.
- The presence of these stars at the time of the observations is confirmed.
- The initial and final elevations given by the witness generally correspond well to those given by Stellarium (minimal difference, a few degrees for Sirius and 14° for Arcturus, between the witness's indications and the actual elevations).
- The azimuthal positions reproduced in the sketch attached to the PV are consistent with those of the two stars (observation towards southwest). The strangeness, for the witness, resides in: 1- The appearance of PAN, observed primarily through binoculars. This appearance, of a shape changing, presence of bright points and observation of various colors, is typical of a scintillation phenomenon of a star observed at low elevation and subject to atmospheric turbulences. 2- The erratic movement of the PAN, typically caused by a self-propulsion phenomenon. It is likely that the witness's lack of astronomical knowledge did not allow him to recognize these stars, and that the very close azimuthal positions of the two stars during the two observation phases led him astray, making him think it could be the same phenomenon. Classification as "A", observation of the star Sirius during the first observation and the star Arcturus during the second.
