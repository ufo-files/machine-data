Description: On January 2, 2012, at 4:30 PM, a witness observed a triangular object with flashing red and yellow lights. The object appeared to spin occasionally and remained stationary. The witness alerted the Chavenay Air Navigation Service, which also confirmed the phenomenon. The observation lasted three hours. No other testimonies were collected. The GEIPAN called the Chavenay service, and the correspondent reported seeing a very bright light point. After a quick analysis, this observation could merely be the planet Venus, and not the object reported by the witness. That evening, Jupiter was also noted near the Moon (see attached astro map). As Venus is quite low on the horizon, its scintillation causes the colors to vary greatly. The witness, recontacted, specified that he had identified Venus, and that the glowing object was close to the direction of Venus, but fixed for two hours, without however indicating its precise direction or apparent height on the horizon. The observation direction corresponds to the Château de Plaisir (Les Saussaies): a glowing tethered balloon could have produced the same visual impression. The GEIPAN was unable to verify this hypothesis. Given the impreciseness of the testimony regarding the observation direction, and the weak strangeness of the case (a fixed object for two hours), the absence of photos despite the length of the observation, the lack of other testimonies, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" due to a lack of information.
