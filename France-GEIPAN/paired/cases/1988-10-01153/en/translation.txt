Summary: Multiple observations of varying-intensity glowing balls passing through the sky: re-entry of an upper stage of a rocket into the atmosphere.

Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named NUKU HIVA (987) 1988 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On October 22, 1988, around 3:30 AM, several witnesses in French Polynesia observed very bright, white or red-orange balls according to their testimonies. This luminous phenomenon had a straight trajectory in the sky, and no particular noise was heard. Four late testimonies were collected. The gendarmerie investigation also noted that that night, two crews from the New Zealand and UTA companies reported observations of phenomena, including long condensation trails off the Marquesas Islands. Several luminous balls moving together in the sky with different intensities and ogival shapes for some: all these elements are characteristic of a meteoroid entering the atmosphere with fragmentation or a space craft. The "craft" hypothesis prevails because it was observed for a relatively long time (greater than 1 minute) and the angular displacement in the sky was not very rapid. At the time, the GEIPAN had less experience with this type of testimony and access to information to locate the craft responsible was more difficult. Today, it is possible to identify in the NORAD (North American Aerospace Defense Command) directories a list of objects or craft that entered that day (there are several) and, thanks to the final orbits provided by NORAD and tracing tools, to locate the one passing under geometric conditions consistent with the observation and therefore responsible for it. As indicated in the attached reconstruction, around 1:45 PM TU (4:15 AM local time) on October 22, 1988, the spatial object #19597, the upper stage of the rocket that launched the Raduga 22 satellite, passed near the observation site under geometric conditions consistent with those observed, that is, from NW to SE, as described by T3 and T4, the two best-placed witnesses (closest) to understanding the trajectory. Given its location, it can be understood that T2 primarily perceived only the W to E component of the trajectory. On the other hand, the trajectory corresponding to T1's observation does not match at all (tracing error in the report?). Considering the other testimonies and also the indications (see report) from two commercial aircraft crews who described the same phenomenon overflying Nuku-Hiva (with an indication of W to E direction for the one who mentions it), we consider that T1's testimony cannot be opposed to the hypothesis. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: atmospheric reentry of a rocket upper stage.
