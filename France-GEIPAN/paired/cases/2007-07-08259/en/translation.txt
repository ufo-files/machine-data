Summary: Observation of silent movements of a white-blue vaporous light phenomenon in a cloudy sky: mistaken for projections from a sky tracer (laser projector).
--- seg-4686cf47669acec50e338230 [needs-review] ---
Description: On the morning of July 6, 2007, around 1 a.m., a driver and his wife witnessed a vaporous, blue-white, ring-shaped luminescent phenomenon moving silently and slightly tilted in the cloudy sky. On the outer edge of the ring, they also observed several bright circles. The phenomenon seemed to follow their vehicle, frightening the witnesses, between the exits of Remiremont (88) and Dinozé (88). Upon arriving home, the witnesses continued observing the phenomenon. The discontinuous observation lasted approximately 35 minutes over a distance of just over 20 kilometers. The appearance of the PAN described by the witnesses is very similar to images projected at the base of clouds by a sky tracer (multi-beam laser projector). The investigation (see the investigation report) demonstrated that the Epinal disco had such equipment at the time of the observation, and that the visibility and behavior of the PAN are consistent with the testimonies. Additionally, in 2007, the laser projector operated on Thursdays, in addition to Fridays and Saturdays. In 2015, it is still possible to verify the explanatory hypothesis on Thursday nights from 11 p.m. to Friday, Friday to Saturday, and Saturday to Sunday, when the weather is cloudy. This case can be classified as A: Misidentification with a sky tracer (laser projector), primarily due to the quality of the witnesses' memories, the notes taken at the time of the incident by the witness, and the meticulous reconstruction carried out by the investigators.
