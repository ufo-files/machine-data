Summary: Observation on a photograph of a fairly flat shape: probable photographic artifact (bird).
--- seg-73cc48988e17ad4c3d8df494 [needs-review] ---
Description: On Thursday, September 19, just before 10:30 a.m., while waiting for a meeting, the witness took photos of the Eiffel Tower and the La Défense district, visible in the distance from the window of a room on the 6th floor of a building in the 15th arrondissement of Paris. The sky is clear, providing good visibility. At 10:27, he takes a first photo, followed by a second one 2 to 5 seconds later, using an optical zoom x10. When enlarging the second photo, he notices a shape located between the Eiffel Tower and the La Défense towers that he does not find in the first photo nor by directly observing out the window right after. He stops his observation upon the arrival of his colleagues around 10:30 a.m. The object, illuminated by the sun, has a relatively flat shape and appears to have two colors: yellow below and black above. The witness estimates the distance between 2 and 8 km and its size at several meters, although he cannot confirm it precisely. He adds: "It is possible that it is closer and smaller than that." Furthermore, his research on Flightradar24 did not allow for the identification of an aircraft that could be the source of the observation. At the end of the meeting, around 12:12, he takes new photos that show planes, whose appearance does not in any way correspond to that of the observed object. The witness submits a Technical Questionnaire (QT) duly completed to GEIPAN on September 29, 2024. This case presents good consistency* with a detailed description and photos. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The hypothesis retained is that of a bird quickly passing through the field of view of the device:

- Indirect observation, not made with the naked eye
- PAN not present on the first photo (at a few seconds' difference)
- Despite a very short exposure time (0.0078s), the rapid passage of an object in front of the lens induces an artificial longitudinal distortion in the direction of its movement (here horizontal), similar to that of the PAN. Moreover, it also leads to a "dilution" towards gray of the black that composes it, which has an effect on the estimation of radiometry. A bird such as a coal tit could very well be the cause of the observation: it has both the yellow and black colors, is small in size, and is well present in Paris intra-muros, even in September. Thanks to the image authentication and processing tool of the GEIPAN (IPACO), a radiometric study (comparison of luminance variations) allowed the PAN to be repositioned relative to other elements of the landscape to estimate its distance from the witness (see Annex 1). Using this method, precise results should not be expected; it is only intended to get an idea of the distance to the PAN. Thus, the radiometric values of the PAN and the Eiffel Tower being close, on the one hand, and due to the "dilution" towards grey of the black that composes it, on the other hand, it can be considered that the PAN is located in a wide possible area between the lens and the Eiffel Tower, and very likely closer to the lens than to the Eiffel Tower. It is certain in any case that the PAN is not at the level of the Defense towers in the background nor at the level of the witness's estimates (between 2 and 8 km). On Google Earth, the distance between the witness and the roof landmark is 92 m. The angular length of the PAN measured is approximately 0.12°, at this distance this gives an PAN length of approximately 18.5 cm (see Annex 2). Or this value includes a deformation aberration along the longitudinal axis that is amplified by the x10 zoom (called "movement"). If one considers that the angular length of the "movement" is equal to one-third of the total length (the classic value usually considered), then the length of the "movement" is 0.04° and the angular length of the PAN is approximately 0.08°. Using IPACO again, for an object sized like a blackbird (11 to 14 cm), with an angular length of 0.08°, we get a distance of 80 to 100 meters (see Annex 3), which is fully consistent with the results of the radiometric study. The IPACO tool also allows for the evaluation of movement speeds based on the angular length of the "shift" and the distance. Starting from a "shift" value of 0.04°, for distances between 80 and 100 meters, we obtain a speed of 25 to 32 km/h (see Annex 4). This is also entirely consistent with the travel speed of a bird of the type coal tit. The case is classified B: probable photographic artifact (bird).
