Description: On August 18, 2024, around 9:30 PM local time in Verny (57), a witness observes three large orange lights forming a triangle in the sky. They move slowly from north to south, rise, and disappear into the clouds. Two more similar lights follow, fading at the same location. Several people present at the scene confirm the observation. The lights are round, orange, large in size, and completely silent. The primary witness remains calm and focused, choosing not to record the event despite requests from others. A family member captures a short video sequence, but the lights appear smaller. A neighbor also observes the phenomenon, bringing the total number of witnesses to five. The case is classified as of medium strangeness and good consistency (multiple witnesses, though one unique testimony and analyzable video exist), indicating a probable observation of sky lanterns. According to the criteria for GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered from a testimony. The description and visual aspect of PAN are entirely typical of this type of observation (moving orange light points, sense of mass), especially as the apparent trajectory of PAN is very close to the wind direction (as noted in the investigation report). Analysis of the video using IPACO® reveals no objective elements to refute this hypothesis. The limitations of the provided video - it is not original, heavily compressed, and suffers from technical information loss due to WhatsApp transmission - prevent further investigation. IPACO® is an image analysis and processing software for GEIPAN (IPACO.fr). The video under examination is consistent with the hypothesis of three light sources of the sky lantern type, however, the origin of these lanterns could not be determined during the investigation. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B," a probable observation of sky lanterns.
