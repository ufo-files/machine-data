Summary: Long observations over two consecutive days of a luminous phenomenon in the sky: astronomical observation of Venus.
--- seg-715df9085fdb89bfe163cfe5 [needs-review] ---
Description: On August 23 and 25, 1980, around 3:30 a.m., a witness observed from their 4th-floor apartment a very bright light point positioned in the same place in the sky. The witness described this point as a stationary star that appeared in the East. On August 25, the observation continued for approximately 2 hours using binoculars before the phenomenon faded away, gradually losing its brightness. No other testimony was collected by the gendarmerie. This observation case (under the name of BOUSSY-SAINT-ANTOINE (91) 23.08.1980) is part of cases classified "C" by the GEPAN, although the hypothesis of an observation of the planet Venus was considered probable at the time. This case was recently re-examined (see the investigation notes document). This case is inconsistent (single witness), and the description of the PAN observed is quite precise. The witness's sincerity and credibility were never questioned. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the planet Venus, particularly visible at the time of the observation (magnitude of -4.26). Moreover, Venus was clearly present in the observed area of the sky (the hypothesis of a mistake with Venus had been suggested by the GEPAN). It is not the witness's visual perception that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, night observation). In the current classification of GEIPAN, this case of weak strangeness is classified "A," an observation related to a mistake with Venus.
