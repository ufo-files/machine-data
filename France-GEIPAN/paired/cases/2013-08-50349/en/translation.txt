Summary: Nighttime observation by a driver of a glowing ball descending from clouds: very likely a misidentification with a ball lightning.
--- seg-be42244aa64a44ef98f5da56 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: Recent testimony (February 2017) of an old observation (August 2013). On Friday, August 2, 2013, around midnight, a driver was traveling under a severe thunderstorm when she saw a large, very bright white ball emerge vertically from the clouds and slowly descend behind the trees. No particular noise was heard during the observation, which lasted about ten seconds. The frightened witness continued on her way. No other testimony about this phenomenon was collected. The case was submitted to an expert in lightning studies. The expert confirms that this type of observation is not rare: it happens that one or more luminous spheres descend (like the slow fall of a distress rocket) from the base of a cumulonimbus during stormy weather. The phenomenon is part of the multiple facets of globular lightning. Another case filed in GEIPAN has a clear drawing by the witness illustrating the phenomenon of lightning descending from the clouds as well: it is about WISSOUS (91) --.--.1960. Given the antiquity of the observation, we did not confirm the weather conditions, but the witness clearly described a thunderstorm and also thought of ball lightning, though without certainty. GEIPAN classifies this observation as A, highly probable misperception with ball lightning.
