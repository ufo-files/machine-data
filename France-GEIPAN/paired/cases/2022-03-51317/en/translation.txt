--- seg-1971b820a60bccf0b547bda5 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On March 19, 2022, the witness observed from their garden in Marzan (56) a PAN described as a bright white ball, similar to a large star, moving in a straight, regular line in a cloudy sky. After a few minutes of observation, the witness sees the PAN slow down and then ascend into the clouds it is illuminating before disappearing. According to the GEIPAN criteria, the consistency of the case, defined by the quality and quantity of reliable information, is considered average. The case relies on a single testimony accompanied by a video deemed unusable, although the testimony is relatively complete and precise. Two hypotheses were explored (see the investigation report): one involving the observation of the International Space Station (ISS) and another involving the observation of a luminous balloon carried aloft by the wind at altitude. These two hypotheses account for the appearance of PAN, resembling a bright white star, as well as the initial trajectory, which was regular and straight-line. Regarding the ISS, although it was within the observation time frame, the witness reported observing an aircraft shortly after the passage of PAN. The map provided by the CNOA (National Center for Air Operations) indicates that only one aircraft was present in the observation sector, passing around 20:38/20:39, before the ISS emerged from the south-west horizon at 20:42 and disappeared behind the east-north-east horizon at 20:53. There is therefore a contradiction between the observation time of PAN reported by the witness (20:45/20:48) and the flight schedule of this aircraft, identified with certainty (20:38/20:39). Therefore, it can be concluded that the PAN is not the ISS, which, a fortiori, does not change course as the witness described. The hypothesis of observing a glowing balloon, in addition to adequately corresponding to the appearance and initial trajectory of PAN, also better explains the only real strangeness, namely its change in trajectory and its effective disappearance into the clouds. Indeed, thanks to the study of the fine-mesh AROME data provided by Météo France, we know that there was a wind corridor oriented west-southwest at 2000 m and 2250 m altitudes that was quite favorable for the movement of this balloon. Given the very weak winds and potentially variable conditions, this balloon could have altered its trajectory at the end of its flight and entered the cloud layer (cloud base at 2200 m), as described by the witness, and illuminated the clouds before disappearing. It is nonetheless surprising that a LED balloon could have been identified at such a high altitude. The case is classified B, probable observation of a luminous balloon.
