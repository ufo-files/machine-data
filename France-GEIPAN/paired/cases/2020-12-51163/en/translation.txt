--- seg-49732bdb3e8f59aea24d6cdb [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the night of December 31, 2020 to January 1, 2021, around 12:10 AM, a witness was walking their dog on the street. The sky is partly cloudy. He then spots a first phenomenon similar to a large orange, coming from the northwest and heading silently towards the southeast. As soon as the phenomenon is seen, a second, perfectly identical one appears, following the same trajectory and at the same speed of movement. A third PAN will also be observed by the witness. No noise is heard during the observation which lasts a maximum of one minute. The case does not present any strong strangeness upon reading the testimony. The one that remains, always delicate to qualify, is the witness's feeling. The consistency of the file is a little weak. The witness did not complete the questionnaire in the "Questions" section. However, the content of the testimony and the attached information allow, with certainty, to envisage a release of lanterns on the occasion of the New Year. The witness does not envisage a release of lanterns. The observation, in fact, remains compatible with that of these lanterns, due to: - the appearance, first, three extremely bright spheres, described by the witness as orange-colored. - the trajectory followed by the three spheres, completely compatible with the direction of the wind. Knowing that at least two weather stations indicate a wind from the NNW to the SSE at a speed of ~5/7 km/h. This wind is not deterring by its speed (see the weather document). On December 31, at 00:10, on the evening of New Year's Eve, midnight has just struck, New Year's celebrations are underway. The witness even indicates a firework. It is probable, if not logical, that a release of lanterns, near the witness, took place on this occasion. The moment is propitious for events of this festive type. The witness is surprised by the appearance of these orange spheres, which quickly fly towards the sea. The case study concludes to a misidentification of Thai lanterns. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": Thai lanterns.
