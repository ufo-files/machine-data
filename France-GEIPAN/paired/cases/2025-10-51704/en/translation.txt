Summary: Filmed observation of the slow and silent passage of a dark object at dusk: probable observation of a cluster of festive balloons.
--- seg-2d5a602fc96ab307fdeb0c39 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 12, 2025, at 7:37 PM, a witness and his brother are cycling on a rural road bordering a forest in Bischwiller, as night falls and the sky is clear. No ambient noise is perceptible when the witness sees a large black object, located at approximately 15 to 20 meters away. The object is estimated to be 2 meters high by 1 meter wide, with an angular elevation of about 40°. It moves slowly on a slightly descending trajectory, in a stable and silent manner, without any light emission or mechanical noise. Environmental conditions remain stable, with no perceptible wind. The witness then films the object using an iPhone 16 Pro in 4K at 60 frames per second. The automatic night mode of the device artificially lights the sky, giving the impression of greater distance and slightly altering the perception of the actual size of the object. The capture was made in wide-angle mode (0.5x), and no zoom could be applied before the object disappeared behind the trees, without any change in speed or trajectory. The total duration of the observation is a few seconds. The witness retains the original video file, including the precise date, time, and geolocation, allowing for complete documentation of the event. The consistency* is good, although only one of the two witnesses reported their observation to GEIPAN, with a video of PAN being usable. For the witness, the strangeness is formed by the unusual appearance of the PAN, observed under conditions not allowing the distinction of details. According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The only hypothesis explored, that of observing a cluster of festive balloons, is corroborated by the following elements: - The reconstruction on a map of the witness's position and the initial and final azimuts of PAN (see Annex 1) allows for validation of its movement from northeast to southwest. The Météo France and Météociel weather bulletins indicate a dominant wind from the north-north-east sector. Despite a small uncertainty, the trajectory and direction of movement of the PAN are well in line with the overall wind direction (see annexes 2, 3, and 4). - The trajectory is straight. - The shape and color are not clearly discernible due to twilight observation conditions. - An analysis using the dedicated image processing software for GEIPAN, IPACO*, allows for a clear distinction of a cluster of rounded shapes (see annex 5). - The observation took place on a Sunday evening, with the weekend being conducive to festive events involving such balloons. An exhaustive search was conducted on the Internet to find the origin of the release of this cluster of balloons (party rooms, cultural, sporting events, etc.) without success. It could be a private evening (birthday, any kind of celebration...). The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'B': probable observation of a cluster of party balloons. IPACO: image analysis and processing software for GEIPAN (IPACO.fr).
