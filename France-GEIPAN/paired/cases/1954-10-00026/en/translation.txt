Summary: Close observations of the movements of a light phenomenon with color variations: lack of information.
--- seg-399fbc56dca3b8210b62cf28 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 31, 1954, around 8:00 PM, a farmer (T1) and his employee (T2) in a tractor observed, upon arriving at a meadow about twenty meters from the path, a luminous phenomenon that frightened them. T1 described "a glowing craft turning a few meters from the ground." T2 described "a ball of fire spinning about 4m from the ground" with "rays of various bright colors framing it." T1 turned off the tractor lights, and the witnesses saw the object ascend at an oblique angle at a 'normal' speed towards the south, crossing the valley of the La Somme river. When the witnesses turned on their headlights again, the object appeared again, spun around, and headed towards the southwest. No ground traces were seen by the shocked witnesses. The following day, the farmer told the deputy mayor that the object had a "circular shape, about the diameter of a standard round table" and he thought he heard "the same humming as a bee leaving its hive." The colors observed were orange, with a blue center that made him close his eyes momentarily, and the upper part was brighter. The gendarmerie learned of the observation through the press, interviewed the witnesses verbally, and took their statements on November 4. The gendarmes were taken to the observation site, but found no traces in the grass. The gendarmerie investigation also uncovered two additional witnesses: a merchant in Long (T3) who observed on the same day and at the same time "a ball of orange color" emitting "various colored lights similar to the glow of an electric arc welding," "about a meter in diameter," and moving towards the southwest "quite quickly." A butcher (T4) reported to the gendarmes that he saw above the trees bordering the Somme "a cylindrical craft" that as it moved away towards the southwest "assumed an elongated, even oval shape" and "emitted several orange lights." A few moments later, these two people saw the first two witnesses arrive, "disfigured" by fear. This observation case with two groups of two witnesses at two different locations in the commune is very consistent. There is no doubt about the existence of the phenomenon. Several aspects of the phenomenon suggest globular lightning: spinning appearance, multicolored and blinding, bee swarm noise. However, we lack information to confirm this hypothesis (weather conditions perceived by the witnesses, historical weather records). The GEIPAN classifies this case as C due to the lack of meteorological information.
