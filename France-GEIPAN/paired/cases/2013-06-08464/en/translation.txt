Description: On June 3, 2013, a witness observed the southeast movement of a puzzling bright phenomenon (different times are given: June 6 in the questionnaire he states 11:18 PM and between 11:15 PM and 12:00 AM; and by email he states the phenomenon at 10:48 PM (see attached email document); on August 2 in the statement he states around 11:30 PM). On June 5, the same witness observed the same phenomenon at exactly 11:45 PM. The witness observed a very white light, about 4 times larger than a star. The phenomenon moves close to the witness's home. It follows a very rapid zigzag trajectory (see the witness's sketch attached) before disappearing from the witness's field of vision. After verification on Calsky, the observation on June 5, 2013, is confirmed as the ISS. For the date of June 3, the given times lack precision to affirm the witness also saw the ISS (see attached Calsky document). The changes in direction described by the witness obviously contradict the ISS hypothesis: but this self-motion illusion, known as autocinematic illusion, is common during ISS observations. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of the ISS (the imprecise observation time leaves a slight doubt).
