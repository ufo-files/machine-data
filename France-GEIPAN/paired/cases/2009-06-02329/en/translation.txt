Description: On June 18, 2009, between 11:59 PM and 12:13 AM, an amateur astronomer photographed the star cluster M13. He was then surprised to find, in several of his photographs, a point with a red line that appears to indicate the movement of an object. The witness did not make a direct observation. He will provide the original raw photos, but unfortunately they were lost due to a computer failure. This case of astronomical observation is uncommon. The observed movement over a fifteen-minute period seems to rule out typical low Earth orbit satellites with very fast relative motion. The Amazon BrzTank satellite (a large debris object, likely) could be a good candidate; its brightness can vary if it is rotating, like most uncontrolled satellites. However, its angular velocity of 1.89'/s would cause it to move approximately 28° over the 15 minutes of the photo sequence, which is not the case, as it remains within the telescope's field of view. It could also be a passing asteroid near Earth; this last hypothesis seems unlikely, especially since the observed area is far from the ecliptic where most of these objects orbit. The GEIPAN did not have the raw photos for this analysis. An optical or photographic artifact (internal or external parasitic reflections) can also be considered. The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'C' due to a lack of information.
