Description: On March 26, 2012, around midnight, a witness in Ploeren (56) observed from his terrace a bright object moving in the sky towards the south. Intrigued, he contacted the gendarmerie, who sent a patrol to the scene, confirming the witness's observation. The patrol then moved to Baden and continued observing the phenomenon. Another gendarmerie unit, positioned at the Etel lighthouse, confirmed the presence of a bright, flickering light in the south at an elevation of 20° between 0:45 and 1:05 a.m. A final observation was made from the Gâvres GERBAM. The object disappeared at 1:50 a.m.

This observation is complex due to reports from multiple witnesses in different locations, and it is not sufficiently precise regarding the azimuths and elevations of the phenomenon during different observation phases. The descriptive accounts of the observed object share characteristics with very bright stars near the horizon (color changes, apparent lateral and forward-backward movements). However, it is challenging to determine the specific celestial body observed, suggesting that 2 or 3 stars may have been noticed sequentially: Sirius, Procyon, and Betelgeuse, due to temporary obscuration by a cloud veil following an afternoon rain shower.

It's also notable that none of the witnesses mentioned Sirius or any other star, which could have helped pinpoint the phenomenon relatively to these stars and rule out this hypothesis. Given the absence of any observed phenomenon by the patrolling plane and on radar, it can be concluded that the phenomenon was extremely distant.

Considering all these elements, the GEIPAN concludes that the observation was likely of Sirius, followed by Procyon and/or Betelgeuse.
