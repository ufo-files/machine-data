Summary: Observation of the movement and disappearance of a bright point in the night sky: observation of the flash of satellite Iridium 52.
--- seg-a3aca3d0041f0a16bab80d7b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On June 25, 2015, around 3:15 AM, a witness observed for 10 to 15 seconds a bright point moving in a straight line, identified as a satellite. Then, they saw another brighter, white and orange light point advancing at high speed, without blinking or making any sound. This one seemed to stop, suddenly changed course, and disappeared. The satellite passage simulator "Calsky" indicates that at that time, many satellites are visible on trajectories close to the north-south or south-north direction. The satellite COSMOS 2428, visible on an apparent NNW to S trajectory between 3:00 AM and 3:12 AM, seems to be the one the witness saw (without certainty, as other satellites could fit), but it was another very bright light phenomenon on a south-to-north trajectory that disappeared after 10 to 15 seconds. Both the Calsky and Heavens-above records indicate the visibility of a flash from satellite Iridium 52, orbiting on a south-to-north trajectory, from Nantes at 3:25 AM. See information on Iridium satellite flashes. The witness then describes a sharp turn, a sudden acceleration before the disappearance: this type of description is common for satellites entering the Earth's shadow or whose brightness suddenly ceases due to a change in orientation relative to the sun. The sudden drop in brightness is unconsciously interpreted as a distance increase. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: observation of the flash of satellite Iridium 52.
