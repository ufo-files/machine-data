Summary: Observation of the silent and rapid movement of white lights in the sky; forming a compact triangle: observation of an aircraft (Falcon at low altitude).
--- seg-a54a5fa4f6ec5c27034241ae [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Sunday, October 2, 2016, at 21:57, a witness is on the 4th floor of their apartment. Their gaze is drawn to 7-8 identical white lights, like those of planes moving in the sky. A light detaches from the group, forming a compact triangle. No noise is heard during the observation of approximately 10 seconds. Only one testimony will be collected. It is highly likely that the observation corresponds to the passage of a Falcon aircraft at a relatively low altitude (4900 meters), as shown by the attached reconstruction document depicting this passage. The time and direction of passage match exactly (the record is made in Universal Time, which corresponds well to 21:59 in standard time). The plane passes closest to the witness at 3800 meters and flies at 4900 meters altitude. The angular elevation is 52° when the witness reports 15-30°, but this discrepancy falls within the typical margin of error for a witness's estimation of angular elevation without ground references (e.g., "so many times the height of the roof"). A peculiarity noted by the witness is the description of a series of glowing points. The length of a Falcon is 20 meters. The angular size of the plane seen at this distance (calculated from altitude and ground distance) is 0.19 degrees, or 40% of the angular size of the Moon (~0.5 degrees). The unaided eye can distinguish many details (including craters and seas) within the Moon, and even a 40% portion of the Moon. It is therefore entirely possible that the witness distinguished the Falcon's windows, especially since they appear in contrast with the lunar reliefs. The witness, more familiar with planes flying higher, could only distinguish one to three classic lights without any alignment effect, as in this case. The witness contacted by the investigator confirms the angular order of magnitude of the entire set of lights (that of the Moon). A more difficult peculiarity to explain is the presence of a single glowing point above the horizontal line. The strong coincidence on all other points allows us to maintain the hypothesis, as aircraft may have specific lighting in addition to their standard lighting (especially for non-commercial jets like this one). We should also note that this additional light may be due to a perception or memory error by the witness. The fact that the plane's passage is silent (while the window is open) can be explained by the presence of a southerly wind (as reported by Infoclimat) blowing at 15-20 km/h against the propagation of a noise coming from the northeast (the plane's position). The GEIPAN records also contain numerous cases where the witness's auditory perception is masked by attention or visual strangeness. The perception of an airplane noise a few minutes after the observation likely corresponds to the next plane passing to the west of the witness, benefiting from wind-carried noise (see reconstruction with both planes). Notably, the witness does not mention the passage of the Falcon, which nevertheless passes at the exact moment of the observation and within the witness's field of view. This also supports the Falcon hypothesis for PAN. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Airplane observation.
