Summary: Observations of the silent movement of several very bright phenomena in the night sky: likely misidentification with Thai lanterns.
--- seg-405e0b9b33eefe712810d6f6 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 12, 2018, during an astronomy observation evening, two witnesses were intrigued by the observation, first with the naked eye, of the silent movement of two groups of light phenomena resembling "stars." Using a telescope, they observed these "lights," which turned out to be "nice gray-blue balls moving rapidly on an upward trajectory." The observation lasted about ten minutes before disappearing. One of the witnesses attempted to take a photo with his smartphone, unfortunately the photo was unusable. Through analysis (see the investigation report), we have demonstrated that all the data from the testimonies correspond to the hypothesis of a confusion with two groups of Thai lanterns. The elements supporting this hypothesis are as follows:

- Ball shape, the lanterns being observed at a certain distance,
- Low to medium brightness, resembling that of a twinkling star, the candles on the lanterns flickering due to the relative wind caused by their movement,
- White or bluish color, compatible with the possible colors of lanterns, even if they are not the most common,
- Silent movement,
- Observation duration up to 15 minutes,
- Time of year (summer), day of the week (Saturday night to Sunday), and time (night) particularly conducive to lantern releases,
- Constant, slightly upward linear movement, entirely compatible with that of lanterns moving in the absence of wind or with a weak wind.

Additionally, although the data regarding altitude wind are not solidified, they remain favorable for lantern releases under optimal conditions. We also attempted to locate the starting point of the lantern release and proposed a location that fully aligns with the hypothesis (a locational spot surrounded by prairies conducive to lantern releases, located in a narrow valley surrounded by mountain crests forming a corridor that facilitates the movement of lanterns). However, it was not possible to demonstrate the actual presence at the observed day and time of a local celebration justifying the use of such lanterns.

The GEIPAN classifies this observation as B, a probable misidentification with Thai lanterns.
