Description: On the night of February 28 to March 1, 2017, around 2 a.m., a witness was intrigued by a particular glow in the sky. He describes a large, matte black "boomerang" shaped craft with white lights and other flickering orange lights. The last one seems stationary and silent. Surprised and almost frightened, the witness sits down on the living room couch. The PAN will have disappeared when it resumes observation. Only one testimony was collected. The witness was frightened by what he saw and as a result provides little information. He did not extend his observation to determine any possible movement of PAN and, more importantly, to describe the end of observation of this PAN. The only available elements are consistent with an observation of a group of orange and white lanterns, although a weeknight is less conducive to this type of festive event (although a birthday is not always celebrated on a weekend); the perception of a black shape does not contradict this, as it is verified that witnesses of lanterns often believe they see a black shape connecting the lights (perception phenomenon). The absence of perceived movement does not allow for correlation with wind direction and thus strengthens the hypothesis of lanterns. No civil or military aeronautical traffic report provides an explanation for this observation (but radars do not always detect objects flying at low altitude). The GEIPAN does not have enough information to process an observation that is otherwise limited in its strangeness as it remains compatible with a release of lanterns. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of information.
