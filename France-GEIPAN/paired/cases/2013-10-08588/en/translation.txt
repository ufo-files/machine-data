Summary: Observation of a luminous phenomenon in the sky: Lack of information.
--- seg-a6074f4a9d7f0651ef5b234a [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 11, 2013, around 10 PM, several people observed a bright yellow light phenomenon in a cloudy sky. The phenomenon flickered and then went out instantly. Only one testimony was reported. The GEIPAN investigated several hypotheses for this observation of a fixed yellow light point for 2 minutes:

- Star or planet: The witness reported a very cloudy sky, confirmed by weather reports from Nîmes and Avignon. Additionally, in the direction close to that indicated by the witness (Southeast), there is no very bright star visible through a thin cloud layer.

- Sun reflection on a geostationary satellite: No such event was recorded at that time (it would be around 8 PM), and the cloud cover would obscure this phenomenon.

- Thai lantern: The light described by the witness could correspond to a Thai lantern. Friday evenings are conducive to such releases. For a stationary light to be visible, there would need to be a tailwind (Northwest) or headwind (Southeast) relative to the observer, which is not exactly the case as it's a North wind. This hypothesis remains to be considered.

- Airplane: The light described by the witness could correspond to landing lights of an airplane approaching or departing from Avignon. Flightradar24, which lists civil aircraft equipped with transponders, does not mention any aircraft in this area at that time, but a military aircraft seen from the front remains a possible hypothesis.

The GEIPAN does not have the information to confirm either of the last two hypotheses, which remain fragile. The GEIPAN classifies this unusual case as category "C" due to a lack of information.
