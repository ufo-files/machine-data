Summary: Sequential observations of intense lights in the sky by a motorist: observations of sun reflections on planes.
--- seg-cca0b7b9da83387529269c86 [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 13, 2017, at 5:45 PM, a driver had their attention drawn by an intense white light in the clear sky. The light disappears for 15 to 20 seconds, then reappears more intense than before. The observation resumes a little further along the route. Only one testimony is collected. It is very likely a reflection of the sun off of airplanes. The witness himself puts forward this hypothesis, but is surprised by the slow movement in the sky, while admitting that it is difficult for him to assess the movement. He writes about this: "Although they were immobile, but good with the distance... one might perceive movements incorrectly," and we can also add that the witness was driving. There is indeed a good geometric condition for a mirror effect of the sun on planes. There is, on that evening, a succession of aircraft flying in the same direction as the witness and with a heading facing the sun (~240°). In other words, the planes, the sun, and the witness are virtually in the same plane, and since the sun is on the horizon, a mirror effect can be created from sun -> plane -> witness due to the underside of the aircraft's fuselage and even more so from the wings of the planes. The phenomenon is sensitive to geometry, whose evolution with the movement of the aircraft can explain the appearance and disappearance. It is likely that the 20-second interruption corresponded to the end of the phenomenon on one aircraft and the appearance of the phenomenon on another, so that the cumulative observation of several minutes probably did not exceed one or two continuous minutes on (one or more) same aircraft. An airplane observed with an initial incidence angle of 30° (witness data) descends to an elevation of 20° in one minute and 15° in two minutes. This angular change remained weak and may not have been noted by the witness. The attached reconstruction allows us to retrace the final phase of the observation with two planes, one located further and further south than the other, which corresponds well for the witness to a PAN lower and further left. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: airplane observation.
