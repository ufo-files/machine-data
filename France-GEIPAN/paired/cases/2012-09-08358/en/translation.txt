Summary: Long observations of the silent passage of several dozen glowing spheres in the night sky: observation of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-06b52d7aafb9879dff79224f [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Saturday, September 29, 2012, two witnesses observed in the night sky the silent passage of thirty to forty bright orange-red spheres. They appeared at regular intervals and moved in a straight line on the same north-south axis. They seemed to gather above the forest of the Charles Castle in Montmarault. The witnesses decided to go to the location but found the spheres gone. The information provided by the witnesses led the investigation towards the observation of Thai lanterns, whose characteristics were found: orange glow, moving pushed by the wind, without noise. The description of the phenomenon corresponds to the physics of lanterns, illuminated by a candle. This candle generates hot air allowing the lantern to rise into the sky, similar to a hot air balloon. To the witness, it appeared as a sphere (lantern envelope) of orange color (candle light color). The temperature being stabilized within the lantern envelope, and in the absence of strong winds, it maintained a stable altitude. This phenomenon produced no noise. The weather conditions in this region, according to Météociel, included a north-south wind of 10 to 15 km/h, 6/8 cloudiness, 12°C temperature, and 1020 hPa pressure. These floating objects were therefore pushed from north to south, which corresponds to the trajectory of the phenomenon described by the witness and the gendarmerie report. However, the witness's evaluation of the lanterns' speed at 50 km/h is significantly overestimated. This is due to the poor estimation of the distance of an unrecognized object. For the same angular velocity (the only element perceived by a witness), an overestimation of distance leads to an overestimation of speed. The Saturday evening date is also suggestive of a family or friend gathering. A wedding evening is mentioned in the gendarmerie report. The successive lighting of the lanterns corresponds to the witness's description of lanterns following the same trajectory (wind direction), spaced 20 to 25 seconds apart. As for the gathering of all these spheres above the distant forest, this corresponds to a perspective effect, similar to train tracks appearing to converge at the horizon. Similarly, the witness's perception that the first spheres were waiting for the others falls within the same perspective effect. As for the disappearance, it corresponds to the end of the candle's combustion. Without a heat source, the invisible lantern falls. Consequently, the GEIPAN is classified as A: Observation of Thai lanterns.
