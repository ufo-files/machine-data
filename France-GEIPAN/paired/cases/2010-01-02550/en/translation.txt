Summary: Observation of the movements of several bright points during the day: probable misperception due to sun reflections on bird flights.
--- seg-ef19c0e9c1d561615d48bfa2 [needs-review] ---
Description: On June 20, 2017, in the late morning, the witness, at a friend's place, was intrigued by the sound of a helicopter. He went to the balcony and searched for it in the sky. The witness then saw, in broad daylight, bright lights whose number successively went from 1 to 3, then to 6. They appeared and moved in the clear sky with rapid changes in direction before suddenly disappearing. Later the same day, the witness again observed similar movements of a bright light. The next day, around 12:10, returning to his friend's place, he saw, in the same area, a bright light in a cloudier sky, but did not dwell on it. Only one testimony was collected. Several hypotheses are considered: a misperception with flying lanterns or balloons or birds (see the investigation report). The rapid and curved movements of the PAN are fairly consistent with these explanatory hypotheses. The observation of flying lanterns is, however, less consistent as it occurred in broad daylight, removing the playful aspect. This hypothesis is not retained. The balloon hypothesis could be linked to Music Day, but it should be noted that the weak wind does not really match that of the PAN and the movements follow varied trajectories. A misperception with circling birds in the sky presents elements that can explain the observation. The visibility of circling birds in a blue sky, with no reference point to fix a distance for the witness, depends on several factors: 
- the lighting of a bird component with high reflectivity (white wings for a bird while the body is darker). On June 20, in the late morning, the sun is very high in the sky; therefore, the lighting of the wings alone is not direct (observed from below), but rather indirect through the effect of atmospheric diffusion, just as the wings of an airplane or its fuselage can sometimes be the only visible elements; 
- the significant observation distance preventing the witness from distinguishing anything other than these most reflective elements; 
- the birds' own movements, circling while gliding, which makes their wings visible (larger reflective surface) than during flapping flight (but the visibility limit of wings between the two types of flight is difficult to fix, as it depends on many unknown parameters); 

The strangeness of the case appears low based on the bird hypothesis. Consistency is average as there is only one witness and no photos of the PAN were taken. Even if there is a slight doubt about the actual date of the observation (the witness mentions the presence of clouds that were not there or very few), his descriptions are fairly precise. However, it is surprising that there is only one witness, while the observation took place over a heavily urbanized area in broad daylight. In summary, the rapid and curved movements of the PAN could possibly correspond to balloons or birds. This hypothesis presents more consistency with the elements provided by the witness. Unfortunately, the testimony is unique while the observation took place in broad daylight over a heavily urbanized area. Of low strangeness and average consistency (one witness, no photo of the PAN, doubt about the observation date), this case turns out to be a possible misperception with sun reflections on birds. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B, probable misperception with sun reflections on bird flights.
