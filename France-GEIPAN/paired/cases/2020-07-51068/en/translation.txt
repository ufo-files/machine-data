Summary: Two-phase observation of the passage of several colored lights forming a lost V that reappeared as a white light in the night sky: Phase 1 observation classified as D (Unidentified Phenomenon) after investigation, and Phase 2 classified as "B," probable observation of the ISS.
--- seg-b9943c570a1ede6aeea8e233 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 20, 2020, around 11:35 PM, an observer outside in METZ (57) using binoculars to watch the Neowise comet noticed a phenomenon in the northwest that initially appeared to be a commercial jet making a low-altitude turn. The overall shape, in the form of a "V", presents a central red light surrounded by several fixed and intense colored lights that obscure the object's contours. The phenomenon first evolves slowly before being lost from view for 15 seconds. It is then observed as a dazzling white light, viewed through binoculars at the end of the flight path. The observation, totally silent, lasts between 45 seconds and a minute before the phenomenon disappears to the east behind the top of the trees. Only one testimony was collected. These two distinct sequences led to splitting the analysis into two phases. The spatial and temporal coincidence of these two independent events contributed to reinforcing the witness's feeling of strangeness. The consistency of this case, initially assessed as "average", was re-evaluated as "good" following the field investigation. This reevaluation is based on the witness's cooperation, which allowed for the isolated nature of the account and the absence of photographic or video evidence to be mitigated. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Several hypotheses have been explored for both phases (see the investigation report): Phase 1: that of a civilian or military aircraft, a luminous balloon, or a Thai lantern. Phase 2: that of observing the passage of the ISS (International Space Station). Regarding Phase 1, the hypothesis of observing a civilian or military aircraft was studied, while the others (LED balloons or Chinese lanterns) presented too many inconsistencies in view of the PAN's characteristics. Although the CAPCODA* radar map reveals no trace of an aircraft that can be equated with PAN, some parameters suggest the observation of a heavy military transport aircraft, such as a Hercules C-130 or A400M in a training flight TBA*, and in particular: - the presence of a fixed red light (confirmed during the on-site investigation) and at least one fixed green light, assimilable to position lights. The absence of anti-collision lights is entirely possible during military exercises, these lights not necessarily being turned on. -The change in shape (from the alignment of the lights to a more or less pronounced reversed "V") is caused by the change in perspective as the aircraft makes a turn. -The apparent size is described by the witness as being "the wingspan of a large commercial airliner," with the witness initially thinking "a commercial airliner at low altitude." -The speed is described by the witness as "very reduced," which is typical of observations of military transport aircraft, which have relatively low cruising speeds (sometimes less than 200 km/h). The investigation also established that a large-scale military exercise, named CASEX, began on the day of the observation, mobilizing many French and German aircraft within a regulated area. Although this exercise was located at a significant distance from the witness's position, the possibility that a military aircraft flying at very low altitude might have evaded radar or been operating with a transponder off was considered. However, the military aeronautics expert of the GEIPAN considers the presence of such an aircraft highly unlikely in this context. This conclusion is reinforced by the complete absence of perceived noise during the event, even though wind conditions were favorable for sound propagation. Regarding Phase 2, the measurements obtained during the investigation confirm that the International Space Station (ISS) was traveling on the trajectory described for the phenomenon that evening. The overall appearance, timing, duration of observation, and complete absence of perceived noise are convergent elements in favor of this identification. The mention of flashes and consecutive blinding observations with binoculars and the hazy appearance around PAN do not align with this hypothesis, without us having been able to assess with certainty if the conditions of using the binoculars may have exacerbated these perceptions. If the observed object had not been the ISS, the fact that the witness did not spot the space station as it was precisely in the same time frame and on the same trajectory remains inexplicable. The absence of simultaneous detection of a second object strengthens the hypothesis of a confusion between the phenomenon and the passage of the station. According to the breakdown of this observation into 2 phases, the investigation work did not allow the perceived strangeness by the witness to be reduced by comparing the observation to a known phenomenon for the first phase, while a consolidated hypothesis better fits for the second phase. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies phase 1 of the observation as 'D', unidentified phenomenon after investigation, and phase 2 as 'B', probable observation of the ISS. *CAPCODA: Air and Space Defense Operations Planning and Control Center (Air and Space Force). *TBA: Very Low Altitude.
