**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases previously classified as Type "D" that are now being re-examined. This re-examination aims for more accurate conclusions, not to diminish or enhance any particular classification category. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or explicit changes in classification noted in an investigation report. This observation case previously named GROIX (56) 13.04.1982 is among cases classified "D" by the GEPAN and recently reviewed.

On the island of Groix (Morbihan), in the night of April 13th to 14th, 1982, at 2:45 a.m. (the most probable observation time, according to the gendarmerie), three individuals witnessed a luminous phenomenon in the sky. While traveling in a vehicle to various locations on the island, the witnesses felt that the PAN was following or stopping alongside them during their travels. One witness returned home and woke up a fourth witness, who confirmed the presence of a very bright, stationary object in the sky around 3 a.m. that he could not recognize. The observation continued in different locations on the island until 5 a.m. for some witnesses, and the fourth witness made a similar observation the following day.

The gendarmerie, inadvertently notified on April 20th, 1982, conducted an on-site investigation a few days later, finding no additional witnesses. The investigation revealed no EDF disruptions or unusual observations from the Lann-Bihoué Naval Air Base. The credibility of the witnesses is not in question. The gendarmes noted the phase of the moon as a last quarter. No traces on the ground or in the environment were found in the areas indicated by the witnesses.

This case is moderately consistent: there are four witnesses, but with contradictions and imprecisions, particularly regarding the date and the speed or direction of the PAN's movement. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, etc.) with well-known astronomical objects: the Moon (see investigation notes). The appearance of the PAN typically includes a half-circle, yet the gendarmerie's investigation report states that the moon was in its last quarter. T1 compares the PAN to "a small planet that approached Earth."

The PAN was observed for several hours (3 hours) and moved slowly in the sky, according to a movement described as from West to East (despite some contradictions between witnesses)... similar to the Moon's movement. Furthermore, T4 states that the following night, in the same part of the sky, he saw "an object with identical shapes to the previous one...: the moon was indeed back." The moon was full (phase 0.7), clearly present in the relatively narrow sky area (East, Southeast) described as the location of the PAN, while the witnesses clearly stated that they did not recognize the moon.

- If they claim not to have recognized it, it means they didn't see it either, otherwise at least two of them would have naturally said, "by the way, we could clearly see the moon next to the PAN" to emphasize that it wasn't the moon.
- On the other hand, there is at least one who says, "It was pitch black, there was no moon."

They couldn't have failed to see it during such a long observation period, with a clear sky and a moon position at the end of the observation at 20 degrees elevation above the ocean in the direction of observation. This type of misperception, usually caused by clouds or cloud veils partially covering the moon, is common in GEIPAN records (see GEIPAN site with keyword Moon). Specifically, this case illustrates the "following ball" illusion (perceptual illusion due to the witnesses' own movement), as the PAN seems to stop when the witnesses are stationary and resumes its course when the witnesses start moving again.

This perception generates strong emotions (with perhaps ideas or hypotheses about encountering an "intelligence"), which can lead to a loss of precision in perception or memory. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies this case as A: Observation of the Moon.
