Summary: Observation from two different locations in Haut-Rhin (68) of the movement of several bright points in the sky: very likely observation of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-78125e1873f74f7c400b9ed1 [needs-review] ---
Description: On February 22, 2015, between 6:45 PM and 6:55 PM, several people in Habsheim (68) and Eschentzwiller (68) observed moving bright points in the sky. The witnesses' accounts and the photos provided clearly suggest Thai lanterns, which was the only hypothesis considered: the context is quite favorable to lanterns on the following points: - There was the Carnival of Mulhouse on February 20-21 and 22 - There was the Carnival of Basel, which began on February 23 at 4 a.m. with the Morgenstreich (a procession illuminated by lanterns, not flying!) - In all of Alsace, neighboring Germany, and Switzerland, it is a period of Carnival (celebrations in most villages, spread over more than a month). - It was still the period of the Chinese New Year (February 19). - It was a Sunday, which also allows for the possibility of another association or private celebration. The witness situation (filed with various visuals) suggests that the phenomenon was fairly close and therefore at a relatively low altitude. The movement is slow and the wind very light. The progressive and random disappearance of the lights is best explained by lanterns (or variants). Non-geometric formation flight as well. The white color for one of the witnesses and more so the orange for the other are also compatible, as is the absence of noise (one got out of the car, the other opened the window). The two photos are typical of lantern (or light) observations (with "blurring" due to the low light, thus the low shutter speed). Negative aspects of the hypothesis: The wind was very light and from the southeast, which is therefore not directly compatible with the witness's statement from Habsheim (68) that they saw them moving from right to left (i.e., towards the nearby Germany). The second witness describes them as rather stationary, which is not inherently incompatible, but not frankly consistent with the presumed wind, which, as we recall, is very light (3-5 km/h), therefore unstable and subject to local variations. We cannot therefore assert that the objects were flying with the wind, but their very slow movement is not contradictory to this hypothesis. - No mention found (so far) in the press or on the web. - Only two testimonies about a potential significantly higher (nearby cities + villages + highway). In view of the descriptive and behavioral characteristics, as well as the very festive context of the region on that day, the GEIPAN classifies this case as B: very probable observation of Thai lanterns.
