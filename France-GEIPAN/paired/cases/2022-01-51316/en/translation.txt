Summary: Brief nighttime observation of the slow movement of two white, then orange, lights: probable observation of sky lanterns (or flying lanterns or Thai lanterns).
--- seg-cb855e49d9ebe5be1c17b987 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On January 9, 2022, at 7:53 PM, an observer captivated by an astronomical observation of the Moon, using his A2-GTI mount equipped with a Nikon P1000, observed towards the red star Betelgeuse, two white glowing points that seemed fixed at the height of that star. The witness then has the reflex to direct his mount towards these two points in order to observe them. Without any conclusive results, he observes with the naked eye that these two PAN are moving by shifting to the right (SSE), taking on an orange hue and a more or less spherical shape. The movement is slow. The two PAN disappear by gradually turning off. The observation lasted a few seconds. The case presents an average consistency: one witness, no images, but some technical elements (shape, color, direction of movement). The strangeness, after our study, is quite low (see the investigation report). The strangeness reported by the witness seems essentially due to him finding a "similar" testimony on a social network in Pas-de-Calais. It was not possible to locate this witness and confront their observations. The appearance, shape, mode of disappearance, colors (especially near the end of the observation), weather, time, and day (a Sunday evening) are all elements pointing towards the observation of sky lanterns or LED light balloons. These 2 hypotheses are very close (wind-carried objects). The orange color and the sudden disappearance lead us to favor the hypothesis of celestial lanterns (or flying lanterns or Thai lanterns). The direction of movement of the PAN does not perfectly match the wind data from the weather station located 8 km from the witness's position, which can be explained by the very low wind speed, gusts, and the overflight of a city, which can cause orientation changes. The GEIPAN is classified as B: probable observation of sky lanterns (or flying lanterns or Thai lanterns).
