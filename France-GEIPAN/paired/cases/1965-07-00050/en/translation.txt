Description: On July 1, 1965 at 5:45 a.m., a farmer in his lavender field hears a whistling that intrigues him. He moves to find that a dark, matte object unknown to him is sitting on the ground. He observed for 4 to 5 minutes the presence of two individuals who quickly climbed back into the craft. The last one takes off and goes into a tilt before quickly disappearing in the direction of Manosque. A few minutes later, the farmer discovered traces on the ground at the location of the craft: these traces had the shape of a star with a cylindrical hole in the middle in a muddy soil. He returns in the evening around 8:30 PM with his daughter and notices that the ground has become "hard as cement." The next morning, July 2, he talks about this case, and very quickly many curious people move to see the tracks that will be trampled. Around 7:30 PM, the local gendarmerie learns of this event through public rumor. The witness will be heard for the first time on July 2, 1965, at 20:00 by the local brigade (see report 445). Around 10 PM, the witness will accompany the gendarmes to the scene. He will be heard again from 11:15 PM to 11:30 PM for additional information. A protocol of observations at the observation site and ground traces will be established on July 3rd by another brigade (see protocol 105) with measurements, photographs. The witness will provide a very detailed additional report on August 18, 1965 (see report 145). No further testimony will be collected. No other evidence was found around the location of the observation by the gendarmerie. At the time of this observation, the GEPAN, established in 1977, did not exist; successive responsible parties of the GEPAN/SEPRA/GEIPAN naturally took interest in this case, received or collected documents about it, without however publishing an in-depth investigation; a GEPAN investigation would likely have been unproductive due to the time elapsed since the event, and following the investigation work already conducted by the Gendarmerie and by several investigators, including Pierre Guérin and Jacques Vallée. Due to its strangeness, this case is classified "D".
