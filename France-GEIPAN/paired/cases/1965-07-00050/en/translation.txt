Description: On July 1st, 1965, at 5:45 a.m., a farmer in his lavender field hears a whistling sound that intrigues him. He moves to investigate and notices a dark, unfamiliar craft on the ground. For 4 to 5 minutes, he observes two figures quickly ascending into the vehicle. The craft takes off and flies off at an oblique angle before quickly disappearing in the direction of Manosque. About ten minutes later, the farmer discovers ground traces at the craft's location: these traces form a star shape with a cylindrical hole in the center in wet soil. He returns in the evening around 8:30 p.m. with his daughter, and notices that the soil has become "hard as cement." The next morning, July 2nd, he discusses the incident, and soon many curious people arrive to see the traces, which are trampled. Around 7:30 p.m., local gendarmes learn of the event through public rumor. The witness is interviewed for the first time on July 2nd, 1965, at 8:00 p.m. by the local brigade (see protocol 445). Around 10:00 p.m., the witness accompanies the gendarmes to the site. He is interviewed again from 11:15 p.m. to 11:30 p.m. for additional information. A protocol of observations at the observation site and of the ground traces is drawn up on July 3rd by another brigade (see protocol 105), with measurements and photographs taken. The witness provides a detailed additional report on August 18th, 1965 (see protocol 145). No other testimonies are collected. The gendarmerie does not find any other clues around the observation site. At the time of this observation, the GEPAN (established in 1977) did not exist; all successive responsible parties of the GEPAN/SEPRA/GEIPAN were naturally interested in this case, received or collected documents about it, but did not publish a thorough investigation; an GEPAN investigation would likely have been unproductive due to the time elapsed since the event, and after the investigation work already conducted by the Gendarmerie and several investigators, including Pierre Guérin and Jacques Vallée. Due to its strangeness, this case is classified as 'D'.
