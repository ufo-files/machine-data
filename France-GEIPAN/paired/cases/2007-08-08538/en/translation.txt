Summary: An automobilist observed a large glowing disk in the landscape: lack of information and cross-checking.
--- seg-3ec29b99fb7c3e325116f33c [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: Recent (2013) testimony of an old (2007) observation. On August 15, 2007, at 11:45 PM, a witness, while driving, observed a large luminous disk that he initially mistook for the moon. The view of the phenomenon was obscured by the landscape for several minutes, after which the PAN was no longer visible. Astronomical verification shows that the Moon was not visible at that time (end of crescent, down for over an hour). However, it is noted that the planet Jupiter is very close to the observation direction indicated by the witness, and it is particularly bright (magnitude -2). It is nonetheless surprising that it was perceived as large as the Moon. The weather report from Meteociel indicates a cloudy sky with intermittent rain. It could be thought of as a diffusion effect of low clouds creating a halo around Jupiter (see winter halo). In a completely different context, it could also be the lighthouse of an agricultural vehicle on a ridge, seen through mist after rain. It is very difficult a posteriori to validate or not either of these hypotheses. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" due to lack of information and cross-checking.
