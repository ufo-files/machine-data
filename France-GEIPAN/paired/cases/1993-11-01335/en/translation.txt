**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named ECQUEVILLY (78) 15.11.1993 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been re-examined.

On the night of November 14 to 15, 1993, around 4:30 a.m., a witness is awakened by his dogs barking. He gets up and notices that not only his dogs but also his horses are looking in the same direction. The witness goes to another room in the house and sees through the window (approximately 300 meters west of his residence, according to the gendarmerie) static lights illuminating the ground. Despite having the window open, the witness hears no noise. However, he observes that the lights are moving towards him. When they reach his home, the witness sees a circular light composed of white, yellow, and slightly blue lights of varying intensities. Black light flashes are also observed. The observation lasted between 20 and 25 seconds. The witness steps outside his residence, but the lights have disappeared.

On November 24, 1993, the witness went to the Gendarmerie to give a statement and later provided sketches of his observation. No traces were found, and no other testimonies were collected during the Gendarmerie investigation.

GEIPAN re-contacted the witness in July 2018. Through analysis (see investigation report), we demonstrated that the PAN observed by the witness was ultimately of a relatively modest size (approximately 5 degrees, rounded). This modest size contrasts with the sketches made by the witness, which suggested that the PAN might have been larger. However, this angular size remains too significant to align well with the only somewhat credible hypothesis of a helicopter confusion. This hypothesis would imply that the helicopter was close to the witness (approximately 55 meters for a type EC145 helicopter at the time) and therefore audible, in the context of nighttime silence, with the window open, while the witness heard no noise. Other testimonial parameters also prevent validating this hypothesis, such as the significant number of lights observed, the absence of navigation and anti-collision lights, and the ground lighting by "black light."

The consistency is good, with a detailed testimony and an on-site investigation conducted shortly thereafter by the gendarmes. Although the witness did not have the reflex to take photos and videos, and no angular measurements were made during the investigation, we were still able, thanks to the photographs taken on-site by the gendarmes, the sketch, and the witness's testimony, to accurately reconstruct the trajectory of the PAN and visualize its appearance.

In the current GEIPAN methodology, this level of strangeness and consistency requires conducting a "field investigation" with the witness for a cognitive reconstruction and interview. This last step can positively or negatively impact the evaluation of strangeness and consistency. However, it is not possible for a case revisited so long after the event. Therefore, the evaluation of this case is made according to the current criteria for considering strangeness and consistency (see GEIPAN's current methodology on April 27, 2017). As if the field investigation had been conducted and was completely neutral (no additional input), which is sometimes the case.

In contrast, to mark this methodological gap, the classification is done based on the A B C D system rather than the current A B C D1/D2 which, for unexplained classifications, corresponds to a stronger validation of the unexplained character. In this case, the case is the boundary between an unexplained case and an unexploitable case due to a lack of consistency in the current file, without a GEIPAN on-site investigation. Consequently, the case is classified as D: unexplained.
