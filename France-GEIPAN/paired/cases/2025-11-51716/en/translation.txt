Description: On December 10, 2025, at approximately 23:03, the witness was outside their home on the Giens peninsula. They observed three or four bright flying objects moving at an extremely high speed on a north-south axis in a particularly clear sky. According to their testimony, the objects left behind them long trails of green, red, and blue, suggesting a powerful and unusual propulsion. The scene lasted about five seconds. The strangeness perceived by the witness is mainly explained by the unusual combination of several factors rarely observed simultaneously: the brevity of the phenomenon, its high speed, the presence of several parallel trails, and very contrasting colors (see the investigation report). A fragmented meteor can create the illusion of a coordinated formation of several flying objects moving in unison. In reality, it is not several distinct vehicles maneuvering together, but fragments naturally following the same trajectory due to the speed. The intense brightness in a night sky, enhanced by cloudiness, can give the trails a smooth, homogeneous, and almost artificial appearance, resembling controlled light beams. Finally, the sudden appearance and rapid disappearance leave little time for rational analysis, strengthening the impression of a structured and intentional phenomenon.

The technical analysis of the testimonies and collected data allows for a certain origin of the observed phenomenon to be established with certainty. The characteristics observed converge towards a meteoritic entry, more precisely a meteoroid that fragmented upon entering the atmosphere. The parallel, distinct, and homogeneous light trails can be explained by an early fragmentation of the main body, with fragments maintaining a similar trajectory. The vivid colors reported (red, green, blue) are physically compatible with atmospheric ionization and reflect the mineral composition of the meteoroid. The gradual extinction over one to two seconds also corresponds to the classic behavior of a meteor fading at high altitude. Furthermore, the dense cloudiness observed at the time of the event is likely to have diffused and smoothed the appearance of the trails, enhancing the impression of clear and homogeneous "rays."

All the described characteristics confirm the hypothesis of an atmospheric entry. This conclusion is all the more solid as a strict temporal correlation has been established: at the same time, three independent witnesses located in Nice and Antibes reported observing bolides through the Vigie-Ciel network. The investigations conducted help to reduce the strangeness of this observation. Therefore, the GEIPAN classifies this case as a "A" observation of an atmospheric entry of a bolide.
