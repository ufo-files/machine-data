Summary: Observation with the naked eye and then with binoculars of a stationary, luminous phenomenon in the starry night sky: observation of Mars.
--- seg-671d3478427ec2f6ec4228f2 [needs-review] ---
**Description:** On the night of August 6th to 7th, 2018, around 01:12 AM, a witness on their outdoor terrace was surprised by the sight of a stationary luminous phenomenon facing them. The witness decided to observe this phenomenon with their binoculars and was amazed to see a glowing circle of various colors with two very bright red and yellow lights at its center. The witness contacted the gendarmerie and then went to bed at 01:30, disturbed by the observation. At 05:05 AM, they woke up but the phenomenon (PAN) had disappeared. No other testimonies about this phenomenon were collected. This testimony illustrates the confusion that can arise from a misidentification of a star, especially when the witness uses binoculars to observe it. The witness's precise drawings allow us to accurately reconstruct the direction of observation, which is directly south. Mars (very bright, magnitude -3.6) is located above the mountains, as shown in the drawing. At 20 degrees above the horizon, Mars stands out clearly from the mountain peaks, which do not exceed 5 degrees in height from the witness's perspective. The witness (classically) overestimated the height of the PAN in the sky (45 degrees). Saturn is also present, 30 degrees to the right (west), less bright (magnitude 0) but still brighter than the rest of the stars in the area, leading the witness to believe there were two very bright red (Mars) and yellow (Saturn) lights with white stars surrounding them. The witness then aimed their binoculars at Mars. A witness who has already been the victim of a misidentification of a star is often even more confused when using binoculars, as they often have difficulty focusing on the star (binocular artifacts) or see strange phenomena when observing a star with binoculars for the first time. The sky was clear, and if the witness had been observing a PAN other than Mars, they would have very likely indicated the presence of Mars given the quality of their location in their drawings. Consequently, the GEIPAN concludes that it was an observation of Mars.
