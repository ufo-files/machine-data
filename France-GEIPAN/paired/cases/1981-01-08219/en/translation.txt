Summary: Observation of the joint movement of three bright points in the night sky: satellite observation.
--- seg-dbc383f08b21efbf22b057ef [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: In March 2012, the GEIPAN received a questionnaire reporting an observation made on January 15, 1981, at 5:50 AM in Perpignan. That morning, just before starting work, a witness was intrigued by the south-north passage, in a clear sky, of three bright points strictly aligned and equidistant from each other. The observation lasted 3 minutes before the points disappeared from the witness's field of vision. Only one testimony was collected. It is a post-observation testimony 35 years later. The case presents little strangeness. The time, duration, and trajectory are compatible with satellites. What surprises the witness results from a satellite group effect, which can be either a cluster of close and collaborative satellites or entirely distinct satellites evolving on parallel orbital planes. The second hypothesis is more likely because swarms did not exist in 1981 (except perhaps in US confidential programs). This hypothesis implies, in principle, a greater angular deviation between objects than in the swarm case, but this is difficult to clarify given the antiquity of the testimony. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: satellites.
