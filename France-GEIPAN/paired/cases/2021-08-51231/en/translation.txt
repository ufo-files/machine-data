**Description:** On the night of August 9, 2021, around 1:00 AM, a witness lying on the terrace of their room at the Hôtel de la Cité in Carcassonne was observing the night sky for shooting stars, during the astronomical period corresponding to Earth's passage through the Perseid meteor shower. The witness then observed an unusual phenomenon, which they termed "OVNI." The observation lasted less than 10 seconds. For the witness, it was "a formation of equally spaced lights, articulated in a delta wing configuration, roughly a dozen per branch." These lights were "powerful, without flash or blinking, of constant, unvarying intensity, matching the brightness of the brightest stars at that time, moving at a speed estimated at 4 or 5 times that of the fastest satellites observable every night (usually in the opposite direction) without any sound. Total silence. In 4 or 5 seconds, the sky was crossed. The movement was regular, like a ruler drawn in the direction of the arrow, from northwest to southeast, never changing its speed..."

The size appeared "immense," comparable to the length of an outstretched arm. "These lights seemed to be in a formation, obeying the point at the head of the line, rather than being part of a joint structure, since suddenly, the last three lights on the top branch in the simulation made brief, sharp lateral movements, immediately returning to the 'orthodox' direction of the entire formation's movement. Like a formation flight of migratory birds in a nearly square 'V'; but it wasn't that, unless they were all strapped with long-range beacons under their bellies and flying at supersonic speeds."

Only one testimony was collected for this phenomenon. The strangeness felt by the witness was due to the observed light, which the witness believed could not be from birds, as well as the movement speed of the phenomenon. The observation description is well-documented, enriched with a drawing and numerous precise details. The witness themselves posited the hypothesis of migratory birds, though they did not understand the lighting effect of the entire 'V' formation flight. The speed appears compatible with the hypothesis of bird observation. Regarding the strangeness due to the light, the witness is located in Carcassonne, inside the city, whose walls are illuminated from the outside by powerful projectors aimed at highlighting the beauty of the city's contours, visible from several kilometers away. These numerous ground projectors, regularly spaced against the walls and directed towards the sky, brightly illuminate the entire fortress for display purposes. Migratory birds passing vertically over this city can appear luminous. This observation is typical of a bird flight in a 'V' formation, where the chest reflects the brightness of projectors aimed at the city's walls and towers.

The region is overflown by migratory birds (Great Cormorants, Sand Cranes, White Storks in July/August). The witness reported the sudden movement of the last three lights on one branch and their return to the 'V' formation configuration. Birds can briefly veer and return to the formation. Indeed, the scenario where one bird veers and the two behind follow is coherent. The direction from northwest to southeast (towards Narbonne, Gruissan) is consistent with migratory flights of White Storks towards a known ornithological area in this region rich in migratory bird passages: an LPO (French Ornithological Society) article reports "For post-nuptial migration observation, the favorable period extends from mid-July to mid-November (with bird passages still very perceptible until the end of November or even early December for some species escaping the cold snaps from Northern Europe). In the best years, 20,000 to 25,000 raptors can be counted, and 5,000 to 10,000 storks (primarily White Storks)."

An ornithologist's analysis of potential migratory birds is as follows: "We can rule out cranes (too early) and storks or any other large glider that only flies during the day. The only large birds that fly at night are herons but they generally do not form 'V' formations. The candidates best matching the date, time, and formation flight could be Great Cormorants. These birds are entirely black but they have a more or less marked white patch under the head just after the beak (also clear) and another patch at the base of the leg but only in the nuptial plumage. The reflection of light on the white parts and/or the beak is not impossible."

**GEIPAN** is classified as **"B":** probable observation of a bird flight in a 'V' formation.
