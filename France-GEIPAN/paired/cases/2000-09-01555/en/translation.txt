Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more accurate in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D is part of a recent batch of cases that have been re-examined. On September 1, 2000, around 5:30 a.m. (approximately 3:30 UTC), a man briefly sees a geometric shape composed of bright points in the form of a triangle. The PAN disappears behind a building. No noise is heard. No other testimony is collected. The routine investigation conducted by the GEIPAN at the time (SEPRA) with aviation authorities shows that the airport radar confirms the recording of three unknown traffic tracks detected at 5:40 a.m. in the southeastern Toulouse area. The recording, which is not confirmed by military authorities, is entrusted to radar specialists at the DGAC for an expert opinion. The results of the expertise are clear: these tracks are not significant. They are in fact the result of a parasitic response caused by an aircraft beyond the effective range of the radar. The coincidence with the witness's observation led for a moment to a belief in the confirmation of the materiality of their observation. However, it turned out that the GEIPAN had classified the observation itself as unexplained. A revisit of this case 18 years later finds no reason to change the analysis of the radar artifact. On the other hand, the unexplained nature of the observation cannot be maintained in light of the consistency of the testimony. This testimony is very vague:

- What was orange, the bright points or the entire shape? Based on the witness's drawing, it can be assumed that it is only the bright points, but what does the witness mean by "shape"?

- How long did the observation last? The expression "I barely had time to see it" could mean a fraction of a second or up to 10 seconds. In the first case, one might think of shooting stars or the return of meteoroids with fragmentation, but angular spacing of the points (size of the triangle) and the presence or absence of halos would also be needed. In the second case (observation lasting 10 seconds), the first hypothesis (shooting stars or atmospheric re-entry) is ruled out.

- According to the shape of the bright points and the behavior of the light, it could be a flight of white birds retro-illuminated by urban lights (a mistake already recorded; search for cases on www.geipan.fr using the keyword "bird").

- It is only known that it was heading from north to southwest, which goes against objects carried by wind originating from the south (7 km/h). There are too many unanswered questions to explore, confirm, or deny the hypotheses that the GEIPAN has learned to pose (perhaps not the case 18 years ago) when a witness sees several bright points in a triangle. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
