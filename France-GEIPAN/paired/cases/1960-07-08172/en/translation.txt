Description: Recent testimony of an old observation: the witness speaks of an observation made around 1960 without further details (the given date here is an arbitrary day and month). In late afternoon, two people are on the porch of a house. A storm is threatening, the sky is dark gray. Looking towards the N-E, they see a "white, vented fire drop" descending vertically from the storm clouds. The observation of this "drop" with a pointed top lasts one to three seconds: the phenomenon disappears behind a curtain of trees. No noise, and no fire on the ground followed. The case was submitted to an expert in lightning study. The expert confirms that this type of observation is not rare: it happens that one or more luminous spheres descend (like the slow fall of a distress rocket) from the base of the cumulonimbus during strong pre-storm weather. Generally, the junction of the sphere with the ground or a structure results in a single lightning strike, almost always during a dry storm. The phenomenon is part of the multiple facets of ball lightning. This case is classified as a "B" observation, probable ball lightning.
