Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case named PELLOUAILLES-LES-VIGNES (49) 24.04.1995 is part of a recent set of cases that have been re-examined. This case concerns the observation on April 24, 1995, around 9:30 PM of a PAN by three witnesses in a known location in the commune of SAINT-SYLVAIN-D’ANJOU (49). The witnesses are outside when they notice in an uncultivated field the presence of a PAN. They describe a large oval-shaped mass, gray "concrete" in color, stationary in a field between 10 and 15 meters high. No noise was heard, particularly when the PAN moved and disappeared at a very high speed towards the NE. A red glowing point is observed during the short observation. The witnesses immediately go to the field at the presumed location of the PAN but find nothing unusual. The Gendarmerie, when contacted, will conduct interviews with witnesses, and the press will publish an article the day after the observation (article not attached to the report). The diffuse appearance of PAN in an initial phase, then becoming clearer, compact, and colored towards the end of the observation, along with its behavior consisting of a phase of immobility followed by a phase of rapid movement, supports the hypothesis of a confusion with the impact of a mobile disco projector onto the low cloud layer (see the investigation report). This impact would have been observed by witnesses first far from its original source, then, with the spotlight oriented more vertically at the end of the observation, further from the witnesses but closer to that source. This explains, as the shot approaches the vertical, the reduction in size, the less flattened shape, the brighter luminosity, and the clearer color (of the source). These "laser" lights (commonly referred to as "skytrackers") were widely used for advertising purposes, often by nightclubs in the 1980s and 1990s. The GEIPAN has recorded numerous observations resulting from misidentifications caused by these installations. (see www.geipan.fr and case search with keyword 'laser'). Moreover, in the police report, the gendarmes report that a newspaper article published the day after the observation suggested that a "laser lighting test had been carried out by a nightclub at the same time as the observation." This could not be confirmed, as the press article was not provided by the gendarmes, and the discotheque owner never presented himself to the gendarmes, despite several summons. The three witnesses mentioned a red glowing point, smaller than the main shape and accompanying it. In 1995, red lasers already existed, though with lower power and therefore shorter range than today. The skytracer 90s are not lasers, but classic light projectors. Laser technology allows for thinner and brighter beams. This could explain the (point) impact spot of a laser tested that day and coupled with the classic projector. But nothing supports such an explanation. There is an element of residual strangeness because this has never been observed in laser witness accounts of GEIPAN. The case consistency is poor. Important data is missing, such as azimuth and elevation angle measurements of PAN and angular size. The witnesses did not provide sketches of the PAN, despite requests from the gendarmes during the hearings, which somewhat impairs the assessment of the reliability of the testimonies. An on-site investigation at the time could have clarified many points, in addition to those mentioned above, and potentially confirmed or eliminated the hypothesis of the disco test, if the manager of the suspected disco that caused the confusion had been interviewed or visited at that time. We have a plausible hypothesis due to the overall aspect and a potential explanation regarding the origin of the disco. Nevertheless, the red point noted by the witnesses constitutes a singular point for this hypothesis and confers a certain level of residual strangeness. In view of this, the consistency is poor and too weak to validate an hypothesis with such strangeness resulting from it, even weaker to give an unexplained character if the hypothesis were not to be retained at all. It is not possible to conclude. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
