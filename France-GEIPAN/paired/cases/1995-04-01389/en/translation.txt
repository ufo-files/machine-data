**Description:** The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases previously classified (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the accuracy of conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named PELLOUAILLES-LES-VIGNES (49) 24.04.1995 is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases.

The case involves the observation on April 24, 1995, around 9:30 PM of an unidentified flying object (UFO) by three witnesses in a rural area of the commune of SAINT-SYLVAIN-D’ANJOU (49). The witnesses, who were outside, noticed a large, oval-shaped, gray "concrete" object stationary in a field, between 10 and 15 meters high. No sound was heard, even when the UFO moved and disappeared at very high speed towards the northeast. A red glowing point was observed during the brief observation. The witnesses immediately went to the field where the UFO was reportedly located, but found nothing unusual. The local gendarmerie was contacted, and the press published an article the following day (the article was not attached to the incident report).

The UFO's initial diffuse appearance, then becoming clearer, compact, and colored towards the end of the observation, along with its behavior—a phase of immobility followed by rapid movement—support the hypothesis of a confusion with the impact of a mobile disco laser projector on a low-level cloud layer (as detailed in the investigation report). This impact would have been observed by the witnesses first from a distance, then, as the projector was oriented more vertically at the end of the observation, further away but closer to the source. This explains the reduction in size, less crushed shape, brighter and clearer color (of the source) as the shot nears vertical.

These "laser" (or, more commonly, "skytracker") projectors are often used for advertising purposes, frequently by discothèques in the 1980s and 1990s. GEIPAN has recorded numerous observations resulting from misunderstandings caused by these installations (see www.geipan.fr and case search with the keyword "laser"). In the incident report, the gendarmes mention an article in the press published the following day, suggesting that a "laser lighting test" had been conducted by a discothèque at the same time as the observation. This could not be confirmed as the press article was not provided by the gendarmes, and the owner of the discothèque never presented himself at the gendarmerie despite several summons.

The three witnesses mentioned a red glowing point, smaller than the main shape and accompanying it. In 1995, red lasers already existed, though with limited power and range compared to today. Skytrackers from 1990 are not lasers but conventional light projectors. Laser technology allows for thinner and brighter beams. This could explain the (point-like) impact mark of a laser being tested that day and coupled with a conventional projector. However, there is no evidence to support such an explanation. There is a residual element of strangeness as this has never been observed in GEIPAN's laser witness testimonies.

The case's consistency is poor. Important data, such as azimuth and elevation angle measurements of the UFOs, as well as angular size, are missing. The witnesses did not provide sketches of the UFO, despite requests by the gendarmes during the interviews, which somewhat compromises the reliability assessment of the testimonies. An on-site investigation at the time could have clarified many points, beyond those mentioned above, and possibly supported or ruled out the disco test hypothesis if the manager of the suspected discothèque had been interviewed or visited.

We have a plausible hypothesis due to the UFO's general aspect and a potential explanation regarding the original discothèque. However, the red point noted by the witnesses is a singular feature for this hypothesis, adding a certain level of residual strangeness. Given this, the consistency is poor and too weak to validate a hypothesis with such residual strangeness, and even weaker to attribute an unexplained character if the hypothesis is not retained at all. A conclusion cannot be drawn. Therefore, GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Lack of reliable information.
