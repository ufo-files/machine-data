Summary: Multiple reports of bright phenomena above the horizon in the night sky: brief flashes of certain satellites in the Starlink® constellation due to the setting Sun as observed.
--- seg-afbb4ea46099e653f33bfd8b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: A witness reports in January 2026 having made multiple nocturnal observations of an unexplained phenomenon with friends and colleagues on several occasions. He witnesses this nocturnal phenomenon again on January 12, 2026. White lights, sometimes with a reddish reflection at the bottom, move horizontally with slight vertical variations above the horizon. Their intensity gradually increases and then decreases before disappearing. The environment is clear and urban, without any light source that could explain the observed phenomenon. Only one technical questionnaire (QT) is collected. Similar observations are reported by pilots during nocturnal flights between the Marquesas and Tahiti. Associated photographs with the observation are retrieved by GEIPAN via the Polynesia Cyclone Infos website, without available timestamps. The overall case analysis relies on QT criteria C4 to C10, in addition to non-original photographs retrieved. The description of PAN provided by the witness, combined with the appearance shown in the photographs, indicates that he observed "Starlink flares." More precisely, it involves brief illuminations of certain Starlink® satellites by the setting Sun, as seen by the observer. This conclusion is based on the conditions allowing for the observation of these phenomena, as described in GEIPAN's note "A new recurring observation type of PAN: Starlink (or cluster) flares" published on GEIPAN's website in this investigation note. Characteristics consistent with this hypothesis are: - Shape and brightness: punctual with maximum brightness in the middle of the observation. - Trajectory: multiple objects with crossing trajectories. - Sun's position: setting between 30 and 45 degrees below the horizon for the observer. - Location of the phenomenon: directly in line with the setting Sun at a low elevation < 15°. - Presence of numerous Starlink® satellites in operational orbit according to observation azimuths. The consistency* of this case, according to GEIPAN's criteria, is considered good, despite only one testimony. This is supported by multiple reports accompanied by photographs and videos on Polynesian social media and weather websites on the same date. *According to GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. However, at the time of the observation on January 12, 2026 (at 8:00 PM), the Sun's position below the horizon (>-30°) does not allow for optimal observation conditions that were present between 9:04 PM (-31°) and 10:00 PM (-41°) from Papeete that day, as specified in Stellarium* screen captures in Annex 1. This hypothesis reduces the perceived strangeness by the witness, therefore, GEIPAN classifies this case as "A," observation of Starlink® satellite flares. *Stellarium is a planetarium software.
