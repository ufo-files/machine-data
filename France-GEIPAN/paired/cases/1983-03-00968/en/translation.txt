**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the accuracy of conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named SAINT-RENAN (29) 1983 is part of a recent batch of cases re-examined.

On March 14, 1983, between 8:00 and 10:00 PM, several witnesses in a single hamlet within the commune of Plouarzel (29) observed a yellowish-light phenomenon that appeared stationary above the tip of a megalithic monument. The PAN was perceived as static, then in motion. Intrigued, the witnesses decided to investigate the phenomenon more closely and were surprised to find that it seemed to follow the road and motorists. A fifth witness reported his observation the following day, March 15, at 9:00 PM while driving towards Saint-Renan (29).

Witness T1 arrived around 9:00 PM to alert his fellow witnesses T2, T3, and T4 at their homes. They all initially described their impression of the phenomenon shown by T1 as that of a large star, expressing confusion about the "excitation" of T1.

- T2: "At first, I thought it was a large star, and I initially refused to believe in a OVNI."
- T3: "At first, I saw this bright, yellowish point that seemed 3-4 times larger than a star."
- T4: "My parents, brothers, and I went outside, and Mr. Q pointed out a star in the sky towards northwest. Initially, I found nothing unusual."

The star that T2, T3, and T4 believed they saw directly above the menhir actually existed; it was Venus (272° azimuth and 10° elevation), a large star. The reference to the menhir from T2, T3, and T4's residence location was very precise, leaving no doubt. T1 was very excited by his initial observation while driving. He fell victim to the follower-ball illusion (a perceptual illusion caused by the witnesses' own movement), as the PAN seemed to stop when T1 parked and resumed its course when he started driving again. In reality, the star's direction relative to the car remained fixed when the vehicle didn't change direction. When the car and road changed direction, the star's fixed pivot point relative to the road could give the impression that the star was moving in the sky. T1 stated, "It followed a road, drawing broken lines." This perception generated a strong emotional response (possibly accompanied by the idea or hypothesis of encountering an 'intelligence'), which could lead to a loss of precision in perception or memory.

T1's other observations, both before and after this, are likely due to the same phenomenon. Persuaded of the PAN's movement, T1 successfully convinced T2 to get in the car, leading T2 to the same illusion, enhanced by the effect of suggestion or conditioning created by T1. T2 reported, "It seemed to me that this phenomenon moved from one side of the road to the other." While T3 and T4 remained in the courtyard as T2 and T1 were in the car, they perceived the PAN as stationary upon T1 and T2's return, while T2 (T3 and T4's parent) was now convinced of its movement.

During a second vehicle experience with T2 and T1, T3 and T4 eventually saw the PAN move, but all indications suggest they themselves moved in the courtyard while trying to follow the car's trajectory and the PAN's (assumedly following the car) reaction (T4: "At that moment, while my parents had traveled about 100 meters, I clearly saw the bright point descend into the sky"). T3 also noted, "At one point, I was blocked by a barn and moved to follow the object's trajectory."

The references to a star-like aspect were prominent in the testimonies, and what deviated from this was classic for Venus at a low elevation and/or viewed through a car windshield (changing color, ovalization, varying intensity, and the induced impression of varying distance). The PAN's disappearance indications (T3: "It moved towards Ouessant and I didn't see it again," T4: "It seems to me that this phenomenon descended quite low near the road") perfectly correspond to the local configuration of Venus' setting. The mistake with Venus is beyond doubt. This illustrates:

- A classic mistake due to the follower-ball illusion created by the witness's movement.
- An effect of suggestion on other witnesses, who, despite initially believing they were seeing a star, were led into the same illusion.
- Emotional influence among family members: T3 and T4 adopted the idea and, consequently, the perception of movement once these concepts were acquired and communicated by their parent, T2.

The testimony of T5 is sparsely documented. It likely involved the observation of Arcturus at a 70° azimuth on the same path T5 had followed the previous day, probably overlooked by T5 if it hadn't been for the "media reports of the appearance of a OVNI on March 14 and 15."

The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: astronomical misunderstandings (Venus and Arcturus).
