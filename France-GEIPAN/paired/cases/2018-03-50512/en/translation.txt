Description: On March 31, 2018, between 21:30 and 21:41, a witness from their balcony was intrigued by a bright, intense ball of light with varied colors. The PAN follows a descending trajectory and slowly disappears behind the Notre Dame de La Garde hill. Only one testimony is collected. The witness saw Venus. He uses the following expression: "The most descriptive analogy is as if a star were slowly falling from the sky, in slow motion." Indeed, Venus, low on the horizon, "falls from the sky slowly," as during the observation duration (10 minutes), Venus goes from 2° to 0.5°. It's slow, but when observed at the horizon, the movement is clearly perceptible. Venus (283° azimuth) is indeed slightly to the left of Notre Dame de la Garde (290°) and to the right of Rigel (237°) as the PAN described by the witness. But the witness rules out Venus because he is convinced that Venus is hidden by the hill of Notre Dame de la Garde. And he is wrong. The attached reconstruction shows that in the Venus pointing direction, the highest obstacle is about 60m higher than the base of the witness’s building, which is approximately 50m higher than the witness on the 4th floor. Located at 3900 meters, this point corresponds to an elevation of 0.7°, thus Venus at 2° east is visible, and it is normal that after 10 minutes it has set below the horizon (at 0.5° for an obstacle at 0.7°). The appearance of the PAN is perfectly consistent with Venus low on the horizon, with changing colors due to atmospheric effects. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as: Venus.
