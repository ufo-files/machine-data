Summary: "Abduction" in Cergy-Pontoise.

Description: The names of the various protagonists have been anonymized, as in the case of the official reports. The story that follows had a significant media impact due to the main protagonists who never sought discretion, quite the opposite. The various events that comprise it occurred over a relatively long period of time and in quite diverse locations. The amount of information is therefore significant and was, among other things, the subject of Technical Note No. 6 GEPAN, referenced in the case documents. On November 26, 1979, at approximately 5:00 a.m., the local police station received a phone call from T3 informing them that his "friend had been abducted by a OVNI." A patrol was immediately diverted to the scene. Seven police and gendarmes soon arrived, along with three witnesses, T3, T2, and T1, friends of the missing person and witnesses to the "abduction." The witnesses explained that they were preparing to sell jeans at the nearby market when they observed a light above the road. T1, driving the car, approached the light. It then positioned itself above the car, enveloping it in fog and blinking balls. When the fog cleared, the light "flew away" into the sky. It was then that T3 noticed T1 was no longer in the car. Before the authorities, the three witnesses displayed very different attitudes, ranging from indifference to great excitement. From the initial hearings, discrepancies emerged in the testimonies, and the reconstructions conducted also led to contradictions regarding the witnesses' schedules the previous evening and the morning of the disappearance, the car's position at the time of the events, and the light's trajectory. The Gendarmerie informed GEPAN on November 26, but the press was also notified by one of the witnesses on November 27, and journalists and private investigators flocked from all over the region. By November 28, radios and newspapers reported the case in all its details, with opinions and interpretations multiplying with various interviews. However, no new elements emerged until December 3, 1979, when the mother of T1 informed the Gendarmerie of her son's return and T3 told a radio station about his friend's return. The gendarmes indeed found T1 at T2's place with his family and a journalist. T1 stated that he had ended up in a cabbage field near T2's building. GEPAN will attempt to gather as many objective information as possible on this widely publicized case. It will be extremely difficult for him to do so, as witnesses constantly avoid providing concrete details, preferring the company of journalists and some private investigators with whom they are much more forthcoming. As press conferences and statements progressed, the witnesses' statements changed, and it quickly became evident to the GEPAN's investigators that the reported events were not credible. This hypothesis was strengthened by a blood analysis showing that it was unlikely that the "disappeared T1" had been in zero gravity. The discovery of a row of dried cabbages "damaged by the glowing ball" in the field where T1 reappeared reignited emotion in this small town. The investigation quickly showed that these cabbages were perfectly normal and had been neglected by their owner to serve as fertilizer, which was later confirmed by an analysis of samples taken by GEPAN. Although the witnesses maintained their testimonies, even before the Public Prosecutor, GEPAN considers this to be a hoax, whose narrative undermines the scientific work he is trying to promote.
