**Description:** On August 20, 2014, at 11:05 PM, a witness in a field preparing to put away their drone saw a set of 12 red-orange lights in the night sky. The lights, arranged in a geometric pattern, formed a trapezoidal shape in motion before fading out one by one. Several other people present made this observation. Only one statement was collected. Let's analyze the elements at our disposal: a free-form text statement from a witness describing what they saw and visual elements from other witnesses. It is difficult to distinguish between direct and indirect testimony. There is no GEIPAN questionnaire. The gendarmerie report simply states the receipt of this free-form text. The phenomenon appeared low on the horizon, composed of a dozen (estimated by witnesses) red-orange-red lights "arranged in a geometric pattern forming a kind of trapezoid forming a right triangle at its right end." The considered object was assumed to be immense (about 100 meters), bounded by the lights. Witnesses did not see inside the object, assuming some kind of fog, one of them (not the author of the free-form text) noting "that the stars are invisible through the surface bounded by these glowing points." The weather conditions recorded at "La-Roche-Sur-Yon," approximately 10 km to the east of the observation location, on Météociel, indicated a northwest wind of 7 km/h on average. These witnesses were marked by previous observations, including a misidentification with Jupiter and stars that led to a GEIPAN report in 2009. Other facts not sufficiently documented were classified as C. The description of the phenomenon by the witnesses is compatible with the observation of lantern releases during a summer festive evening. The red-orange color and their successive fading are also characteristic. The description does not explicitly state the direction of movement, but the witnesses' path to follow the PAN allows us to reconstruct it. It is compatible with the direction of the wind (north) measured at 10 km. Finally, it was a summer evening conducive to such festive events. The consistency of the testimony is low. We do not have a gendarmerie investigation or a GEIPAN questionnaire, which is particularly reflected in the absence of a record of movement direction. The description mixes a direct testimony (the author of the text) and reported testimonies that are not usable by GEIPAN. Nevertheless, in view of the typical elements of lanterns, this lack of consistency does not contradict this explanation, as the other option could have been the classification of the testimony as unusable. Note that this testimony describes the joy that witnesses can feel from the sensation of having observed "UFOs," joy all the more intense as it responds to an expectation of seeing them for themselves after years. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as B: observations of Thai lanterns.
