Summary: Two brief observations of the rapid and silent movement of a dark triangular object with fixed lights at the corners: high probability of hoax or lack of sincerity from the witness.
--- seg-42f9888eea4a5b6610a80f7c [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 27, 2014, at 7:54 PM, a witness in their courtyard observed for 2 seconds the rapid northeast-southwest movement of a dark triangular object with fixed navigation lights at its corners. No noise was heard. The witness took two photographs that they found unusable. The witness made the same observation at 11:43 PM, but the PAN's trajectory was in the opposite direction. No other testimony was collected. The case was published on February 23, 2016, and classified C due to a lack of consolidated information and cross-references. It is currently under reconsideration. The witness's sincerity is heavily questioned by several points (see the investigation report):

- A contrast enhancement on both photos (which the witness attributes to the PAN's successive passages on the first night) highlights the presence of a dark mass (which can be attributed to the well-present facade of a building bordering the courtyard opposite the clearly visible chimney on the photo) and what is described as a moving PAN (and identified as such on the photo by the witness) does not move from one photo to the other (as it remains at the same level relative to the dark mass). It is impossible to explain how these fixed lights could have appeared so mobile and fleeting to the witness;

- Initially, the witness found nothing corresponding to their observation in these same photos: "The camera couldn't capture the movement despite its setup. Unusable photos." They then sent them to GEIPAN claiming they showed the presence of __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s without any reservation;

- Neither the dates nor the times of the photos correspond to those of the two PAN passages the witness associates with these photos. The hypothesis of a date and time malfunction of the camera is not strongly supported as the gap between the photos is 3 minutes while the witness provides passage times of the PAN spaced by nearly 3 hours, down to the minute (7:54 PM and 11:43 PM). Such precision rules out the hypothesis of a memory error regarding the passage spacing. The misidentification between photos is also not strongly supported as these photos are explicitly associated with the observation since the witness identifies the PAN on them;

- The witness does not respond to GEIPAN's request to specify the photos' date. The only way to explain these inconsistencies other than deliberate fabrication of evidence would be if the witness, impressed by their visual observation and regretting not finding any trace in the photos, ultimately attributed the peculiarity found in the photos to the PAN. However, these fixed lights would only be visible in photos, not to the naked eye, which is highly unlikely. In any case, such a hypothesis would also highlight the witness's lack of sincerity by their absence of reservations or questions in such a situation, not to mention their non-response to GEIPAN's email requesting clarifications on the dates and the environment where the photos were taken.

This testimony is of average consistency: vague, from a single witness, accompanied by two photographs that do not show the phenomenon, with imprecise shooting conditions. However, the consistency is sufficient to establish the hypothesis of a lack of sincerity from the witness. Consequently, GEIPAN concludes that the available elements do not allow for any physical explanation of the PAN, but they do support the hypothesis that the peculiarity is very likely due to a lack of sincerity from the witness, not on the fact of having seen something strange (no element allows us to exclude this), but in the testimony given about it.

Classification: A: High probability of hoax or witness insincerity.
