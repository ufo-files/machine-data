Summary: Observation of atypical movements of a bright point in the sky: observation of a festive mylar balloon.
--- seg-ae7b6add5637075931ef6a5e [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 27, 2023, around 08:15, the witness, from their home in Brézolles (28), observed a bright point in the sky to the left of the Sun. The object moved along an apparent trajectory from north to south, gradually turning to the right relative to the observer's position. After a brief stop, the PAN resumed its trajectory, continuing to the right, before disappearing. The case consistency is assessed as average. Although complete data were collected regarding the PAN (azimuths, elevations, and apparent size), the case relies on a single testimony with no visual support (photo or video). According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The air traffic restitution by CAPCODA* shows nothing notable except for usual high-altitude commercial activity. The investigation established that the witness very likely observed a balloon (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the description provided by the witness, consistent with the characteristics of a wind-carried balloon briefly reflecting sunlight: round shape, gray color, and comparison with a metallic object. The PAN's trajectory remains compatible with the wind direction measured at moderate altitudes, below 250 meters, as confirmed by detailed data from the high-resolution meteorological model AROME. Furthermore, distance, size, and speed calculations are consistent with the characteristic parameters of a wind-carried festive balloon. The sudden stop, perceived as the main oddity by the witness, could be explained by local and brief variations in wind speed, which are possible at altitude. However, these variations may not be detectable, even with the high-resolution data provided by Météo France's meteorological models. The witness's estimated apparent speed corresponded to an aircraft flying higher than a balloon. Analyses show it also matches the speed of an object carried by the wind at the altitudes considered by the investigation. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A," observation of a festive mylar balloon.

*AROME: Application of Research to Operations at MEsoscale, a model with a very fine mesh (1.3 km mesh) for forecasting in France (METEO France)

*CAPCODA: Center for Air Planning and Conduct of Operations and Air Defense (French Air and Space Force).
