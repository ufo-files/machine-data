Summary: Observation of the movement of a white glowing point with direction change and acceleration: the phenomenon, though not strange, remains unexplained.
--- seg-f36f075915deda341b65aa7d [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 26, 2009, at approximately 10:20 PM, a witness observing the starry sky from their terrace spots a bright white light moving in a linear S-N trajectory at an elevation of 25° to 35°. The witness initially follows the light for 7 to 8 seconds, mistaking it for a satellite. After briefly looking away for one to two seconds, the witness is surprised to no longer see the light on its trajectory. However, they spot a bright light making a turn towards the west for 2 to 3 seconds (in the direction of the Habsheim airport). The light then takes off on a high-speed linear trajectory passing directly overhead. No unusual noises are heard during the 10 to 12-second observation, which ends at 11:48 PM. The witness remains puzzled by the overall trajectory of the light, including a change in direction and the significant acceleration of the phenomenon without any sounds. While nothing confirms that it was a single light source, several hypotheses are ruled out (as detailed in the investigation report): aircraft passage (no radar traces in the Rixsheim area during the 11:30 PM to midnight time slot), celestial bodies (Jupiter was too low on the horizon), and satellite passage (the COSMOS 1151-Rocket passed overhead, but at a 57° elevation). Despite not being particularly strange, the phenomenon remains unexplained. The D1 classification is justified by the uniqueness of the testimony and the impossibility of estimating the distance to the phenomenon.
