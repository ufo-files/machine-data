Summary: Observation of the movement of a white glowing point with direction change and acceleration: the phenomenon, though not strange, remains unexplained.
--- seg-f36f075915deda341b65aa7d [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 26, 2009, around 10:20 PM, a witness on their terrace observed the starry sky. It follows for 7 to 8 seconds the linear S-N movement of a white light point at an elevation of 25° to 35°, which it associates with the movement of a satellite. After briefly (one to two seconds) looking away from the bright point, the witness is surprised to no longer see it on its trajectory. However, he does find a glowing point initiating a turn to the west lasting 2 to 3 seconds (towards the Habsheim airport). This bright point then takes a linear trajectory at high speed passing vertically over the witness. No particular noise was heard during the observation from 10 to 12 seconds and which ended at 23:48. The witness remains intrigued by the overall trajectory of the bright point including a change in direction and the significant acceleration of the phenomenon without any noise being heard. While nothing allows us to affirm that it is the trajectory of a single light point, several hypotheses are ruled out (see the investigation report): passages of planes (no radar trace in the Rixsheim area during the 23:30-00:00 time slot), presence of celestial bodies (Jupiter too low on the horizon), and passage of a satellite (the COSMOS 1151-Rocket passage, but at 57° elevation). The phenomenon, though not strange, remains unexplained. The D1 classification is justified by the uniqueness of the testimony and the impossibility of estimating the distance to the phenomenon.
