Description: On Saturday, December 7, 2019, between 8:40 PM and 8:45 PM, the witness, at home, was alerted by a deep rumble resembling that of several fighter jets. Going to their balcony facing the city center, they briefly spotted a "ball of fire" before it disappeared. During the second intensification of the noise, two additional balls appeared at the same location. These objects were described as yellow with a red border, leaving no trace or trail. Initially, the witness's description and suggestion pointed towards the hypothesis of military aircraft. The National Center for Air Operations, contacted, confirmed the presence of several military aircraft nearby (see the CNOA radar trace map). Two of these aircraft were heading in the direction indicated by the witness between 8:40 PM and 8:42 PM. However, these aircraft were much farther away than reported by the witness and were moving towards Embrun, not Mont Guillaume, making them invisible to the witness. The next hypothesis considered is that of Thai lanterns. The testimony indeed corresponds to the usual characteristics of lanterns: groups of bright points visible for a few seconds to a few minutes, round or slightly oval in shape. The description of a "very bright and very clear" light is also representative of lanterns, whose predominant colors are orange and red, which could correspond to the observation of yellow balls surrounded by red. Furthermore, the time of observation (Saturday night into Sunday) is conducive to the release of lanterns during festive celebrations. The gradual or rapid extinction of lanterns, due to fuel consumption, could explain the disappearance of the observed objects. A crucial element in identifying lanterns is wind. Lanterns are only launched in light winds (around 5 km/h) and move in the direction of the wind. The direction of the objects reported by the witness, "towards Mont Guillaume," corresponds to an orientation of O-NO. Data from four mountain weather stations indicate a very high variability in average ground winds, with speeds ranging from 1.8 to 18 km/h (only measured at 8:00 PM for the Embrun station) to 1 km, 16 km, and 20 km from the observation location. Average ground winds vary from 0 to 7 km/h, which is compatible with light winds. Additionally, the witness mentions a very short portion of sky traversed by the objects and their identical position at the beginning and end of the observation. Given the weakness of the winds, the mountainous and urban area, the direction and wind sense can vary significantly. The apparent inconsistency between certain weather data and the observation might not be significant, and it is not relevant to base oneself solely on the direction of the wind and the geographical context. It should be noted that the short duration of the observation does not allow the witness to determine with precision the exact orientation of the objects. The perceived strangeness by the witness results from the combination of two distinct phenomena: the rumble of fighter jets and the glowing balls. The analysis shows that the noises correspond to fighter jets in maneuver in the region at that time, while the glowing balls are very likely lanterns. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of celestial lanterns.
