Summary: Observation of the movement of orange light phenomena in the sky: probable Thai lanterns.
--- seg-7087d6ebc6e21034ae0aba3d [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On June 28, 2013, at 10:58 PM, a witness observed luminous phenomena moving in a straight line above the Lyon buildings: three orange glowing points forming a smaller triangle were followed by three more orange points also forming a larger triangle. Finally, only a bright point appears. No particular noise was heard during the discontinuous observation of several minutes. The bright points disappear, hidden by the urban landscape. The witness observed and filmed two flying orange light points in formation. This observation was the subject of articles in local press: Progres article. The witness indicates a movement from West to East, but an analysis of the location map and video shows a movement from North to South, or North-Northwest to South-Southeast. The objects therefore came from the North, which is compatible with objects carried by the wind (North wind recorded at Bron according to Meteociel). In another article from Le Progrès on 03/07/2013, a release of lanterns during a private party was reported. According to the weather report, it is not possible that the lanterns released in the La Confluence district (south of Lyon) were what the witness saw that evening. The characteristics of the observed objects: orange balls moving according to the wind, point towards the hypothesis of the passage of Thai lanterns. The stable triangle formation is common for a release of 3 lanterns; it is perfectly normal for the 3 lanterns to remain at equal distance if the wind is stable (which is not always the case). These lanterns could have been launched a few minutes earlier, to the northwest of the witness, probably from the Croix-Rousse district, during one of the multiple private or public parties that have taken place on the last Fridays of June. The GEIPAN did not conduct the research to identify potential perpetrators of the release. This GEIPAN case is classified "B": probable observation of Thai lanterns.
