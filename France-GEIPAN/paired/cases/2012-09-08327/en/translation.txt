Description: On September 14, 2012, at approximately 6:25 a.m., a witness observed for 5 seconds in a clear sky an object of blue color and triangular shape with bright points all around. The stationary object suddenly fell and abruptly disappeared in the sky. No other testimonies were collected. This observation does not resemble common misperceptions:

- The star map reveals no notable stars to the West that could have caused an illusion.
- The hypothesis of a satellite or a group of satellites has been ruled out due to the absence of corresponding objects listed on the Calsky site.
- At this very early hour, the hypothesis of a drone or recreational vehicle seems highly unlikely.
- The description of the phenomenon is far removed from the appearance of an airplane, although it is possible that an airplane was in the witness's field of vision at that time in that direction. The Meteociel report for Toulouse-Blagnac indicates a strong west wind. Above Montgiscard, airplanes approaching for landing at Blagnac align themselves along the North-West axis of the Blagnac runway: an airplane facing the witness, before its turn towards the NW, could have appeared as a group of bright points, only visible before the turn towards the NW (see map). This last hypothesis, on the edge of acceptability, too fragile, is not retained as it is not confirmed by other corroborations (air traffic report, photos, another witness). The GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" due to lack of corroborations.
