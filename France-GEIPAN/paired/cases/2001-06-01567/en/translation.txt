Summary: Likely observation of lightning effects in a cornfield
--- seg-e4f2892146ad874ce01a40ac [needs-review] ---
Description: On June 12, 1981, in the late afternoon, a farm worker observing a field of corn noticed a circular area approximately 20 meters in diameter where the plants appeared to be sick. In the center of the area, several plants had disappeared, and further away, the plants were withered, as if affected by intense heat, with this effect diminishing with distance from the circle's center, disappearing at a distance of about 10 meters. At the center, the soil was cracked, and a depression about 80 cm deep formed, containing five holes about 4-5 cm in diameter and 20 cm deep. The whole scene resembled a trapezoid. An indescribable odor persisted at the center of the circle. Soil and vegetation samples were collected for later analysis. These analyses confirmed that the central area had been subjected to intense heat. Indeed, it had a significantly lower humidity rate than samples taken further away. The temperature experienced by this area was probably above 100 °C. Meteorological data indicated that a severe storm with heavy rain occurred in the night of June 8-9. The lightning hypothesis is favored as it explains the blowing effect that the corn plants in the affected area seemed to have suffered, as well as the temperature increase at the center of the area. The induced currents in the soil by a lightning discharge could also explain the observed holes.
