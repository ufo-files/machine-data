Description: On May 30, 2020, 10 to 20 minutes after the Crew Dragon's takeoff, the witness observed, from their home in Saint-Germain-en-Laye (78), for a maximum of one minute, a high-speed object that appeared to be an airplane or rocket, with a large flame at the rear, traveling in a direction indicated by the witness as west-southwest (questionnaire) and west-east (email of 06/02/2020). The witness thought he saw the Crew Dragon capsule. The launch of Falcon 9 carrying the Crew Dragon capsule took place on May 30, 2020 at 21:22 French time from Cape Canaveral. The first level separated at T0 +2'30'' and the second level at T0+12'. The CNES orbitography service confirmed that the capsule and launcher body were visible from France around 19:45 UTC, and at that time, the setting sun allowed for observation. The capsule's trajectory passed north of Saint-Germain-en-Laye, so it could not have been the observed object. The launcher had already performed its passivation/desorbitation maneuvers when it flew over France as well, so it was impossible to produce a precise orbit, but certain hypotheses did indeed place the second stage of the launcher south of Saint-Germain-en-Laye. This hypothesis that was initially adopted by the GEIPAN: classification A "rocket stage". In addition to the initial report of this case, a reader provided a different analysis, suggesting a commercial airliner. The GEIPAN took into consideration this possibility and resumed the analysis by comparing the two hypotheses, in particular by analyzing the video in more detail to estimate the speed of the PAN. For both hypotheses, the west-southwest movement is consistent with the witness's description. The arguments in favor of this new airplane hypothesis are: - the shape of the object, - the double flash seen in the video when zooming in, which is characteristic of aircraft signaling lights, - the streaks, which are consistent with jet trail marks, - the actual presence of an airplane in the mentioned direction: Boeing 777-243 (ER) flying from London to Rome, as reported by Flightradar. This is the flight AZ209 / AZA 20 LF. However, the time mentioned is a little later than the time given by the witness (21:57 instead of 21:32-21:42), but such a difference can be attributed to a possible imprecision in the testimony and remains compatible with the airplane hypothesis. The speed analysis derived from the video allows the hypotheses to be distinguished. Indeed, thanks to the presence of the moon on the video above the PAN, it is possible to make an approximate calculation of the time it took for the PAN to pass in front of the moon. Calculation of the speed of PAN: The Moon is 0.5° and a calculation based on the video leads to a speed of PAN traversing it in approximately 0.5 seconds. For the 10 km altitude airplane hypothesis:

• Angular velocity = angle in radians * time
• For 360° => 2π x 10,000
• For 0.5° => 2π x 10,000 x 0.5/360 = 87 m
• This corresponds to a speed of 87/0.5 = 174 m/s or 630 km/h: compatible with an airplane. For the 60-70 km altitude launch stage hypothesis, we would obtain a speed of the order of 1050 m/s, too low for a space object, which invalidates the rocket hypothesis in favor of the plane hypothesis. The speed analysis shows that the speed obtained cannot be that of a space object and thus strengthens the airplane hypothesis. So it is very likely that this observation was of an airplane, specifically the Boeing 777 on flight AZ209. The GEIPAN revises its opinion, and the aircraft hypothesis is retained for a Class A classification.
