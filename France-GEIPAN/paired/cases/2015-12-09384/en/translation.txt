Description: On December 30, 2015 at 7:50 a.m., a driver at a roundabout observed for 30 seconds a large red glowing point descending very slowly from a clear sky along a very gentle curved trajectory. The witness parks but will not have time to pull out their phone to take a picture: the phenomenon will have disappeared. No other testimony is collected. The witness is driving in a northwest direction, just before sunrise in the southeast. A red point observed in the opposite direction of the sun, still slightly below the horizon, could be a reflection of the sun on a high-altitude flying object, for example, an airplane. A check on Flightradar indicates an aircraft about 100 km from the witness in this northwest direction, the aircraft is in slow descent. The very good atmospheric visibility, due to the light north wind blowing that day, made this observation possible; see the Nîmes weather report on Météociel. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of the sun's reflection on a high-altitude aircraft. Given that the witness was in transit, the direction of observation of the phenomenon is not precise enough to be certain of this explanation by this specific aircraft. This remains an hypothesis, compatible with the observation report.
