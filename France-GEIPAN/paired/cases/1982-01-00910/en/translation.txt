Summary: Long observations of the slow and silent movement of a hundred glowing objects in the sky: likely observations of festive balloons (sky lanterns).
--- seg-90455972710fa9afa766cd0f [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or explicit changes in classification in an investigation note. This observation case previously classified as D and named NICE (06) 1981 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. On Saturday, December 19, 1981, between 10:30 and 10:50, several witnesses observed between 50 and 100 flying objects very high in the sky. Most of these objects were round, white, and bright, moving in a single formation in the direction of southwest with uneven distances between each object. Their speed was slow, and they did not emit any noise. The last three objects had a different shape, resembling some kind of tube. These last ones changed color, alternating between white and orange. These objects disappeared, hidden by the landscape and clouds. Two witnesses filed reports with the gendarmerie. The description of the PAN given by the witnesses closely resembles a mass release of balloons (see investigation notes). It is worth noting that the observation took place on a Saturday morning, the last day of school before the 1981 Christmas holidays. A week before Christmas, the observation could thus be explained by a mass release of balloons with letters written by students. The trajectory indication, described from north to south, appears reliable as the witnesses point in the same direction and saw the PAN pass near their vertical (which favors the assessment of the direction of movement). This direction is not exactly the same as the wind, which is from north to south. However, we note: - that the wind is measured at sea level while the potential balloons are at least 6 kilometers away and more than 100 meters above (given their small appearance) and could be even higher as they were presumably launched from Nice; - that we are in a city and near mountains (factors favoring local variations). These are elements that can explain why the wind at the level of the PAN has a direction 40° different from that measured in Nice. Furthermore, the speed and duration of the objects forming the PAN are consistent with a weak wind measured at around 7 km/h. The aspect of the colors and lights of the PAN varies among witnesses. T2 remembers different colors between PAN "with round shapes and different colors (yellow-green-red-blue)"; T1 remembers a "bright white color giving a reflection resembling that of light on an aluminum plate." These two descriptions are compatible with balloons of different colors, reflecting the sun strongly (the geometry of sun-balloons-witnesses is favorable to the reflection). T1 distinguishes different PAN (at the end of the formation). The shape is no longer round but that of tubes, and the color of each of them alternates between orange and white. This can be explained by the introduction, into this mass release mainly composed of round balloons, of a few more complex balloons, cylindrical, with two faces, and of different colors. Note that T2 did not distinguish different PAN from the others, except that "overall, some would light up and then go out." On this last point, the entire testimony of both witnesses can be explained by a non-symmetrical shape of (some) balloons, favoring tumbling and thus varying reflection or color according to the reality of the balloons or according to the particularities and precision of the witnesses' perception. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of festive balloons (sky lanterns).
