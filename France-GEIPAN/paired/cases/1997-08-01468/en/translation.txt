Summary: Long-duration observations of a luminous phenomenon that appears to follow one witness during their vehicle journey: astronomical misperception of Jupiter.
--- seg-d2a9d1a865ec9aeb97046fee [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: GEIPAN continues to publish all its archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. Its publications include older cases classified at the time that are now being re-examined solely to improve the pertinence of the conclusions. Thanks to new technical resources (software) and the investigative experience acquired over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This observation is among the cases classified D and was previously named SAINT-ANDRE (974) 1997. On August 15, 1997, around 05:00, a travelling vendor (T1) making his rounds in the municipality of Saint-André observes a stationary luminous object about forty metres above the ground. He subsequently finds that the phenomenon follows him and becomes frightened. On reaching Bras-Panon, he wakes his sister (T2) and her family, and they all see the PAN. Since that date, T2 says she regularly observes the PAN in fine weather, but much farther away. T1 provides no information about the PAN's position; this can only be deduced from T2's statement. The phenomenon described contains strong indications of an astronomical object: - For T1, the features of a "following PAN" are present, a perceptual illusion caused by the witness's own movement when the PAN's origin is very far away, as with a celestial body. The PAN seems to stop when the witness is stationary and resume its journey when he starts again. This perception generates strong emotion, perhaps with the idea or hypothesis of dealing with an "intelligence". - For T2, the PAN is in the same place in the sky at the same time on several days. The account also contains strange features that might appear to oppose the celestial-body hypothesis: T1 perceives the PAN, at least at the beginning, as shaped "almost like a tortoise", "with headlights". T2's perception is different and less strange: "my brother came to wake me around five to half past five and told us to look in the sky, there was a big light. He told us he had seen it close up and that it had passed above his head. He was very frightened when he arrived at the house. My husband, my son and I looked in the direction he showed us (Salazie), and for a very long time we observed a very sparkling light. Sometimes it moved to the right, sometimes to the left; sometimes the light was very strong and sometimes it weakened." Nevertheless, T1 is certain that T2 and her family observed the PAN, saying: "I told them what I had just seen, and they came outside with me, accompanied by their seventeen-year-old son, to observe the object. They all saw it; it did not move from its position. The shape was a little difficult to discern, but the two headlights were very clearly visible. We remained watching it until five fifty, when I left to continue my rounds. The craft never moved from its position. The rest of the family continued watching until the device disappeared. They told me it apparently plunged suddenly behind one of the many cliffs in the Salazie area." For T2, what distinguishes the PAN from a star is that "it is much too big; when looking, there are things like rays around it", and possibly the movement described above. But T2 also specifies: "Since that date, the mysterious thing is always there in fine weather. Around eighteen thirty, when night falls, the bright phenomenon is visible. It is always in the same place, and we observe small coloured lights (red, green, orange) that seem to leave the ball or come from that direction and head towards the sea, and afterwards we no longer see them. We see this thing very high in the sky and cannot distinguish exactly what it is. When T1 came to wake us, we looked at the phenomenon; it was at the same height as on the other days. I could not draw it. I think binoculars would give us a better chance of seeing it and distinguishing what kind of object it is." What T2 describes as present every day at 18:30 and disappearing afterwards is like the 05:00 PAN ("the mysterious thing is always there"), and is therefore towards the west. T2 describes the 05:00 PAN as "in the direction of Salazie", which is westwards at an azimuth of 250° from T2's location. This is unequivocally Venus, which indeed becomes visible at 18:30 low on the horizon before setting. T2's misidentification of Venus is fairly typical (see www.geipan.fr, case search with the keyword Venus), often caused, as here, by its strong luminosity and changing colours as its light passes through the atmosphere at low elevation. T2 describes the PAN seen by T2 and T1 at 05:00 on August 15 as present in the evening at 18:30. Indeed, at 05:00, in the same place in the sky as Venus at 18:30, there is Jupiter: barely 20° separates the two bodies, with similar elevation around 20°. Jupiter shines more brightly than all the stars, almost as brightly as Venus (magnitude -2.8 versus magnitude -3.9), and can show the same changing light (see the reconstruction document). The PAN seen by T1 and T2 at 05:00 is therefore certainly Jupiter. The other particular strangeness described by T2, slight movements of the PAN, is a well-known perceptual phenomenon called autokinesis (see the article about the autokinetic effect). The strongly strange feature described by T1, a tortoise with headlights, has no definitive objective explanation, but is not sufficient to challenge the Jupiter hypothesis given the agreement of the other elements. T1 says that during the joint observation with T2 the shape was difficult to discern, but "the two headlights were very clearly visible", whereas T2 describes only a sparkling light whose glow was strong or could weaken. There may have been thin cloud, causing variations in brightness for T2 and a blurred, changing shape around the light for T1, perhaps less so during the first observation. The double light seen by T1 could result from an optical aberration caused by spectacle lenses; it is not known whether the witness wore glasses. Such aberration is typically caused by vehicle windows and windscreens, which could have operated in the first observation phase and fixed the memory in the second phase as well, unless spectacles were involved in both phases. T1's intense fear, probably linked to the illusion of the following ball and described by T2 ("he was very frightened when he arrived at the house"), could have impaired perception and activated interpretive mechanisms: craft, headlights. This observation is interesting in several respects: - The strangeness that observing a very bright celestial body can create through additional physical or human phenomena: atmospheric separation of colours, shapes created by thin cloud, autokinesis, the following-ball illusion and so on. - Differences between witnesses in perception and experienced strangeness during the same observation. - A possible influence of one perception of strangeness on another. T2 probably found what was seen every evening at 18:30 stranger after her brother's alarm over the 05:00 PAN. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of Jupiter.
