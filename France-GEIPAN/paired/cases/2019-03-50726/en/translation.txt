Summary: Observation of the passage in the early morning sky of a bright white light: certain misidentification with the International Space Station (ISS).
--- seg-3f37b73630d90746588bd8b8 [needs-review] ---
Description: On March 12, 2019, at 06:36, a witness outside their home is intrigued by a light in the sky moving on a straight trajectory without any sound. The PAN is spherical and white in color with a brightness comparable to Venus. The witness mentions seeing a plane pass with a trail and noise at the time of observation. They go back inside to get their camera and take 8 photos. The observation lasts 1 minute and 30 seconds before the PAN disappears as it moves away. No other testimony is collected. With a low strangeness and good consistency (single witness, fairly precise testimony, photos of the PAN), this case turns out to be a certain misidentification with the ISS (see the investigation report). The description and photographs of the PAN strongly evoke this type of misidentification: the observed object is white and shiny, with a brightness comparable to Venus, and the duration of the observation is consistent with that of the ISS. Additionally, the ISS was passing through the sky at the time indicated by the witness and on a trajectory corresponding to that of the PAN. Notably, the observation schedule is without doubt, as the witness was able to observe the passage of a commercial jet above them at the beginning of the observation. A virtual radar reconstruction allows for the identification of this jet and thus confirms the observation time. The case is classified A, certain misidentification with the ISS.
