Summary: Quick observation of the passage of a bright light from a white ball followed by a trail: observation of an atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid.
--- seg-3cef150d1a37c555d48a244b [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 17, 2017, at 7:05 PM, a driver on D 6036 heading towards Saint-Bonnet-du-Gard (30) was surprised to see, for a few seconds, a powerful light passing through the sky from a white ball followed by a trail. No particular noise was heard. No other witness reports will be made to GEIPAN. This very bright phenomenon was observed simultaneously in a large part of eastern France, especially in the northeast (Alsace, Lorraine, Vosges) by numerous witnesses and at least 4 automatic cameras located in Chaligny, Bollwiller, Val d'Ajol, and the Paris Observatory (see attached documents). The east-west trajectory is well described by all witnesses. As often, witnesses have the impression of a phenomenon very close by, while these bolides occur at altitudes of 100 to 40 or 30 km. The characteristics of this bolide (duration, west-bound trajectory) are those of an atmosphere re-entry of a meteoroid, a small rocky body, probably from the asteroid belt. The parameters recorded by the BOAM network cameras in northeastern France allowed the establishment of the following characteristics of the bolide: AllSkyCamFrance "The 'unified' trajectory given by the UFOorbit software resembles that to the north (from Verdun to Reims): Vg (velocity): 14 km/s, duration: 6.1 s, maximum magnitude (from Chaligny): -7, absolute magnitude: -5.5, start > altitude: 87 km, latitude: 49.08°N, longitude: 5.49°E; end > altitude: 38 km, latitude: 49.20°N, longitude: 4.09°E, inclination: 25°, distance traveled: 115 km." The bolide appeared to the witness at a direction of N - NE, at an elevation (above the horizon) calculated to be around 5° to 2° (taking into account the curvature of the Earth), which corresponds well to the witness' schematic drawing. GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the atmospheric re-entry of a meteoroid. This object may have been large enough to produce a meteorite impact in western France.
