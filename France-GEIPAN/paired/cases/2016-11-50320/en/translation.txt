**Case Summary: Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) Observation**

The GEIPAN (Investigator) has concluded the examination of several cases pending classification. He publishes a synthesis with available data from both witness testimonies and investigations at the time (see attached document). A witness and his son, driving around 5:15 PM, observed an unusual "long and pointed sky phenomenon" with their binoculars. The witness stopped and observed the PAN with 8x25 binoculars, but no further details were visible. Upon arriving home, the witness attempted to locate the PAN with 12x50 binoculars and found two static, triangular objects, but darker this time. The objects were eventually obscured by clouds around 6:00 PM.

The witness provided a detailed account of his observation to the GEIPAN. Only one testimony was collected on these phenomena. The witness seems to describe two distinct types of __UFO_PROTECTED_001__s:

1. "A... unusual light phenomenon (long and pointed towards the sky) to the southwest (from Cholet) at about 20° from the ground and approximately 1 degree high."
2. "B... two invisible to the naked eye (apparently static) triangular objects, dark this time (I believe these are the same as the first, farther away, with a different orientation, i.e., not facing the sun)."

The second phase includes a reconstructed photograph depicting the scene, showing the two __UFO_PROTECTED_001__s as inverted "V" shapes with a point at their base, positioned slightly offset in height and azimuth (see attached document). The witness stated that the first PAN was white and "blindingly bright" (like a streetlamp in broad daylight), while the second was darker. No movement of these objects was observed by the witness.

In response to the question about trails or halos, the witness interestingly noted, "The two descending branches of the triangle resembled airplane trails (but usually, they are parallel, and an airplane always evolves in the sky, even if it's first on the horizon)." The witness thus considered simple airplane trails but dismissed the hypothesis based on his knowledge of these phenomena. However, airplane trails are not always parallel. In a very stable atmosphere (little or no wind, low humidity), they can be very short and, depending on the number (bi-jet), the arrangement of the aircraft's engines, and the turbulence they generate, they can diverge as they exit the fuselage (see example #4 in the attached document). The dark color likely resembles example #15 in the document. It is typical of trail observations during twilight: the sun has been down for a few minutes, the sky is still clear, and according to the position of the terminator (a fictional line marking the boundary between the sunlit and shadowed areas of the Earth), these trails can take on colors ranging from white to gray/black, through yellow, orange, and even red.

The first PAN observed earlier (5:15 PM) was very likely a persistent condensation trail from an airplane passing through a temporarily wetter area at high altitude, still well-lit by the sun, which, observed from Cholet, set three minutes later at 5:18 PM on November 24, 2016. The contrast with surrounding darker clouds gives the impression of "blinding brightness." It's possible that one of the later observed trails came from the same airplane as the first, though it's difficult to prove.

The immobility of these __UFO_PROTECTED_001__s can be explained by two factors:
- For the first one, since it's likely a persistent condensation trail, for as long as wind at high altitude remains light, it can persist in place or almost, for a relatively long time.
- For the seconds, two parameters come into play:
   a. The involved airplanes are probably very far and high (the witness stated the __UFO_PROTECTED_001__s were invisible to the naked eye and low on the horizon), so their movement is apparently slow and may be imperceptible, at least over a short time and in a clear sky, which wasn't the case here.
   b. The witness mentioned that many clouds were present and moving quickly (so relatively low), which prevents the witness from perceiving the slow movement of the airplanes and their trails, which are also regularly obscured.

The only seemingly contradictory element, at least for the second __UFO_PROTECTED_001__s, is the duration of observation, as the witness stated, "continuously from 5:30 PM to 6:00 PM." Given that they were observed from very far and low on the horizon, it seems challenging to envision airplanes being observed for so long, especially without the witness eventually perceiving their movement. At 5:30 PM, the sun was at 1°36 below the horizon and at an azimuth of 241°, i.e., southwest, just like the __UFO_PROTECTED_001__s (verified from the photo provided by the witness). Therefore, situated in the same direction as the sun while being very far from the witness, the condensation trails from the implicated airplanes should, in all logic, still be illuminated by the sun and exhibit a white or orange coloration, not a dark one if they were observed as early as 5:30 PM.

For these trails to appear dark, it's essential they are no longer illuminated by the sun, especially if they are in the same azimuth as it. Thus, the only possibility is that the beginning of observation of the last two __UFO_PROTECTED_001__s started much later than 5:30 PM; it could thus have been shorter and more compatible with the hypothesis.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B: Misidentification with Airplane Condensation Trails."
