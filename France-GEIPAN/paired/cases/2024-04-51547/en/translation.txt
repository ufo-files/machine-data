Summary: Observation of the passage of a blue streak in the night sky: probable atmospheric reentry of a space debris.
--- seg-75aeb822c1cae234b5a7e573 [needs-review] ---
Description: On August 4, 2024, at 11:23 PM, a witness on a beach in Saint-Michel-en-Grève (22) observed a PAN in the form of a short, blue streak moving from northwest to southeast. The witness took the time to capture a 30-second exposure photograph, clearly showing the object. The case consistency is good, with only one witness, but the photograph of the PAN was fully exploitable for the investigation, which explored two hypotheses: the atmospheric reentry of a meteor or a space debris. The appearance of the PAN, a short, blue streak moving straight across the sky, is entirely consistent with these two hypotheses (see the investigation report). However, calculations and measurements made on the photograph using the specialized software IPACO* estimate that, for a transverse movement relative to the witness, the PAN had a velocity of approximately 3 km/s, meaning a precise speed and direction of movement. This value is significantly lower than the characteristic speeds of a natural atmospheric reentry, ruling out that hypothesis. It is therefore more likely the atmospheric reentry of a manufactured space object, probably a debris burning in the dense layers of the atmosphere. The Operational Center for Space Surveillance of the CNES was questioned about the possibility of detecting such debris, but the filters used only detect and list debris with a friction surface greater than 10 m² (and estimated mass > 2.5t); a sufficiently small debris could therefore have gone unlisted. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable atmospheric reentry of a space debris.
