Summary: Observation of the movement of five orange light sources, then a slightly opalescent and transparent ovoid phenomenon: probable observation of Thai lanterns followed by a reflection on a satellite.
--- seg-dfeccf6f95fedd0861245695 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 14, 2013, around 11:45 PM, a witness observed the movements in the Toulouse sky of 5 orange light sources. The witness then made a second observation of the movement of an oval, slightly opalescent, and transparent phenomenon that detached from the dark background of the sky. The beginning of the observation (orange light sources moving in a straight line) closely matches the observation of Thai lanterns flying from West to East with the light West wind (see Meteociel record). The date of July 14 is, of course, conducive to this type of event. The 2nd phenomenon rather resembles a reflection of the sun on a satellite. The Calsky site indicates at this very moment a reflection of this type on satellite Iridium 70, but observable in the northeast, quite low on the horizon, which does not correspond well to the testimony. It could also be a reflection on another unrecorded satellite. The coincidence between these two independent events is obviously surprising, but not impossible. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of Thai lanterns, then a reflection on a satellite.
