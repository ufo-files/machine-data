Description: On July 29, 2024, at approximately 6:30 a.m., two witnesses were outdoors in the commune of Échirolles (38). They observed to the west a white glowing point moving at a slow and steady speed, following a south-north trajectory at a constant elevation of around 60°. No noise was perceived during the observation. After 5 minutes, the phenomenon disappeared towards the northwest, obscured by the surrounding vegetation. The consistency was deemed satisfactory, although only one of the two witnesses completed a questionnaire. A 44-second video of the phenomenon, captured by T1, was recovered and analyzed for the investigation.

According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Two hypotheses were considered: that of an airplane and that of a balloon, more specifically, a cluster of balloons. The airplane hypothesis, which initially presented similarities with PAN (elongated shape resembling a fuselage, common white color, steady and straight-line movement), proved implausible, mainly due to the absence of such an aircraft on radar maps. The cluster of balloon hypothesis is more plausible, based on the following elements:

- Overall elongated shape and presence of elements within this shape resembling individual round balloons.
- White color, common for balloons, and bright, as they were illuminated by the rising sun.
- Slow, steady, and straight-line movement, consistent with the behavior of wind-carried balloons.
- Measured and calculated data from the video are compatible for a set of wind-carried balloons, approximately 80 cm to 1 meter long, located between about 40 meters and 70 meters from the witnesses.

Wind data show it was highly variable, with a shear phenomenon at all levels between ground level and 500 meters altitude, and across a wide sector in the direction of observation. This phenomenon is visible in the fine-mesh AROME* data restitution, which model can sometimes differ from real-time data, possibly the case here. Therefore, the orientation of the south-to-north wind in the balloons' movement sector is possible but not demonstrable.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a cluster of balloons.

AROME (Application of Research to Operations at MEsoscale) is a model with a very fine mesh (1.3 km mesh) for forecasting in France (METEO France).
