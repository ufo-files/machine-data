Summary: Brief observation of a bright light appearing to come from a grey, elongated shape in the Paris sky: likely observation of sunlight reflecting off an aircraft.
--- seg-fd95c085ed970117541c1f79 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Saturday, May 20, 2017, between 8:00 and 9:00 PM, a witness was intrigued by a bright point in the Paris sky. Viewed through binoculars, the light seemed to emanate from an oblong, gray-silver shape. The object appeared stationary during the 15 seconds of observation. Only one testimony was collected. It is likely the witness saw an airplane in a fleeting condition of mirror reflection from the sun. The witness initially thought it was an airplane, stating, "The light intensity suggested the approach of an airplane from the front, its beacon pointing in my direction, but the bright point seemed stationary."

"Stationary, it seemed solid, evoking a metallic body." The airplane was observed at an elevation of 20° or 30°, possibly 20 or 30 kilometers away; the sky was indeed clear. In 15 seconds of observation, its movement was approximately 3 kilometers, which for a transverse movement to the line of sight, represents an angular change of 6 to 9° (depending on the distance), not easily detectable.

The witness reported the observation with binoculars: "The light came from an oblong, gray-silver shape. The brightness and color resembled those of the moon in broad daylight. Certain areas of the shape were darker than others, notably a small circular part." The sun was in the west-northwest, on the horizon. Many airplanes were flying southeast. It's possible one of them had its nose positioned in an exact mirror reflection of the sun towards the witness.

The precise geometric conditions linking the sun's direction, the witness's position, the airplane's position, and its heading resulted in a strong light intensity (only a single bright point to the naked eye because the airplane is far away) that was inherently fleeting: a particular airplane and a brief moment. The view through binoculars allowed for the precise content of the bright point (it was indeed not a star), and the oblong shape then visible ("Stationary, it seemed solid, evoking a metallic body") came from the fuselage, while the darker circular part likely came from the wing (a side view towards the witness) and its attachment to the fuselage: the whole constituted a distinct geometric body from the fuselage, not meeting reflection conditions, and superimposed on the oblong shape. This type of misperception with an airplane is not unusual.

Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Airplane observation.
