Description: On December 5, 2015 at 17:55, a motorist and his son observed for 1 minute a stationary yellow-orange phenomenon as bright as Venus in the sky. No particular noise is heard. The witness turns his head away and when he looks back again, the phenomenon is gone. The observation of a large bright point in the sky for one minute can have several origins:

- Reflection of the sun on a satellite, Iridium, or other: a check on the Calsky site indicates no candidate satellite.
- Reflection of the sun on the fuselage or wings of an airplane: at this time, when the sun is less than 7° below the horizon, the airplane would need to be above 15,000m to still be in sunlight.
- An airplane seen face-on, headlights illuminated: a check on the Flightradar site indicates that an airplane taking off from Carcassonne passes just within the observation axis of the witnesses at this exact time. This aircraft likely took off with its headlights on, and the pilot would have turned them off once reaching a sufficient altitude, or the witnesses were outside the light cone of the aircraft's headlights. In the face of the witnesses, who were also moving in their vehicle, this bright point appeared stationary for one minute. After the headlights went out, or the witnesses emerged from the light cone, the navigation lights, much less powerful, in a still somewhat bright sky, could have escaped the witnesses' gaze who were looking for a powerful light. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the lights of flight RYR 1761.
