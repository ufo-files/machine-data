Summary: Multiple reports of light phenomena by a pilot during a flight over the Mediterranean: observation of Starlink satellite flashes in operational orbit.
--- seg-296f95e2843433e61f235442 [needs-review] ---
Description: During the night of April 9 to 10, 2023, the pilot of flight AFR from Kinshasa to Paris observed the movements of bright points in a rather random manner with variations in intensity alternating between phases of immobility and "sudden movement," at a speed that could be "that of a satellite." This observation starts at 1:30 (UTC) until approximately 2:15 (UTC). The maximum intensity (at the level of Venus) is reached at the beginning of the observation primarily on 2 "objects" that appear to orbit around each other while maintaining the same azimuth relative to the plane. Then their intensity decreases before their disappearance and reappearance after one to two minutes, followed by "non-straightline" movements that are roughly identical. In all, approximately 4 to 5 other objects behave in the same way, but with much lower light intensity, similar to the surrounding stars. Several hypotheses have been raised following the description of the PAN (see the investigation report): - Maneuvers of military aircraft over the Mediterranean (near Sardinia or Corsica) that could not be confirmed. - Observation of sunlit satellites. For this hypothesis, the precise analysis of the PAN's position relative to the stars places it at approximately 45° above the position of the Sun, which was then between 45 and 30° below the horizon during the entire observation. This demonstrates perfectly favorable conditions for a new type of PAN observation, namely, an observation of Starlink satellites on their operational orbit, briefly made visible by the specular reflection of the Sun on their frames, as indicated in the following literature, and illustrated for example in the web article starlink-satellites-flaring-in-cassiopeia. We were then able to validate this hypothesis thanks to the active collaboration of the witness, who observed the PAN again during another flight, in accordance with the observability conditions predicted by the GEIPAN. The case consistency is judged good due to the presence of several witnesses and a complete and precise testimony. The witness, very cooperative during the investigation, was able to see the PAN during another flight thanks to the observability conditions predicted by the GEIPAN, thus formally validating the hypothesis and strengthening the consistency of the case. This observation confirmed that the witnesses encountered operational Starlink satellites in orbit, briefly illuminated by the Sun (Starlink "flare" phenomenon). The case is classified A, observation of Starlink satellite flashes in operational orbit. This observation presents very great similarities with other cases investigated by the GEIPAN and published on the GEIPAN website. [AERO AFR] from ABIDJAN (CI.AB) to PARIS (95) on 04/08/2023 [AERO TVF] from AMMAN (JO.AM) to ORLY (94) on 01/31/2023 [AERO TVF] from EREVAN (AM.ER) to ORLY (94) on 12/13/2023 These four cases form the basis for an investigation technical note and a publication in the News section.
