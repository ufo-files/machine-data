Description: On September 6, 2015, at approximately 10:40 a.m., a driver was surprised by a shadow that seemed to block out the sun. While driving and leaning forward, the driver noticed, through the windshield, a triangular shape slightly curved on the sides with a brighter circle at each end. The whole object was a matte black color. The front passenger, alerted, opened the window but saw nothing. The driver continued on her way. A little further, both witnesses saw the phenomenon move away in the direction of Breteville (50). No noise was heard, and no lights were seen. Only one testimony was collected. The plane hypothesis is the most likely (see the investigation report). Indeed, on September 6, 2015, there was an event 13 kilometers further west. It was to honor the World War I pilots, and two French Rafale military planes flew over the Etretat Bay around 10:30 a.m. It is quite possible that one of these planes flew directly over the observation site and was the source of the phenomenon. The witness's description of the plane could suggest a Rafale, with differences that may result from difficult observation conditions (the main observation was quick as it was done by a driver just before a roundabout). The absence of noise most surprised the two witnesses, and the fact that apparently no one else observed this phenomenon—we were on a Sunday morning, there were walkers, other vehicles... But under certain conditions, one may not hear the noise of a plane (on this subject, see the excellent study by Gaelle Fedoce), and the GEIPAN also knows from experience that a "visual surprise" can mask or inhibit sound perception. The fact that there were no other testimonies actually supports the plane hypothesis. Other people could have observed the same thing as the two witnesses in better conditions and recognized a plane, in which case there would have been no need to testify. Without radar data for this observation and the exact trajectories of these two Rafales, we cannot definitively validate the hypothesis that a Rafale flew over the witnesses' car. The second hypothesis, that a civilian or military drone in the area was responsible, is less likely (too rapid angular change). Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable misperception with an airplane.
