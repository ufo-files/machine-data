Description: On July 2, 2019, witnesses observed a stormy sky around 10:00 PM. Suddenly, their attention was drawn to a bright, red-colored light moving rapidly, disappearing behind the mountains. The same bright light reappeared several times quickly; one witness was able to take photographs with a strong zoom. Only one testimony with photographs was collected. The witness stated, "Our attention was drawn to a bright, red-colored light that was clearly moving rapidly...I maximized the zoom, which gave me, thanks to the smart zoom (crop in the sensor), a magnification of 1475mm, and I obtained fairly good quality shots that I am attaching." The strangeness felt by the witness comes from observing the zoomed photos, even though the bright point of light was also observed with the naked eye. Thus, the analysis of this observation is based on the analysis of the photos (see the attached photo analysis document):

- Photos # P1010510.JPG, P1010511.JPG et P1010512.JPG: The camera clearly failed to focus for these three images, which exhibit a very strong blur. The orange spot seen is the strongly defocused phenomenon, generating a circular spot resembling bokeh, a voluntary artistic blur on more or less punctual light sources: in their current state, these three images are totally unusable.

- Photo #P1010513.JPG: Focus is slightly better here, allowing the distinction, after enhancement, of tree branches in the foreground on the right and below, as well as a cotton-like, irregular, reddish vertical shape that helps orient us towards an explanatory track: red distress rockets equipped with parachutes. The red-orange color, the witness's description, and the behavior align very well with this hypothesis. These rockets light up once in the air, which explains why the witness observed two appearing on the spot. They can then move upwards and eventually descend due to gravity. They can also sometimes malfunction or not stay lit for long, which explains why two were observed motionless, probably for a very short period (a few seconds at most). On this photo, the rocket has finished its ascent, marked by the pyrotechnic smoke trail on its left, distorted by the local wind, and has begun its descent. It is strictly prohibited to use these rockets outside of genuine distress cases, as it unnecessarily monopolizes significant rescue resources. However, pranksters do not hesitate to launch them for festive occasions... It is also possible that these are military illumination rockets, also equipped with parachutes, that military use during night exercises to illuminate the terrain, although the red color in this context is rare. Nevertheless, no military exercise was identified in this case.

- Photo #P1010514: Although less pronounced than for the first three photos, a focus blur is also present here. The presence of tree branches in the foreground (part of which can be seen in the defocusing disks) explains that the camera focused on these branches and not on the PAN in the background. This image is unusable.

In conclusion: The red-orange color, the witness's description, and the behavior of the __UFO_PROTECTED_003__s align very well with the hypothesis of illumination rockets. The strangeness of the photos taken is explained by the zoom factor used. However, as we were unable to identify the origin of the rocket launches, this hypothesis remains probable but not certain. The GEIPAN classifies this observation as B: probable misperception with illumination rockets.
