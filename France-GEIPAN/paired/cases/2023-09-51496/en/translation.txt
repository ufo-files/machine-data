Summary: Observations of the silent passage across the sky of a dark PAN composed of several shapes, one of which had a gap revealing the sky: probable observation of balloons.
--- seg-617c29b9d81b25347edac2de [needs-review] ---
Description: On September 6, 2023, around 7:40 a.m., a witness (T1) and his spouse (T2) observed from a roof window on the upper floor of their residence in Châteaugiron (35) a PAN. T1 describes it as a brown double rectangle, while T2 interprets it as two solid ellipses, one with a hole revealing the sky (see witness sketches in Annex 1). The PAN moves slowly, without making any noise, from northwest to southwest. The total duration of the observation is estimated to be approximately three minutes. T1 manages to photograph the phenomenon as it departs (annex 2). Both witnesses complete a Technical Questionnaire (QT) and submit it to GEIPAN in September and November 2023, respectively. On November 25, 2023, a field investigation is conducted, including cognitive interviews with both witnesses. These individuals confirm the information gathered through questionnaires with no contradictions. In view of the shape of the PAN, only the hypothesis of a set of 2 balloons carried by the wind was studied: - shape: two ellipses, horizontal and vertical. The hypothesis of two "O"-shaped balloons, intertwined, is consistent (see T2 drawing, very evocative). - The dark brown color: non-determining, all colors are possible. - The size of the PAN estimated by witnesses at arm's length is approximately 3 cm. It turns out that at least a twofold overestimation must have been reported (test by witnesses of the angular size of the Moon). Starting from an apparent size of 1.5 cm, we obtain a size of the order of 1 meter to 53 meters at a distance, which is plausible for a set of 2 balloons. - The linear trajectory with oscillations is entirely consistent with balloons carried by the wind; the geometry of the set may be unstable due to their dissymmetric shape, leading to oscillations. - The Rennes-St-Jacques station, the closest one, gives a weak north-northwest wind, which is consistent with the movement of PAN; the Arbrissel station, a bit farther away, gives a west wind (annex 3, weather with UTC hours). - The elevation is not decisive, but its constancy is consistent with that of balloons evolving along a straight trajectory remaining parallel to the ground. - The apparent speed is regular and relatively low, which is consistent with that of a passive object carried by the wind. The consistency* of this observation is good with 2 witnesses and a photo. However, it could not be investigated. Additionally, a field investigation accompanied by a cognitive interview of the 2 witnesses was conducted. Despite an unusual appearance, perceived as strange by witnesses, the accepted hypothesis is a set of 2 interlinked balloons carried by the wind, however, there remains uncertainty regarding its exact speed and direction of movement. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of balloons. According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.
