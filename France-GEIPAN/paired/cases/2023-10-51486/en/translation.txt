Summary: Multiple reports of fast-moving light points on different trajectories: observation of Starlink® satellite flashes.
--- seg-140e7a5aff12161f2f96160e [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The main witness (T1) undertakes a flight between Santiago de Compostela (SCQ) and Edinburgh (EDI) on October 24, 2023. During the cruising phase, west of the French coast, in the presence of the copilot (T2), they observe an unusual nocturnal phenomenon, without equivalent in their aeronautic experience: bright points appear and disappear, moving in varied ways, on straight or slightly curved trajectories. During another flight the following day on a similar trajectory, T1 reports an identical observation. Only one testimony has been collected to date, of low strangeness and good consistency (multiple witnesses, photos of PAN). This case turns out to be an observation of Starlink® satellite flashes (see the investigation report). According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The description and visual aspect of PAN are consistent with this type of observation: bright points with variations in brightness and apparently random trajectories, consistently located in the same region of the sky. An analysis performed using the Stellarium software, after identifying visible stars on photos of PAN, confirms that it is positioned vertically to the apparent position of the Sun, while the latter is located between 30° and 45° below the horizon. This configuration corresponds to favorable conditions for observing Starlink® satellites in orbit, as detailed in a specific news item published on the website of GEIPAN and which can be found precisely on the page: New phenomenon explained: Starlink satellite flashes. The case is classified as "A", observation of Starlink satellite flashes.
