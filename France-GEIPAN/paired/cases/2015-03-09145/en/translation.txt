Summary: Brief observation by a witness on a scooter of an oval-shaped object with different-colored lights: lack of reliable information.
--- seg-a271cc71cbea152a61c3c460 [needs-review] ---
Description: On March 10, 2015 at 4:30 a.m., a witness was riding a scooter on a rural road. He sees through the trees and over the fields lights that he takes for wind turbine lights. As it approached, it then distinguished for 4 to 5 seconds a black oval object with colored lights alternating between yellow, orange, green, blue, and red. The object appears static above a livestock feeder. When the witness reaches the end of the tree alley and faces the object, it disappears instantly. No noise, smoke, or odor was noticed. Only one testimony is collected. Several hypotheses have been considered to try to explain this oval shape with multicolored lights: wind turbines located 2km away, a drone (a common practice in the village), or a model aircraft (the existence of a club a few kilometers away). None of these hypotheses can be retained (see the investigation report). The consistency is correct in terms of the level of information. On the other hand, it is weak in terms of the reliability that can be attributed to the testimony: - indeed, what is striking is the strong coincidence between the testimony of T1 (18 years old) and that of the two younger siblings (12 and 7 years old) for another observation made 6 weeks earlier in the same commune. See the case LOUDEAC (22) 01/31/2015. The common points are: the black oval, the yellow, blue, green, and red lights, the dimensions, and in particular the extremely close detailed drawings; - the GEIPAN investigation then concluded to a probable misidentification with a neighbor's drone, since that neighbor used a drone with multicolored diodes and acknowledged that the observation was likely his. If the date and flight location could have been confirmed, the case would have been classified as A, not B; here, the drone hypothesis faces two major obstacles not present in the previous observation: proximity and large angular size, instant disappearance; - the possibility of a drone at night in a rural area is suspicious but not excluded, as the pilot (see the investigation report of the previous case) practices immersion (remote flight outside visibility). If that is the case, our witness could not have perceived the drone at such close proximity and disappearance; - the other possibility to consider is that of a contamination between witnesses within the same family. During the observation and on-site investigation, the witness is not yet informed of the conclusion of the previous case. He is, of course, familiar with the elements described and drawn by his siblings, and he knows that an GEIPAN investigation has already been conducted on the matter within his family. He could thus have appropriated the presence at the site of this mysterious phenomenon that motivated the movement of several official and national GEIPAN "experts" and entered a state of high alert. This leads to attributing (consciously or not) the characteristics of this phenomenon to any other oddity encountered, whether it is anecdotal (and perhaps unnoticed without this context of awakening) or real and even strong; without being able to provide elements in one way or another, one cannot rule out the extreme of conscious contamination, namely the hoax: the witness would then have seen nothing strange but still wants his observation and investigation, like his brothers and sisters. To explain such a coincidence, there are no other possibilities than the actual return of the drone with significant errors in the testimonies or (conscious or not) contamination by the previous observation. In both cases, this highlights a lack of reliability in the testimony, without discrediting the witness who is young (18 years old) and may have been placed, individually or collectively with their family, in a heightened state of concern and vigilance due to the previous, unexplained observation. In the end, we have no hypothesis that can explain this observation, but the consistency is too weak to give an unexplained character. The GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
