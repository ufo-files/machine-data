Summary: Observation of the movement of a luminous phenomenon in the night sky: certain observation of the star Capella, accompanied by an illusion of movement and a physiological effect of micro-nystagmus.
--- seg-c3fa0d0da7d4f8485e2a2b40 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On March 7, 2015, around 1:35 AM, a driver and his passenger were intrigued by a glowing object moving across the sky. The phenomenon was first spotted while driving on D37, and the driver stopped on the emergency lane, observing the object for 3 to 4 minutes. The passenger took a photograph with a camera. No other witnesses were interviewed. The driver initially reported observing the PAN on his left while driving straight, and that the PAN moved (to the right) when he took a turn (to the left). This well-known phenomenon is a perceptual illusion created by the movement of observers watching a stationary object that is far away and lacks references, especially at night. In fact, the object does not move at all, but it appears to do so based on the movements of the vehicle. This is confirmed when the witnesses decide to stop on the side of the road to observe better. The PAN "freezes" because the witnesses are no longer moving. Based on the information gathered in the investigation report (attached document), namely: - its shape, color, brightness, and apparent size - its identical azimuth and altitude - the proven illusion of movement - the proven "micro-nystagmus" effect - the probable disappearance behind clouds, we can conclude that the case involves the certain observation of the star Capella, accompanied by an illusion of movement and a physiological micro-nystagmus effect. The star is likely obscured by a cloud bank. This case should be classified as "A" - observation of the star Capella. One might be surprised by the significant distortion between the witnesses' accounts and the trivial explanation given. The key to this misunderstanding lies in the incorrect estimation of the distance to the phenomenon: as it appears above the water tower, it is immediately interpreted as an object close to the ground; its apparent movement across the horizon is then perceived as a real movement. Finally, the variations in light intensity, probably due to an invisible cloud veil, are interpreted as distances or approaches.
