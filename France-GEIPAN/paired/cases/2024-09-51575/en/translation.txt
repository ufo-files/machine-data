Description: On September 9, 2024, around 23:00, a witness (T1) is standing on the porch of their home in Ambres (81) when they spot in the sky, heading northeast, an intense bright point emitting a beam of white light. The PAN is moving in a straight line towards them, initially resembling the behavior of a conventional aircraft. As the PAN approaches, T1 distinguishes, on each side of the main beam, a red light and a blue light, as well as a white lighting at the rear, positioned in a way reminiscent of that on the vertical stabilizer of an aircraft. As the PAN flies overhead, T1 observes a white glowing rectangle within which several lights of different colors flicker. Determined to extend the observation, the witness changes position. He then perceives a distinct engine noise characterized by a gentle and continuous vibration. The PAN continues its trajectory at a constant speed until it disappears on the horizon. The witness's spouse (T2) steps outside to observe the PAN, but it is already too distant for detailed observation. The case consistency is low: although there are 2 witnesses, only T1 agreed to testify and no photos of the PAN were taken. The sole testimony contains a dating error and an imprecision regarding the time and duration of the observation (see the investigation report). *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation concludes that the witnesses observed an aircraft of an airliner type during its approach to Toulouse Airport, based on the following elements:

- Standard aeronautical lights of red, white, and green (T1 mentions blue, but this color is sometimes confused with green), located in the usual positions for aircraft.
- The initial powerful white light beam likely corresponds to landing lights.
- The observation duration of 30 to 40 seconds is consistent with observing an aircraft crossing the witness's sky at a normal speed and altitude for an approach phase.
- Consulting air navigation data confirms the presence of an aircraft corresponding to Lufthansa flight DLH23U, in approach to Toulouse Airport. Its trajectory is perfectly compatible with that described for the PAN. The craft overflies the T1 witness sector at 23:16, one minute after the end of observation time mentioned in the testimony questionnaire, which is also indicated roughly. - The noise perceived by T1, a "gentle blowing sound of an engine," is consistent with that of an aircraft flying at an altitude of approximately 2000 meters. - The T1 testimony contains numerous explicit references to an aircraft, suggesting that the observation does not present an unusual character until a specific moment when the PAN flies over its position at very low altitude. At that moment, T1 describes an intense bright area - compared to a 'pool of light' - containing flickering shades of pink, purple, green, and orange, located vertically above the witness, at the location of the beam initially perceived as a 'lighthouse'. It is likely that this description corresponds to the observation of the structure supporting the landing lights, possibly located on the front train, or a portion of the fuselage partially obscured by the intensity of the lighting. The identified aircraft was flying at an altitude of approximately 2,000 meters when it passed directly overhead T1, which significantly limits the ability to visually identify fine structural details. The perceived presence of flashing lights of different colors in this area remains difficult to interpret with certainty. However, a plausible hypothesis is that of an optical effect related to the powerful lighting of the headlights, producing colored reflections on particles suspended in the atmosphere. The GEIPAN classifies the case as 'A', observation of a commercial aircraft.
