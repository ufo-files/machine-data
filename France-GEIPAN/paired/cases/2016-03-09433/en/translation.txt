Summary: An observation in the sky of a bright point followed by a black shape seemingly releasing matter: observation of a commercial aircraft and intermittent condensation trails.
--- seg-1662ea7ad062989849084231 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On March 29, 2017, at 7:19 a.m., a driver on the A34 highway heading towards Charleville-Mezières witnessed a peculiar phenomenon in the clear sky. The witness was surprised by a bright point, followed by the observation of a black shape that could be an airplane. The object appeared static and seemed to discharge a material, possibly a mixture of smoke and water. The witness approached the observed craft and took photos and a video. He then noticed a triangular shape and two large wings. A red light was present at the center of the object, which eventually disappeared from the witness's view. The observed object was identified as an A380 airplane under specific conditions (Flight Number: SQ336 -SQ336 9V-SKF - Call Sign: SIA336 - Airbus A380-841 - from Singapore to CDG): see the attached investigation report. The strangeness caused by the stability or low speed of the PAN (at least during part of the observation) results from a pivot effect: as the witness and the airplane move in directions with opposite components, the line of sight between the witness and the PAN tends to pivot around a fixed point where the witness, who cannot assess the distance evolution, believes the PAN to be quasi-stationary. The witness thought that material was pouring off the PAN, but in reality it was intermittent condensation trails as the airplane climbed and encountered variable conditions for trail formation. We invite you to discover videos of A380 condensation trails by following the internet links on page 18 of the investigation report. Another oddity resulted from the variable brightness in intensity and color of the PAN. The airplane reflected the sunlight back to the witness (which was not visible to the witness). This reflection varied or was intermittent depending on the geometric evolution (it was a certain part of the airplane that was reflecting). As the airplane descended, the sun, as seen from the airplane, approached the horizon and became reddish (rapid sunset as seen from the airplane), and the reflection on the airplane also changed color. All these reflection phenomena could be reconstructed by simulation. Therefore, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of a commercial airplane.
