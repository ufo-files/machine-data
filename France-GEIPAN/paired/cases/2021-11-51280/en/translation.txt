Description: During the night of 11/26/2021 to 11/27/2021, between 11:00 PM and 12:30 AM, in Villeneuve-d'Ascq (59), the witness observed, in two sessions of approximately 15 minutes each, "something" that initially resembles an extremely bright star, with occasional blue luminosity, and nearly permanent movements, slightly curved but light, while remaining in the same area. During the second observation, the phenomenon is still present but moves approximately 3 to 4 meters up and to the right. The estimated elevation is between 15° and 30°, with an initial estimated azimuth of 140° and final azimuth of 160°. No other testimony was collected. The very long duration of the observation points towards an astronomical observation. The JPL/NASA Horizon Systems, NAO site allows the identification of the object in question, which is the star RIGEL. The site provides the following characteristics:

- At 23:00: Azimuth 137°, Elevation 22° 
- At 23:30: Azimuth 160°, Elevation 29°
- Rigel's magnitude during this period is 0.2, making this star very easily observable in the sky. This is consistent with the witness's mention of it being "extremely bright." - Finally, Rigel is a very hot star that appears blue, which is also consistent with the observation description. The perceived strangeness by the witness is due to: - the extreme brightness of the PAN, - its insensitivity to wind and stagnation in a single area for a long duration, - its light, straight-line, nearly permanent movements. The first two elements are consistent with the characteristics of the observation of the star RIGEL, which is very bright, and moving by about twenty degrees during the observation period. The movements are therefore very slow. The last point is explained by the phenomenon of autocinematic or autokinesis, a common perceptual illusion when observing a bright point in the dark (see the article on the autocinematic effect and on the GEIPAN site in . The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": observation of the star RIGEL.
