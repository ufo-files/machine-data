Description: On October 12, 2023, around 11:30 PM, while observing the stars from his terrace in the commune of Espinasse (63), the witness spots a PAN to the southeast. He describes it as consisting of at least two intense white lights flashing in an unsynchronized manner, moving quickly in a straight line from south to north. The witness states that this PAN took between 15 and 20 seconds to cross the sky at an initial angular elevation of 30°, reaching between 45 and 60° at the end, when it was lost to sight in the northeast. He adds that he briefly entered his home to grab his phone and film the object, which took approximately 4 to 5 seconds. He also mentions hearing, towards the end of the observation for a few seconds, a rumble similar to that of a commercial airliner ("the usual rumble that large commercial planes make when heard from far away"), though he doesn't know if it's related to the PAN.

The consistency* of this case is average, with a single witness attempting to film the PAN, and the resulting video being of little use. However, the information provided in the questionnaire remains fairly comprehensive. According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered from a testimony. The witness initially thought it was an airplane, but the oddity of the observation lies in the object's flashing, which intrigued him. He specifies: "Commercial airliners flash in red and white, and don't move as quickly when at high altitude." This contrast with his knowledge of airplane characteristics reinforced the unusual nature of the observation.

The witness resides in an area overflown by a military air corridor, and is accustomed to fighter jet passages, which generally fly at low altitude directly over his house. He notes: "I'm familiar with the sound of these planes, and they pass very quickly, much faster than what I observed."

The hypothesis we explored was the observation of a patrol of 4 military Rafale jets, whose presence is confirmed by the CNOA (National Center for Air Operations) and the green radar tracks visible on the return map, for a movement from southwest to northeast (refer to Document CNOA Map). The following elements are also consistent:

- Regular white flashing, consistent with anti-collision lights,
- The witness hearing a sound similar to that of airplanes,
- The actual presence during the observation period and in the reported witness observation azimuths (from southeast to northeast) of the 4 Rafales, passing at their closest point at approximately 5.5 km from the witness,
- The very short observation duration, with the planes taking roughly 30 seconds to move from southwest to northeast of the witness, at a speed measured on the CNOA map of approximately 970 km/h.

Additionally, note that the direction of the planes' movement, towards the northeast, away from the witness, aligns with the change in elevation of the PAN, which goes from 30° to 45/60° at the end. These planes are perfectly capable of flying, for example for stealth reasons related to an exercise, with a minimum of lights on, which explains the absence of flashing red anti-collision lights, which are lit when these planes are on the ground or nearby. The classic position lights of green, red, and white colors may also not have been lit by the pilots or were obscured by the bright anti-collision lights and the distance separating the witness from the planes, which were flying at an altitude of approximately 33,000 feet (10 km). This high altitude also explains the lower noise level and the feeling of lower speed reported by the witness compared to previous observations of fighter jets.

Classification: A, observation of 4 fighter jets.
