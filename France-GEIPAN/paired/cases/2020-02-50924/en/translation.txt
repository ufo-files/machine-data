Description: On Friday, February 28, 2020, at 7 PM, the witness is returning home when a circular or spherical object of orange-tinted color appears in the sky, with 4 lights arranged in a square. Its outline is not perfectly clear, its apparent size is that of the Moon, with a movement from West to East. The witness estimates his angle with the horizontal at 60°. The duration of the flight path was approximately 3 minutes, after which the object gradually faded, transitioning from gray to disappearing entirely in the sky. The witness states that "The Thai lanterns do not seem to match." Only one testimony will be collected. Analysis of the Thai lantern hypothesis: - aspect: 4 lights arranged in a square, orange color. The witness's drawing of the reconstruction may seem strange in light of the lantern hypothesis, but the nocturnal observation of a small object (apparent size of the Moon) is conducive to generating perceptual illusions, so the drawing could represent four close lanterns with orange halos that somehow "merged" and not a single object. - Nebulosity: The witness mentions a clear sky, and the Météo France (Arome) data indeed states that clouds only begin at 5000m, so visibility is satisfactory for a release of lanterns. - Trajectory: Straight line from West to East, corresponding to the direction and velocity of the wind reported by nearby stations (265° and 300°). - Wind speed: A release of lanterns can only occur in light winds. The Toulon-le-Baou and Six-Fours-les-Plages stations, located respectively 6 and 8 km from the observation location, report average winds of 1 km/h and 3 km/h, which are indeed light winds. The Saint-Mandrier station, located 1km away, reports a wind speed of 14km/h, but being in a maritime area, average speeds can vary significantly depending on location, so this is not a determining factor. - Disappearance: The passage through gray is typical of a lantern's extinction, and a witness's feeling during extinction is either a vertiginous ascent or a fainting. - Duration: The observation duration (3 minutes) is compatible with the flight duration of Thai lanterns. In conclusion, the observation has characteristics compatible with the Thai lantern hypothesis. However, the origin of the release not having been found, the GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable misidentification with Thai lanterns.
