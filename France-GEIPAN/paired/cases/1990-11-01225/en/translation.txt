Summary: Observation by thousands of people of an exceptional atmospheric reentry: reentry into the atmosphere of the third stage of a Soviet PROTON rocket.
--- seg-c59131a0f654490265f3861a [needs-review] ---
Description: At 8:00 PM on November 5, 1990, the security services of CNES were overwhelmed with calls from numerous gendarmerie brigades reporting a strange, luminous phenomenon that had crossed a large portion of France around 7:00 PM. For over a week, SEPRA received numerous calls from witnesses requesting explanations regarding "a huge, luminous triangle" that was traversing the sky. Among the witnesses, J-P. Haigneré, a spaceman at CNES, confirmed the strangeness of the phenomenon. At the balloon launch center in Aire-sur-Adour, technicians delayed a launch to observe the phenomenon! The SEPRA service was reinforced for the occasion, and information gathering began with civil and military aviation authorities, meteorological services, and NASA regarding a potential atmospheric reentry. NASA's response arrived on November 8, stating for November 5 the reentry of object "20925/1990 – 94C / GORIZONT 21 PLATFORM / USSR." The orbitography services of CNES confirmed the trajectory of this reentry, which crossed France from the Gulf of Gascogne to Alsace. This information was provided to press agencies on November 9, 1990. Notably, a witness, a member of a particularly effective observation network, informed SEPRA as early as November 6 that it was the 90/94C reentry and that it had passed at 103 km above the Gulf of Gascogne, emerging in eastern France at 83 km altitude. The passage over a debris cloud from a third stage of a Soviet PROTON rocket occurs at an altitude of 100-50 km and a decreasing speed of 7-2 km/s (25,000-7,200 km/h). However, a witness, regardless of who, cannot estimate the distance of an unknown object, let alone glowing points, at night. Nevertheless, their brain unconsciously estimates a distance based on the light intensity of the phenomenon. (See the explanation on estimating the size and distance of an unknown phenomenon.) This fusion debris cloud, followed by streaks, stretches across space for several dozen kilometers according to the shape and composition of the metallic pieces. And it is the light power of the phenomenon that made it appear close to the witnesses. This illusion of proximity is observed with every bolide observation. This illusion of perception, known as distance illusion, is well understood by visual perception psychologists. Similarly, contour illusion or shape illusion: in this case, when an observer sees a set of points moving together, their brain constructs a virtual object bearing these points. See the article on the psychology of shape. However, the majority of testimonies regarding the atmospheric reentry of November 5, 1990, describe the observation of a dark craft with multiple lights, flying at low altitude, from west-southwest to east-northeast. The testimonies are consistent with the observation of a fusion debris cloud resulting from the fragmentation of the third stage of the PROTON rocket reentering the atmosphere: - the observation time and its duration, - the multiple glowing points, - the observation direction: see the new simulation of the atmospheric reentry of November 5, 1990, - the apparent size (witnesses were surprised to see that the apparent size did not vary during the passage: proof that the object was very far away). GEIPAN classified this case as "A" for the observation of the atmospheric reentry of the third stage of a PROTON rocket. Several witnesses, very shocked by their observation, did not accept this explanation.
