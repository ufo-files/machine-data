Summary: Short observation at dusk of a round, black object with flashing lights and a very loud, jerky noise heard; rapid movement and disappearance: probable misidentification with a military helicopter.
--- seg-a4ff0c71bb750c5b7cef644f [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the evening of June 11, 2020, two children (T1 and T2) were startled by a loud, abrupt noise while bouncing on a trampoline. They then observed a large round black object with flashing red lights and white lights directed towards the ground. This PAN quickly disappeared. The mother of one of the girls (T3) also heard the noise but was unable to see the object. Witnesses T1 and T3 testified to the gendarmerie the following day, T2 did not wish to give a statement. According to Geipan's criteria of average strangeness and consistency* (multiple witnesses, but only one visual witness interviewed by the gendarmerie, a vague description, no photos or videos of PAN), this case appears to correspond to a probable observation of an aircraft, more specifically, a military helicopter (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Witnesses perceived a loud noise, similar to that of a helicopter.
- The Gendarmerie confirmed the presence of several helicopters in flight in the area at the time of the observation, although the flight plans of these aircraft could not be consulted.
- Young witnesses observed a "large black mass": in twilight, the precise shape of a helicopter is not discernible. In addition, military helicopters are typically painted with dark colors, making them visually discreet. - The witnesses observed flashing red lights on the perimeter of the PAN. It is possible that these lights are red navigation lights that, due to their spatial proximity to white collision lights and/or the observation distance, visually blend together. It is also possible that it is a flashing red anti-collision beacon, typically used at low altitude during an approach to landing or on a runway. Additionally, the trajectory of PAN caused T1 and T2 to only be able to see the left side of the object, where the fixed red navigation lights are located (as observed from behind). This could explain why they did not report green lights, which remain visible only on the right side of the aircraft. However, the number of lights described and drawn by the young witnesses (a "full" of red lights, with seven drawn), appears excessive in comparison to the typical configurations of navigation lights and collision lights on helicopters. - The presence of two white lights illuminating the ground, as written, remains consistent with searchlights of a helicopter, although generally these aircraft are only equipped with one of these lights. - Two-second pause above the witnesses. Furthermore, note that the observation conditions of the PAN were not optimal: the presence of obstacles such as trees, houses, and hills, the darkness of night, and an observation duration of only a few seconds. Under such circumstances, it is understandable that the witnesses could not identify a known object, especially if it was the close and low-altitude flyby of a helicopter, an uncommon event. This is even more likely if these young witnesses have never had any prior experience observing helicopters. The GEIPAN classifies the case as 'B', probable mistaking with a military helicopter. *According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony.
