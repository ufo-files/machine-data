Description: On the night of August 11 to 12, 2012, around 00:25, a witness observed the sky and noticed an object flying at low altitude west of Cassiopia. As it approached the roof of the building, the object became more visible, described as dark with three ends forming a triangle, and bright white circles. The object moved silently and passed over the roof, disappearing from the witness's view. Only one testimony was collected. The white lights, considered separately, strongly resemble Thai lanterns carried by the wind:

- The direction of movement reported by the witness aligns with the wind direction that night (refer to the Infoclimat website).
- The event occurring on a weekend night in August, a time often associated with festive events (lantern releases), supports this hypothesis.
- The witness described a less-known feature of these lantern lights: below the vertical axis, the light is not full as the burner's bottom is a dark or black disk (as shown in this video), which corresponds to the description ("The perimeter of these three circles was illuminated... while the center was dark, almost as dark as the object's color").

The witness stated they saw a triangular object but added: "Dark color barely distinguishable from the night sky. Little contrast between the object and the sky as it was nighttime." They also described the lights at the triangle's ends: "Below the triangular object, a round shape (circle) was located at each of the three ends (tips of the triangle)."

It is highly likely that this is a classic misperception (numerous UFO reports of triangles and polygons caused by Thai lanterns) known as the false shape perception. The eye fills in the space between isolated bright points with a common shape that it perceives (Psychology of Form). Once this mental schema is established, the triangle can also be perceived with some thickness ("Its edges seemed quite wide, thick"), simply because Thai lantern lights also have some height thickness (cylindrical objects).

Therefore, the case is classified as B: Thai lanterns by GEIPAN.
