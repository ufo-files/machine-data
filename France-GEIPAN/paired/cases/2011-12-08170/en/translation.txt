Summary: Observation of silent movements in the clear sky of a luminous phenomenon with an oval shape and orange color: no overlaps.
--- seg-12efad6e94191eaa672bdc8e [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 2, 2011, between 9:15 PM and 9:30 PM, a witness observed a bright phenomenon passing through the Paris sky from their window. A bright point moved quickly on a straight trajectory. After 5 to 6 seconds, this point ignited, and the witness saw an oval object of orange color appear. No trail or halo was noticed. The object then began to spin, stopped, then zigzagged after three turns before departing at a slow speed. Unfortunately, it was hidden by a building. No particular noise was heard. The observation lasted approximately 20 seconds. Only one testimony was collected. This observation initially appears as a routine atmospheric re-entry of a meteoroid (bolide), but ends in a completely unusual way in a spiral, according to a scenario entirely impossible for a celestial body. At this time of year, very few satellites are visible; the first part of the observation could correspond to the observation of a satellite temporarily reflecting sunlight directly (flare), but the end of the observation does not correspond to this hypothesis at all. It is also possible that this is two independent phenomena, but spiral-moving bright objects are extremely rare, especially at this speed. Given the uniqueness of the witness in a highly urbanized area, the consistency of this case is insufficient to characterize such an unusual phenomenon. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" due to lack of corroboration.
