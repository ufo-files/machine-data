Description: On August 13, 2013, a passenger on a Nantes-Rome flight observed, on the left side of the plane and at a distance of several kilometers, a vertical black shape protruding above the clouds. The witness describes a black, matte, airplane-tail-fin-shaped object. The object appears stationary and then turns horizontal and takes the form of a "very thin black saucer at its height." The PAN disappears after approximately one minute of observation, but the witness had time to take a picture of it. The object is described as having a very large size ("maybe 3 times the size of a commercial airplane or more"). The neighboring passenger also made the observation, but does not wish to testify. No verbal exchange was made with the other passengers during the flight. Only one testimony is collected. The apparent strangeness of the case lies essentially in the size of the phenomenon as estimated by the witness (see the investigation report). But the estimation of the size, distance, and speed of an unknown object is strictly impossible, even for an experienced observer. The strangeness of the case therefore remains strong. The characterization of the observation relies primarily on the testimony, as the photos taken are not close enough to clearly show the PAN. This observation case is atypical and does not resemble the usual misperceptions that a GEIPAN is capable of recognizing. - The PAN is not a cloud: compact black mass, first revealing an angular silhouette then elliptical after slow rotation on its longitudinal axis; - The PAN is not an airplane or drone: estimated size far too large given the observation distance; no wings; - The PAN is not a balloon or airship: stationary at the same level as the air lanes used by commercial airplanes (altitudes between 10,000 and 12,000 meters); - The PAN is not an object re-entering the atmosphere, such as an asteroid or debris (it maintained the same altitude throughout the observation); - The PAN cannot be confused with a star: perfectly clear weather, a "pure blue" sky, and an azimut of 0° relative to the observer's line of sight, which precludes any confusion; - The PAN does not appear to be a shadow of an airplane on clouds, as the projected shadow would be visible on closer clouds. The consistency of the case rests only on a single human testimony, but an interview conducted by an investigator with the witness confirms its reliability. The photos reveal no decisive details in favor of any flying object. The consistency remains therefore very average. Given the strong strangeness of the reported phenomenon, the consistency of the available data does not allow the GEIPAN to provide a consolidated opinion on the origin of the observation. The GEIPAN classifies this case as Category C: Lack of reliable information.
