Summary: Long observation of a mobile bright point in a small sector of the night sky: observation of Sirius.
--- seg-f39ca3f58ec254d1b104fca3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the evening of January 2, 2019, a resident of Téloché (72), accompanied by her son, observed in the sky, at a great distance and high altitude, an unusual light. This light changed color, alternating between red, blue, white, and green. It displayed great mobility, disappearing and reappearing quickly and repeatedly. The observation, conducted jointly by the two witnesses, lasted approximately 35 minutes. The witness filmed the PAN. With a low strangeness rating and good consistency (*two witnesses, but a single testimony, a video of the PAN*), the case turns out to be an astronomical misidentification with the star Sirius (see the investigation report). *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The description of the PAN and the length of the observation time are quite typical of an astronomical misidentification. In the direction indicated by the witness, the star Sirius exhibits all the characteristics of the PAN: greater brightness than other stars, colored twinkling giving the impression of rapid and restricted movements, and the presence of a nearby star. Only the angular height of the PAN appears overestimated compared to Sirius' actual position, which is common in PAN testimony. The case is classified as "A," observation of Sirius.
