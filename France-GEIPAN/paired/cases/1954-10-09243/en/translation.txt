Description: On Wednesday, October 27, 1954, at the Mailly camp (in the commune of Mailly-le-Camp), two individuals, including a colonel, observed around 5:45 PM a very bright segment detaching from the clear blue-green sky, "slightly to the north of the setting sun" (2 to 3 minutes after sunset). The object was at an angular height of 45° to 20°, described as stationary for 10 to 15 seconds during a total observation duration of 25 to 30 seconds. This segment, resembling shiny metal, was tilted at 30° from vertical before suddenly becoming a single point for 3 seconds before disappearing. Its size was between one-third and half the diameter of the Moon, or 10' to 15'. Only the colonel's testimony was recorded in an observation report, and some handwritten report explanations are illegible. The witness believes he likely saw an airplane making a turn, illuminated from below by the setting sun. This type of observation, frequent today due to intense air traffic, can be seen every clear evening shortly after sunset. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of an airplane illuminated by the setting sun.
