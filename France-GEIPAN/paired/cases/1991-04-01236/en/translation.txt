Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named LA FOA (988) 19.04.1991 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On April 19, 1991, around 5:00-5:30 PM, three witnesses were at the top of a treeless plateau at the Amieu pass in the commune of SARRAMEA in New Caledonia. They suddenly observed the passage of two luminous phenomena in the clear sky. Despite their brief observations (2-3 seconds for T1 and T2, and about 10 seconds for T3), they described a circular phenomenon ("disks," "round shape," "ball") with varied colors (T1 "silver," T2 "fluorescent green," and T3 "blue, pink, fluorescent green") moving silently on a straight East-West trajectory. The witnesses lost sight of the phenomenon as it was hidden by trees. T1 thought it might be the disintegration of a satellite. A gendarmerie investigation ruled out an aircraft theft. With a low but consistent strangeness (3 witnesses, fairly precise testimonies), this case is very likely an observation of a meteoroid re-entry (see the investigation report). The description provided by the witnesses of PAN is indeed characteristic of this type of phenomenon: very fast movement of two luminous objects, observation duration of 2-3 seconds. This conclusion is much easier today than it was 27 years ago. The GEIPAN has since accumulated many testimonies, and today several sites record testimonies of natural meteoroid re-entries. It can be observed that these re-entries can be seen during the day, with one or more lights (fragmentation), with or without sound. The case on April 8, 2018, recorded in the USA, is an example among others of similar cases to that of our witnesses. It was daytime, there were several observers, several lights (fragmentation), no trails, and no sound (see the example on the IMO site). The short duration of the observation and the East-West trajectory contradict a potential satellite re-entry. This was also confirmed by the absence of any recorded artificial re-entry that could correspond. The case is classified as A: very probable observation of a meteoroid re-entry.
