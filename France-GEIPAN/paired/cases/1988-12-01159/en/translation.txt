Summary: Multiple ground and in-flight observations of the atmospheric reentry of an aerospace debris: piece of a rocket nozzle from the Soyuz launcher (which launched the COSMOS 1984 satellite) recovered in a field near Bourges (Fr).
--- seg-8f1015a39e52d4e058b04a15 [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 21, 1988, at approximately 0:50 a.m., numerous witnesses across French territory, aligned along a North-South axis, observed a very bright white object with a yellow-orange trail moving across the sky. Pilots also reported observing the phenomenon in flight. The speed of the event was extremely fast, and no sound was heard. At the end of its trajectory, the object split into several pieces. The following day, south of Bourges (18), a farmer found a piece of metal sheeting approximately 70 cm long in his field (see investigation report No. 1189). After examining the recovered piece, it was determined to be a fragment of the Russian Soyuz launcher's nozzle (see reconstruction in the "investigative elements" documents). This rocket had been launched on December 16, 1988, from Plesetsk (USSR) carrying the COSMOS 1984 satellite (a type Yantar 4K2 optical reconnaissance satellite that re-entered on February 13, 1989).
