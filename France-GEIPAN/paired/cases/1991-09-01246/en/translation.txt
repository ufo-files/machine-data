Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type 'C' cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as 'C' in 1991 by the SEPRA and previously named Cournon-d'Auvergne (63) after the gendarmerie brigade. On September 19, 1991 at 5:25 AM, the gendarmerie of Cournon d'Auvergne (63) was notified by a witness residing in Clermont-Ferrand (63) of a bright point in the sky. The on-duty gendarme successfully observed the PAN. A second anonymous witness contacted the gendarmerie at 5:30 AM to report the presence of the PAN, and a third call at 5:40 AM from the Clermont-Ferrand police station confirmed the facts. The PAN was described as appearing like a "large star," "emitting a strong white light." The apparent size of the PAN was very small, as it was compared to a "bright point." Observation with binoculars provided no details. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the planet Venus. Furthermore, this planet was well present and of high magnitude in the observed area of the sky, while the witnesses made no mention of it. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (surprise, fatigue, night observation). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of weak strangeness is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception with the planet Venus.
