Description: On January 1st, 2022, at approximately 01:10, two individuals in Lézennes (59) observed in the sky 7 to 9 bright white points resembling stars moving quickly in a straight line and in a "V" formation from south-southwest to northeast. This formation remained "perfect and coherent" throughout the 5 seconds of observation, after which the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s were lost from view. The witness specified that the stars were visible between the elements of the formation. Only one testimony was collected. Consistency* was average, with only one of the two witnesses having reported their observation to the GEIPAN and the absence of photos or videos of the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s. 

According to the GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Two hypotheses were considered: observation of LED light balloons or a formation of birds (as detailed in the investigation report). The balloon hypothesis aligns with the appearance of the PAN in terms of: a point-like shape, according to the observation distance, white color (common for such balloons), and observation during a period conducive to the release of such balloons (New Year). However, the formation in a "V" shape is unlikely as balloons naturally disperse with the wind after release.

The bird hypothesis is supported by the following:

- The witness specified that "it was possible to distinguish the stars between the bright points," indicating that the PAN was composed of several distinct light sources rather than a single object. The observed configuration appeared stable and maintained throughout the observation.

- The white color of each element of the PAN could correspond to the ventral white plumage of certain birds, which could be illuminated by ambient light scattered towards the sky (light pollution) from the nearby large Lille agglomeration.

- The "round" shape could correspond to the body of a bird, with wings potentially indistinguishable due to observation distance or in the case of a species with a light body and darker wings.

- The very brief observation duration (around 5 seconds) likely limited the witness's ability to distinguish each individual, including possible wing beats.

- The elevation of 75° reported by the witness for the PAN might be overestimated due to lack of visual reference. It could not be verified in the absence of a photo of the sky vault at the time of observation. An elevation nearly directly overhead of the observer would make the ventral lighting of a bird more visible. This lighting is also random.

- The observation area is rich in ornithology, partly due to the presence of a natural space in the direction of the __UFO_PROTECTED_000__s' movement. An expert assessment by the Bretagne Vivante association confirmed these elements, stating that given the festive context (observation immediately after New Year's) and geographical location, it is likely birds disturbed and flying in formation at night between wintering sites, such as mallards or egrets, birds with ventral white plumage.

The GEIPAN considers that the perceived strangeness by the witness(es) can be explained by this hypothesis, but with insufficient information to be certain. The GEIPAN classifies the observation as: probable observation of birds flying in formation.
