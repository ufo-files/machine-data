Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case and named FLEURY-SUR-ANDELLE (27) 04.01.1994 is part of a recent set of cases reviewed (see the investigation report). On Tuesday, January 4, 1994, at 11:50 PM, a witness watching television had their attention drawn to a yellowish light that was growing larger in the sky. Through the window, he spots a bright, cone-shaped PAN with multicolored horizontal bands spinning on its own. The witness locates the PAN below the cloud layer, to the NW above the Bonnemare castle but at a relatively close distance. The witness observes that the PAN recedes and then returns as a yellow sphere before growing larger again. Panicked, the witness goes to bed without waking his wife. Three to four minutes later, he then hears noise outside and sees regular flashes. He notices that his dog is frightened and he himself feels muscle contractions in his legs and lower back. The witness does not report the disappearance of the PAN. He is still very shocked by his experience. The witness contacts the gendarmerie the following day. No further testimony will be collected. This GEIPAN testimony is primarily characterized by its lack of consistency: - we have no observation duration information, although it can be assumed that it is short. Indeed, a duration of more than a few minutes is generally reported by the witness. We have no angular height information in the sky, no position variation in the sky, no angular size of the PAN, and most importantly, the witness did not observe the disappearance of the PAN as, panicked, he went to lie down; - the reliability is poor. There is a very high state of stress: "in a panic, I went up to my room," "in a state of panic," "out of fear, I ran up my stairs," "very terrified, I hid under my blankets." Regarding the final phase of the description, since the witness is then lying under the sheets in a panicked state, the descriptions of pale circles may not be connected to the PAN observed previously and could be the result of a visual illusion caused by the state of stress. The fact that the dog barked, which is not in its habits, could be due to it sensing its master's stress or due to the movements on the ground outside heard by the witness (tree branches? animal?). The state of stress was already present during the visual observation phase, which also alters the reliability attributable to these descriptions. The description of the PAN and the presence of clouds suggest the hypothesis of a misidentification with a skytracer. This type of misidentification was, indeed, common during the 1990s. (see the many cases on the Geipan site: www.geipan.fr). The hypothesis was indeed considered and studied during the gendarmerie investigation. The latter made contact with the local hall and nightclub managers. It turns out that no pyrotechnic display or laser show took place on the evening of the observation. A skytracer generally targets strong angular movements in the sky that the witness does not note. On the other hand, without indication of the duration of visual observation, the witness was able to observe a short phase of stability of the light spot. It is especially noted that the observation took place on a Monday evening, an atypical day for a skytracer, unless it was for testing and calibration. Given the lack of decisive descriptive elements, it is mainly the negative neighborhood investigation by the gendarmerie that opposes this hypothesis. The hypothesis of an aircraft can also be considered. The observation direction of the PAN corresponds exactly to the direction of the Rouen Vallée de Seine (76) airport, located 14 km northwest. Thus, the description of the PAN as a bright point approaching the witness could suggest an airplane taking off from that airport and heading towards the witness. The multicolored conical shape could then be explained by the fact that the aircraft was close enough to the witness, and by the fact that it was partially obscured by the cloud layer, making it hard to identify while still emitting its lights. It is worth noting that the witness states that the PAN is surrounded by "bright, very pretty colors." The lack of noise reported by the witness can be explained by the fact that the witness is observing the PAN from inside their house, with closed windows, and a television on in the early stages of the observation. The movement of the PAN away and back can be explained by the fact that clouds partially obscure the aircraft, leading to changes in angular size and light intensity that the witness can reasonably interpret as changes in distance. The visual aspect remains nonetheless quite unusual for this type of misidentification. The perception of rotation of the cone is curious except if it is a helicopter in stationary hover turning on itself. The witness does not describe alternating white or red light flashes from collision lights, but there are other verified observations of aircraft without this type of perception at GEIPAN. The actual descriptive elements that are decisive for confirming or refuting this hypothesis are missing. For an aircraft to be visible according to a cone, it must be close enough (a few kilometers), and the witness should have perceived an angular movement, unless the observation is short but we have no indication of duration, or unless it is a helicopter. An aircraft always ends up moving and disappearing, this phase provides characteristic information, but we have no observation of the disappearance. The fact that we have no evidence information on the presence of an aircraft is not determinative as 25 years after this traffic information is not available. On the other hand, it is noted (see PV) that a person from the village saw at the same time a moving light in the sky, but did not pay attention, thinking it was an airplane. In the end, the strangeness of the case exists because it is not possible to consider one hypothesis as probable to more than 50%. On the other hand, the lack of consistency does not allow us to either confirm or refute any hypothesis, and thus validate an unexplained character for this case. It is not possible to conclude. The GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
