--- seg-08fe23f6f00abf1020026481 [needs-review] ---
Description: On August 8, 2012, at 11:10 PM, a witness observed for one minute the passage from south to north in parallel of two very bright objects at a very high altitude. The witness's testimony is very brief, and no other testimonies have been reported. When reviewing the satellite passage log generated by Calsky, we note at this time (11:10 PM) several satellite passages: first, the International Space Station (ISS), extremely bright (-4.3 magnitude) with the Russian Progress cargo spacecraft and the Japanese HTV-3 cargo spacecraft docked to it. Its apparent trajectory is from southwest to northeast. We also note at nearly the same time two other satellites much less bright (IGS5 H2A Rocket and Alos, with magnitudes of 2.7 and 3), on a south to north trajectory, succeeding each other at approximately 80° intervals. While the witness may have easily missed the spectacular passage of the ISS, it is surprising that he describes a passage from south to north, and there is only one very bright point visible. If these were the two other satellites he observed, they were not very bright. Due to this uncertainty and the imprecision of the testimony, GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of satellites, without being able to precisely determine which ones.
