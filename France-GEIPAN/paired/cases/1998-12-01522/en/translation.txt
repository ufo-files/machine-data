Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named ROSHEIM (67) 22.12.1998 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been re-examined. On December 22, 1998, at approximately 6:05 PM, a witness observed in the sky a "serpent" shaped light formation composed of red and white disks. This luminous shape moved silently "in mass" forming a loop in the sky. The witness observed it for about one minute. The witness gave a statement on January 15, 1999, on the advice of a friend. No other testimony was collected. It is still very important to start from the witness's interpretation and consider the objections they may raise (see the investigation report). - He spontaneously thought of disco light projectors, and the objection came later when he compared it with photos showing these lights: "When I first saw this thing, I thought it could be a nightclub light. In the days that followed, I saw photos showing light beams emitted by nightclubs, and what I saw on December 22, 1998, in Rosheim, did not correspond to that at all." - The witness concludes his statement with, "This is the first time I have observed this kind of phenomenon. Never in my life have I seen anything like this in the sky, except for 'lasers' from nightclubs." The analysis establishes a high probability of confusion with impact spots in the sky from ground-based light projectors (laser-type nightclub lights): - At the time of observation, the phenomenon was new to the GEIPAN, but the large number of explanations since (see www.geipan.fr website, search for cases with laser or Skyrose phenomenon) allows us to assert that the appearance and movement described by the witness are consistent with impact spots from these ray projectors on low clouds. - Weather conditions are compatible with the production of spots on a sky screen (clouds) without visibility of ascending beams. The witness reports a starry sky, which remains compatible with the presence of clouds in a large part of the sky. - Nightclubs already used projectors, and the pattern and movement of the spots in the sky were achievable with the technology of the time. - A key element reinforcing the hypothesis, the witness did it himself, and we can explain what led him to reject this hypothesis (and ultimately testify): the first available photos at the time (and the internet did not exist or was in its infancy) must have represented laser effects in the sky in the most spectacular version with ascending beams, while ascending beams are not always visible, and that was probably the case that night. Although the testimony is relatively complete with a good description of PAN and the existence of azimuth records, it is old (almost 20 years) and it seems difficult to find elements to confirm the hypothesis so long after. Additionally, there are missing apparent angular size records of PAN and estimates of its angular height on the horizon. An on-site investigation at the time would have allowed us to deepen these points and make certain verifications regarding the existence of a structure (nightclub, hall, park...) capable of using projectors in the context of festive activities the night before Christmas. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable laser observation.
