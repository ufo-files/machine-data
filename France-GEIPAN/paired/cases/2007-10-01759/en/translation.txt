Summary: Observations of a large shape flying slowly and silently in the sky: likely observations of helium-filled party balloons.
--- seg-6d244111573cfcde2ee74bdf [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 2, 2007, around 1:00 PM, a witness in their garden noticed in the sky a particular phenomenon: a large, diamond-shaped object of several colors flying silently at low altitude. Intrigued, this witness decides to follow the phenomenon in a car for 5 to 6 km between Neuville-les-Dames and Vonnas. He takes photographs during "the pursuit" and meets 4 other witnesses in the parking lot of a horticultural business in Vonnas, also intrigued. The witness then continues on the road towards Mézeriat, but loses sight of the phenomenon. Following an appeal for witnesses in the daily newspaper Le Progrès, many people will come forward. The trajectory reconstruction consistent with the observations indicates a movement direction from south-south-west to north-north-east, very close to that of the south wind reported in Lyon. The average speed of the object during the observation (20 km/h) is a little lower than the wind speed (22 km/h). These two observations clearly direct the investigation towards the hypothesis of a lighter-than-air airship, and thus carried by the wind (see investigation report). The aspect described by the witnesses resembles a cluster of small, festive mylar balloons, inflated with helium. She may have escaped from her owner, or on the contrary, was assembled to carry a small payload. This cluster of balloons may have traveled a great distance before the observation, making it very difficult to estimate its origin. This observation case, with multiple witnesses and photos, turns out to be very consistent, but ultimately not strange, as the object's movement is compatible with the direction and speed of the wind. Following the investigation, the GEIPAN classifies this case as 'B': probable observation of festive balloons filled with helium.
