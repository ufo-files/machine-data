Summary: Successive observations of flashes near Jupiter: Misidentification with debris of the Resurs 1-3 rocket stage.
--- seg-9415c6fb78ec3b660ed016cb [needs-review] ---
**Description:** On the evening of August 16, 2019, a resident of PORT-LA-NOUVELLE (11) decides to observe the Moon with his binoculars as it rises. He also observes Jupiter, when he sees a "flash" just above the planet, then, a few seconds later, the same flash repeats at the same location, and 4 or 5 seconds later, a third flash, slightly higher than the first two, occurs. The witness continues to scan the sky but sees nothing else. Only one testimony was collected. A favored hypothesis (see investigation report): astronautical misperception. The brief and powerful flashes, with an irregular rhythm, are characteristic of this type of misperception, involving a decommissioned satellite or a discarded rocket stage in rapid orbit rotation. In any case, the observation cannot be explained by a misperception with an aircraft: first, no aircraft had a trajectory corresponding to that of the PAN; second, the witness would very likely have observed other flashes. Moreover, since he observed the PAN with binoculars, he should have seen green and red position lights. A misperception with a rapidly rotating satellite object for this case implies that the object causing the misperception passed very close to Jupiter between 10:38 PM and 10:40 PM (time indicated in the witness's first email), on a trajectory oriented from south to north. A Calsky reconstruction shows that two objects can correspond to this description: Resurs 1-3 rocket and NOAA 18 (see Figure 9 in the investigation report). - Resurs 1-3 rocket is a Russian Zenit-2 rocket stage launched in 1994. It is noteworthy that this object passed directly in front of Jupiter at 10:44:35 PM. - NOAA 18 is an American weather satellite launched in 2005. It passed within a few degrees of Jupiter at 10:43 PM. Although NOAA satellites are known for exhibiting powerful and irregular flashes once deactivated, a misperception with NOAA 18 cannot explain the observation since this satellite was still active at the time of the observation according to the OSCAR website. The only object that can explain the observation is therefore the Resurs 1-3 rocket, whose apparent motion was slow (0.46°/s), which is consistent with the proximity of the flashes (Figure 10). It is worth noting that the Resurs 1-3 rocket is a cylindrical object, measuring 10.4 meters long by 3.9 meters in diameter. This strong disproportion can explain the intense variations in the object's brightness (flashes) if it is rotating rapidly. Although there is only one witness for this observation and the PAN was neither filmed nor photographed, the testimony has good consistency as it is fairly precise. The elements provided by the witness (description, brightness, direction) confirm that the bright planet serving as a reference is indeed Jupiter. Although not precise, the time indicated by the witness in his first email is fully consistent, especially regarding the position of the Moon in the sky and terrestrial references. Only the angular height of Jupiter, and therefore the PAN, is overestimated, without this having any significant impact on the case. Overestimation of angular heights is very common in PAN testimony. With a low strangeness and good consistency (single witness but fairly precise testimony), this case turns out to be an astronautical misperception. The description provided by the witness strongly suggests a rapidly rotating satellite (deactivated satellite or rocket stage). A reconstruction of satellite passages shows that only one object corresponds to the trajectory described by the witness (north-south trajectory, passage near Jupiter), namely the Resurs 1-3 rocket stage, launched in 1994. The slow apparent motion of this object explains why the three flashes occurred in the same area of the sky. The case is classified as A, misperception with a debris from the Resurs 1-3 rocket stage.
