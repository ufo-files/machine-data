Summary: Pilots reported observing a spherical object colored aluminum; likely observation of a stratospheric balloon.
--- seg-a93d2b794b3cfb12d1c300bd [needs-review] ---
Description: On September 20, 1956, at 3:50 PM, two pilots of a patrol in flight observed the movement of a spherical object colored aluminum. Intrigued and intending to get closer, the pilots gain altitude and realize it is impossible to catch up to it. They abandon the pursuit at 16:10. Following a request to the director of the observatory of Puy-de-Dôme in Clermont-Ferrand (63), it appears that a transparent balloon of approximately spherical shape with a slight bulge at its base was observed throughout the day of September 20 in the Clermont sky. It constantly derived at very low speed at 20km altitude before becoming invisible at 7:05 PM no longer being illuminated by the sun. The observatory ignores the origin of the object by stating that the weather services in the region and itself have not released any balloon. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: observation of a stratospheric balloon whose origin has not been found. The feeling of being followed by the object and the impossibility of getting closer to it are characteristics of perceptual illusions during the observation of distant objects. In our case, the very large balloon was approximately 17km from the pilots. The closure at an altitude of 32,000 feet (10,000 meters) only reduced the distance to approximately 10 km, which remains significant.
