Summary: Pilots reported observing a spherical object colored aluminum; likely observation of a stratospheric balloon.
--- seg-a93d2b794b3cfb12d1c300bd [needs-review] ---
Description: On September 20, 1956, at 3:50 PM, two pilots on a patrol flight observed a spherical object with an aluminum-colored surface. Intrigued and intending to close in, the pilots gained altitude but realized it was impossible to catch up with the object. They abandoned the pursuit at 4:10 PM. Following a request to the director of the observatory in Puy-de-Dôme, Clermont-Ferrand (63), it was determined that a translucent balloon with a roughly spherical shape and a slight bulge at its base had been observed throughout the day of September 20 in the Clermont sky. It drifted constantly at a very low speed at 20 kilometers altitude before becoming invisible at 7:05 PM, no longer illuminated by the sun. The observatory is unaware of the object's origin, stating that neither the regional weather services nor they themselves had released any balloons. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: observation of a stratospheric balloon whose origin was not found. The feeling of being followed by the object and the impossibility of getting closer are characteristic of perceptual illusions when observing distant objects. In our case, the balloon, very large, was approximately 17 kilometers from the pilots. Even when closing in to an altitude of 32,000 feet (10,000 meters), the distance was only reduced to about 10 kilometers, which is still significant.
