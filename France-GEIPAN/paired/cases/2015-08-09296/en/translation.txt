Description: On August 16, 2015, between 12:40 and 12:45 AM, a witness, accompanied by friends, observed in the sky three equally spaced white lights forming a perfect triangle. The object moved slowly and silently before suddenly disappearing. His friends did not have time to see the phenomenon. No other testimony was collected. Between the lights, the witness distinguished a thin black triangle. This triangle could be the result of a perceptual illusion known as contour illusion or shape illusion (see Psychology of Form). The movement was observed towards the West; if these are lights carried by the wind, their movement seems incompatible with the West wind reported in Béziers weather. However, the observation was very brief, and a local breeze is always possible along the coast. This case has a low level of consistency: very brief observation, witnesses present did not notice anything; if we accept the contour illusion hypothesis, this case is not particularly strange, and could correspond to illuminated balloons (LED balloons or Thai lanterns), frequently launched on holidays (it is the evening of August 15, in a busy tourist area). The wind report does not allow this hypothesis to be confirmed or refuted. Given these uncertainties, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" due to lack of information and corroboration.
