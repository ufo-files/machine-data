Summary: Observations of the passage of filaments and sparkling discs in the sky; collection and analysis of samples: observation of ballooning spiders.
--- seg-6f7fc8bc574c9a8aef7f28d4 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 26, 2017, two witnesses on Chambrette beach in the commune of Verdon-sur-Mer (33) were intrigued by white filaments falling from a clear sky. One witness grabbed some filaments to examine them. Then, both witnesses saw the rapid and silent passage of two shimmering disks that moved side by side in the sky. The filaments continued to fall for a few minutes before everything returned to normal. The witness managed to catch a filament. The witnesses describe two simultaneous phenomena: 1. filaments 2. disks, whose speed, shape, and color did not correspond to known aircraft. The ballooning of spiders hypothesis accounts for most of the characteristics described by the witness. Silk threads (also known as "virgin's threads") are threads created by spiders to move through the air and by the wind. This mode of locomotion is called "ballooning." The threads can be rolled into a ball, which can give the double appearance of threads and disks seen from the ground. Two arguments could have seemingly opposed this hypothesis: 1. The wind coming from the south or west according to weather archives. However, the witness locally described "a southeasterly breeze" consistent with the direction of movement and therefore the hypothesis. 2. The distance estimated by T1 and T2 (5000m for T2, 10000m for T1), but it is known that evaluating the distance of an unknown object is difficult to exploit without a reference point. The testimony is consistent, with credible witnesses and concordant observations. Following a first publication in March 2018, the witness shared with us in mid-September a sample of a filament recovered during the observation (see § 3 of this investigation report). It was analyzed by a laboratory of the CNES. This analysis confirms that it is indeed silk fibers mixed with particles, mostly iron oxide (see annex of CR). This case had previously been classified B, but this new element, which constitutes near-conclusive proof of the hypothesis, allows us to reclassify it as A and republish it: observation of spider ballooning.
