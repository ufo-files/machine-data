Description: The witness reports in 2025 an observation made in 2022. On November 7, 2022, around 10:45 PM, a witness, at the window of their kitchen in their home in Paris (75), photographs the courtyard of their building, illuminated by the full moon visible in their field of vision. They take a first photograph and notice the presence of a small blue sphere above the buildings on the other side of the courtyard. After taking other photographs, they confirm the presence of this blue ball on those subsequent images, retrospectively on their computer. Of good consistency, with the presence of two photographs that could be analyzed, although there is only one late testimony, this observation turns out to be a parasitic reflection of the moon in the smartphone camera lens used by the witness.

According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. This hypothesis is based on the following elements:

- The GEIPAN image analysis software, IPACO, could be used and unequivocally demonstrates on both photos that it is such a reflection, caused by the presence in the image field of the full moon, which appears overexposed (high brightness). This reflection is caused by the internal technology of the photographic detector, leading to position it opposite a strong light source, according to a central symmetry axis passing through the optical center of the camera lens (see annexes 1 and 2). The methodology inherent to the analysis of this artifact can be consulted on this page regarding optical reflections.

- Round shape, similar to that of the moon
- Color which can be blue, green, or red, typical of the outer protective layer of the external lens of the objective. This type of phenomenon can only be visible on the device control screen, or retrospectively, on a computer, for example.

The GEIPAN classifies this observation as "A": parasitic objective reflection artifact.
