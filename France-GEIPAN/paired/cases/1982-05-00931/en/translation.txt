Summary: Observation of a bright phenomenon lighting up the entire landscape and the silent passage of a round object: probable observation of an atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid.

Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named PONT SAINT-ESPRIT (30) 1982 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On April 28, 1982, around 11:47 PM, a witness is surprised by a lightning flash that illuminates the valley. He then observes a luminous phenomenon with a green outer color and a white center. The whole thing moves silently and very quickly at low altitude in a westerly direction. The witness describes a round object that does not change its shape or color throughout the observation. No other testimony has been collected. The testimony left unexplained at the time and reexamined 35 years later appears consistent with numerous descriptions received by the GEIPAN and identified as atmospheric entries of meteoroids or space debris. The characteristic points here are "lightning flash illuminating the valley," "very bright light, green on the outside and white in the center." It is common to have multiple testimonies; today, automatic cameras record and detect these entries. We cannot therefore guarantee this hypothesis, but it has a probability greater than 50% given the described aspect. We lack descriptive elements of duration that would help to determine whether it is a meteoroid or space debris entry. The lack of registration for the second type leads us to consider the first type, which is the most frequently observed and the least well-registered (at that time). The strong light in the sky illuminating the valley also supports this. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: observation of atmospheric entry of a meteoroid.
