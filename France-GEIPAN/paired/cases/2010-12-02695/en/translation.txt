Description: On December 30, 2010, at 6:30 PM, a witness observed in a clear sky several white oval rings and bright points moving in the sky. The observation lasted 45 minutes. The description of the PAN, composed of a multitude of white light spots with periodic movements, suggests the observation of a light show from a multi-beam projector of the Skyrose type. These projectors are often a source of confusion due to the original dance they project onto clouds, and their beams may not be visible when the atmosphere is clear, as after rain. However, in this case, the witness reports a black sky, with few stars but also "not heavily clouded" (see QT p.5/8). If the sky transparency was good given the strong winds throughout the day, detailed weather data at point 3.1 indicate fog and drizzle in Le Havre, Dieppe, and Rouen, and incomplete satellite images do not allow us to know the cloud cover at the time of the observation. Based on this (fog), the state of the atmosphere does not seem compatible with the observation of spots projected onto the cloud cover, as fog should have revealed the beams and thus the direction of the projector. Furthermore, there does not seem to be a disco in Senneville, but the announcement of an exceptional New Year's Eve party may have motivated a party organizer. With low strangeness and average consistency, this observation case cannot be firmly concluded, although the hypothesis of a Skyrose-type projector, fully consistent with the observation report, appears plausible on the eve of New Year's Day. This case is classified C due to lack of independent testimony and cross-checking.
