Summary: Long, discontinuous observation of a bright PAN in the shape of a "V" remaining stationary in the pre-dawn sky: observation of Sirius.
--- seg-b49d3b23d5be7319b15b4398 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On September 17, 2019 at 05:45, when opening his window, the witness observes "something that (he) finds strange." The PAN observed is a "bright white point of very intense light." Sometimes the intensity seemed to weaken slightly before returning to brightness. From this point, there were two less intense tracks, one descending from the tip towards the right, the other rising from the tip towards the right. It had the shape of a "V". The witness has the impression of a triangular shape. This phenomenon is not moving. It remains motionless and makes no noise. The witness leaves his observation position for a few minutes to prepare for his work. Returning to the window, the PAN is still there, still in the same position. The witness decides to take 3 photos. The first photo reveals a bright point and movement. The other 2 photos reveal 3 bright points in a "V" shape. When, an hour later, her mother gets up, she notices the presence in the sky of the phenomenon. It is then 06:50. The observation lasts over an hour. The PAN is high in the sky and well visible throughout the duration of the observation. It will take the dawn for its brightness to fade and eventually disappear, as the witness testifies. The witness mentions an azimuth of E-SE and an elevation of 60°. The photos allow for the correction and evaluation of azimuth and elevation at respectively 135° and 18° (see the investigation report). The duration of observation and the description of the PAN point towards an astronomical observation. On that day, at that time of day, we find Sirius with a magnitude of -1.46, of very high intensity consistent with the witness's impression. Sirius moves from an azimuth of 135° to 149.5° and from an elevation of 13.5° to 20.20°. This is consistent with the information in the testimony. The witness did not notice any changes in azimuth and elevation, which is normal, as these movements remain slow and hard to observe with the naked eye over an hour without any reference points or landmarks. The witness's perceived strangeness is related to the "bright point" whose "intensity seemed to fade slightly before becoming bright again" as well as the perception of "two less intense lines, one descending from the tip to the right, the other rising from the tip to the right." It had the shape of a "V". It is likely that the witness who saw a "V" shape is describing what he saw in the photo, rather than what he actually observed with his bare eyes. It is quite common to think that a zoom on an image is more precise, more performant than visual observation. By doing this, the artifacts increase. In this case, the perception of the "V" is very likely created by the photos. It is a motion effect that creates a "V" in photos 2 and 3. Photo 1 shows a blur with a very different geometry. At visual observation, the witness perceived effects of scintillation (varying brightness). The scintillation could have disturbed the photos, but movement remains the main disturbing element in photo taking. The scintillation is a phenomenon due to atmospheric turbulence. It created the perceived intensity variation visually. The consistency of the testimony is good. Specific elements are provided in the testimony. An explanatory sketch and several photos of the location help to corroborate essential elements of the testimony, particularly regarding the angular height. The testimony also contains photos of the phenomenon. We have two witnesses, even though only one testified. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of Sirius.
