Description: A witness reports in 2012 an observation made in July or August 1954 around 8 PM (see questionnaire) in Sainte-Clotilde (commune of Mers-el-Kébir) near Oran (Algeria); the witness, at the time, was 15 years old. He later places his observation on a weekday between August 24 and 27. In Sainte-Clotilde, late in the day, people at a table are suddenly surprised to see a bright orange-fire disk fall from the sky. It follows a descending trajectory towards the sea but stops mid-air and remains motionless above the horizon. The disk then takes off at high speed in an ascending trajectory at 45° towards the East before disappearing. The observation lasted approximately two minutes. Only one testimony was collected on this phenomenon. However, an article in Le Provençal from August 27, 1954, reports a bolide observation seen from Plan-de-Cuques (13) on August 22, 1954, at 8 PM. The hypothesis of a bolide observation can explain a large part of the observation described in this case. Nevertheless, given the many uncertainties surrounding this case: the exact date is unknown, the memory is old and potentially altered, the second part of the observation can only be explained by a non-verifiable physiological cause, the GEIPAN classifies this case as 'C', an unexploitable observation due to a lack of reliable information.
