--- seg-220b38a21dc0b840ac8df150 [needs-review] ---
Description: On June 2nd and 3rd, 2023, around 10 PM, a couple residing in Cannes (06) observed a very bright and stationary object in the sky during two consecutive evenings. On June 3rd, a second couple of friends was also present and confirmed the observation of the phenomenon. However, only one witness filled out and submitted a questionnaire to GEIPAN. The observation was made from several locations within the city of Cannes. The witness reported an observation direction pointing south. However, analysis of the photographs taken indicates an orientation between west and west-northwest. Using binoculars, the object appeared to be composed of several uniform-sized bright points in constant motion, giving the whole a variable appearance, sometimes spherical, sometimes in the shape of a crescent. Each observation lasted approximately two hours. Three photographs of PAN were taken on the evening of June 3rd using a smartphone. In these images, the object appears as a white bright point. The details observed with the naked eye and described by the witness through binoculars (distinct shapes and bright points) are not visible in the pictures. Furthermore, on July 1st, 2023, the witness reported a new observation of the same phenomenon, which he was able to film this time. The video again shows a white bright point, with no reference points to accurately locate it in the sky. Due to its very low strangeness and good consistency* (four witnesses, but one unique testimony, photos and video of PAN), this case turns out to be an observation of the planet Venus. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The visual aspect of PAN, as well as the adjusted observation direction by the investigation, are perfectly consistent with the observation of this planet, made twice at the same times on consecutive evenings (see the investigation report). On the photographs, only one bright point is visible, while two close points would have appeared if Venus had not been the source of PAN. Additionally, PAN remained observable until Venus' setting and was spotted again on July 1st, 2023 in the evening, but with a reduced angular height, in perfect consistency with the expected position of the planet on that date. Furthermore, the shape variations of PAN described during the binoculars observation correspond to a perceptual artifact resulting from a poor instrument setting. In fact, precisely focusing binoculars on a single isolated bright point, without distance reference, is particularly challenging. Under certain conditions, the perceived image can overlap with that generated by the eye (entoptic vision phenomenon), creating an illusion of movement of the observed object. The case is classified as "A", observation of Venus.
