Summary: NORT-SUR-ERDRE (44) 25.11.1990. Observations from a vehicle of the silent movement of three varied fluorescent green shapes in the sky: very probable observations of beams and impacts on cloud with "skytracker" type projectors.
--- seg-076146779c456dfafc62596a [needs-review] ---
**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined to enhance the relevance of conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This previously classified observation (D) named NORD-SUR-ERDRE (44) 21.11.1990 is part of a recent batch of reexamined cases (see investigation report). It concerns the observation of luminous phenomena at night by four witnesses driving a car on the night of Saturday 24 to Sunday 25 November 1990 around 00:45. The witnesses include one adult and three children. A fourth child was present but refused to observe the phenomenon. Only the adult (T1) and one of the children (T2) reported the incident to the gendarmes. The witnesses stated that while driving from Nort-Sur-Erdre (44) to Casson (44), they saw three PAN of varying shapes and sizes (elongated, rounded, oval) in the night sky, glowing a fluorescent green. These "objects" moved back and forth across the road, silently, while remaining grouped together. They were occasionally lost from view during the journey. The last time they were seen, they were heading towards the commune of Suce-Sur-Erdre (44). The gendarmes spoke to several people living along the route, but no other testimony was collected about this phenomenon. The witnesses very likely observed the effects of a ground-based projector beam, which creates a visible trail as it passes through atmospheric zones, forming a bright spot when it encounters a cloud screen. This conclusion is based on the following consistent points:

- The observation date, in the night from Saturday to Sunday, traditionally a festive time when nightclubs often stay open late.
- Cloud cover. It was present and partially obscured the sky (5/8 octas) at a low ceiling (600-800m) over two levels;
- The movements of the PAN were typical of skytrackers, controlled from the ground by an automated motorized system, causing the projectors to move back and forth in various patterns; T1 also stated, "It was moving at the same speed as me."
- Disappearances. The brief disappearances of the PAN can be explained by intermittent cloud cover. When the beam passes through "gaps," there is no impact.
- Brightness. The PAN was described by the witnesses as being dim to fairly bright, similar to reflections of projectors on clouds. Additionally, the witnesses, noting an increase in brightness, were likely closer to the light source once they arrived home.
- Location. The observation indicates that the ground-based source of the luminous impacts on clouds was likely located in or near Nantes, where numerous nightlife establishments were using skytrackers in 1990.
- Beam presence or absence. The beam could be visible or not, depending on local weather conditions. The presence of fine particles suspended in the atmosphere is essential. In this case, it involved water droplets generated by localized showers, probably occurring during the observation on various observation axes.
- Conditions. At the time of the observation, a low-level cloud layer partially covered the sky, and localized showers had occurred in the hour preceding the observation and were likely still ongoing, as the present clouds were conducive to generating such showers. Prior rainfall might have "cleaned" the lower atmosphere in some areas, removing potential particles and preventing beam visibility.
- Shapes. The witnesses described changing, alternating, or simultaneous shapes: round, oval, and/or elongated and narrow. The beam is visible as it crosses an area with water droplets (showers under cumulus clouds) and gives the appearance of a "truncated" elongated shape. It may reappear further along and higher up, extending when the beam impacts a higher cloud layer and creates a second elongated shape, depending on the angle of the shot. Without a cloud impact (beam gap) or clear sky area, only one shape is present.
- Color. The color green was indeed present in 1990 for skytrackers. The mention of "fluorescence" is likely an exaggeration, reflecting more the quality and intensity of light as it passes through water droplets, creating the impression of "fluorescence."
- Case consistency is poor, lacking angular information (dimensions of PAN, heights on the horizon...) and duration of observation mention. Laser animations, now easily recognizable, were only in their early stages in the 1980s and 1990s. It is not possible today to find the origin of this animation due to the passage of time (nearly 30 years) and the need to search within a radius of several dozen kilometers from the observation location.
- The absence of a trace is not decisive. We have observations from this period where investigations successfully located the animation, confirming that equipment producing such effects was sold to nightclubs in France. (See gendarmerie investigation report for the observation case ILE BOUCHARD (37) 11.12.1993).
- The perfect consistency of the appearance with the meteorological situation noted, the occurrence on a weekend night near a large city, and the high usage of skytrackers in nightclubs at that time are factors leading us to consider this hypothesis very probable.

GEIPAN concludes with a "A": Very probable observation of projector beam and cloud impact phenomena, likely from a nightclub located in Nantes or its suburbs.
