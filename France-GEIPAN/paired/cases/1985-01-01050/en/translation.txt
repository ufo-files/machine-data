Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type 'C' cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified 'C' by SEPRA (Atmospheric Re-entry Phenomenon Study Service). On February 22, 1985, around 7:40 PM, several witnesses spotted in the clear, starlit sky a particular luminous phenomenon heading west. The phenomenon was described as a green core from which green arrows detached; red lights also blinked regularly from the core. The O-N movement of the luminous phenomenon was slow, and the observation lasted about 1 hour. Following an appeal for witnesses in the local press, a witness of a similar observation on January 31, 1985, came forward. This case is consistent (four witnesses, only two of whom could be interviewed), and the description of PAN observed is accurate. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The described phenomenon has many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, trajectory) with a perfectly known astronomical object: Venus. Moreover, Venus was clearly present in the observed sky area, while the witness did not mention it, and it was particularly bright. In the current classification of GEIPAN, this weak strangeness case is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception with Venus.
