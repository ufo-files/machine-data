--- seg-41445e93d281754eb1e728d3 [needs-review] ---
Description: This observation case is part of cases classified as 'C' in 2008 by the GEIPAN. It was subject to a new review (see document: investigation notes). On March 17, 1978, around 1:30 AM, a witness observed a round, glowing object moving slowly in a south-north direction. No unusual noises were heard during the 10-minute observation, first with the naked eye and then with binoculars. The object slowly descended towards the horizon. A gendarmerie investigation was unable to provide new elements regarding this phenomenon. The case is consistent, and the description of the PAN observed is fairly accurate. The witness's sincerity and credibility were never questioned. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, brightness) with a perfectly known astronomical phenomenon: the Moon at sunset. Additionally, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, while the witness made no mention of it. This is not a matter of the witness's visual perception but rather the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, surprise). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of low strangeness is classified as PAN A: an observation related to a misperception of the Moon at sunset observed under partial cloud cover.
