Description: On November 18, 2012, around 8:30 PM, a witness observes, both with the naked eye and binoculars, a light in the sky. This light is stationary and flickers (green and red flashes); its center is black and its brightness varies. The witness moves, shows the light to several other people nearby, then contacts the gendarmerie. A patrol is sent to the scene and also confirms the phenomenon. The gendarmes alert the Center for Navigation Air West (CRNA-O) near Brest, and the air traffic controller writes a report stating that neither a crew in flight approaching Quimper nor he himself made any unusual observations on that date. He also indicates that the military were not on duty in the area that evening and that the primary radars of Lanvéoc and Landivisiau detected nothing unusual. Despite a neighborhood investigation, only one testimony about this phenomenon is collected. The witness observes a stationary light changing color towards southeast: a fixed light in the sky suggests an astronomical phenomenon, and the color change may be due to flickering. A light west wind and very humid atmosphere are noted that day; however, there is no very bright star in that part of the sky; Jupiter is instead located directly east. The CNRA protocol provides additional elements, such as the altitude of the PAN from the horizon and the fact that it was not visually/radar detected by the crew of an aircraft approaching Quimper or by the primary radars of Lanvéoc and Landivisiau. The report also states the absence of military activity in the area. Based on the objective elements outlined in previous chapters, we can conclude that this case should be classified as "B," likely a confusion with a helicopter. The phenomenon has all the characteristics of a helicopter observed at night from a great distance; possibly a Civil Security helicopter on a VFRn mission near Quimper Pluguffan airport, corresponding to the observation's azimuth. This testimony is of average quality (too concise, lacking precision, and missing essential data), and has low consistency as it comes from a single witness without a photo. The observation is not particularly strange because the PAN can be easily characterized both in appearance and behavior. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a Civil Security helicopter approaching Quimper Pluguffan airport.
