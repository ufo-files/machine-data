Description: On July 28, 2008, around 8:30 PM, a witness walking his dog noticed, in the north-northwest above the tennis court fence of his village, an orange-colored cloud with a black outline. This large, elongated cloud was completely motionless, and the witness did not hear any noise. He then spotted a kind of blue flame above the cloud; the cloud slowly approached, passing between the community hall and the witness, heading towards a field, and transformed into an electric ball. The witness described it as looking like a "giant sea urchin" with electric spikes all around it. No particular noise was heard. The UFO (unidentified flying object) PAN moved away towards a hill. The witness realized he could take a picture and ran home, unable to immediately find his phone, thinking he should return to the site before the PAN disappeared. Back at the scene, the ball was still present above the hill, and the witness saw two black objects nearby moving away from the sphere. The witness observed the scene for 2 to 3 seconds, then the sphere descended behind the hill and disappeared. The witness returned to the site later that evening and noticed a strong smell in the air, "like burnt electrical circuits." In the following days, the witness experienced effects on himself (irritated eyes, feeling of heat, insomnia). The witness remained shocked and stressed afterward. No other testimony was collected by GEIPAN. This multi-phased observation of a very close light phenomenon is quite strange. No elements cast doubt on the witness's sincerity and the reliability of his account. The witness describes a gaseous phenomenon, changing shape and appearance. When called on the phone, the witness clarified that on the evening of the observation, when he returned home to get his mobile phone to take a picture, he was ultimately unable to find it; he also stated that he felt tingling in his eyes that evening and, two days later, a sensation of heat. This quite atypical observation report was submitted to GEIPAN experts in 2012, who did not offer an explanation. As some parts of the observation report suggest globular lightning, this case was presented at a scientific symposium on ball lightning in 2015; some present scientists saw clear characteristics of such a phenomenon; the physiological effects on the witness are rather in favor of this hypothesis. The ball lightning phenomenon does correspond to the strangeness perceived by the witness, but the observation duration of 10 minutes is very long for this type of phenomenon. However, as it was not possible to correlate this event with storm activity, GEIPAN does not have concrete elements to support the hypothesis of globular lightning phenomenon. The strangeness is strong, but the consistency of the case is average (one witness, no photo, no precise position of PAN). GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" due to lack of reliable information.
