--- seg-70a1b90a87eda422ce90214c [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 13, 2014, at 8:50 PM, a witness looking towards the northwest observed for 10 seconds the movements of an orange light coming from his right (see his diagram), which made a 90-degree turn to the east, then stabilized before ascending vertically and disappearing. No noise or trail was noticed. The witness reported a wind from south-southwest at 10-20 km/h. The Marignane weather report indicates a light south-southeast wind. The Flightradar site does not mention any commercial aircraft passing north of Pertuis, corresponding to the beginning of the witness's observation. Due to the orange light possibly moving with the wind (if we assume a southwest wind), one hypothesis considered was that of a Thai lantern in the final stages of combustion: the first change in direction could correspond to a decrease in burner power, causing the lantern to descend, the perceived distance thereafter being actually the flame's extinction, interpreted as the distance from a supposed constant light source. Given the uncertainty about the wind direction (variable wind, inconsistent Marignane weather report with the witness's indications, proximity to the Luberon mountain relief), the observation day (Monday) being unusual for lantern releases, and the complex final trajectory, this hypothesis is too fragile to be retained. Without photos or videos, or another testimony, the GEIPAN lacks elements to continue the investigation. This observation case is classified "C" due to lack of information.
