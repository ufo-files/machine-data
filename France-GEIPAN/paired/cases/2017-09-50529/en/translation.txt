Description: On September 02, 2017, at 10:30 PM, a witness observed for 1 to 2 minutes the silent passage over Ploemeur and Lorient of around twenty lights with a pale white color. The witness calls a neighbor using his mobile phone. The lights are quickly lost from view as they are hidden by trees in a forest near the witness's home. One single questionnaire is collected. The witness very likely saw a flight of Thai lanterns, every part of his testimony reflects the multitude of reports recorded at GEIPAN for alleged and verified lantern releases: - the witness says "They were silent." A light flickered and illuminated under each device. "On the other hand, no navigation lights or strobe lights were visible, which is indeed the typical aspect of a lantern; - the whole object was moving in the direction of the wind, which is well from SSO to NNE, as measured at 4km away (Infoclimat); - it was a clear night on a weekend, conducive to festive events." The witness refutes this explanation of the GEIPAN because: - it's too big: it's common for a witness to misjudge the distance of an unknown object, and by doing so, they misestimate the size of the object, as well as its speed. He was closer than he thinks; - there was no wind: the witness is at the door of his house, which is not an ideal place to assess the wind, and even less so for a wind at 100 or 200 meters altitude (that of the lanterns); - the witness is greatly impressed and seems not to believe in such a simple explanation. It is common for witnesses to be impressed, see for example this testimony ( CHATEAUFORT (78) 10.07.2011 ). It's also because many know how to recognize lanterns that no other witness came forward that night. Our witness thinks the opposite and states that lanterns would have definitely triggered other testimonies. The witness "lived a unique moment," that much is clear, but the most probable explanation is that it was lanterns. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
