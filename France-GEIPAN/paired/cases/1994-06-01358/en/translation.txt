Summary: Nighttime observation by a driver of a PAN with multicolored flashing lights: probable observation of an aeronautic scale model.
--- seg-769051554fea40d9b1607f91 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case is part of a set of cases recently reviewed. On June 11, 1994, around 11:45 PM, a driver on a rural road observed the presence on the right side of the road of an object moving parallel to her at very low altitude. The witness describes a PAN "in the shape of a lemon" with a "cable" on top and dotted with multiple flashing multicolored lights. The witness loses sight of the PAN before following its further movements. The duration of observation is not specified by the witness who witnesses its rapid disappearance. She will try to see him again, but in vain. Only one testimony will be collected. This observation case, which translates to a strong apparent oddity (initial classification as D), turns out to be a probable confusion with a scale model (plane or helicopter), piloted from the nearby flight club. This conclusion is based on the following elements (see the investigation report):

- The direction of observation was primarily towards the overflight zone of an aeroclub located nearby;
- The altitude at which the PAN was observed (very low) is consistent with the altitude limitations inherent in the use of remote-controlled models;
- The presence of flashing lights, like those of aircraft collision lights. These lights have colors that are, for the most part, consistent with those used in air navigation; - very small angular dimensions, preventing the distinction of details. These small angular dimensions, coupled with the observation conditions, at night and from a moving vehicle with closed windows, did not allow the witness to identify the shape of the miniature model as such. The last one was only visible because of the lights it had; - moreover, the witness could not hear any noise, as he was driving with closed windows during the observation. In addition, the noise power emitted by the engine of a remote-controlled model is not the same as that of a full-size aircraft. Residual oddities could find a satisfactory explanation in light of the hypothesis: - the "cable" observed by the witness could be part of the structure of the scale model (e.g., the drone or tail of the craft) brightly lit by the lights; - the "doubling" of PAN is caused by a brief change in the model's orientation relative to the witness, revealing more clearly a constituent part, likely the fuselage, less lit than the rest. It may have also been briefly obscured by a vegetation element (branch, etc.); - the trajectories of the PAN, which the witness describes as "moving at the same speed" as him and circumventing obstacles. At night and without well-defined references, it is impossible to affirm with certainty what trajectory the PAN is following. In addition to the illusion of the "following ball" giving the witness the impression that the PAN is moving "in sync" with their vehicle, a variation in brightness or speed inherent to the model could give them the impression of a distance increase or a "deep" change in trajectory, even though nothing ultimately demonstrates this. On the other hand, the witness has no tangible evidence to assess the distance at which the PAN is located. It could be relatively close or farther away. As a result, associating its trajectory and variations with the nearby landscape makes no sense. "Straight-angle" or "perpendicular" trajectories are not surprising for small models, capable of performing acrobatic maneuvers quickly in all directions and on a very low radius of action. - The disappearance of PAN likely occurred suddenly, as PAN was obscured by the dense vegetation surrounding the aeroclub site. The sudden and instantaneous disappearance of a glowing object, observed at night, can give the impression of a "lightning departure." One might be surprised by the use of a night-time model, but one must remember that we are in 1994 and the regulations regarding the nighttime use of these devices were not as restrictive as they are today with the massive arrival on the market of drones. Moreover, this practice at night may have remained rare enough to be unknown to the gendarmes or they may have dismissed the hypothesis as not visually compatible. The consistency is average with a single witness, no photos or videos. The testimony is imprecise, in particular regarding angular data (positions as well as sizes), but the Gendarmerie investigation (on-site photographs) was able to reproduce the positions quite precisely. In the end, the consistency is sufficient to support the hypothesis despite its residual uncertainties. Note: other hypotheses were explored and rejected: plane, mast of a boat on the Loire. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of an aeronautic model (small aircraft).
