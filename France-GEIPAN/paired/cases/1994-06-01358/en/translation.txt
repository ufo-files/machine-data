**Description:** The GEIPAN (Group for the Investigation of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This previously classified D case is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases.

On June 11, 1994, around 11:45 PM, a driver on a rural road observed an object on the right side of the road moving parallel to her at very low altitude. The witness describes the object as a "citron-shaped" UFO with a "cable" on top and adorned with multiple flashing multicolored lights. The witness loses sight of the UFO before following its movements again. The observation duration is unclear as the witness only mentions its rapid disappearance. She attempted to see it again but was unsuccessful. Only one testimony was collected.

This observation case, initially classified as D due to its apparent strangeness, is found to be likely a confusion with a scale model (airplane or helicopter) operated from a nearby airfield. This conclusion is based on the following elements (refer to the investigation report):

- The direction of observation was primarily towards the overflight zone of a nearby airfield.
- The altitude at which the UFO was observed (very low) aligns with the altitude limitations inherent to using scale models.
- Flashing lights, similar to anti-collision lights on aircraft. The colors of these lights correspond to those used in aerial navigation for the most part.
- Very small angular dimensions, preventing the identification of details. These small dimensions, coupled with the observation conditions (nighttime and from a moving vehicle with closed windows), did not allow the witness to recognize the model's shape. It was only visible due to the lights it possessed.
- Additionally, the witness could not hear any noise as they were driving with closed windows. Moreover, the noise power emitted by a scale model's engine is not comparable to that of a full-size aircraft.

Residual oddities found an acceptable explanation:

- The "cable" observed by the witness could be part of the scale model's structure (e.g., stabilizer or tail) brightly lit by the lights.
- The "doubling" of the UFO is caused by a brief orientation change of the model relative to the witness, revealing a constituent part, probably the fuselage, less lit than the rest. It might have also been briefly obscured by a vegetation element (branch, etc.).
- The UFO's trajectories, described as "moving at the same speed" as the witness and circumventing obstacles, are difficult to confirm due to the lack of defined references at night.
- Beyond the illusion of a "following ball," a change in brightness or speed inherent to the model could give the witness the impression of a distance increase or a "depth" change in trajectory, despite nothing ultimately proving this.
- The witness lacks tangible elements to estimate the UFO's distance. It could be relatively close or farther away. Consequently, associating its trajectory and variations with the nearby landscape is meaningless.
- Straight or perpendicular trajectories are not unusual for scale models, capable of performing acrobatic maneuvers quickly and on a very small radius of action.
- The UFO's sudden disappearance is likely due to it being obscured by dense vegetation near the airfield. The sudden and instantaneous disappearance of a glowing object observed at night can create the impression of a "lightning departure." One might question the use of a scale model at night, but it's important to remember that it's 1994, and regulations regarding the nighttime use of such devices were not as restrictive as they are today with the widespread market presence of drones.

The investigation's consistency is average with a single witness, no photos or videos. The testimony is imprecise, especially regarding angular data (positions and sizes), but the Gendarmerie's on-site photography could accurately restore the positions. Ultimately, the consistency is sufficient to support the hypothesis despite residual uncertainties.

**Note:** Other hypotheses were explored and rejected: an airplane or a mast of a boat on the Loire River. Therefore, GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of an aeronautic scale model.
