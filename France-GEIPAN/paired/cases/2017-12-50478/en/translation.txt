Summary: Observation of two bright points passing through the sky, maintaining a consistent distance between them, without flickering: likely observation of two satellites following the same trajectory.
--- seg-956d8af75bd88852663ef03e [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Thursday, December 28, around 05:00 AM, a witness observed for thirty seconds the north-south passage of two bright points in a clear sky. No unusual noises were heard. The PAN disappeared behind the horizon. Only one testimony was collected. A pair of satellites (two NOSS satellites orbiting together) at the end of their visibility followed roughly the same trajectory as the PAN at the time (approximate time given by the witness) of the observation (see the investigation report). According to Calsky data, their magnitude is low enough to be seen with the naked eye, lower than SPICA (as indicated by the witness), but still within the visible range (mag 3). The witness's estimated observation duration (between 30 seconds and 1 minute) is not very precise to confirm a satellite passage. However, given that the witness did not have a watch, he may have underestimated the observation duration. This imprecision does not allow us to be certain of the satellite hypothesis, but the probability is high considering the peculiarity of a pair of satellites. This case should be classified as "B" for a probable observation of two satellites following the same trajectory.
