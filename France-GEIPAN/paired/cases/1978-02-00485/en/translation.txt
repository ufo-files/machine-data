Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type 'D' cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case previously named MONT LOUIS (66) 1978 is part of the recently revisited Type 'D' cases. On February 26, 1978, a witness reported to the gendarmerie an observation of a PAN made on February 16 at 6:40 a.m. for one minute. While driving his truck on RN 116 in the commune of SAUTO (66), the witness saw, in front of him, above the Tet valley, a luminous phenomenon that he could not explain. He stopped and got out of his vehicle. At first, the PAN was stationary above the valley, horizontal to the witness's line of sight, before departing at very high speed towards North-North-East without making a sound, passing directly over the witness. The PAN was then accompanied by a glowing trail, comparable to that of fireworks. A second PAN, oval-shaped and dark matte in color, followed the first PAN. The on-site investigation by the gendarmerie did not yield any other witnesses. No anomalies were detected on the EDF power grid. No traces on the ground or on vegetation in the surrounding area were found. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, trajectory) with a rare astronautical phenomenon: a satellite re-entry. Moreover, there was indeed a satellite re-entry on the trajectory and at the indicated time, on February 15, 1978: that of Cosmos 250 (see investigation notes). The weather data for February 15, 1978, are consistent with those of the witness, whereas those for February 16 are not, which supports the date error. The initial position of the PAN is indicated in the gendarmerie report below the horizon, with a mention "according to the witness's statements." This would not be compatible with an atmospheric re-entry. Except that the witness does not directly state that the PAN was below the horizon but seems to infer it ("at the horizontal of my gaze, therefore below the peaks of the surrounding mountains") by remembering that it was horizontal to his gaze, which is a vague notion. The perfect consistency between the trajectory of the PAN, its timing, and that of the Cosmos 250 atmospheric re-entry on February 15, 1978, leaves no doubt about the misidentification. This case is moderately consistent (one witness), and the description of the PAN observed is moderately precise. The witness's sincerity and credibility have never been questioned. However, due to his late testimony, he made a date error in his observation and misremembered the initial position of the PAN relative to the horizon, which is perfectly understandable. In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of low strangeness is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misidentification with an artificial atmospheric re-entry, involving the satellite Cosmos 250.
