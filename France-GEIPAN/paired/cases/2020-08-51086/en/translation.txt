Summary: Observation of silent movements of a PAN disk-shaped object with luminous reflections: observation of a Mylar balloon.
--- seg-b45beae4596ea7f67ed37e98 [needs-review] ---
Description: On August 5, 2020, around 7 PM, four hikers were exploring Mont Mézenc (07) when they suddenly spotted a luminous object floating in the air, appearing to rotate on its own axis. The witnesses described the PAN as "a large white silver moon" or "a large bubble." Only one witness reported the observation. He instinctively took photographs throughout the silent movements of the PAN across the landscape and before it disappeared into the sky. The witness even captured a short video showing the PAN moving towards SSE. The consistency of the report is strong thanks to the photos and the short video provided. Without this information, the photos and the help of the short video, it would have been difficult to conclude on this case.

The examination of the case and photos (see the investigation report) led the GEIPAN to conclude that the object was a fantasy Mylar balloon (possibly in the shape of a letter (Q or O) or a number (0), released or lost not far from the witnesses. This conclusion is based on:

1. The appearance of the PAN, whose letter shape is visible in one of the photographs. Additionally, the aluminum-coated envelope of this type of balloon reflects sunlight intensely at times, creating characteristic light flashes that were clearly observed by the witnesses with the naked eye and are visible in the photographs.

2. The measured and calculated data using the IPACO software confirmed the possibility that the object was a balloon 0.80 or 1 meter in size, located respectively at about 215 to 320 meters from the witnesses during the first part of the observation, and at about 1870 to 2300 meters during the second part, within the limits of theoretical visual acuity.

3. The movement of the PAN, initially slow and uncertain (rotations, changes in trajectory...), affected by topography due to its low altitude, and then, in a second phase, with a more regular and straight-line movement, caught up in the general wind flow oriented south.

GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of a Mylar balloon.
