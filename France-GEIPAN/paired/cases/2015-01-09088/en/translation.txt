Description: On January 4, 2015, at 7:45 PM, a witness, a passenger on a regular flight, observed a luminous phenomenon moving on the ground at the same speed as the plane. Other passengers, approached, also observed the phenomenon. At the time of the observation, the plane was just before the outskirts of Paris. The witness specified over the phone that the phenomenon was to the left of the plane, at an angle estimated between 50° and 70° from the vertical of the plane. Relative to the plane, the phenomenon was either in front of or behind, sometimes requiring the witness to turn their head back. Note that the full Moon, at 99.6% full, is located due East at 90.8° at an elevation of 23.26°: it is in the witness's field of vision. However, they did not mention it, probably because their gaze was focused downwards. As the phenomenon appears to follow the plane, the hypothesis of an optical phenomenon with a star is immediately favored. The reflection (called specular) of the Moon on lakes and flooded areas is very likely the origin of the phenomenon, highlighting wet or flooded areas against the black of the ground. The filaments described by the witness are probably flooded paths or small canals. Weather reports indicate rain in northern France on February 2 and 3. Note that the angle of 50° to 70° given by the witness is compatible with this hypothesis: it should ideally be the complement of 23°, that is, 67°. The variability of the phenomenon from front to back comes from the plane's change in course: the phenomenon always appears due East; if the plane flies directly south, the witness sees it directly to the left of the plane; if the plane flies southeast, the phenomenon then appears at the front left of the plane. The GEIPAN does not have the necessary information to exactly correlate the plane's route with the phenomenon's apparent position. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": very probable observation of Moon reflections on water bodies and flooded areas.
