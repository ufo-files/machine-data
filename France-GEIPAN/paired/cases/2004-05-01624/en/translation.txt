Description: On May 14, 2004, at 21:44, a witness, an amateur astronomer, observed for several minutes the slow southerly-to-north-eastern (SO-NE) passage of a very bright point in the sky. The object made a second pass on a different trajectory but still heading north-east at 23:30. Intrigued by this phenomenon that he cannot explain, the amateur astronomer was the only witness interviewed by the gendarmerie. This case, formerly named CERET (60), will be classified C by the SEPRA due to the lack of additional testimony.

Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, a re-examination of this case was possible (see the "Investigation Notes" document). The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, movement...) with a well-known astronautic object: the International Space Station (ISS), which was indeed present on the trajectory indicated and at the times provided by the witness. The issue is not the witness's visual perception but the interpretation they make of their observation, due to a preconceived notion that satellites cannot be visible at the time of their observation.

In the current classification of GEIPAN, this case of low strangeness is classified as PAN A, observation of ISS passes.
