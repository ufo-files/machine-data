Summary: Brief observation of the swift and silent passage of a very bright point through the Toulouse sky: likely observation of a Thai lantern.
--- seg-21e10e40b5f39ba8f5b8d0a3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: At 1:00 AM on Sunday, March 11, 2020, a driver was surprised to see in the Toulouse sky the rapid movement of a very bright point. No particular noise was heard during the movement. The witness observed several successive extinguishments, then after 6 seconds of observation, the PAN went out completely. No other testimonies were collected. Of average strangeness and good consistency (single witness, but quite precise testimony), this case appears to be a probable misperception with a Thai lantern (see the investigation report). The description of the PAN strongly suggests this explanatory hypothesis: the PAN is bright and yellow, and moves in the direction of the wind, at least during the final part of the observation. This observation took place during the night from Saturday to Sunday, a traditional time for this type of launch. The change in the PAN's trajectory can be explained by fluctuations in the wind at altitude. Similarly, the intermittent extinguishments of the PAN during the final part of the observation are typical of the extinction of a Thai lantern burner. However, a release in a city is uncommon, but the site was near the banks of the Garonne, which could be a favorable location for a lantern release. The witness's limited field of vision (urban environment, observation from inside a car) explains why the observation only lasted a few seconds. The case is classified as B, probable misperception with a Thai lantern.
