**Description:** On September 23, 2024, in Banne (Ardèche), a witness (T1), on vacation with his wife (T2), parks his campervan with five others behind the village church. At 10:05 PM, T1 steps outside to check his vehicle's remote and spots a bright glowing point in the northeast direction. This point wavers and changes color, shifting from a yellowish-white to red. After alerting his wife, he returns to his campervan to retrieve his smartphone. He films a first video lasting 1 minute and 14 seconds at 10:08 PM, followed by three burst photos taken at 10:09 PM. The observation continues intermittently. At 10:12 PM, T1 steps outside again to record a second video lasting 1 minute and 54 seconds. Finally, at 10:55 PM, he takes six new photos. The phenomenon remains visible throughout these observations. After that, T1 goes to bed. The case exhibits good consistency* despite the absence of T2's testimony, and through the use of photos and videos, which were exploitable for studying the file. According to GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Only one hypothesis was explored: the observation of first-magnitude star Capella. This hypothesis is based on the following:

- A geographical reconstruction using the data provided by T1 placed the PAN at an azimuth and elevation very close to Capella's position at the time of the observation. Indeed, using the Stellarium software, the position of star Capella was accurately determined: an azimuth of 32° and an elevation of 10° (see map_astro_1). For the PAN, the information transmitted by T1 through the QT is: direction northeast, elevation of 45°. This direction, consistent with Capella's, is confirmed on the reconstruction photograph (see map_geo_1), without being able to provide an exact value due to the approximation regarding T1's position. The elevation difference (around 35°) can be explained by a common overestimation by PAN witnesses.

- Strong twinkling caused by strong atmospheric perturbations generating the twinkling of the star low on the horizon and rapid color changes.

- Long observation duration, typical of an astronomical phenomenon.

- Completely overcast sky with no visible stars, but the sky could be partially clear very far below the horizon, making a first-magnitude star visible.

The oddity arises from the impression that the PAN is moving (in the videos) while "it's not moving," remaining in the same location for a long period. In reality, at a very high zoom, the slightest movement of the hand holding the smartphone, imperceptible to the witness, will be visible in the video, especially since no reference points (landmarks) are visible on the videos, as T1 himself indicates, which would have allowed him to realize that the PAN is not actually moving. However, there is uncertainty regarding possible visible movements with the naked eye, which is not clear in the testimony but could be explained by micro-ocular mobility. Note that this case should be compared to that of SAINT-URCIZE (15) 20.09.2024, reported by the same witness. GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the Capella star.
