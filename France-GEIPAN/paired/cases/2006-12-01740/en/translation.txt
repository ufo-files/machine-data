Summary: Observations of the slow and silent low-altitude passage of 7 dark objects maintaining a constant course: likely observations of a cluster of balloons moving with the wind.
--- seg-ca328665fa4a8a1fbc879d51 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named LE HAVRE (76) 2006 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On December 15, 2006, in the afternoon, several witnesses in the middle of the city observed the slow and silent passage of 7 brown objects at low altitude in the sky. The objects are described as "hockey pucks" and maintain a constant heading during their movement. The hypothesis of passive objects carried by the wind, such as balloons, is plausible and the most probable explanation for the nature of the PANs observed by the witnesses (see the investigation report). The testimonial data collected by the gendarmes, as well as their subsequent calculations, such as the direction of movement and the speed of the PANs, and their constant heading, are consistent with the measured wind direction. The color and especially the shape (as described by T1 and T2, in the form of "hockey puck") are not common for balloons, but this cannot invalidate the hypothesis. There are many balloon models with very diverse shapes and colors. It should be noted that these PANs are seen during the day, from an urban area, at low altitude in the sky (T2 indicates that they passed behind buildings), and for at least 10 minutes. It is very likely that these PANs were seen by other people without being perceived as strange, which would further support the balloon hypothesis. The consistency is correct, with witnesses providing fairly detailed accounts of their observations and gendarmes conducting the recording and situation map. One could, however, regret the absence of angular measurements of the sizes of the PANs and their movement, which would have facilitated the calculations of possible dimensions, distances, and speeds. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a group of balloons moving in the wind.
