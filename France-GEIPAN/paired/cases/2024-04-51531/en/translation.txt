Description: On April 6, 2024, around 9:14 PM, the witness was walking through the streets of Valenciennes when they observed two "round shapes" of orange color at a moderate altitude coming from the south. These objects moved silently and at high speed towards the witness. The latter reported the presence of a dark halo around the first object. Subsequently, the two PAN seemed to gradually disintegrate as they headed north, giving the impression that they were "consuming" themselves due to a lack of "fuel." Only one testimony was collected on this matter. The consistency* of this case is considered satisfactory, given the quality of the testimony, the detailed information provided by the witness, and a video submitted as a supplement. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The hypothesis adopted (see the investigation report) is that of the observation of two celestial lanterns; it is based on the following elements:

- Round shape and orange color, typical of lanterns
- Compatibility of the wind with the apparent movement of PAN
- Synchronized movement without noise
- Possible observation of a halo, corresponding to the lantern's envelope
- Observation duration of two minutes, compatible with the average lifespan of lanterns (approximately 6 to 8 minutes)
- Observation of the extinction, as the lanterns ran out of fuel. This final phase is described perfectly by the witness.
- The observation was made on a Friday evening: lanterns are most often released on weekends for celebrations.

GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Celestial Lanterns.
