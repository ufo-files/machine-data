--- seg-edf0dd2e4797e4a8bbd52edb [needs-review] ---
Description: A witness observes for one minute a half-moon, translucent, white, and glowing phenomenon. The PAN is very high in the clear sky, moving very slowly before "disappearing." The witness has time to photograph the PAN. Only one testimony about this phenomenon is collected. The observation, made and photographed by the witness in their garden between 12:30 and 1:00 PM, is a high-altitude cloud phenomenon (cirrus uncinus). This type of cloud is not commonly seen, which likely led the witness to consider a completely different hypothesis. This analysis is based on the following facts (see the investigation report):

- Bright white color
- Cloud disappearing in the shape of a half-moon or comma
- Slow movement, carried by wind at high altitude

The witness reported high brightness, which was likely caused by the sun ("These high-altitude clouds (between 6 and 12 km) are mainly composed of small, scattered ice crystals: the sun always passes through them. Luminosity phenomena such as halos or coronas can appear on these clouds. See the METEO-FRANCE link). For the witness, the strangeness was the disappearance of the phenomenon, but it is fully explained by a weak/moderate wind generating the comma shape. The consistency is good, even though only one witness observed the PAN, photographs were taken and used for analysis. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": misidentification with cloud observation.
