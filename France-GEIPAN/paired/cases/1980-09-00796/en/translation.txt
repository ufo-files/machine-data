**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to ensure more accurate conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The previously classified D case, named SAINT-ETIENNE (04) 1980, is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases. On August 30th at 1:30 PM, a driver on a tour sees a bright spot in the sky while looking up from his vehicle. He describes a "gigantic" gray-or matte rectangular craft with two rows of black dots on the left side and a yellow and red triangle at the front. Another triangle is reported on the right side. The PAN appears to follow the car during slowdowns, accelerations, and direction changes, and it has eluded the driver's view several times. The 6-year-old daughter of the witness, who was in the car, provides the same description as her father and is the last to see the PAN over the village before it suddenly disappears into the sky "without taking a specific direction."

Based on the available information, this observation is unusual in several ways (see the investigation report): The apparently very large angular size of the PAN described by the witnesses:

- T1: "gigantic," "immense," "it could have been between 1/2 and 1 hectare in size. It might have been 100-150 meters above the ground."
- T2: "large craft" (and a passenger in the back of the car) also draws a picture where the PAN above the car extends beyond the car's roof.

It is highly surprising, if the PAN was as large as described by the witnesses, that no other reports were made to the gendarmes, especially since the observation took place during the day, for an extended period, and on a multi-kilometer trajectory.

With the available data, no known meteorological, astronomical, or aeronautical phenomenon can account for the characteristics of the PAN as described by the witnesses.

In the current methodology of the GEIPAN (since 2008, see the classification methodology on their website), for an unusual observation to be classified as such and as unexplained, it must have strong consistency, meaning high levels of information and reliability.

In terms of information, it is regrettable that the gendarmes did not conduct a more in-depth investigation beyond interrogating the local residents without success to find potential additional witnesses. We would have liked to have had the following information:

- an independent sketch of the PAN by T1;
- photographs of key locations mentioned by T1, as part of an investigation reconstructing the vehicle's trajectory;
- measurements of the angular dimensions of the PAN and its components;
- more details about the disappearances of the PAN: at what exact moment, in what manner (gradual fading, sudden disappearance... rapid departure, etc...);
- the same remarks about the reappearances of the PAN: did the witnesses actually observe a reappearance (in what manner?) or did they simply look at the PAN, which was already present?
- regarding the PAN itself: was it visible against a blue sky background? Against a cloudy background? If so, was it partially or totally obscured by clouds at any point? Did the entire PAN appear "solid" to the witnesses, or were there less perceptible, more blurry parts, or difficulty distinguishing them?

At one point in his narrative, T1 claims the PAN was visible "above the Villermé forest" (on the right side of the road taken by the witnesses). It was no longer above the vehicle at that moment. T2 also briefly spots the PAN "above the village" at the moment when, upon arriving home, T1 parks the vehicle in the garage. It would have been interesting to have more details about the appearance of the PAN, its height on the horizon, its angular dimensions, etc... at these precise moments of the observation.

In terms of reliability, the testimony of a 6-year-old child, without providing decisive information compared to that of her father, does not significantly enhance the reliability of her father's testimony, especially since we do not know the conditions under which the child was interviewed (presence or absence of her father).

Overall, the consistency is insufficient (especially in terms of information) to attribute an unexplained character to this a priori unusual observation. Therefore, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
