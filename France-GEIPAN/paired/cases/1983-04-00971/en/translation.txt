Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more accurate in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named BUZANCY (08) 20.04.1983 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. On April 20, 1983, around 11 PM, a farmer noticed a bright red glow through a window of a stable. Intrigued, he stepped outside and realized the light was coming from a ball in the sky directly above Saint-Pierremont. The witness perceived rapid movements over a short interval and radiation. After a quarter of an hour, the witness called his neighbor and then the Gendarmerie. After an hour, the phenomenon had reduced to a single bright point that suddenly disappeared. Two testimonies were collected. The gendarmes participated in the end of the observation, observing the PAN indicated by the witnesses. There is little doubt that the PAN observed by both the witnesses and the gendarmes is Venus (see investigation notes). The position in the sky matches, and the rest of the description is typical of misidentifications with Venus, as recorded in large numbers by the GEIPAN. The reddish-yellow, very bright color among the white stars is perfectly consistent with the appearance of Venus at a low elevation. The color (generally variable, T1 describes changes) results from the effects of the thick atmospheric layer (low elevation). The perceptions of small, jerky movements or oscillations described by the witnesses and gendarmes could be caused by atmospheric effects and/or a perception error when fixating on a star (see the article on autocinetic effect). The sudden disappearance observed by the gendarmes around midnight could correspond to the beginning of obstruction by trees (visible in the background of the photo taken by the gendarmes to reconstruct the position of the PAN), as Venus had only 2° of elevation. It could also correspond to the presence of some clouds, an assumption that is very plausible given the weather analysis and can explain T1's indication of 3 temporary disappearances of the PAN. Other photos taken by the gendarmes to reconstruct the position of the PAN in previous observation phases do not allow for this reconstruction as they lack landmarks or are not localized (the gendarmerie is not able to provide this location 35 years later). Nevertheless, the general movement of the PAN, as recalled from the testimonies despite errors and inconsistencies in the report, is consistent with that of Venus. Moreover, none of the witnesses differentiate the PAN observed also by the gendarmes at the end of the observation from another PAN that may have been seen earlier, it is the same PAN that was "much larger and closer" (as T1 says) at the beginning of the observation, which is also consistent regarding Venus, higher up in the sky. The testimony is marred by several inconsistencies, some of which could have gone against the Venus conclusion. However, the analysis allows these inconsistencies to be addressed by identifying the sources of error and imprecision of the witnesses or the gendarmes. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Venus observations.
