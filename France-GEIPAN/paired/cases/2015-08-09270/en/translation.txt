Description: On Monday, August 10, 2015, between 1 a.m. and 2 a.m., two individuals witnessed a colorful luminescent phenomenon in the sky that intrigued them. One of the witnesses took photographs. Only one testimony was collected. The long observation (approximately 1 hour) of a slowly moving light point in the sky suggests an astre. It is noted that the star Arcturus was at an azimuth of 283° and an elevation of 13°, exactly in the direction of the witness's observation (intersection N104 / A10 at azimuth 282°, estimated elevation of 15°), thus quite close to the horizon; The witness observed the phenomenon on subsequent days, but sometimes with a different appearance, featuring fewer colors. On the evening of the first observation, disturbed weather was noted with pressure and wind variations (see the Météociel record). Star twinkle is very strong at low elevation, especially if the atmosphere is disturbed (wind, heat), which was the case that evening: a slow multicolored flicker can frequently be observed when viewing stars or planets at low elevation with binoculars. The witness did not mention seeing any star in their field of view, although they should have necessarily seen Arcturus in that direction. The photos do not provide much information due to significant shaking and low luminosity. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the star Arcturus.
