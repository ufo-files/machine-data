Summary: Nighttime observation of a low-flying triangular shape delineated by lights: observation of a helicopter in flight.
--- seg-c2dbc854391657245c41f9ab [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On November 13, 2015, around 10:15 PM, two people in a car were intrigued by the low-altitude passage of a triangular object whose shape was delineated by three non-flashing white round lights. Two red lights were seen at the center of the object. It smoothly moved away without noise into the cloudy sky. The witness, a former military member, does not recognize any airplane or helicopter. Only one testimony was collected. The GEIPAN requested military air traffic control (CNOA) for the recorded air traffic in the area at that time: the CNOA radar recording indicates a confirmed aircraft, on a hospital mission, at low altitude, which perfectly corresponds to the trajectory, location, and passage time described in the testimony. This is illustrated by the superimposition of radar traces on the attached map (see Reconstruction). The low-altitude and irregular trajectory (observed through radar traces and the observation) is characteristic of a helicopter. The radar recording cannot confirm the passage at a stationary flight at such a low altitude (15m) but is compatible with it. It is worth noting that estimating the distance of light points at night is highly subjective. Observing a triangle with continuous lights, one white at each vertex and two red in the center, is compatible with that of a helicopter. One might wonder why the witness did not see the red and green colors of the permanent navigation lights, nor the white flashing lights, but the helicopter about to land likely had its white headlights on, obscuring other lights. Note that an aircraft can use various lights in addition to the regulated ones (navigation, anti-collision, etc.). The Hellenic Navy AB-2012 ASW (night Flight) video shows a case with three red lights, two of which are fixed and the third can be perceived as fixed and comes from the rotating anti-collision red light, and here too, the red and green navigation lights do not appear. Regarding the absence of perceived noise from the object while the radar indicates it is only 200m in altitude, the witness states that the car window was slightly open, but the vehicle's engine was still running, which is not an ideal condition. However, it is common in PAN observations that the focus on a visually "striking" observation masks the accompanying auditory perception. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies this observation as A, perfectly identified: Helicopter.
