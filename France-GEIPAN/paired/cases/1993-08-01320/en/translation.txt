**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the accuracy of conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named VIGY (57) 1993 is part of a recent batch of cases re-examined (as detailed in the investigation report). On August 11, 1993, around 3:30 AM, a witness (T1) driving his car observed a very bright, stationary yellow light above a farm and then the Hayes Castle. The witness described a large, spinning, silent light that was larger than a star. The subsequent description of the object was that of a disk topped with a dome or resembling an "upside-down mushroom." The light intensity gradually dimmed, and the PAN appeared to move away in the direction of Charleville-sous-Bois. Upon returning home, the witness asked his mother (T2) to observe the phenomenon. She climbed a chair and saw a uniform, fixed yellow light in the starry sky before quickly returning to bed. T1 observed the PAN until it disappeared completely. The total observation duration was 20 minutes for T1. Cross-referenced data with the geographical situation suggest a northeast direction, consistent with a confusion with the rising Venus (azimuth 58°):

- T1's first observation ("Le Marais" to "Ferme St Léon") corresponds to an azimuth of 62°.
- The second observation ("Ferme St Léon" to "Château de Hayes") corresponds to an azimuth of 56°.
- T2's sighting was less precise as she indicated the Charleville-sous-Bois forest, with the azimuth of the forest area around Charleville-sous-Bois ranging between 38° and 62°.
- The PAN was low in the sky, similar to Venus.
- T2 observed the PAN above the forest ("I saw a very bright light in the sky just above the Charleville-sous-Bois forest") while climbing a chair, indicating that the PAN and the forest were close enough angularly to clear together over a nearby obstacle due to a small angular field gain (climbing a 50 cm chair gains 0.6° for an obstacle at 50m and 3° for an obstacle at 10m).
- Venus' visibility: T2's description indicates that the sky was clear in the observation direction, and there were no ground obstacles for Venus' visibility despite its low height in the sky (at least 3° at the beginning of the observation).
- Angular stationarity of the PAN: The stationarity noted by both witnesses (and over a 20-minute duration for T1) supports the hypothesis of a star.
- T1's mention of a feeling of movement of the PAN towards Charleville-sous-Bois (along the observation axis) is subjective and related to the light intensity decrease mentioned at the same time.
- The intensity variation results from changes in atmospheric thickness or the occurrence of a cloud veil.
- The witness, not recognizing Venus, logically interprets the intensity decrease as the PAN moving away.
- The disappearance of the PAN, observed only by T1, is perceived as the culmination of the intensity reduction and distance.
- Appearance: The initial description by T1 of a large, bright yellow light ball is a typical confusion with Venus. This celestial body is brighter than stars (high intensity creating a ball-like appearance) and colors are introduced by the high atmospheric thickness, with Venus observed at a low height in the sky (3°).
- The mention of an "elongated shape with a dome in the center" or a "upside-down mushroom" is harder to explain, but given Venus' low position on the horizon (3°), it's possible that atmospheric turbulences distorted its image.
- A cloud veil could also be responsible for diffusing Venus' light. Indeed, for T1, this shape (different from the initial ball) was only distinguishable later, and for T2, who only observed briefly later, the light was inside the PAN ("This light was yellow like the color of car headlights. It was uniform. What I saw was an illuminated object").

The above elements show a very strong coincidence with a Venus observation. Only the second part of the observation's shape description is slightly more singular compared to the many misidentifications recorded with Venus at the GEIPAN. But it's not unique, it can be explained, and thus does not challenge the highly probable nature of the Venus hypothesis. Consistency is average, both witnesses filed reports at the Gendarmerie, but the testimonies lack direct and precise evaluations (elevation, azimuth, etc.). There is some inconsistency in T2's behavior, who notes the strangeness ("This is the first time I observe similar phenomena"), especially after a very precise shape description, but then stops the observation (after 5 or 7 minutes) before the PAN disappears. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: highly probable Venus observations.
