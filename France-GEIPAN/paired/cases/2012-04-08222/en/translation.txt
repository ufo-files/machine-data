Description: On Wednesday, April 4, 2012, at 8:15 PM, a witness is intrigued by the movements in the sky of two bright yellow or white lights that traverse the sky from east to north at a high speed. The first light follows a straight and uniform trajectory, but it's the second that captivates the witness' attention as it constantly moves around the first, tracing ellipses, making sudden sharp turns, coming into contact, then quickly returning to a position behind the first. The observation ends after about fifteen seconds when both lights disappear through or behind a cloud. After a field investigation that clarified the azimuths and a cognitive interview that didn't yield any new significant information, this observation case remains of very high strangeness given the second PAN's movements around the first; this observation case defies usual misperception hypotheses (see the investigation report). Due to the uniqueness and brevity of the testimony, the hypothesis of a temporary visual illusion (ophthalmic migraine, phosphenes) remains, but the witness appears to have perfect vision and hasn't reported experiencing similar effects: without supporting elements, this hypothesis was rejected. The case consistency is rated at 0.7 as the witness is unique and without reservation. The case strangeness is rated at 0.6 due to the object's non-linear movement but at a distance. Therefore, this observation case is classified as "D1" - unexplained.
