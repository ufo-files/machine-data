Description: Around 9:20 PM, a pedestrian's gaze is drawn to a reddish-orange object that appears to be on fire in the sky. The witness takes their car and finds themselves under the object, photographing it. The object deviates from its trajectory and disappears from the witness's view. The Calsky log does not mention a very bright satellite at that time. There was a re-entry of the TIROS2 satellite into the atmosphere that day, but it occurred in the early part of the day, which does not correspond to the observation at all. The witness's description of "a flame in the sky" suggests a Thai lantern flying towards the southwest. The Cannes Meteorology log indicates a light wind varying from the north and shifting to the west between 9:00 PM and 10:00 PM. The direction of the object's movement could be consistent with the wind direction. The photos taken accurately reflect the typical appearance of Thai lantern photos. The time and location (a Sunday evening during the Cannes Festival) are quite conducive to various festive displays. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": observation of a Thai lantern. The witness refutes this hypothesis.
