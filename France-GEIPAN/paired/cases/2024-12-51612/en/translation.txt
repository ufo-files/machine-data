Description: Both witnesses of this case observed, from their terrace, a luminous phenomenon in the sky of Pleudihen-sur-Rance (22): a large incandescent sphere, orange-yellow in color, accompanied by a trail, probably yellow. The object follows a curved trajectory from west to east, reaching approximately 75° elevation, then disappears behind trees after five seconds of observation. No noise is perceived. Its apparent size is estimated to be that of a ping-pong ball held at arm's length. The witnesses speculate that it could be a falling meteorite or a burning object entering the atmosphere: "a meteorite falling to Earth or a burning flying object falling to Earth." This observation case has an average consistency (only T1 completed a Technical Questionnaire GEIPAN, no photo or video of PAN) and a low strangeness level. According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The observation turns out to be that of an atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The FIREBALL IMO (International Meteor Organization) site, which catalogs natural and artificial atmospheric reentries, recorded at the time of the PAN observation, a total of 73 testimonies from witnesses located in a vast geographical area including southern England, as well as central-western and western France, thus covering the region where our witnesses are located. See the event on the IMO site. Notably, one of these 73 witnesses is from the same commune as the observation of PAN (annex 1).

Although some witnesses report divergent visual characteristics, particularly regarding color, the majority describe an object corresponding to the characteristics of the Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon (UAP), namely an exceptionally bright object, with a color close to yellow, leaving a trail, all observed over a short period.

- The estimated orientation of the trajectory varies among witnesses, but it is generally consistent for some of them, who mention an east-west oriented trajectory. The automatically calculated trajectory by the IMO site (from southwest to northeast) is also generally consistent with that of PAN (annex 2). Given the very short duration of visibility of this atmospheric reentry, it is likely that it is a natural object of bolide type rather than an artificial object. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the atmospheric reentry of a bolide.
