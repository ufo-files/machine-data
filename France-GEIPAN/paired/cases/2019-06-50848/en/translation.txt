Summary: Filmed observation of the erratic movement of a dazzling light phenomenon in the sky: Observation of a ball lightning phenomenon, category globular lightning.

Description: On June 18, 2019, at approximately 11:00 PM, a witness, while closing their shutters, noticed an intense light moving in an unusual manner across the landscape. The witness described a glowing (or circular) red light that flickered, moving back and forth and seemingly jumping from one location to another in a vast expanse of the sky. To get a better view, the witness stepped outside onto their door step; the circle disappeared, leaving a very bright yellow line that then faded away. The witness went to retrieve their mobile phone in case the phenomenon reappeared. The witness continued observing the sky, and at 11:10 PM, a glowing (or circular) light appeared, its movement equally strange, identical to the previous one. This time, the witness filmed the phenomenon. At the bottom of this circle, there was another glowing (or circular) light with identical movement. Then, the glowing points disappeared without much explanation. The witness waited again, and after 20 to 30 minutes, another phenomenon of the same type appeared. It was 11:40 PM, and two glowing points, sometimes yellow and sometimes red, moved in erratic motions. The two points, which seemed to grow, approached each other and disappeared instantly. The single witness made two videos. The last image of video 20190618_232823.mp4 corresponds to a colored party ball of light. The witness's description of the observation corresponds to a case of ball lightning of the "globular lightning" type. This hypothesis is consistent with the visual appearance, behavior, apparent trajectory, and altitude. The testimony was submitted to the Lightning Research Laboratory, which unambiguously recognized a ball lightning phenomenon, category globular lightning. The geographical area was exposed at the time of the observation to a powerful multicellular thunderstorm very active with no less than 123 impacts within a 10,000-meter radius around the observation point (see attached Lab Station map) between 9:00 PM and 1:00 AM. The video, accessible at the following link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3k-y88zh5fs&list=PL8WQV0xeqD2bapVOfx2ooBoVOETayZTNr, provides additional information on the ball lightning phenomenon. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: observation of a ball lightning phenomenon, category globular lightning.
