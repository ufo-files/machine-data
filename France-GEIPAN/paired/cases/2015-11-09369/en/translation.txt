Description: On Sunday, November 29, 2015 (not November 30 as indicated in the protocol) a driver was surprised to see four orange lights appear successively in the night sky. All of them move slowly on a straight trajectory without any noise. The witness films the scene with his mobile phone. Only one testimony is collected. All elements and what can be reconstructed from them are consistent with an observation of lantern releases (see the investigation report): - the movement is consistent with the direction of the wind; - the appearance, color, and brightness of the PAN are consistent; - the passage of multiple PAN one after the other is typical; - it is a weekend day conducive to a festive event. The only doubt would pertain to the first point, as the witness provided no specifics regarding the direction of movement of the PAN and their perception of the wind (were they sheltered or not?). But the analysis of the video indicates a probable direction of movement (from South to North) consistent with the direction of the wind measured at nearby stations at 15 to 20 km. Which, given the other points, allows the hypothesis to be maintained as probable. The case consistency is poor due to the lack of information provided by the witness. Fortunately, the video, despite its poor quality, allows for a probable sense of movement to be given. As a result, the GEIPAN is classified as B: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
