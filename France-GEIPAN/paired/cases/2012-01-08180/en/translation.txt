Summary: Observation of a very bright phenomenon at low altitude that seems to follow the witness during their car movements: probable observation of the planet Venus and the star Sirius
--- seg-0944ab03981707a184e37031 [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 24, 2012, around 7:45 PM, a driver traveling on a rural road noticed a very bright, stationary, white cylindrical object at low altitude. The witness stopped their vehicle and waved. No noise was heard. The driver continued on their way and realized the object seemed to be following them. Upon returning to their village, the driver went into their garden and noticed the lights had turned orange-red. They then told their wife about the observation and called the gendarmes. The gendarmerie present that evening found no lights, and no other witnesses were found through a neighborhood inquiry. The observation of a low-lying bright point that seemed to follow the witness and stop when they stopped suggests an astronomical body, with a constant observation direction (azimuth). In the direction indicated by the witness at the beginning of the observation (around 250°, south-west) (see the map, the two arrows on the right) is indeed the very bright planet Venus (magnitude -3.5) at low elevation (10°) (see Stellarium map). In the second part of the observation (see the map, the arrow on the left), the witness looked towards the south-east: it was another bright star clearly visible: Sirius, the brightest star in the sky (magnitude -1.45) but less bright than Venus, naturally appearing farther away to the witness. Given the uncertainty about the observation directions, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of Venus and Sirius.
