Description: While walking his dog in the commune of HEM (59) around 9 PM, a witness observed three reddish-brown rings arranged in a triangle slowly moving on a straight trajectory from northwest to east. The witness barely distinguishes a dark structure between the rings. After an observation of about ten seconds, the PAN disappears on the spot, to the east-southeast, as if it were turning off. The testimony was collected one year after the observation and represents the only available statement regarding this case. The consistency* of this case is average, with one witness and no photos or videos of the PAN. *According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The level of strangeness perceived by the witness is high, as he/she was mesmerized by the observation (triangular shape, dark mass, size, presence of torus-shaped lights, and absence of noise). A field investigation, conducted by two GEIPAN investigators, complemented the initial remote investigation, including an interview and a recreation of the observation. This approach took place approximately one and a half years after the observation (see the investigation report). The investigation explored several hypotheses:

- The lantern hypothesis was ruled out due to the PAN's movement, which did not align with the wind direction.

- The aircraft hypothesis was not pursued due to a lack of confirming radar tracks. It should be noted that these two hypotheses do not explain the red-tore shaped lights described following the observation. - The hypothesis of one or several drones equipped with custom lights accounts for some of the observed characteristics: - Straight trajectory. - Estimated speed for a distance from the witness less than or equal to 100m. - It is possible to customize one or several drones by equipping them with specific lights, such as LED strips. However, we have not found an example of a drone equipped with three "toric" lights as described by the witness. The following elements, however, are either weak or incompatible with this hypothesis: - Distance estimates based on the actual sizes of various consumer drone models lead to entirely incompatible results, unless one considers an exceptionally large drone or if the apparent size of PAN was significantly overestimated by the witness. However, it should be noted that the estimates provided by the witness in the questionnaire and during the on-site investigation, one and a half years later, show only minor discrepancies. - The area is prohibited for drone overflight and night flying, although this does not rule out that a careless user may have violated this. - No noise was heard in a very quiet environment, with a carrying wind at the end of the observation and a moderate distance for a standard drone size. The hypothesis of a military drone was considered, but no definitive conclusion could be drawn regarding an anomalous craft. No concrete element allows this hypothesis to be confirmed. Although the testimony was collected one year after the observation and uncertainties remain regarding a possible overestimation of the size of PAN, no hypothesis allows for a conclusive identification of the observed phenomenon. The case is classified as "D1", unidentified phenomenon after investigation.
