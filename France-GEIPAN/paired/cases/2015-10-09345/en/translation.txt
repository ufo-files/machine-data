Summary: Long observation of two bright points in the sky: observation of the conjunction of Venus and Jupiter.
--- seg-ad495ffaa4ba2343f93f00c8 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 28, 2015, at 4:30 a.m., a witness was intrigued by the presence in the southeastern sky of two very bright white points. During an observation lasting approximately thirty minutes, no unusual noises were heard. The two points moved little on a straight upward trajectory. The witness took three photographs. Clouds later obscured the two bright points. No other testimonies were collected. The witness's indications correspond to the configuration of Venus and Jupiter that night, one above the other with a few degrees of separation, at an elevation of around 10° (Venus being brighter below, as shown in the witness's photo and the Stellarium reconstruction). The only discrepancy was in the observation azimuth, but contact with the witness confirmed (based on local geographic references) that he was indeed looking to the east. Similarly, the elevation was estimated at around 30° while the photo shows it at approximately 10°. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of an astromical conjunction (Venus and Jupiter).
