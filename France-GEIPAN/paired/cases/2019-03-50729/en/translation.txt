--- seg-9bb4a8435904827f37bab3a3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On March 16, 2019, at 01:55 (not 03 or 15 March as incorrectly indicated in the questionnaire), a witness in their garden had their attention drawn to the silent passage of 7 points described as "white disks, somewhat blurry" in the sky. This geometric formation crosses the sky from south-east to north-east in 4 to 5 seconds. It is then hidden by the roof of the residence. Only one testimony was collected. Analysis indicates that the witness very likely observed a group of white-plumed birds, probably gulls, moving silently towards a nesting site (see investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The witness resides in a housing estate surrounded by wetlands conducive to the nesting of such birds, which, if not migrating, travel between their feeding grounds along the coast or by rivers and their nesting sites on inland bodies of water.

- The observation period, in spring, is the breeding/nesting season for some species (ringed gulls) but also the spring migration period for others (little gulls).

- Rectilinear, regular movement in a group or formation (gulls are gregarious birds).

- The measurements given by the witness (angular size, observation duration), after a slight adjustment, allowed calculations to be made that yield a speed and dimension consistent with those known of these birds, for a movement at an altitude of 20 to 30 meters.

- The birds suspected of causing the confusion have a portion of their body black (head and wings) at this time of year, which prevents them from being totally visible from the ground, only the ventral side with white plumage being visible, which also contributes to an underestimation of their actual size by the witness and to the unvisibility of their wings and flapping.

- The actual shape of the birds is not visible, several factors, combined or not, preventing the witness from clearly distinguishing them: observation distance, possible fog (not confirmed), diminished visual capacity of the witness...

- The visibility of the white plumage of the birds is primarily due to contrast with the black sky background and thanks to residual luminosity, giving them this pale, whitish color.

The testimony is of good quality (concise, fairly precise, neutral, and written quickly after the observation), but it remains of average consistency as it comes from a single witness without a photo. The observation is ultimately not strange as the PAN have a linear trajectory and an average speed. Their appearance and behavior resemble that of common objects (birds) observed under particular conditions. This case is to be classified as "B", as a probable observation of light-plumed birds moving in formation.
