Summary: Observation of a fixed dark red light in the cloudy sky: probable astronomical misidentification with the Moon.
--- seg-e2fb660bf8bc3506a0f87c50 [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 21, 2013, around 4 a.m., a witness is intrigued by the fixed presence of a dark red light in the cloudy sky. The witness's observation direction (Courcelles) corresponds to an azimuth of 230°. The witness describes a round, red object four times larger than the Moon, at an altitude of 25°, and notes that the sky was cloudy, with no visible stars. It is observed that the Moon is precisely at an azimuth of 231°, at an altitude of 4.6°. It is highly likely that the witness fell victim to the classic illusion of the Moon, which always appears very large at sunrise or sunset. Partially obscured by a cloud veil, the Moon did not reveal its surface details, which would have allowed the witness to identify it. After a midnight awakening, it is common for rare phenomena to greatly surprise witnesses. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable Moon observation (the significant deviation in the phenomenon's altitude in the sky justifies this case not being classified as "A").
