Summary: Observations of a very bright red phenomenon in silent motion on a descending trajectory; lighting up the forest below PAN: likely observations of a distress rocket.
--- seg-09061c915a280402c9ccafcf [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MONTMARAULT (03) 1990 is part of a recent set of cases that have been re-examined (see the investigation report). On April 30, 1990, around 10:20 PM, witnesses in two different locations within the same commune observed the silent movement on a descending trajectory of a very bright red phenomenon. One witness (T1) made the same observation 5 minutes apart. The observation durations differ but are of short duration. T3 reported that the PAN illuminated the forest below. The disappearance of the PAN is described as being hidden by the landscape or gradual. The case description evokes two well-known types of misperceptions, namely an atmospheric re-entry or a rocket fired by a resident. The slow movement and the long duration of the observation are quite typical of an artificial atmospheric re-entry, but none of them correspond to the trajectory of the PAN. Furthermore, the fact that the PAN illuminates the forest below indicates that it was likely quite close to the ground. The description of the PAN resembles many other cases of misperceptions with distress rockets referenced by the GEIPAN (case search with keyword "rocket" on www.geipan.fr, example: MOIRANS-EN-MONTAGNE (39) 26.01.1985). The explanatory hypothesis aligns with the fact that the PAN is seen in a limited geographical area and that the phenomenon repeated itself a second time a few minutes later. A genuine distress case would have left a trace, but most GEIPAN cases involve playful rocket launches (off-use or end-of-validity). The case consistency is good with multiple witnesses and coherent descriptions. The case is classified as B: probable misperception with a distress rocket.
