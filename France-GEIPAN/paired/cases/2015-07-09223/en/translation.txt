Description: On Thursday, July 2, 2015, at 10:30 PM, several people observed the slow, silent north-south movement of a bright, orange-colored ball of light, which disappeared behind the clouds. Only one testimony was collected. The observation of a floating fireball in the sky following a trajectory that seemed random suggests an object aloft carried by the wind, such as a Thai lantern. The Infoclimat network indicates a light west wind at Mas-de-la-Barque, 5 km away. Météo-France reports a weak, unstable wind at Mende, 35 km away. Given that the observation took place in the heart of a relatively narrow valley, coupled with the fact that low-altitude winds in mountainous areas are very complex, it is impossible to determine the actual wind at low altitude. This Thai lantern hypothesis, which remains consistent with the observation, cannot be confirmed. The GEIPAN considered a plasma-type phenomenon: however, there is no thunderstorm context or seismic environment. The GEIPAN classified this case as "C" due to a lack of corroboration.
