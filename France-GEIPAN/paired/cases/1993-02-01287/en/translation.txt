Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type "D" cases and now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. The observation case formerly named LUSIGNAN (86) 1993 is part of cases classified "D" by the SEPRA and recently reassessed. On February 28, 1993, around 5:30 or 5:45 a.m., a driver traveling on D150 between SAINT-SAUVANT (86) and LUSIGNAN (86) observed on her left a very powerful flash of white light and saw an object in the shape of a dome with three glowing rounds. The observation lasted 3 to 4 seconds, the time it took for the witness to continue on her way. The following evening, the witness went to the gendarmerie to report the incident. Little information has been gathered about this PAN. The witness described the PAN's ground elevation as a maximum of 2 meters, estimated from a distance of 300 meters, a distance confirmed by mapping given the forest's position relative to the road (see investigation notes). This is a significant distance for evaluating ground height; moreover, the witness emphasizes the intensity of the lights on the PAN, it is possible, and even probable, that the witness was unable to distinguish whether these lights were attached to the ground. Thus, an important element of strangeness perceived and described by the witness (the PAN out of ground) cannot be validated. Furthermore, the gendarmerie's location of the PAN places it at the level of a drivable earth path. In conclusion, this description indicates with a high probability the presence of one or more high-lit vehicles (agricultural, hunter's 4x4, etc.), even at this early hour. It is not the witness's visual perception that is in question. Note that the witness made the observation under poor conditions, while driving and for part of it through a rearview mirror, that she did not stop to observe more precisely the characteristics of the PAN, and in particular its evolution. These elements attribute low consistency to the testimony, yet still sufficient to validate the above hypothesis. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Ground lights.
