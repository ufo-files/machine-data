Summary: Observations of the presence of orange-colored light points in the night sky followed by a rapid disappearance: probable observations of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-c37cc9ba54b1eb908fe4eaaf [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 26, 2015, at 8:30 PM, while gathering wood behind his home, a witness was intrigued by the silent presence of a fixed orange glowing point. Suddenly, this point took off at high speed towards the South and disappeared behind a hill. Emerging to gather more wood, the witness observed up to four glowing points that disappeared one after the other. The observation, relayed by the witness along with his wife and adult daughter, was accompanied by photos, one of which showed no movement during a 1/4 second exposure. This photo exhibits all characteristics of a Thai lantern observation: an orange glowing point, no flashes, no noise, stationary flight, then disappearance (during a rapid movement) corresponding to the end of the candle. The date, a Saturday evening, the day after Christmas, conducive to family or friend gatherings celebrating Christmas, can also be considered.

Complementary analytical element: The weather conditions provided by the Météociel report for Mende (25 km East) at 8:00 PM indicate an overcast sky 8/8, no precipitation, visibility 20 km, southeast wind below 10 km/h. The possibility of a lantern is supported by:

- The overcast sky: no visible stars, a cloud base estimated by the witness at 1,500 meters: not a commercial airliner, not a satellite, or the International Space Station (ISS).
- A light southeast wind, in the direction of the glowing points: which explains the apparent immobility of a floating object pushed by the wind.

For the first observation, the sudden and rapid escape, leading to disappearance, corresponds to the end of the candle's combustion in the lantern, leading to its fall and extinction. The witness's interpretation of a southern direction of flight is an error in judgment; it corresponds to the sudden descent associated with the dimming due to the candle's extinction.

As for the second observation of a point splitting into three points and one separate point, it corresponds to a new launch of lanterns, three of them separating due to the wind, associated with a parallax effect with the observer.

The result of this investigation leads to propose, with a high probability, the observation of Thai lanterns launched on a Saturday evening during Christmas celebrations. In conclusion, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
