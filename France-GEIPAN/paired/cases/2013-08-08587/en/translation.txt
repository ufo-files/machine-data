Summary: Observation of a stormy sky and confirmation through photography of light streaks of color: misidentification with a photographic artifact.
--- seg-71d89a51776fabb5f392e722 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 4, 2013, during a very hot night, a witness (estimated time around 3 or 4 AM) took a photo of the storms in the distance before going to bed (photo DSCN5593). Later in his sleep, a violent light wakes him and his spouse: both think it's a storm. The witness steps out onto the balcony and takes a second photograph. That's when he notices a chain of white-yellow light streaks present on the photograph (photo DSCN5594) and remains intrigued. What the witnesses saw does not create any particular strangeness. My wife said: "The lightning is coming here." We observed bright streaks interpreted as lightning (see questionnaire). The strangeness and object of the testimony seem to come from what is seen in photo DSCN5594. The analysis of this photo indicates the presence of photographic artifacts that are likely caused by the duration of the exposure (8 seconds) and the movement of the camera within the field of lights, even of moderate intensity, present nearby before the end of the exposure. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies this case as A: Misidentification with a photographic artifact.
