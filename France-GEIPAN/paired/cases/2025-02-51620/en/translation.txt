Summary: Post-observation of a photograph of a "green crescent": artifact of a parasitic reflection of the Moon in the smartphone lens.
--- seg-137529edee2a61874319110a [needs-review] ---
On February 1, 2025, at 8:28 PM, the witness, stationed on a rural road near the village of AVESNES-LES-AUBERT (59), captured a series of time-lapse images using their smartphone to photograph the Moon and the planet Venus. The sequence lasted approximately four minutes. An image extracted from the time-lapse, along with the complete file, was submitted to the GEIPAN on February 11, 2025, via a download link, accompanied by a duly completed Technical Questionnaire (QT). The witness did not visually observe the PAN, but only realized it a posteriori while reviewing the results of their shots. Initially, they considered the possibility of a digital imaging artifact, before noting that the object's movement, in their words, "resembled the motion of a satellite." An astronomical analysis from Lille using the Stellarium tool (a free software that displays a photorealistic representation of the sky in real-time) confirms the presence of the Moon and Venus, visible in the sky at the time of observation (Annex 1). Despite only having one witness, the analysis of the short video and photograph allows us to conclude that the observation is likely a parasitic reflection of the Moon in the smartphone's lens used by the witness. This hypothesis is based on the following:

- IPACO*, the photo analysis software of the GEIPAN, integrates a specific tool for detecting such reflections. This tool was used in this analysis and unequivocally demonstrates that it is a reflection caused by the full Moon present in the video and photograph's field of view. This reflection consistently positions itself opposite a bright light source, following a central symmetry axis passing through the optical center of the lens.

- A geometric center offset was observed between the image's center and the lens's optical center. This offset can be attributed to a slight shift between the center of the image sensor and the center of the smartphone's optical system. This difference was clearly identified and measured using the dedicated tool in the IPACO software (see Annexes 2 and 3).

- Examining the video reveals the simultaneous movement, always according to a central symmetry axis, of the Moon and its reflection (the PAN noted by the witness), as well as Venus, which also produces its own reflection, though less visible than that of the Moon. Two local measurements (in pixels) between the Moon and Venus and their respective reflections show that the offset is exactly the same (436 pixels). (Annex 4).

- The crescent shape, similar to the Moon at the time of the shots (it was illuminated at 13.2%). The reflection's shape is inverted, in line with the application of central symmetry according to the optical axis.

- The color can be blue, green, or red, typical of the outer protective layer of the lens's external lens. However, we do not know if, for the specific model used by the witness, this color is indeed green. The witness was unable to observe the phenomenon with the naked eye; it was only visible on the device's screen during the shot or a posteriori when reviewing the images on a computer. The witness also confirms having noticed the presence of the phenomenon only after viewing the photographs. The GEIPAN classifies this observation as A: parasitic artifact - lens reflection of the imaging device.

*According to the GEIPAN's criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.

*IPACO: Image analysis and processing software for the GEIPAN (IPACO.fr).
