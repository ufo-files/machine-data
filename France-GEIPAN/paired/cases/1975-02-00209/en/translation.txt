Description: On February 18, 1975, around midnight twenty, a witness driving on a rural road was startled by a bright spherical object moving vertically and silently oscillating across the landscape. No other witnesses were reported. Astronomical verification indicates that the Moon is near its setting in the west-northwest (azimuth 290), in the shape of a half-moon (first quarter). The report includes a copy of the cadastre which appears to be oriented with north at the top; the current cadastre available through Geoportail (IGN) does not show the same orientation. Using the current reference, the witness also observed the phenomenon in the west-northwest with an azimuth close to 287 (see attached map), that is, in the direction of the Moon: a confusion with the Moon, possibly partially obscured to appear roughly spherical, is plausible. Misjudgments of distance and size are very common in PAN observations, especially for celestial bodies near the horizon. However, with this hypothesis, it is difficult to explain the back-and-forth movements of the phenomenon described by the witness; it is strictly possible to consider that low clouds may have partially obscured the half-moon. Since the witness does not mention the Moon, even though it was in the direction of his observation, it is highly likely that it played a role in the observation. In conclusion, unable to refine with the witness the apparent movements of the phenomenon, and verify local atmospheric conditions, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" - Unidentified Phenomenon due to lack of precise data.
