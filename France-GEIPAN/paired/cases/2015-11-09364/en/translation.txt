Description: On November 27, 2015, between 10:30 and 11:00, a witness at their residence observed and filmed the movement of several objects in the sky. One witness statement was collected. The witness saw several PAN (refer to the two attached videos). Except for one of spherical shape, these PAN are aircraft, some flying high and leaving a white trail, and others flying much lower (and thus without a trail) after takeoff from Blagnac. Indeed, analysis of air traffic (Flightradar) shows a situation of two aircraft moving in opposite directions, north-south for the low-flying aircraft and south-north for the other, which is exactly consistent with one of the videos and the witness's description, including the elongated and wingless appearance, a classic feature of low-altitude aircraft viewed under incident sunlight and at a low angle (10:30 AM in November). The PAN of spherical shape is likely a toy balloon, with an angular size of approximately 0.5 degrees (estimated from the size (a few cm) of the residual leaves of the neighboring tree in the video field and 30 meters from the filming location) and an elevation between 15 and 20 degrees (also according to the neighboring tree). This could be a child's balloon at very low altitude or a solar thermal balloon (a few meters in diameter, see solar balloon flights) at a distance of a few kilometers and an altitude that can reach 1 or 3 kilometers. The thermal balloon is more likely because the ground wind (west to east at the Blagnac station) does not easily explain the strong north-south component of the observation, while the wind measurement at higher altitude is indeed north-south. This situation of wind inversion with altitude can also explain the inversion of trajectory distinguished at the end of the video related to the spherical PAN and described by the witness as a "circle arc." In fact, the balloon would be within the altitude range (~1km) corresponding to the wind inversion (refer to the document on the reconstruction of the trajectories of the PAN aircraft, the geometry of the observation, and the video of the PAN spherical object, as well as the ground and higher-altitude wind directions). Consequently, considering only the spherical PAN (the others being aircraft), GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a thermal balloon.
