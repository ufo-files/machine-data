Description: On September 3, 2025, both witnesses, on foot at the entrance to the Château du Clos Vougeot (21), observed at 9:44 PM a white, oval-shaped light moving north at approximately a 45° elevation. The phenomenon described curved, repetitive movements above the clouds, occasionally revealing a white trail, sometimes obscured by cloud cover. No noise was heard, and the cycle of movements lasted about five seconds. Witness T1 recorded three videos and one photograph. The observation lasted for nearly five minutes before the light disappeared. The witness ruled out the possibility of a ground projection, stating that the source seemed to come from a high point and no light of terrestrial origin was visible. This case exhibits notable consistency, even though only one witness (T1) completed the questionnaire. Several videos and one photograph were taken, and their analysis reinforces the value and reliability of the collected information. According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. For T1, the strangeness of the phenomenon lies in its unusual appearance and repetitive movements, without any observed ground trace. The investigation explored the hypothesis of observing a "skytracker," a powerful ground-based projector that beams light into the sky and is characterized by regular, repetitive movements, often circular, as described by T1. The analysis of the videos and photograph confirms all elements reported by T1. It shows that the PAN appears against the background of a low, uniformly overcast cloud, dimly lit by light pollution, likely from the city of Dijon, located generally in the direction of observation. The study of available meteorological data at Dijon-Longvic Air Base, approximately 15 km northeast of the witnesses, indicates the presence of a low to medium overcast ceiling (8/8 octas between approximately 1,900 m and 2,400 m altitude). See Annex 1. These conditions are conducive to the formation, at cloud base, of a bright spot generated by a powerful projector oriented from the ground. The white color is common for such equipment, and what T1 describes as a "trail sometimes hidden by clouds" likely corresponds to a portion of the beam manifesting upon contact with suspended water droplets. Regarding the potential use of skytrackers at the time of observation, Dijon is located approximately 15 to 20 km northeast of the witnesses' position, a distance consistent with the usual range of these projectors (up to twenty kilometers). However, no exceptional activity over the city was reported on the night of the observation, and no information was found regarding the possible use of skytrackers. The repetitive movement of these lights, however, does reduce the strangeness of the observation when considering this hypothesis; it may appear that the light comes from above the clouds depending on the density conditions of the cloud cover. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a skytracker used during a private evening/event.
