--- seg-4ed99e24e2f6c70d2aa656c6 [needs-review] ---
Description: Recent testimony (2/23/2015) of old observations. The GEIPAN investigates an observation made in Port-La-Nouvelle (11). On August 4th or 5th, 1997 at 11:45 PM (the witness cannot recall the exact date), two individuals lying on lounge chairs were surprised to witness two luminous phenomena appearing in the clear sky with a 10-minute interval. The first phenomenon was described as flickering orange spots forming an equilateral triangle. The observation lasted 3 seconds. The silent movement of this object was very rapid in a NNE-SSO direction. The second phenomenon was described as two perfectly rectangular lights of yellow/orange color moving on the same trajectory as the first PAN. The lights moved in parallel and at a short distance from each other. Only one testimony was collected. The first observation, a dark triangle bounded by three orange lights flying at low altitude, requires interpretation through the psychology of perception: the witness undoubtedly saw the three orange lights, but the materiality of the triangle is not certain and is likely the result of a well-known illusion, the contour or shape illusion (see Psychology of Form). The witness actually writes, "equilateral triangle, immaterial in appearance." Furthermore, the estimation of distance and thus speed has no chance of being accurate since the witness had never seen this phenomenon, despite comparing it to aircraft they were familiar with (see Estimation of Size and Distance of an Unknown Phenomenon). What the witness objectively saw was a set of three luminous points crossing half the sky together in 3 seconds, at an undetermined altitude, from NNE to SSO. Two other lights followed the same trajectory 10 minutes later. One could think of lighted objects carried by the wind: since the observation date is approximate, it is difficult to know the weather conditions of the day, and in particular to correlate the movement of these lights with the wind direction. For August 4th, there was a light easterly to north-easterly wind in Perpignan, which cannot explain the observed movement. Due to the lack of date knowledge and the impossibility of conducting an investigation more than 17 years after the facts, the GEIPAN classifies this case "C" for lack of precise date and lack of corroboration.
