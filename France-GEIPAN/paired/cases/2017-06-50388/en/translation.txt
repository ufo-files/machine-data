Summary: Observations of dazzling, fleeting lights moving across the night sky: probable satellite observation for the first PAN and unusable testimony due to lack of reliable information for the second PAN.
--- seg-a990431ccd2da232587ce77a [needs-review] ---
Description: On Monday, June 19, 2017, around 10:30 PM to 11:00 PM, a witness observed a bright, sparkling light below Venus in the sky. Its movement and disappearance within a halo intrigued the witness. After half an hour to three-quarters of an hour, the witness again sees the same phenomenon almost directly above them. A photo reconstruction is included in the questionnaire. Only one testimony was collected. The witness provides very precise elements to retrace the trajectory of the PAN. The most precise point is that it occurred to the right and slightly below Venus, in the direction indicated by the photo reconstruction. This reconstruction allows us to establish that the indicated reference is not Venus but Jupiter, which is also very bright that evening.

The analysis of visible satellites that evening (see investigation document - reconstruction attached) allows us to identify one whose final descending trajectory matches that described by the witness in their reconstruction:

- It passes to the right of Jupiter (10 to 15° to the right).
- Around 10:50 PM, within the approximate time frame (not very precise) given by the witness (10:30 PM to 11:00 PM).

The satellite in question (USA 234/FIA Radar 2, a US military satellite and not detailed; see FIA-RADAR) is one of those whose flares are not or barely predictable, but fortunately, it has been the subject of observation records of very strong flares (brighter or "similar to Venus" as noted by the witness):

- Magnitude -0.5 flare observed on May 2, 2012 in Ukraine (see details of USA-234 (FIA RADAR 2) bright flare).
- Magnitude -5 (!) flare observed on April 30, 2012 by the same observer (see images).
- Its twin, FIA-Radar 1, flared to magnitude -1 and 0 on May 25, 2015 (see document double flare from FIA-RADAR 1).

The witness describes the presence of a gray halo during the disappearance of the PAN, which is difficult to explain otherwise than by the conjunction of the flare extinction with the satellite entering behind a light fog not noticed by the witness, as the sky was clear. The correspondence between the trajectory of the PAN and that of the satellite subject to a flare makes this satellite explanation sufficiently probable to be retained despite this residual strangeness constituted by the halo.

The witness describes another phenomenon: "1.5 to 3.5 hours later, almost directly above me, I saw this curious phenomenon repeat but without the halo." We do have an Iridium flare in this new time frame, but it occurs at 34° altitude, not qualified as "vertical." We have few indications from the witness for this second phenomenon. Several satellite objects pass close to vertical, but more precision on this "vertical" and also a direction of movement (which must exist since the witness describes it as the same phenomenon) would be needed. Perhaps one of these objects is prone to fleeting flares. We do not have enough information to explore this second PAN. It should also be noted that it is frequent that a witness, whose curiosity has been awakened after a first PAN, later notes one or several oddities by associating them with the first, and these are then identified as having nothing to do and would not have been noticed without the initial genuine strangeness.

Consequently, the GEIPAN concludes:

- Probable satellite observation for the first PAN (B).
- Unexploitable testimony due to lack of reliable information for the second PAN (C).
