Summary: Low-altitude observation of a white glowing point with color flickering and short-distance movements: observation of Sirius.
--- seg-5ecc679a1afe40d395383c8b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 31, 2017 at 12:55 AM, a witness took advantage of a clear sky to observe the stars towards the East (West being less visible due to the brightness of the moon and the city of Cholet). While scanning the horizon approximately in the direction of Maulévrier, he noticed a strong white light (East/South/East) with blue and red flickering. Five minutes later, he realized the object had not moved much, or at all. He then used his smartphone's SkyView Free app: the bright point was not listed. Continuing his observation of this light point, he noticed it had moved vertically. He observed that the bright point sometimes described jerky, rapid movements in straight lines over short distances. He attempted to take photos and videos with his smartphone, but the low light made the point difficult to see (on the photos). Twenty-five minutes later, the light point was still there, clearly higher than its initial position. The blue and red flickering accompanying the white light did not stop during the entire observation. The white light was significantly brighter than that of the stars. Only one testimony was collected. A location plan provided by the witness informs us about the direction of observation at an azimuth close to 120°, a Stellarium sky map reconstruction shows Sirius in the East South-East at azimuth 120° (altitude 4°) at 12:55 AM (start of observation time) and azimuth 123° (altitude 6°) at 1:10 AM (photo taking time). The absence of Sirius during the sky observation via the app can be explained by a bad calibration or a slight offset between the smartphone image and the sky. A comparison between the witness's photo and the sky map (see investigation notes) clearly reveals an identical position of the PAN and Sirius compared to other stars. Sirius is the brightest star after that of the Shepherd (Venus) and is never very high in the sky at our latitudes; its color changes are caused by atmospheric turbulence. The "jerky movement over short distances" is a known perception phenomenon called autokinetic which occurs when observing a bright point in the dark. The slow vertical movement observed for 30 minutes is indeed that of Sirius in the sky (caused by the Earth's rotation). Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies this case as A: certain observation of Sirius.
