Description: On the night of July 2 to 3, 2025, the witness, at their home in Coulon (79), observed through their open window an unidentified aerial phenomenon (PAN) consisting of 7 to 8 small green lights arranged in a circle. These turn on themselves then move in a jerky manner. The lights then regroup into a "water drop" shape, before passing over the witness' house, making back-and-forth movements while returning to a circular configuration. The observation lasted approximately 5 minutes, until the witness ended the observation. Subsequently, the witness reported hearing two loud noises, followed by dog barks (see Annex 1 for the location). For this case, only one testimony was collected. The case is of *average consistency*, with a single witness who was unable to take a photo or video of the PAN. *According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The strangeness is formed for the witness by the unusual appearance for her of the PAN, and by its rapid and repetitive movements. The investigation studied the hypothesis of the observation of light displays produced by "sky-tracers," powerful ground-based projectors that project their lights into the sky, exhibiting regular and repetitive motions, often circular or back-and-forth, and capable of grouping together and dispersing, exactly as described by the witness with a diffuse appearance of the light. The study of the meteorological data (see Annexes 2) available at the closest location (Poitiers-LFBI, 73 km away) shows the presence of a low and uniform cloud layer (8/8 octas at approximately 1300 m altitude). These data are complemented by the data obtained from Météo France for the observation location, from the fine-mesh modeling system AROME* (6/8 octas at approximately 2000m altitude at the lowest). These conditions are favorable to the generation, based on cloud of impact spots, of projected lights. The observed green color is not the most frequent, but remains possible thanks to colored filters modulating the light emitted by the projectors. Regarding the potential use of such projectors during the night of the observation, the city of Niort is located approximately 9 km southeast of the witness. A sky-tracer can illuminate up to 20 km, making its use technically possible. However, no information indicates that local animations or discothèques used sky-tracers that evening. The possibility of a private outdoor evening, frequent in summer, with one or more sky-tracers rented, cannot be excluded. The two loud noises heard by the witness could correspond to this event (fireworks), without a direct link to the observation of PAN being able to be claimed. Upon analysis, the GEIPAN classifies this case as Category B: probable observation of light displays produced by sky-tracers. AROME: (Application of Research to Operations at Mesoscale) is a model with a very fine mesh (1.3 km mesh) for forecasting in France (METEO France).
