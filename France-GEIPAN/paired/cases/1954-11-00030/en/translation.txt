Description: On November 20, 1954, between midnight 30 and 3:30 a.m., several people will witness a luminous phenomenon in the sky. Only one testimony is collected. The witness describes a PAN moving vertically in a zigzag pattern from SSE in a clear sky. The PAN is sometimes bright and sometimes dark with a halo. This lengthy observation of a bright point in the sky first suggests an astronomical phenomenon: the presence of the star Sirius is noted in the southeast at midnight thirty, moving subsequently towards the south. The observed halo could be the result of an atmospheric veil originating from desert sand. Zig-zag movements can be caused by autocinetic illusion (a common perceptual illusion when observing a bright point in darkness). The observation direction is not described precisely enough to confirm this hypothesis (grossly estimated azimuth to SSE with no temporal change, elevation expressed in meters rather than estimated in degrees). Without precise information regarding the position in the sky, the GEIPAN classifies this case as C: lack of information.
