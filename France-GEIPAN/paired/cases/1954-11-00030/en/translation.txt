Description: On November 20, 1954, between midnight 30 and 3:30 a.m., several people will witness a luminous phenomenon in the sky. Only one testimony is collected. The witness describes a PAN moving vertically in a zigzag pattern from SSE in a cloudless sky. The PAN is sometimes bright and sometimes dark with a halo. This lengthy observation of a large glowing point in the sky initially suggests an astronomical phenomenon: the presence of the star Sirius in the SE at midnight 30 is noted, then its movement towards the South. The observed halo could be the result of an atmospheric veil from desert sand. The zigzag movements may be caused by autocinetic illusion (a common perceptual illusion when observing a glowing point in the darkness). The direction of observation is not described precisely enough to confirm this hypothesis (grossly estimated azimut in SSE without temporal evolution, elevation expressed in meters rather than estimated in degrees). Without precise information regarding the sky position, the GEIPAN classifies this case as C: lack of information.
