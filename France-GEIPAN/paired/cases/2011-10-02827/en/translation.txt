Description: On Monday evening, October 17, 2011, around 8:40 PM, the witness is standing in front of their home's entrance door when they spot a group of three white glowing points, appearing roughly five times the size of stars, crossing the southern sky towards the west. The three lights move in a clustered, triangular pattern, and seem to spread apart before regrouping and disappearing over the western horizon. The overall movement speed was comparable to that of shooting stars, with the observation lasting less than 5 seconds. Of good consistency and low to moderate strangeness, this observation case suggests two fairly common phenomena: Thai lanterns, but the observation duration is far too short (unless the witness misjudged the observation time), or an atmospheric reentry (bolide, meteorite fall) more consistent with the observation duration, but the multiplicity of objects is rare. Without further investigation to decide between the two hypotheses, this case is provisionally classified as C.
