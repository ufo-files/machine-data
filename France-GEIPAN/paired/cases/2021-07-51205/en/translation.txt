Summary: Observation of two white, oval, wingless objects moving on very close trajectories in the sky: observation of two commercial aircraft.
--- seg-18ebfb3309f3e01dc61d7055 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Thursday, July 1st, around 8:45 PM, a witness observed from their balcony in Manosque (04) two unidentified aerial phenomena (PAN) crossing the sky. These objects present a bright white appearance and an oval shape. The observation lasts less than a minute, until they disappear from the visual field. The next day, on July 2, the witness submits to the GEIPAN a fully completed Technical Questionnaire (QT), accompanied by a panoramic photograph of the observation site taken approximately 15 minutes after the event (Annex 1), as well as a sketch of the observed phenomenon (Annex 2). No other testimony regarding this observation was received by the GEIPAN. The GEIPAN requests the Air and Space Defense Operations Planning and Command Center (CAPCODA) on August 19, 2021, to obtain a radar return. This last one is transmitted on September 28. The only hypothesis studied is that of 2 planes crossing each other. Knowing that the witness spontaneously mentioned this possibility: "I start to look up at the sky precisely where I see what I first take for two planes." Due to a single witness and despite the absence of photos or videos of the PANs, the consistency* of the testimony is considered good. The perceived oddity by the witness is due to the fact that the 2 PANs seem very close (he even thinks of a possible collision) and he cannot distinguish their wings. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The radar return provided by CAPCODA presented interpretation difficulties due to an inappropriate scale and the low readability of the displayed information. In addition, the analysis conducted using the Globe.adsbexchange site confirmed the simultaneous presence of two aircraft crossing at 18:47 UTC, along the witness's observation axis (south-south-east orientation). From its position, these two objects could appear aligned on the same apparent trajectory. These are the flights:

- RYR576A, flying at an altitude of 40,000 feet (12,192 m), located at a horizontal distance of 39,735 m from the witness (ground projection measured on a map) (see Annex 3).
- IBE32EM, flying at an altitude of 36,975 feet (11,270 m), located at a horizontal distance of 51,768 m from the witness (ground projection measured on a map) (see Annex 4). The RYR576A flight is visible from the witness's position at an elevation angle of approximately 17° (calculated via Arctan (12192 / 39735)), while the IBE32EM flight appears at an angle of approximately 12° (Arctan (11270 / 51768)), resulting in a difference of roughly 5°. This small deviation can create the illusion of converging trajectories, or even a potential collision, which is not actually the case, as the two craft are at different altitudes and following independent paths (see Annex 5). It is also noted that these actual elevations are significantly lower than those estimated by the witness (between 30° and 45°), which is common: witnesses generally tend to overestimate low elevation angles. These reduced angles and the great distance separating the aircraft from the witness make their visual identification difficult, even more so as the wings can visually blend with the horizontal plane of the fuselage at this distance. Regarding the color and brightness of the reported PANs: the witness describes a very bright white, which is consistent with the appearance of the white fuselages of the two aircraft, reflecting sunlight at the time of observation. As for the perceived shape, the significant distance (over 40 km) likely prevents the witness from distinguishing it accurately, especially as solar reflections can alter the perception of the structure. Finally, the apparent curvature of the trajectory could be interpreted as a perspective effect, related to the distance of the craft and the observer's position. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of two commercial aircraft.
