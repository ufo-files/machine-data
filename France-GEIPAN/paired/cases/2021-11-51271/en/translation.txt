Description: On November 12, 2021, between 18:08 and 18:25, the witness, on their terrace with their two daughters, noticed in the sky to the southeast, at an elevation of approximately 20°, two very bright white light points located right next to each other, and at the same height as the moon. These two PAN remain stationary for 10 to 15 seconds of observation, then disappear progressively, with the light intensity dimming until it goes out completely. Only one testimony will be collected. This case is of average consistency: one witness (the other two being children) with no photos or videos of the PAN (see the investigation report). The investigation concluded that the probable observation was of landing lights or headlights of two aircraft. The aircraft are likely military. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Appearance similar to two stars: strong brightness produced by landing or search lights (helicopters).
- White color, identical to that of landing or search lights.
- Modest elevation: similar to that of the Moon, approximately 20°, allowing the visibility cone of the landing or search lights to be in the observation axis. In the assumption that it is a helicopter, some have searchlights that are adjustable. - Brief observation: the landing lights of aircraft can be briefly visible from a great distance when their orientation relative to the witness (face-on) temporarily allows it; the aircraft can then change course, causing the visibility cone of these lights to move out of the observation axis. For the helicopter hypothesis, the question does not arise if the search lights used are adjustable.

- Immobility: The observation distance, probably important, coupled with the temporary movement of planes in the direction of witnesses and the low duration of observation prevents them from distinguishing any possible movements.

- Progressive disappearance: As aircraft change course progressively, the light from their headlights also disappears progressively from the witnesses' view. On the day of the observation, a large-scale joint military exercise was concluding in eastern France, involving numerous aircraft (Mirage 2000, NH90, Gazelle, Tigre helicopters, and an A400M from Air Base 123 Orléans-Bricy). Although this exercise took place away from the observation site, it is possible that the observation was of 2 craft returning to their base at the end of the exercise. However, lacking information regarding the schedules and trajectories followed by these aircraft during their return to base after this military exercise; the GEIPAN was unable to obtain the radar air traffic charts for this date. Classification "B": probable observation of aircraft headlights or lights.
