Summary: Observation of two satellites simultaneously.
--- seg-7904b91ad816d233e5d92fd8 [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 27, 2007, between 11:30 PM and 12:15 AM, a witness sitting on a café terrace observed with curiosity a bright point moving rapidly and at a very high altitude in the sky. This southern point had a descending trajectory. Discussing with her friend, she resumed her observation but noticed that the object was in the opposite direction and moving upward. Examining the sky map, it appears that on that day around midnight, the International Space Station (ISS) disappeared on the horizon a few seconds before the NOAA 14 weather satellite rose exactly in the same part of the sky in the direction of the observation. The trajectories of these two satellites are approximately 90°. The ISS was rising at that time from the S-SO (204°) at 11:45 to head towards the S-E. The NOAA-14 weather satellite rose exactly in the same place (204°) at 12:03, heading in the opposite direction towards the S-O. It is therefore clear that the witness successively saw the ISS and then NOAA 14, giving the impression that the bright point was changing direction.
