Summary: Brief observations of the silent, rapid movement across the sky of a luminous phenomenon on a straight trajectory: re-entry into the atmosphere of a small meteoroid.
--- seg-f5f792271bfda67be5cccfcd [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 14, 1984, in the late afternoon, several witnesses in different communes and departments of France observed a bright, cigar- or ball-shaped object moving silently and quickly across the sky, leaving a trail. The observations lasted for a few seconds before the object suddenly disappeared towards the southwest. The Gendarmerie collected four testimonies from the communes of Marnans (38) and Villers-les-Luxeuil (70). However, other witnesses came forward through local press. These cases did not receive further investigation in 1984, which could have supplemented the two Gendarmerie reports, though they are relatively concise. Extracted from archives, these cases were recently investigated without revisiting the sites (see the investigation report). Based on the descriptions provided by the witnesses (appearance, movement, colors, silence, speed, trail, sudden disappearance), it is highly probable that they witnessed the passage of a small meteoroid (at a minimum, a brilliant shooting star), a rare sight that many people, including these witnesses, have never had the opportunity to observe (otherwise, they would have made the comparison). The meteorological and astronomical context greatly reinforces this feeling, as the observation date corresponds to the peak activity of a very productive meteor shower, in this case the Geminids. Moreover, the radiant of this shower is located at the northeast horizon, which exactly matches the direction from which the PAN seemed to originate, according to the witnesses. The observation times in Marnans and Villers-les-Luxeuil differ by approximately 25 minutes. This difference can apparently be explained as follows:

- In Villers-les-Luxeuil, T1 checks his watch (but the PVGN does not specify if its setting was verified) while the witness in Marnans does not specify how he determined the time;
- The witness in Baulay (70), according to a press article, mentions 5:50 PM, which seems to confirm the timing given by the witnesses in Villers-les-Luxeuil;
- If he is not mistaken about the time, the witness in Marnans could have observed another shooting star than the one seen in Villers-les-Luxeuil, as he could have seen one every 30 seconds on average. Coming from the same radiant, their appearances and trajectories could have been very similar. However, this is unlikely since only one meteoroid was reported by the press. Finally, the significant distance between witnesses in Marnans, Villers-les-Luxeuil, and Baulay (and in other locations) strongly supports the "meteoroid" hypothesis, as if the sightings were simultaneous, the PAN could only be at a high altitude and moving very quickly. This is exactly the case for meteoroids, which are bright at altitudes of a few dozen kilometers and move at dizzying speeds of ~11 to ~70 km/sec upon entering Earth's atmosphere. Other meteoroids were observed during the days and weeks surrounding the observation date (including a meteoroid observed on 12/05/1984 in France and Italy, which generated numerous testimonies, press articles, and other astronomical studies), so it seems well established that Earth was passing through a relatively rich area in its orbit with cosmic dust during this period. This phenomenon is not really strange, as both the description and the context in which it occurs are compatible (or even typical) with this category of atmospheric phenomena. The case is consistent enough to have three witnesses at one site and one independent witness at another site with similar descriptions in quite similar observation contexts. Only a very slight doubt remains about the exact time, which could imply two meteoroids rather than one. Furthermore, the press reports other similar and simultaneous observations. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: this case appears reasonably identifiable by the entry into the atmosphere of a small meteoroid.
