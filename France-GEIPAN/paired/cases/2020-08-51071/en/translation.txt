Description: On the evening of August 26, 2020, a resident of MALEMORT-SUR-CORREZE (19), in the company of friends, was alerted by his partner's son who observed an object moving "from below to above." Everyone looked in the direction indicated and observed, for 6 minutes, the rapid passage of 9 bright objects, with a glare comparable to a star. Some appeared brighter than others, originating from 300° West and moving at very high speed, in groups of 2 units, towards 53° North from their position. No sound was heard during the observation. The moon was in its first quarter, brightly shining directly south, behind the witness during the observation. Of average strangeness and good consistency (multiple witnesses, but unique testimony synthesizing all observations, no photo of the PAN), this case appears to be a probable misidentification with Starlink satellites. The description of the PAN is very consistent with the passage of Starlink satellites. The observation, in fact, took place less than three weeks after the launch of Starlink train #9, whose members were not yet well dispersed and were passing just at the minutes surrounding the observation. However, there is uncertainty about the actual angular height of the PAN. Indeed, it must be specified that at the time indicated by the witness, a Starlink train was indeed passing, but at an angular height lower than that estimated for the PAN (see the investigation report). The case is classified B, probable misidentification with Starlink satellites.
