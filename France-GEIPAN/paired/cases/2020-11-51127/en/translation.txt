Description: On November 25, 2020, around 8 a.m., the witness goes outside her garden, which offers a clear view. In the distance, she initially thinks she's seeing an airplane, but is puzzled because it doesn't leave a jet trail, instead resembling an incandescent tail disappearing behind a cloud. She considers it a meteorite, but deems the speed too slow for such an event. The witness films the PAN. No other witnesses were reported for this phenomenon. The witness states the observation was made in an easterly direction, while the video shows a right-to-left movement (from southwest to northeast). Using Google Earth Pro, the church landmark and the witness's position at her garden entrance allow for an observation azimuth of 130°. However, analyzing the video (refer to IPACO document) and the rising sun's luminosity, the observation azimuth is more likely around 115°, as the PAN is slightly north of the rising sun, which at this time is at 117°. A slight movement of the witness in her garden could result in a difference of several degrees in the observation azimuth. Thus, for the investigation, we will use a reference of 120° +/- -10°. The video (shape, color) suggests an aeronautical misperception: condensation trail. A search on Flightradar24 reveals a possible candidate, the Boeing 747: ATG9926, located between 115 and 130 km from the observation site, moving from southwest to northeast. Such misperceptions occur regularly, as observing condensation trails from planes, under specific meteorological and astronomical conditions, can be particularly spectacular and lead to speculations about their origin, especially during sunrise observations, as is the case here, with specific color effects, shadows, and lights. When air masses at high altitudes are very humid, it promotes the persistence of these trails, which spread and disperse randomly with the altitude winds. See the Contrail website. The aircraft responsible for creating these trails may not be visible or may no longer be visible, often because it is not illuminated by the sun or is already much farther away, leaving behind its long-lasting condensation trail. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": condensation trail.
