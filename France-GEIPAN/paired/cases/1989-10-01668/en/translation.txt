Summary: An farmer discovered a large burnt area in a meadow: likely a fire in a portion of a field due to accidental ignition of flammable hydrocarbons.
--- seg-fa3ff421d7dc47fe30338d50 [needs-review] ---
Description: This SEPRA investigation was conducted to assess an unusual and suspicious burnt-earth trace in a meadow in Normandy. The local brigade, unable to determine the origin and cause of the phenomenon, called upon the SEPRA who promptly visited the site. On October 27, 1989, at approximately 0900 hours, the owner of a field (T1) discovered a large, totally charred area on the ground within the field. The trace is irregular in shape, measuring nearly 17 meters in length and 4 meters at its widest point. At the site of the trace, there is no vegetation remaining; the soil is black, and the ground is spongy, depressed by 10 to 15 centimeters below the average level of the meadow. A high-voltage 20 kV line runs along the plot, approximately fifty meters from the trace. A witness (T2) reported to the gendarmerie the strange behavior of his dog during the night of October 25-26, 1989, around 0200 hours, and the presence of an intense blue beam of light above the affected field. Core samples taken from the area show that the soil is burnt to a depth of 2 to 3 centimeters, and the gray color of the clays indicates that the soil was subjected to a high temperature. Soil samples taken from the suspicious area have a strong odor of petroleum products. The analyses confirm the presence of kerosene in the samples. These analytical results suggest the hypothesis of a kerosene drop conducted by an aircraft flying at low altitude and low speed. The kerosene could have been ignited by passing through the high-voltage line. Indeed, during the investigation, strong sparks from the line were observed at the junction of certain insulators. Sparks could have ignited the hydrocarbon cloud. This hypothesis is supported by witness (T2), who observed a very large blue flame over the area, a typical color of hydrocarbon combustion. This case is classified as B: ground traces left by a fire.
