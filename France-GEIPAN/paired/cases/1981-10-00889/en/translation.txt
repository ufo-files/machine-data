**Description:** The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to ensure more accurate conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. A previous observation classified as D and published under the name BOURG-MADAME (66) 1981 is part of a recent batch of cases re-examined. This case involved three witnesses observing three different objects in three communes of the Pyrénées-Orientales (66) department during September and October 1981. These three distinct UFO observations had initially been incorrectly attributed to the same phenomenon. The failure to detect this led to a perception of extreme strangeness (initial D classification). The new analysis (see investigation report) reveals these are indeed three distinct UFO observations.

- SAINTE-LEOCADIE (66) 19.09.1981
- CORNEILLA-DE-CONFLENT (66) 27.09.1981
- VERNET-LES-BAINS (66) 03.10.1981

Regarding VERNET-LES-BAINS (66) 03.10.1981: At 02:45, a witness looking out the window saw a round, short-base, brightly lit object approaching from the Cerdagne towards the Mariailles ridge. The witness went onto their balcony and saw a second identical object following the same path. A faint noise from the UFOs was heard. Only one testimony was collected. The witness dismissed their own "planes" hypothesis because they estimated the distance at 300 meters, which is incompatible with such a presence. A plane at less than 4 kilometers distance should have been part of the navigation records established during the investigation. However, without angular size information, the reliability of the witness's distance perception cannot be assessed. The two UFOs could be much farther away as they did not pass in front of reliefs that could have fixed a maximum distance. If they are more than 4 kilometers away, given the correctly recorded angular height, these UFOs are then at an altitude outside the available air navigation zone. Conversely, if they are at a closer distance, they should have been recorded. The details described inside the UFOs are visible to the naked eye, even if we must assume a distance greater than 4 kilometers. These elements are compatible with planes. One might wonder why the witness did not perceive these navigation lights, especially the anti-collision flashers, to this level of detail, but the investigators at the time did not ask the witness any questions or request further precision. The perception of a whistle is compatible with a plane, considering the numerous factors influencing this perception, including if the plane is considered more than 4 kilometers away. The testimony and available elements are compatible with the observation of planes whose presence at more than 4 kilometers would be nothing abnormal and remains unrefuted or unconfirmed due to the lack of available navigation records beyond this distance. This compatibility requires the addition of hypotheses, though plausible, that reduce the probability of the explanation without invalidating it. The hypothesis of the witness's incorrect perception of proximity is allowed by the absence of angular size records, while the hypothesis of a genuine perception of navigation lights is allowed by the absence of an investigation protocol that could have allowed the witness to specify further what they perceived and what they did not.

On consistency, we note in particular the absence of angular size records perceived and the absence of any deposition protocol ensuring precision in what is perceived and what is not. We have an hypothesis (planes) that can explain the observation, but the resulting level of uncertainty is too high given the limited consistency of available elements. GEIPAN classifies the case as C: unexploitable due to lack of reliable information.
