Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D and published observation case under the name BOURG-MADAME (66) 1981 is part of a set of cases recently re-examined. This case concerned observations by 3 witnesses in 3 different communes in the department of Pyrénées-Orientales (66) during the months of September and October 1981. These three observations of PAN of different natures were mistakenly considered, in the first analysis, as part of the same phenomenon. The failure to detect it could have led to a perception of great strangeness (initial classification D). The new analysis (see the investigation report) highlights that these are indeed three different observations of PAN. - SAINTE-LEOCADIE (66) 09/19/1981 - CORNEILLA-DE-CONFLENT (66) 09/27/1981 - VERNET-LES-BAINS (66) 03.10.1981 Regarding VERNET-LES-BAINS (66) 03.10.1981: At 02:45, a witness looks out the window to check the time and sees on the horizon, coming from Cerdagne and heading towards the Mariailles crests, a round and short glowing object with a dark base and a well-lit upper part. The witness steps out onto their balcony and sees a second identical object following the same direction as the first. A light noise coming from the PAN is heard. Only one testimony will be collected. The witness dismissed his own "plane" hypothesis solely because he evaluates the distance at 300m, incompatible with such a presence. A plane-to-plane distance of less than 4 km should have been part of the navigation logs established during the investigation. Nevertheless, in the absence of an angular measurement, the reliability of the witness's distance perception cannot be assessed. The 2 PAN could be much farther away as they did not pass in front of reliefs that could have fixed a maximum distance. If they are more than 4 km away, given the correctly measured angular height, these PAN are then at an altitude that falls outside the range of air navigation coverage that we can have. Conversely, if they were at a closer distance, they should have appeared in these observations. The details described within the PAN are detectable with the naked eye, even if we must assume a distance greater than 4 km. These elements are compatible with aircraft. One might be surprised that the witness did not perceive the navigation lights, and in particular the anti-collision flashers, at such a level of detail, but the witness was not subject to any questions or requests for clarification from the investigators at the time. The perception of a whistle is compatible with an airplane, given the many factors involved in such perception, including if the airplane is considered at a distance greater than 4 km. The witness testimony and available evidence are consistent with the observation of planes whose presence more than 4 km away would be nothing abnormal and remains unfalsifiable or unconfirmable due to the lack of navigation logs available beyond that distance. This compatibility requires the addition of hypotheses that are indeed plausible but that reduce the probability of the explanation, without invalidating it. The hypothesis of a close perception error by the witness is supported by the absence of angular size measurements, while the hypothesis of a genuine perception of navigation lights is supported by the absence of an investigation protocol that could have allowed the witness to provide more specific details about what they perceived and did not perceive. In terms of consistency, we note in particular the absence of angular size measurements and the lack of any deposition protocols to ensure the accuracy of what is perceived and what is not. We have an hypothesis (planes) that can explain the observation, but the resulting level of uncertainty is too high given the limited consistency of the available elements. The GEIPAN classifies the case as C: unusable due to a lack of reliable information.
