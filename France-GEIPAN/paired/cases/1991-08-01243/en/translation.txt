--- seg-64462f2f5ea9a08530453920 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This previously classified C observation case is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On August 18, 1991, an ufologist presented himself at the Gendarmerie to report an observation he had heard about the previous day. This observation was made by a group of 5 children (aged 7 to 10) in Gilette on August 16, 1991, after 11:00 PM. The children observed a blue oval light mass (30 cm at arm's length) with holes from which less bright blue lights emerged, varying in intensity and turning orange. This mass was moving rapidly in a pendulum-like motion and remained stationary. After 10 seconds of observation, a blue flash appeared followed by a series of flashes. The mass lit up and went out every 5 seconds. Another testimony describes the object seen from another location and at another angle, suggesting it was flying over the valley. The children were questioned by the Gendarmerie in the presence of their parents, but the testimonies were not the subject of hearing protocols. The present testimony is of great strangeness: - a totally luminous blue object with "holes" through which bright blue lights turn orange; - a size of 30 cm, measured at arm's length (30 meters wide if placed at a distance of 70 meters!); - a pendulum-like, wave-like motion, flashes not touching the ground, ... But this is not a testimony but a description by a third party of observation accounts that did not receive any direct testimony to the Gendarmerie or the GEIPAN. The Gendarmerie simply states that the children who made the observation confirm their statements as reported. This is quite different from a deposition (and answer to a question) given by each of them and transcribed by a gendarme. Today, the GEIPAN does not take into account indirect testimonies. An indirect testimony cannot be consistent as the information is often insufficient and, above all, lacks reliability. In this case, given the strangeness of the case, it would have taken a high degree of consistency for it to be exploitable (see information on the GEIPAN classification methodology). Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: unexploitable testimony.
