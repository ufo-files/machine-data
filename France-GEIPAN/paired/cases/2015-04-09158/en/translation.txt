Summary: Very long observation of a very bright point in the night sky: probable confusion with Jupiter.
--- seg-f35ace34077ee2d3f75bdbb3 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN has finalized the examination of some cases that were pending classification. He publishes today a conclusion with the available elements from both the testimony and investigation gathered at the time. On Wednesday, April 8, 2015, around 9:30 PM, the witness noticed in the sky a light "similar to that of a star/planet but much brighter (brighter than Venus) with peculiar colors (blue, orange, etc.)." The observation lasted 1.5 hours, and the PAN was filmed using an iPad mini. An astronomical telescope was also used. The witness submitted his questionnaire on April 10, 2015, and his video to the GEIPAN. No other testimony about this phenomenon was collected. The characteristics of the PAN strongly suggest an astronomical confusion. Several exchanges discussing this hypothesis took place between the witness and the GEIPAN, but the witness rejected the hypothesis. The GEIPAN's final request was to have the witness specify the position of the PAN in the sky relative to Jupiter and its satellites, which the witness claims to have spotted with an astronomical telescope and which he states are "very close." This request received no response from the witness. The witness indicates that the PAN was located at 200° south-south-east and at an elevation of 60°. Jupiter, viewed from Avignon (84) at 9:30 PM, was at an azimuth of 177° and an elevation of 64° (refer to the astronomical situation document). The difference is therefore 23° in azimuth and 4° in elevation. The iPad mini used to record the video has a diagonal field of view of 66.5°. Since this device cannot zoom, only the PAN is visible on the video, and the sky was clear (clouds could not hide any other stars), it is highly likely that the witness filmed Jupiter, whose presence and appearance he could not or did not bring closer with his telescope. The iPad's mediocre sensor sensitivity also prevents other less bright stars in the vicinity from being captured on the video. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable confusion with Jupiter.
