Description: On January 28, 2014, around 10:15 PM, a witness outside observed for 2 to 3 minutes the movement of a PAN bright light in the shape of a halo, then a very bright red ball with a small blue tail. The trajectory is complex (straight, then stop, followed by a half-turn with slight deviation, again straight trajectory in the opposite direction and at the same altitude, diagonal movement to the left for almost immediate disappearance into the sky). The object is first observed at 500m, then approaches the witness (approx 15m) before moving away again. Only one testimony is collected. An investigator will meet with the witness (see the investigation report). Given the unusual yet very coherent descriptions provided by the witness and the spatial-temporal context of the phenomenon, which makes any human activity capable of explaining the described event highly improbable, it seems logical to admit the current impossibility of providing a reasoned, prosaic explanation. Only an explanation through a globular lightning phenomenon, certainly very little known but whose multiple facets seem to be confirmed by the numerous testimonies collected for over a century, could potentially align with the complex behavior of the phenomenon. There are indeed some similarities with certain lightning-related testimonies (shape, dimensions, color, brightness, slow and flexible movement close to the ground, sudden disappearance) that could lead to classifying this case in this category. But there is no factual element to support this hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies this case as unexplained (category D1), as the description is detailed, the observation rather strange, and made under good conditions.
