Description: On September 20, 2024, the main witness (T1) was on vacation with his wife (T2) after exploring Corrèze and Cantal. They choose to spend the night in a rented motorhome, parked on a rudimentarily equipped rest area near Nasbinals. This area is surrounded by bare hills and located near the watercourses La Peyrade and Le Bès, along the RD 987. At 22:23, while exiting the vehicle, T1 spots a bright glowing light in the sky heading northwest. This point seems to sparkle and changes color, going from a yellowish-white to red. Alerted, T2 joins T1 to observe the phenomenon, but quickly returns inside due to the cold and wind. T1 estimates that the light is located above the Baboyères hill, in the direction of Saint-Urcize (15), and below the clouds. Without any precise visual references, T1 films the light with his smartphone at 10:25 PM. The sequence, taken without flash and with a zoom, lasts 1 minute and 29 seconds. A series of four photos taken in rapid succession at 22:26 reveals different shapes of light. Always intrigued, T1 emerges to record a second video lasting 1 minute and 35 seconds. According to the GEIPAN criteria, this case presents good consistency, given the provision of photos and videos, which could be utilized for the study of the file. Only one hypothesis was explored, that of observing the first-magnitude star Arcturus. This hypothesis is based on the following elements: - A geographical reconstruction carried out using the data provided by T1 (and in particular a day photograph indicating the location of PAN in the sky) allowed the PAN to be located in terms of azimuth and elevation at a site fairly close to that of Arcturus at the time of the observation. Using the Stellarium software, the position of Arcturus was determined precisely: approximately 287° azimuth and 9° elevation (see AC_astro_1). As for the PAN, it was estimated by T1 at an azimuth of approximately 280° (see AC_geo_1) and an elevation between 30° and 45°. The azimuth shift (less than 10°) can be attributed to T1's difficulty in navigating at night without any references (completely dark night) all the more so in a place he does not know. The elevation difference (approximately 20/35°) can be explained by an overestimation often reported by PAN witnesses. The indications "above the hill" and "a little beyond the horizon" provided elsewhere by T1 do indeed confirm a relatively low altitude. - Strong twinkling: T1 indicates that it is cold and windy, which is entirely compatible with strong atmospheric disturbances generating the twinkling of the star low on the horizon. - Long-duration observation, typical for an astronomical phenomenon. T1 also compares the PAN to Venus. Horizon revealing a first-magnitude star. The perceived oddity by T1 is the impression that the PAN moves (in its videos), even though it remains in the same portion of the sky for an extended period. In reality, at a very high zoom, even the slightest movement of the hand holding the smartphone, imperceptible to the witness, becomes visible in the video. This is all the more notable as, as T1 itself indicates, no visual references (landmarks) are present in the videos, which would have allowed him to observe that the PAN is not actually moving. However, uncertainty persists regarding possible naked-eye perceptible movements, a point that remains vague in the testimony. These movements could be explained by phenomena of micro-eye mobility. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the star Arcturus.
