Summary: Successive observations of the movement of colored light phenomena in the night sky: lack of information.
--- seg-40016dcc32327d4ca0518fcb [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 13, 2012, at approximately 2:05 a.m., a witness observed, in a very short span (8 to 10 seconds), "a good dozen" objects resembling pale blue shooting stars, very dim, following short trajectories and crossing "in all directions" and quickly fading out. These PANs were estimated to be "4 or 5 fingers above the hills and roofs of the village." Immediately after, the witness made a second observation, at the zenith, and for 15 to 20 seconds, he observed a red glowing point moving like a satellite to the west. However, this object abruptly stopped for 3 or 4 seconds while its brightness rapidly increased, giving it a spherical appearance surrounded by a blurry red halo. It then moved by performing a series of very fast zigzags consisting of very sharp, angular turns, without jerks or accelerations/decelerations, before returning to its initial trajectory, stopping a second time, emitting a series of vibrations, then stopping a third time before disappearing definitively "at the level of the wind turbines" at a very high speed. No other testimony was collected. The conclusion of the investigation report (see document) states that two observations are reported: - the PANs initially observed have a behavior that cannot be equated to that of shooting stars. - then the observation of another PAN with a behavior not assimilable to any known manufactured or natural object. - Rejection of the laser spot hypothesis in the absence of cloudy or foggy conditions. - Inability to explain the zigzag trajectory by micro-nystagmus perception phenomenon. This observation case is not explicable with the elements available to the investigation. Therefore, to complete or clarify these observations, the GEIPAN requested to meet the witness at the observation site for an interview and reconstruction. Even two years later, this meeting could have been decisive. Despite several phone, voicemail, SMS, and email attempts, the witness could not be reached to schedule an appointment. A most recent attempt also failed. It was therefore not possible to advance in explanation, nor even to consolidate the consistency and reliability of the testimony, which remain insufficient given the level of strangeness of the observations described in the questionnaire. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Insufficient Information.
