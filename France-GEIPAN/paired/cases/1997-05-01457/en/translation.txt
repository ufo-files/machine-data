Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case and named CHATEAUNEUF-SUR-LOIRE (45) 24.03.1997 is part of a recent set of cases that have been re-examined. On March 24, 1997, around 6:30 a.m., a driver on the N60 expressway, approximately 4 kilometers from the Fay-aux-Loges (45) interchange, observed a moving luminous phenomenon in the cloudy sky. The PAN is described as composed of several series of 5 or 6 rows of lights across a wide span. These rows of well-ordered lights, one above the other, and bright, do not allow the witness to discern a precise outline. He sees the PAN heading towards La Ferté-Saint-Aubin (45), then towards Sully (45), before getting back on the same axis heading towards La Ferté-Saint-Aubin (45). The witness slows down but continues on his way towards Orléans and the PAN is lost from sight. No other testimony was collected. Some parameters of this observation suggest a confusion with a disco skytracker (see the investigation report):

- Observation of lights without a "solid" object;
- Geometric arrangement: some skytracker models can create an effect of geometrically arranged lights;
- It is a Monday morning, a disco open on Sunday night could leave its outdoor light show until early the next morning;
- Presence of a cloud ceiling of the stratocumulus type, which could serve as a support for the impact of light beams emitted by ground projectors;
- The observation was not far from a major city (Orléans, located about 20 km west by air from the witness) whose outskirts still had numerous discos open in the late 1990s that could use such skytrackers outside for advertising purposes;
- The skytrackers' range is very high, able to reach over 20 km in diameter around the projectors;
- The movements consist of "back-and-forth" actions controlled by the motorization of the ground projectors;
- Many similar observation cases in the GEIPAN archives, especially at this time (see www.geipan.fr search for cases with the keywords "laser" or "Skyrose"). It is impossible, more than 20 years later, to find traces of the facilities operating such projectors in 1997, even more to verify if they owned such equipment that could have been used on the night of the observation. Most of these discotheques are now permanently closed. But given the practice at the time, the size of the urban area of the possible location, this impossibility is neutral for the hypothesis (cannot neither deny nor confirm). On the other hand, there is one point not in favor of the hypothesis: we have no known example of projectors creating a geometric shape like the one observed by the witness. Although this type of figure is within the capabilities of these equipment, there is an element of uncertainty and probability reduction for this hypothesis. The case consistency is poor due to the imprecision or lack of information: - imprecision regarding the observation location, observation duration, and the shape of the PAN (5 or 6 rows described and 4 drawn); - complete absence of data on the color, angular size, and elevation of the PAN. We have an hypothesis that could explain the observation, but the resulting level of uncertainty is too high given the limited consistency of available elements. The GEIPAN classifies the case as C: a non-identifiable phenomenon due to a lack of data.
