**Description:** The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed to improve the accuracy of conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named CHATEAUNEUF-SUR-LOIRE (45) 24.03.1997 is part of a recent batch of cases reassessed. On March 24, 1997, around 6:30 a.m., a driver on the N60 expressway, approximately 4km from the Fay-aux-Loges (45) interchange, observed a luminous phenomenon moving through a cloudy sky. The UFO was described as consisting of several sets of 5 or 6 rows of lights spanning a wide area. These well-ordered rows of bright lights, one above the other, did not allow the witness to discern a precise outline. The witness observed the UFO heading towards La Ferté-Saint-Aubin (45), then Sully (45), before returning to the same axis towards La Ferté-Saint-Aubin (45). The witness slowed down but continued driving towards Orléans, losing sight of the UFO. No other testimonies were collected. Some parameters of this observation suggest a possible confusion with a disco skytracker:

- Observation of lights without a solid object;
- Geometric arrangement: some skytracker models can create an effect of geometrically arranged lights;
- It was a Monday morning, and a disco open on Sunday night could have left its outdoor lighting animation until early the next morning;
- Presence of a stratocumulus cloud ceiling, which could serve as a substrate for the impact of light beams emitted by ground projectors;
- Observation near a major city (Orléans, located about 20 km west by air from the witness) with many discos still open in the late 1990s, potentially using such skytrackers outside for advertising purposes;
- Very long range of skytrackers, capable of reaching over 20 km in diameter around the projectors;
- Movements consisting of "back-and-forth" commands from the motorization of ground projectors;
- Numerous similar observation cases in the GEIPAN archives, especially during this period (see www.geipan.fr, search for cases with keywords "laser" or "Skyrose"). It is impossible, more than 20 years later, to find traces of establishments using such projectors in 1997, or to verify if they had such equipment that could have been used on the night of the observation. Most of these discos are now permanently closed. However, given the practice at the time, the size of the possible urban area, this impossibility is neutral for the hypothesis (neither confirming nor denying it). In contrast, one point does not support the hypothesis: we have no known example of projectors creating a geometric shape like that observed by the witness. Although this type of figure is within the capabilities of these equipment, it introduces an element of uncertainty and reduces the probability of this hypothesis. The consistency of the case is poor due to impreciseness or lack of information:

- Imprecision regarding the observation location, observation duration, and the shape of the UFO (5 or 6 rows described and 4 drawn);
- Complete absence of data on color, angular size, and elevation of the UFO.

We have a hypothesis that could explain the observation, but the resulting level of uncertainty is too high given the limited consistency of available elements. GEIPAN classifies the case as C: Unidentifiable phenomenon due to lack of data.
