--- seg-6591c9c26c5bd50a2c283871 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 14, 2015, at 7:15 a.m., a witness and his wife observed, through fog, a bright point in the sky that seemed to "waver" and slightly increase in size. Only one testimony was collected. The report on a geographical map of the observation direction described by the witness indicates an azimuth of approximately 135° (Southeast) (see attached map). The sky map shows, in this direction, the planet Venus at 22° elevation (compared to 60° reported by the witness) and very bright (magnitude -3.6). The less bright star Arcturus (magnitude 0.15) is at azimuth 117° and elevation 51°. The phenomenon described by the witness has all the characteristics of a very bright star observed through light fog: the other stars are occluded, and the phenomenon appears slightly distorted. The impression that this light "wavered" is characteristic of the autokinetic illusion (see: Perception of the motion of a bright point in the sky). The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of Venus or Arcturus.
