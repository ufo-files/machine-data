Summary: Two campers observed the silent movement of numerous bright points in the sky along a straight trajectory; high probability of a bird misidentification.
--- seg-b23cdb3c82106506b2da3721 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 23, 2017, at 12:41 a.m., a camper (T1) wakes up, leaves his tent, and looks up at the clear sky. After observing a shooting star, he notices in the western direction dozens of bright yellow lights (around one hundred) moving silently across the sky towards the east. The witness wakes up his friend (T2), who observes for 20 to 30 seconds 6 to 8 white lights moving in a straight line and disappearing well before the eastern horizon. No noise is heard. The first witness initially thinks of Thai lanterns but is not convinced by this hypothesis due to the same brightness all the observed points had during his 2 to 3-minute observation. No other testimonies are collected. Note: The witnesses locate the observation in the commune of La DAGUINIERE, but this commune has taken the name of LOIRE-AUTHION (49) since 2016. The analysis demonstrates that the PAN observed by the witnesses were very likely a group of birds, probably gulls, flying at high altitude in a flock from their feeding zone to their nesting zone (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The color and brightness of the PAN are all identical: white and similar in brightness to bright stars, consistent with the appearance of birds at high altitude, their ventral plumage white-lit by the residual light emitted by the city of Angers, located a few kilometers from the witnesses.

- Their point-like shape, the small size of these birds, and the altitude at which they are located prevent the witnesses from seeing their actual shape, reduced to a glowing point.

- Their significant number, in line with the gregarious behavior of these birds, which can move in mass, up to several hundred individuals, grouped in heterogeneous or formation patterns.

- Their straight-line movement at a constant speed.

- The observation zone is geographically located in a nesting area, along the Loire, and a few kilometers from feeding zones (city of Angers).

- The absence of noise: birds return to their roost at nightfall in silence.

The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": strong probability of observing a group of birds, probably gulls, moving from their feeding zone (city of Angers) to their nesting/roosting area.
