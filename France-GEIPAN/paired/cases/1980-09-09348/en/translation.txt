Description: Old observation (1980) but recently reported (2015). During a vacation camp themed "experimental rockets" organized by CNES, a witness observes with binoculars the disappearance of a rocket named Fuch Off, which they believe was absorbed by a spherical object itself pursued by two fighter jets. This observation report is quite unusual: on the one hand, it's an old observation from 1980, declared to GEIPAN in July 2015, and on the other hand, this observation relates to a micro-rocket launch operation for educational purposes under the responsibility of CNES and ANSTJ. GEIPAN conducted an internal investigation with CNES to identify the actors or spectators of this event. The investigation was very difficult after 35 years, but it was still possible to find one of the operators responsible for this rocket launch operation, who still had all the archives of these launches from that time. He wrote a detailed report of this launch (see his report). The rocket was not recovered due to the particularly dense vegetation on the site. The report does not mention anything regarding the sphere described by the witness. The flight of the fighter jets at that time did take place, but without any connection to the launch of the Fuch Off rocket. GEIPAN has no other hypothesis for the silver sphere than a possible misidentification with a pilot balloon (small-diameter balloon, whose trajectory, observed from the ground, indicates the wind speed and direction). The coincidence between the rocket launch, the passage of the sphere, and that of the fighter jets turns out to be fortuitous. Since the operations manager does not mention any particular problem or any other report of this silver sphere despite the large number of spectators, GEIPAN accordingly classifies this case in category B, as a probable misidentification with a balloon.
