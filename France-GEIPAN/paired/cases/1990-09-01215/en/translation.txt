Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named THANN (68) 1990 is part of a recent set of cases that have been re-examined. On September 19, 1990, around 9:45 PM, a couple and two children observed the movements of a white, milky-colored, oval-shaped object in a sky covered by clouds. The object made movements and rotational motions over the MOOSCH forest before disappearing. A neighborhood investigation revealed four other witnesses who had seen similar phenomena on September 19, 20, and 21. During the observation, the GEIPAN had little or no experience with such observations. The accumulated cases since (search for cases on www.geipan.fr using keywords skyrose, skytracer, or laser) allow us to affirm that this observation is fully consistent with the hypothesis of a light spot on the cloudless ceiling created by the use of a ground projector (see the investigation report): - an "oval" shape, according to the perspective created by the observation angle; - back-and-forth and rotational movements; - the presence of a cloud cover; - white color; - brightness and overall appearance of the PAN described by the witnesses as "opaque," "milky," and "lacking luster," with diffuse light not illuminating the surroundings. Given this strong consistency, the hypothesis should be retained despite the fact that the observation day was a Wednesday evening (not a weekend more conducive to a festive event) and that no trace of any local or private manifestation was found. Indeed, 30 years later, the absence of traces is not decisive; nothing rules out the existence of such an event or a weekday session (lasting only 15 minutes) for an event that would have taken place during a weekend and thus not caused any oddity in the sky due to different weather conditions. The consistency is correct, with an on-site investigation conducted by the gendarmes. However, one may regret the absence of angular measurements and photographs or videos taken by the witnesses at the time of the events. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies it as B: observation of light animations (laser, skytracer).
