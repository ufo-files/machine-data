--- seg-f31da46f5aad0abcd8ab5338 [needs-review] ---
Description: This observation case is among the "D" classified cases in 2000 by the SEPRA. It was subject to a new review (see the investigation notes document). On February 11, 2000 at 3:45 AM, a driver en route to work suddenly observed about 30 meters above the ground a elongated object resembling an airplane wing surrounded by yellow flames, followed by a very bright non-blinding yellow trail. The object moved slowly on a horizontal trajectory, making no noise. The witness stopped and got out of his car, but the phenomenon had already disappeared; in fact, the witness only caught a glimpse of it as it passed in front of his car. During the investigation, no air traffic was identified, nor any disruptions to nearby power lines. This case is consistent, and the description of the PAN observed is fairly accurate. The witness's sincerity and credibility were never questioned. The issue is not the witness's visual perception, but the interpretation the witness makes of his observation through his feelings (night driving, astonishment, surprise at not seeing an airplane shape). The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, movement, etc.) with a known astronomical phenomenon: a meteor. In the current GEIPAN classification, this low strangeness case is classified as PAN B, a probable observation related to a natural meteor.
