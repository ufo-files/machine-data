Summary: Nighttime observations of erratic and silent movements of a dim white patch in an overcast sky: probable observation of a skyrose, powerful external projector, or laser sweeping the sky.
--- seg-018832b3b163ffef472bd2e3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 28, 2023, around 9 PM, Witness No. 1 spotted in the sky a white patch, about the size of a soccer ball, slightly glowing, moving rather quickly, erratically, in loops and zigzags, first in front of and then behind them, over a large perimeter. The sky is fairly overcast with a little wind. He thinks of a "lighthouse or beacon," but sees no trail or beam of light. The observation lasted about ten minutes. Witness No. 2, solicited by Witness No. 1, sees what at first makes him think of a bird moving, quite quickly in all directions, but upon closer observation, he changes his mind. He then describes a light in the shape of a veil, then round, bright white and pale at the same time, passing through the clouds. The witness observes no trail, beam, or ray of light. He notes that there is wind, and that the sky is fairly overcast at the height of this fairly bright shape despite the overcast sky. The videos transmitted by witness #1 are not usable. The view of the sky is very noisy. At times, a blurry bright spot is discernible (see the witness's video clip MOV_0914.mp4). The description points towards the observation of a light projection coming from a skyrose or skytracer (external projector): rapid movement in all directions, circular motions, zigzag, spot, light in the form of a veil, round (corresponding to different impacts on the clouds). In order to analyze this hypothesis, a request for weather parameters regarding cloudiness and visibility was made to MétéoFrance. For the time mentioned by the witnesses, we were able to obtain data from 2 weather stations: Nîmes Garons, 35 km North of the witnesses' position, Montpellier Airport, 40 km North-West (see attached documents). At the time mentioned by the witness, around 19:00 UTC, these stations report:

- a low layer of clouds located between 150 and 570 meters, with a cloud cover of 1 to 3 octas,
- a second layer of clouds located between 510 and 1980 meters, with a cloud cover of 5 to 6 octas,
- a third layer of clouds located between 840 and 3000 meters, with a cloud cover of 5 to 8 octas. This means that the clouds were very low and the sky was very overcast, at the level of these two stations. It is likely that this meteorological situation is similar to the location of the witnesses. These are favorable conditions for an observation of a light projection coming from a skyrose or sky-tracer. It is a powerful external projector, presenting one or several light beams sweeping across the sky. It is generally used to indicate the location of an event or phenomenon. The observation of the light spot varies depending on the scanning performed by the projector and the varied shapes of the clouds. Witness No. 1 had considered this hypothesis, but he abandoned it because he did not see a beam coming from the ground. The strangeness of the observation comes from the absence of observation of the beam of light coming from the ground. The optical beam (usually a laser beam) of a skyrose is not always visible. It depends on the content, in the atmosphere between the ground and the base of the clouds, of suspended particles, which can be water droplets, dust, pollen, pollutants... It is the emission of the light beam by these particles that allows its observation. A low particle content in the atmosphere can explain the absence of observation of the beam of light coming from the ground. In addition, the beam can also be obscured by low clouds. The city hall of Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer (13) was contacted to inquire if an event had been organized on this date. August 28 is a time of festivities within the arena context. They were unable to confirm the use of skyrose (sky-tracer), but they reported that several restaurateurs possess them. The hypothesis of observing a skyrose spot is probable: the characteristics described by the witnesses correspond. Nevertheless, we were unable to identify the source. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: probable observation of a skyrose, powerful external projector or laser sweeping the sky.
