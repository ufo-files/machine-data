Description: On October 1st, 2022, from the terrace of his home in Méthamis (84), facing west, the witness observed three circular, white PAN similar to dim halos located relatively low on the western-southwest horizon. The PAN's movements were described by T1 as "a quick left-to-right movement, maintaining the same altitude and covering an area of approximately 20° across the horizon throughout the observation," then "they performed left-to-right and right-to-left crossovers, intersecting and avoiding each other, covering the same area for about 5 minutes at a high speed without slowing down even during direction changes." Two other individuals, alerted by this witness and observing from their home approximately 1 kilometer away from T1's location, witnessed the phenomenon during the final two minutes. They did not report their observation to GEIPAN. The manner in which the observation ceased was not described by T1. Consistency is average with a single witness (the other two independent witnesses did not report their observation to GEIPAN) and no photos or videos of the PAN were available.

According to GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The analysis confirmed T1's hypothesis of observing a skytracer and explained the peculiarity he perceived during the observation: the absence of light beams (see investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The round shape and halo effect of the PAN are typical of low cloud impact spots.
- The white color is commonly used by spotlights.
- The dim brightness.
- The spotlight itself was not visible to T1.
- With excellent horizontal visibility (60 km), no or few particles capable of supporting the light emitted by the spotlights were present in the atmosphere.
- The presence of fog banks capable of supporting the light emitted by the spotlights.
- Rapid, regular movements consisting of back-and-forth without stops, typical of an automated system.
- The observation day supports the hypothesis: Saturday evening is indeed conducive to the use of such spotlights in a festive context, such as for advertising a specific event at a nightclub, a local party, or for commercial animation.
- The range of spotlights of the "skytracer" type can reach or exceed 20 kilometers, and their light can be visible from even farther away, as in a festive or commercial context around the city of Avignon, located in the observation axis. Although the investigator was unable to pinpoint the exact location of such a spotlight, the hypothesis remains highly probable in light of other testimonial elements.

The case is classified as "A": very probable observation of a skytracer.
