Summary: Multiple observations of a luminous phenomenon in the night sky: observation of the relighting of the second stage of the Falcon 9 and the train of Starlink satellites from the same launch.
--- seg-28adaf7d8040e2c639a50bd0 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN has received numerous testimonies regarding observations of a particular phenomenon seen in the sky on May 4, 2021, around 10:55 PM. The witnesses were located in several departments and regions of France. The various terms to describe the PAN in the clear sky are: halo, cloud, bar, bright point(s), white/yellow silent moving NO-SE. This national case was treated in June 2021 and was the subject of a publication by GEIPAN which classified the case as B: High probability of misidentification with a Falcon 9 launch event during the 25th launch of Starlink satellites. Other similar observations occurred in the months that followed. These incidents have led to additional investigations involving the launch specialists and the Operational Space Surveillance Service of the CNES (see the investigation note). The explanations for the observations made on May 4, 2021 are as follows:

- "Luminous or evanescent ball," "halo": gas bubble formed by gases ejected over a short period (of no more than a few seconds) illuminated by the sun and observed through the diffusion of sunlight onto a black background (for the observer, the sun has just set);

- "Butterfly": symmetric ejection of gases (and possibly ergol compounds in gaseous phase) contained within the tanks (see diagram);

- "Brilliant point": more intense area "in the middle of the butterfly": the location where these gases have not yet dispersed (see diagram);

- "Succession of points": grouped Starlink satellites. This case, initially classified "B," is now fully identified and classified as A: observation of the relight of the second stage of the Falcon 9 and the Starlink satellite train from the same launch.
