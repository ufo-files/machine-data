Description: The witness was driving on the A8 highway towards Nice early in the morning, accompanied by his wife (the driver) and children in the backseat. At Antibes (06), he observed on his left, northwest through the driver-side window, a PAN of elongated shape and red-orange color, reflecting sunlight. The phenomenon appeared from a white cloud mass, descended rapidly vertically, and disappeared behind a rocky formation. The witness estimated the apparent length of the PAN to be about 4 cm using a rule held at arm's length. The observation lasted between 5 and 7 seconds. No other family members saw the phenomenon, as the wife was focused on driving and the children were sleeping in the back. The consistency* of this case is average, with only one witness, no photos or videos of the PAN, and suboptimal observation conditions. *According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation examined the hypothesis that the phenomenon could correspond to a solitary cloud, located at a lower altitude than others and reflecting the light of the rising Sun (see the investigation report). This hypothesis is based on the following:

- The elongated shape observed is compatible with that of a cloud.
- The color matches that of the rising Sun, which was at approximately 7° elevation at the time of observation, during the "golden hour," the period of sunrise or sunset characterized by orange light.
- The presence of a solitary cloud is plausible, as noted by the GEIPAN weather expert: "The hypothesis of a solitary cloud reflecting the orange light of the rising Sun seems credible. According to the Sun-orange cloud axis, clouds above appear bright while those below appear dark." Therefore, the other observed clouds, white in color, are at a higher altitude.
- The perceived "quite rapid" vertical movement could result from a gradual rise in relief in the observation axis. Along the A8 highway in the probable observation area, reliefs on the left could have created the illusion that the cloud was descending.

The investigation also revealed that the apparent altitude of the PAN was likely low, which favors the observation of a vertical movement illusion, with the phenomenon and landscape elements visually close. This can also explain why it was obscured by landscape elements after exiting the rocky formation that stopped its visibility, as the witness did not see it again. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a solitary colored cloud by the rising Sun.
