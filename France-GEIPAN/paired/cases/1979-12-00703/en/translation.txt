Summary: Observations of an orange stationary object in the overcast sky to the southeast, then rapid departure: Astronomical misperception with the rising sun.
--- seg-7219bdd41cac95f1d80b428a [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This previously classified observation case D and named SOLIGNAC (87) 1979 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On December 20, 1979, around 8:45 a.m., three people observed for 30 to 40 seconds a stationary luminous phenomenon in the sky heading southeast. The PAN was described as a very bright reddish-orange rectangular, oval, or circular mass, depending on the testimonies. The witnesses reported a gray overcast with a low cloud ceiling. The PAN suddenly pivoted to take the shape of a cigar and departed, leaving behind an orange trail turning white. The object disappeared into the clouds. Descriptive elements were made in relation to the sun. "The object reminded me of a sunset," "the object in question was larger than the sun," "I thought it was an eclipse" (see investigation notes). The rising sun is precisely in the direction of observation, and its low angular height gave the PAN its dominant color, that is, bright red. The variable shape of the PAN, the short duration of the observation, and the rapid departure of the PAN can be explained by the presence of clouds that masked the sun, visible only through a gap in the cloud cover. This point of the phenomenon is supported by elements of testimony in favor of a PAN in a sky with non-uniform cloud cover. "This object was half-masked and intermittently by a black cloud," "the craft seemed to be propelled, then it disappeared into the clouds." The presence of non-uniform cloud cover, the perfect consistency between the true position of the sun and the location and description of the PAN, and the absence of observation of the sun at a deviation from the observation of the PAN constitute a set that leaves no doubt about the misperception. This case is well consistent (three witnesses have given their testimony at the gendarmerie), the description of the PAN observed is quite precise. Misperception with the rising or setting sun is not a rare case in the annals of the GEIPAN and concerns all types of witnesses. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Rising sun.
