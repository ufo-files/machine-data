Description: An amateur astronomer observed the sky towards Mars on Monday, May 28, 2012, at 1:10 a.m., and suddenly noticed the passage of a white streak followed by a red-orange fireball. The phenomenon then disappeared, hidden by the church roof to the northwest. A muffled detonation was heard. The next day, despite his searches in the area around his residence, no trace of the phenomenon was found on the ground. Based on the objective elements defined in the investigation report (namely, the shape, color, and behavior of the object; the presence of a white streak and distinctive noises; the existence of other testimonies and video recordings showing the same phenomenon during the same time frame, and the disappearance of the phenomenon to the northwest, confirmed by one of the video recordings), we can conclude that the phenomenon exhibits all the characteristics of a bolide (the fall of a meteorite, commonly called a meteor), observed from Normandy and the Paris region. This case is classified as "A" for a certain observation of a bolide.
