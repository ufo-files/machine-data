Description: On May 8, 1979, at 4:40 a.m., a driver observes a puzzling luminous phenomenon. A reddish-orange glowing ball with "pop-top antennas" moves and quickly disappears to the southwest. A gendarme analysis indicates it cannot be the moon, as verified the next day at the same time; the moon was further "left" of the vehicle than the witness's observation. In fact, a precise analysis of the sky shows that on May 8, at the time of the observation, the moon was indeed in the observation axis and disappeared at the observation time. It is therefore clear that the witness observed a quite bright sunset. The presence of clouds likely caused reflections or radiations, and the witness's reported rapid disappearance is easily explained by the fact that she herself was driving. The verification done on May 9 at the same time is not conclusive as the moon is then further west and higher on the horizon, allowing for its unambiguous identification.
