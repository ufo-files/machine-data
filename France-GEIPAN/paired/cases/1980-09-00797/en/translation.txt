Description: On the night of August 17, 1980, at 1:30 a.m., two witnesses were driving on National Road 1. Upon reaching Cap Houssaye, they briefly spotted three stationary human-like figures on a hill overlooking the sea, approximately 15 meters from the edge of the road. These figures, illuminated by the headlights, were described as being roughly 2 meters tall with a robust build, and having orange, oval-shaped swellings on their heads. No unusual noises were heard. The following evening, one of the witnesses saw a very bright blue light on the ocean in the direction of Cap Houssaye from her home. The witnesses did not report this to the gendarmerie until August 30. After being dispatched to the scene, the gendarmerie found no trace in the area. The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to be more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named SAINT-GILLES (974) 1980 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been re-examined. The initial classification of D, unexplained, can be understood as a witness (T2) opposing the hypothesis of divers. However, the reported elements of normal human shapes, except for the glowing under the headlights and the large orange eyes, correspond well to this hypothesis. The possibility of divers at the coast in the middle of the night in August on Réunion Island is plausible, even without an apparent vehicle parked on the road. We note that the frightened witnesses did not stop to observe the evolution of these shapes, nor did they look for a parked vehicle nearby. Without justification for the witness's objection, it is this hypothesis that should be retained. But the observation of a ground phenomenon without an associated description of a phenomenon observed in the sky or at the interface between sky and earth (aerospace) does not fall within the competence of the GEIPAN. Even if the hypothesis of divers is the most plausible, the GEIPAN prefers to qualify the case as "unexploitable" at its level. The case is classified as C.
