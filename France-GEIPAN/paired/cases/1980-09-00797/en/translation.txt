Description: On August 17, 1980, at 1:30 a.m., two witnesses were driving on National Road 1. Upon arriving at Cape Houssaye, they spot for a few seconds three stationary human-like forms on a hill overlooking the sea, about fifteen meters from the edge of the road. These shapes, illuminated by the headlights, are described as being approximately 2 meters in size with a robust build, and having orange and ovoid swellings on their heads. No particular noise was heard. The following evening, one of the witnesses will see from her home, in the direction of Cape Houssaye, a very bright blue light on the ocean towards the location of the observation. The witnesses only report the incident to the gendarmerie on August 30th. Urgently dispatched to the scene, the gendarmerie will find no evidence in the area. The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case named SAINT-GILLES (974) 1980 is part of a set of cases recently reviewed. The initial classification as D (unexplained) can be understood by the opposition of a witness (T2) to the hypothesis of divers. For all that, the reported elements of normal human shapes, apart from the glowing character under the headlights and the large orange eyes, correspond well to this hypothesis. The possibility of divers in the coastal waters at night in August on Réunion is plausible, even in the apparent absence of a vehicle parked on the road. We note that the frightened witnesses did not stop to observe the progression of these forms, nor did they search for a vehicle parked nearby. In the absence of justification from the witness for his objection, it is the hypothesis that should be retained. However, the observation of phenomena on the ground without associated descriptions of phenomena observed in the sky or at the interface between the sky and earth (aerospace) does not fall within the competence of the GEIPAN. Even if the diver hypothesis is the most plausible, the GEIPAN prefers to qualify the case as inexplorable at its level. The case is classified C.
