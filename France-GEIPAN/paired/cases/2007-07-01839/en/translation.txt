--- seg-77da658fd135d33ed6c21534 [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 14, 2007 at 2:35 PM Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), the captain of an AF flight reported a T-CAS (Traffic Collision Avoidance System) alert indicating very close traffic to the aircraft. On the screens, the pilots saw a red blip at 30 nautical miles (55 km) and 300 feet (100 m) above the aircraft. However, no object was visually observed by the pilots despite exceptional visibility. Air traffic control detected no unknown traffic on their radar screen. A preliminary investigation conducted by the Bourget Investigation and Analysis Office for the DGAC concluded that the observed phenomenon was due to false echoes. The expertise performed for the GEIPAN highlights a probable malfunction of an onboard instrument: the active traffic and collision avoidance system (T-CAS).
