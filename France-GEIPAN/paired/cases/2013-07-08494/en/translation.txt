--- seg-84cccb5972177c16b73a422c [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 16, 2013, at 10:45 PM, a witness lying on their terrace was observing the stars. He is intrigued by the silent and swift passage of three "pastilles" of pale-colored triangles across the foggy sky. A slight halo is observed. The objects disappear into the horizon after 30 seconds. Only one testimony is collected. The sky is overcast in Agen. Air traffic is quite heavy at high altitude (7000 to 12000 meters) but cannot explain the observed phenomenon. The GEIPAN considered several hypotheses regarding this observation: - A nightclub laser: the observation time seems short, and the witness who considered this hypothesis refutes it. - Birds (seagulls) illuminated by city lights, but immersed in low clouds: it seems unlikely that birds would remain illuminated as they climb in altitude. The apparent size (2/3 Moon) and circular shape do not fit this hypothesis. The witness who considered this hypothesis refutes it. Given that, the dynamics of the phenomenon are quite compatible with those of birds. The witness mentions that seagulls seemed to react to the phenomenon. - A visual (ophthalmic) problem with the witness or an ophthalmic migraine could have caused the phosphenes. The blurred outlines and the impression of flat lights are compatible with this hypothesis. It seems difficult to verify this hypothesis a posteriori through a medical examination. As none of these hypotheses can be confirmed, and it is a rather distant and vague phenomenon, the GEIPAN classifies this case as category "C" due to lack of information or corroboration.
