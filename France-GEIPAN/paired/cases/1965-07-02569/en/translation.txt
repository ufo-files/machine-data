Description: Recent report of an old observation made on July 23, 1965, around 6 PM. A metallic-looking triangular object is stationary in the sky and reflects sunlight strongly. This case of observation by multiple witnesses of a fixed bright object in the evening sky has all the characteristics of a tetrahedral stratospheric weather balloon. The luminous and metallic appearance is distinctive, the apparent immobility is not uncommon, their very high altitude (up to 40 km) makes their movements appear very slow, and winds at that altitude are very variable. The altitude calculation made by the witness is relevant; it is well consistent with this hypothesis of a weather balloon that reflected sunlight for up to 40 minutes after sunset. That evening, the evident presence of the planet Venus is noted, which was brilliant to the west shortly after sunset. It is surprising that the witnesses do not mention it; but it was much further to the west and lower than the observed phenomenon. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a stratospheric balloon.
