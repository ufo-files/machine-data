Description: The witness reports a nocturnal observation of 10 to 15 seconds of the slow and silent passage over Tarbes (65) of a dark triangular PAN with 5 low-intensity lights arranged in a V. The witness declared on September 23, 2019, an observation made on November 6, 2018 at 23:19 (over a year earlier). It is based on a previous written account. The witness also submitted her testimony on an internet site (neither the account nor the recording of the testimony on the website are dated). There is uncertainty regarding the date of the observation. The witness reported clear skies allowing for the observation of stars, which is not corroborated by the weather verification for the day of the observation, which was a well-covered sky of 6/8 octas (see the investigation report). Only one testimony was collected, so no comparison with other testimonies could be made. However, despite only one witness, the consistency of the case is fairly good since the testimony is quite detailed. One hypothesis is favored, that of a misidentification with illuminated birds by streetlamps. The shape of the PAN and its apparent slow movement strongly suggest a misidentification with birds, particularly migratory birds. The strangeness of the observation lay in the description of the shape ("It was a black triangle, not very big, with 5 lights arranged to create a V. The lights were fairly dim but a solid shape could clearly be made out. It flew over me without a sound"). A group of birds flying together can perfectly create the illusion of a single object composed of several bright points. Moreover, the V-shape is classic for this type of formation and the apparent strangeness diminishes significantly in light of the ornithological hypothesis. It is worth noting that the points are blurry and dim, which is consistent with birds whose plumage reflects the urban lighting of the Tarbes (65) metropolitan area. The hypothesis of a misidentification with migratory birds is strengthened by the fact that the observation occurred in early November, which is the peak migration period, in an area where many migration corridors are recorded. Many bird species migrate at this time of year, particularly cranes and gray geese. The absence of sound remains consistent with the hypothesis of birds. It should be recalled that the sky was covered with a cloud ceiling at 2880 meters, approximately 2500 meters above the ground relative to the city of Tarbes (65). This determines that the altitude of the PAN could not have exceeded this limit, which is again consistent with birds. The migraction.net site provides a lot of information on the subject and constitutes an important observational database. It is worth noting that the observation duration of 10 to 15 seconds seems short compared to the flight of migratory birds, but this duration may have been likely underestimated by the witness who describes a seemingly slow movement ("it wasn't going fast"). In light of all these elements, the case is classified B, probable misidentification with birds.
