Summary: Observation of a bright point moving at a steady speed and on a curved trajectory in the sky: observation of the International Space Station (ISS).

Description: On March 2, 2025, at 6:40 a.m., the sole witness is at the window of their home in Vals-des-Tilles (Haute-Marne) when they spot to the west a bright point at approximately 40° elevation, moving at a regular speed comparable to that of the ISS. As it advances, the UFO PAN changes its trajectory towards the south without passing over the witness, disappearing after 20 to 30 seconds of observation at an azimuth of 160° and an elevation of 30°. The witness considers the hypothesis of the ISS, which they rule out due to the object's dimmer light and, especially, the observed curved trajectory. With average consistency—one unique witness and no photos or videos of the PAN—this case is related to the observation of the ISS (see the investigation report).

According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The confirmed presence of the ISS by the CNES Orbital Center at the time and coordinates reported by the witness for the PAN
- The appearance and behavior of the PAN are identical to those of the ISS: a very bright white point moving at a regular speed. With dawn, it is normal that the PAN's light is dimmer than typically observed for the ISS. As for the apparently curved trajectory of the PAN, it perfectly matches that of the ISS, which is never strictly straight due to the Earth's roundness. The perceived change in trajectory is due to a perspective effect.

Finally, it appears that if the ISS was not the PAN, the witness would have seen another distinct bright point since the ISS orbital station passage is confirmed. The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as "A": observation of the ISS.
