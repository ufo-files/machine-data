Description: This observation case is part of cases classified as "C" by the SEPRA under the name EPINAC (71) 16.03.1994, and is now being reassessed (see the Investigation Notes document). On March 16, 1994, at 20:10 or 20:30, in TINTRY (71), a person walking home noticed a milky cloud in front of them, from which a large orange-colored crescent-shaped light appeared three times for a few seconds. No other testimonies were collected during the gendarmerie investigation. This case is weak (single witness), and the description of the PAN observed is fairly accurate. The witness's sincerity and credibility were never questioned. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: a crescent Moon viewed through a cloud layer. Moreover, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky. The issue is not the witness's visual perception but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, partial observation of the Moon through a cloud). In the current GEIPAN classification, this weak strangeness case is classified as "A," an observation related to a misperception with the Moon.
