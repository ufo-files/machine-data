**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named ARGENTEUIL (95) 13.07.1979 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. On July 13, 1979, around 11:30 PM, two witnesses on a balcony on the 7th floor spotted in the sky a reddish-orange sphere coming from the northwest towards the center of Paris. The PAN appears to hover over La Défense-Nanterre and abruptly goes dark. The observation lasted approximately thirty seconds. Three minutes later, the two witnesses observed at the location of the first PAN's disappearance a flat, bright white shape moving in the sky and disappearing very quickly on an ascending trajectory. The witnesses were stunned. No other testimony was collected. Some parameters of this observation initially suggest a double confusion with an aircraft and then a meteor:

**For the aircraft:**
- Red color, similar to that of aircraft headlights, which can appear red in certain configurations.
- Initial speed estimated by the witness as equivalent to that of an airplane.

**For the meteor:**
- White color.
- High brightness.
- "Ascending into space" corresponding to a trajectory oriented towards the zenith.
- "Flat" shape surrounded by a "round" shape.

However, there are points of uncertainty or strangeness regarding this double explanation:
- The first phase ends with a stop and then extinction. So, the aircraft would be a helicopter, and the dimming of the light would be caused by the arrival of a cloud.
- The second phase starts at the same location. There would thus be a coincidental match if it were a meteor. But, the testimony seems to describe the second phase as a continuation of the first, while a meteor creates an impression of speed that has nothing to do with an aircraft.
- Additionally, it mentions a "sharp turn" after the resumption. "The craft then traced a right angle for a fraction of a second, then ascended into space at a speed defying all known laws of modern technology."

We note that the description is ambiguous. "Ascending into space" could correspond to the classic perception witnesses have when a light dims and disappears. The witness logically assumes that the non-instantaneous disappearance of the light is due to a distance escape of a PAN and there was no necessary perception of angular displacement. The "turn" expression would then come from the "catch-up" of this escape in space with the first phase, which corresponded to a horizontal movement. This interpretation would no longer be compatible with a meteor, but could correspond to the extinction of aircraft headlights or a star's light with the intervention of a cloud for the star. However, no bright star is present in the direction of Nanterre, and regarding the aircraft of the first phase, it would be necessary to explain the change in appearance and color between the two phases. This remains within the realm of possibility since we are already in a context of clouds affecting the intensity of light and thus also the color of lights.

The observation is characterized by its lack of consistency. Essential data are missing:
- Verified observation duration for the second PAN.
- Angular height of the PAN on the horizon.
- Precise azimuths of appearance for the second PAN and disappearance for both PAN (the azimuth of appearance of the first PAN is provided by the witnesses).
- Angular dimensions (length and width) of the PAN.
- Separate testimony from the second witness. The two witnesses were likely heard together and their two testimonies combined into one statement.
- No investigation was conducted by the gendarmes. We have hypotheses that could explain the observation, but the resulting level of uncertainty is too high given the limited consistency of the available elements (information level and reliability).

The case is classified as C: Unexploitable due to a lack of reliable information.
