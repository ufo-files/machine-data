Summary: Long observation of a very bright fixed object to the left of the Moon: astronomical misidentification with Jupiter.
--- seg-b487848fec4583a6852013a4 [needs-review] ---
**Description:** On March 21, 2016, at 11 PM, a witness observed in the sky towards the South a very bright, fixed, white round object, to the left of the Moon. No particular noise was heard. The observation with the naked eye and then with binoculars lasted for an hour. The object had completely disappeared by 4 AM. No other testimonies were collected. The witness was surprised by Jupiter. This is not uncommon; the planet's appearance is much brighter than a star and does not twinkle. Although the witness attributes to it a brightness comparable to the Moon and an enormous size (but still much smaller than the Moon in his drawing), there is no doubt that it is Jupiter. Indeed, the description, made over a 5-hour period, of the PAN's trajectory in the sky relative to that of the Moon, as well as the witness's drawing, perfectly correspond to the celestial configuration that night. In the reconstruction (see PPT document), we find the witness's description: the PAN is Jupiter "to the left of the Moon" and "the star to the right of the Moon" actually visible, is either Alphard (magnitude 1.95 to the right below, as in the drawing) or Regulus (magnitude 1.35 to the right above). Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Misidentification with Jupiter.
