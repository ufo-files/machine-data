Description: Recent witness report (Sept 2013) of an old observation (date uncertainty, October/November). The witness recounts an observation likely in October 1981, around 11:00 PM and lasting 2 to 3 minutes (Saturday, October 17th arbitrarily assigned as the date). The witness, bored by a TV show, looked out the door-window and was surprised to see 5 to 6 orange-red points pass slowly on a straight trajectory, located on the edge of a dark object. After 1 to 2 minutes, the PAN changed direction, taking a rising path before disappearing suddenly into the clear sky. The witness reported a color change (white) during acceleration. No particular noises were heard during the observation. Another witness observed the scene and reported via email in January 2014 seeing, to the west, a string of red lights arranged in a half-circle. The PAN disappeared and then reappeared, departing at extremely high speed towards the northwest in the sky. No other testimony about the phenomenon was collected.

The hypothesis of a misidentification with a particularly surprising agricultural or construction vehicle is not entirely ruled out despite the late hour (11:00 PM in October or November). This was, in fact, the witness's initial hypothesis. This hypothesis would clash with the height of the lights described (4 or 5 meters in the trees), and especially with the rapid disappearance of the PAN at a certain elevation in the sky.

The oldness of the observation and the uncertainty over the date (October or November 1981) make it impossible to explore this vehicle hypothesis. And without another hypothesis, this observation remains strange. On the other hand, it's a testimony given more than 30 years later, thus prone to imprecision or memory error. The second observer (spouse) describes a phenomenon of similar nature but with much fewer details. This latter did not wish to testify (a short email sent to the investigator). The observers did not leave their homes to approach the location, perceived at 30 or 35 meters, where the PAN had moved. This was done the next day, indicating that the strangeness experienced was not very strong, or even not too strong or frightening, as the witness noted "not having been overly disturbed, even though aware of witnessing something surprising."

Thus, this is an observation that remains strange as it lacks a probable explanation with a higher than 20% likelihood. But it's also an observation lacking consistency, in terms of information and reliability (30 years later, no precise date, a present observer who does not wish to testify).

Consequently, GEIPAN is classified as C: lack of reliable information.
