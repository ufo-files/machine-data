Description: On June 12, 2022, at around 6:10 PM local time, in Chanteloup-en-Brie (77), the witness, while swimming in their pool, observed a white light contrasting with the blue sky at approximately 40 degrees above the horizon, heading south. It eventually disappeared after about ten seconds. The witness continued their swim. Out of curiosity, they looked again in the direction of the PAN, hoping to see something again, which they did. They got out of the pool to retrieve their mobile phone and take photographs and two videos. The witness described the PAN as a very bright disk, intermittently visible, and, according to them, stationary in a southerly direction. After 15 to 20 minutes, feeling exhausted, the witness stopped observing. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, the consistency of this case is assessed as good: despite a single testimony, two photographs, and two videos associated with the observation, could be utilized and analyzed (see investigation report).

Consistency refers to the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony. The hypothesis explored, that of observing a highly reflective Mylar fantasy balloon, is supported by the following elements:

- A very slight and slow movement of the PAN along a straight trajectory, evident from the analysis of the photographs. This movement could be heading southeast, in line with the wind present at the time of observation, up to an altitude of approximately 1500 meters.

- Its appearance is entirely consistent with that of such a balloon, whose aluminized envelope very strongly reflects the sunlight's rays, intermittently, depending on the surface exposed to the sun and the balloon's rotation on itself, according to the wind. The low angular size of the PAN does not allow for totally certain determination of the direction of its movement, nor does it enable providing precise and reliable estimates of its potential speed.

Classification as 'A': Observation of a Mylar fantasy balloon.
