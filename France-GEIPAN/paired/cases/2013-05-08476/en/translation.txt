Description: On Monday, May 20, 2013, at approximately 4:05 PM, a person lying on a beach in Menton is intrigued by a phenomenon observed offshore, above the sea. The witness sees a white object at the center of a translucent, concentric aura in the clear sky. The phenomenon moves much faster than a Western-bound plane towards East-Southeast, and the witness hears no noise. The observation lasts 10 to 15 seconds before the phenomenon disappears into the distance. Only one testimony is collected. The witness observes from the Menton beach (see map) a white, elongated object surrounded by a well-rounded aura, flying over the sea at a distance they cannot estimate. The witness mentions clear skies, but local weather (see Météociel record) indicates partly cloudy skies (hazy sky?). This description evokes an airplane observed in the middle of a luminous halo caused by sunlight reflecting off the fuselage, according to an atmospheric optical phenomenon known as the Brocken specter (see atmospheric optical phenomena, but with available information it is difficult to confirm this hypothesis. Moreover, the witness estimates that the phenomenon's speed is not compatible with that of a plane. Without photography or cross-referencing with other testimonies or measurements, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" due to lack of information.
