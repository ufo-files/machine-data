Summary: Long observation of intermittent flashes in the sky: intermittent reflections of the sun on a space debris (rocket body METEOSAT-7 AKM (N25353)).
--- seg-be9c65bc7e6bdb62a123008e [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: While observing the sky towards the southeast on March 16, 2026, around 23:50, the witness spots a bright flash, similar to a star turning on and off (same size, 3 or 4 times the light intensity of a star). The witness then counts approximately 10 flashes from his arrival on his terrace; it takes him 3 flashes to precisely locate the fixed point where the event occurs and get the idea of counting the intervals, namely 4 (approximately) flashes spaced 27 seconds apart, and finally the last 3 flashes were spaced irregularly at approximately 48-52 seconds, and the signal did not return in the following minutes. The observation of all the flashes is re-evaluated during the investigation in less than 4 minutes. Phase 1: Detection of 3 non-timed flashes (unknown time but let's consider 2 intervals of 27s = 54s). Phase 2: Measured: 4 flashes spaced 27 seconds apart, that's 3 intervals of 27 seconds [3x27s = 81s]. Phase 3: End of phenomenon, last 3 flashes spaced 48 seconds and 52 seconds apart then stop [48s + 52s = 100s]. Approximate but realistic duration: 100 + 81 + 54 = 235 s (~3 min 55) During the investigation, this assessment was validated by the witness who specifies that the 30 minutes mentioned in the questionnaire corresponds to the time spent in his garden observing the sky. During the observation, the witness reports observing a shooting star "fall very stealthily" (southward direction), and then, after the last flash, two similar shooting stars to the first appeared simultaneously, very brief and thin, manifesting lower in the sky, diagonal downward, one to the left and the other to the right. These ancillary appearances are very likely related to meteors or shooting stars from the Antihelion zone near the constellation of the Lion (as specified by the witness). The GEIPAN requested the Operational Space Surveillance Center (COSE) to determine which satellites were visible in the direction of observation, according to the elevation recorded, moving at a slow or nearly fixed rate for an observer, and capable of explaining the perception of immobility for 4 minutes. The comparative analysis of candidates identified by COSE shows that active or passive geostationary satellites (THAICOM 3, PALAPA C2, PAKSAT-1, JCSAT-1, OPTUS B1) do not exhibit the necessary physical characteristics to produce structured periodic reflections as described. On the other hand, the object METEOSAT-7 AKM (N25353), due to its nature as debris and its favorable geometry, is the only candidate capable of coherently explaining all the characteristics described of the phenomenon. Indeed, this significant debris is a rocket body with a motor (AKM), of cylindrical shape with a large metallic surface, rotating uncontrollably but with a stable frequency, capable of reflecting sunlight at a regular but possibly intermittent frequency. This type of debris follows a graveyard orbit, elliptical, and it may appear stationary when at its highest point (which corresponds to the 50° elevation assessed by the witness) as its movement is barely perceptible for 4 minutes. The intermittence of the reflection flashes reinforces this impression of immobility. Note that this rocket body is still in orbit and has been tracked since 1997. It was this apparent immobility and the intermittent flashes that constituted the strangeness experienced by the witness. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": intermittent reflections of the sun on the body of the METEOSAT-7 AKM rocket (N25353).
