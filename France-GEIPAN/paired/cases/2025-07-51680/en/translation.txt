Description: The sole witness to this observation case is in their garden in the commune of La Haie-Fouassière (44) on the night of July 17, 2025, between midnight and 2 AM, when they observe numerous white light points moving from southwest to east. The witness also notes peculiarities during their observation: rapid and random changes in trajectory, variations in speed, light flashes, and overlapping trajectories. Only one testimony is collected. Although the witness was able to record video of the PAN, analysis revealed it to be unusable as the PAN was not visible. This element, along with the fact that the witness was the only known present, does not allow the case file to be given a consistency* other than average. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.

The analysis (see the investigation report) focused on multiple satellite observations for the following reasons:

- The point-like shape, white color, and low brightness are entirely consistent with the appearance of such satellites.
- A significant number of light points.
- The said prograde motion, from southwest to east, is consistent with that of the vast majority of satellites.
- The observation took place during an optimal time slot for observing a maximum number of satellites, between midnight and 2 AM, from the end of astronomical twilight until the time when the most visible satellites in low Earth orbit enter the Earth's shadow.

Physiological effects related to the autokinetic phenomenon contribute to the witness's perception of trajectories and speeds that can sometimes appear erratic, rapid, and random. This phenomenon is described in detail on this page (click on "movement of a light point"). It certainly contributes to the strong strangeness perceived by the witness. Light flashes are also sometimes observed: they are entirely typical, occurring in favorable geometric configurations between the positions of the setting Sun for the observer and the satellites. This can generate surface reflections on antennas or solar panels, among other things.

Finally, the significant number of visible satellites makes it entirely possible for their trajectories to cross, with two or three satellites appearing to come very close, or even merge in the witness's perception, without being at the same altitude.

The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as "B": probable reflection on satellites.
