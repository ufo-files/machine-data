--- seg-1027356497ea78aa3c449842 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 18, 2014, around 9 PM, two witnesses were intrigued by a luminous phenomenon in the sky. Observed through a camera with zooming capabilities, the object appeared to be made up of several colors and moved erratically. The witness described it as a larger-than-average, flickering star, eventually identified as a highly mobile, light-emitting object, similar to a kite. The observation lasted for an hour. From a location near the train station, the direction of observation was roughly indicated as "above Draguignan," corresponding to an azimuth of 335° to 360°. It should be noted that the city of Draguignan is not directly visible from this location: the witness's azimuth estimation is likely approximate. The very bright star Vega is visible at an elevation of 8.4° and an azimuth of 317° at 9:24 PM. Contacted by phone, the witness stated that they had seen the same luminous object multiple nights in a row, approximately in the same location. Observing a light point for nearly an hour above the horizon, multiple nights in a row, strongly suggests the observation of a star. The changes in color are due to scintillation caused by atmospheric turbulence; the observed oscillations are also due to scintillation, but also to the autokinetic or autokinesis illusion, a common perceptual illusion when observing a light point in darkness (see the perception of movement of a light point in the sky). Since the direction of observation is not precisely known, the identification of the star is not certain. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a star above the horizon; Vega appears to be a good candidate.
