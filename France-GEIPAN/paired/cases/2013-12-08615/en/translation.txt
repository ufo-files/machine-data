Description: A witness reports an observation lasting 30 minutes on December 10, 2013, around 1 a.m. This lengthy observation of a fixed bright point corresponds to a probable misidentification with Sirius in the south-southeast: Magnitude -1.44, with twinkling. The weather report for the day describes a strong mistral with an atmosphere that is both very clear and turbulent, highly conducive to intense starlight twinkling. The witness also noticed a bright point in the direction of Orange (Southwest) in mid-December between 6:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m., which is the planet Venus. The photos, taken with an exposure time of 4 seconds, show light zig-zags produced by the operator's movements. During email exchanges with the GEIPAN, the witness also reports bright points near the ground, along the axis of Air Base 115 (Orange Caritat Aerodrome), which could be the result of activities at the base. The GEIPAN had difficulty getting the witness to specify the exact observation directions of the bright points observed, and therefore absolutely confirm the hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of the star Sirius
