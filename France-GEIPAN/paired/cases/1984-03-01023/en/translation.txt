Description: On Saturday, March 24, 1984, starting at 5:50 a.m., five nuns from a convent observed a PAN in the early morning sky for 10 minutes. This oval-shaped object glowed in the night, moving very quickly from left to right and up and down. The witnesses stopped observing to resume their activities, leaving the PAN still visible in an easterly direction. This case (formerly named SARAMON (32)) is part of a series of observation cases that are currently under review (see the "Investigation Notes" document). This case is consistent (five witnesses), and the description of the PAN observed is fairly precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a skytracer light display. Additionally, the observation date (Saturday morning) aligns with a typical day for skytracer usage, and there were clouds that could have reflected a skytracer. However, due to the uniqueness of the observation by the same witnesses or by neighbors, and the impossibility of verifying this with the nightclubs of that era, this hypothesis could not be confirmed. The issue is not the visual perception of the witnesses but their interpretation of their observation through their feelings (surprise, observing an artificial device new at the time of the incident during the night). In the current GEIPAN classification, this low-strangeness case is classified as 'C', due to a lack of overlaps despite resembling a misidentification with a skytracer.
