Summary: Sunrise observation from Piton de la Fournaise (Réunion Island) of a bright object with a trail; two witnesses took photographs of the PAN; likely atmospheric entry of a Starlink® satellite.
--- seg-c4ebcb4ba31d23188cbeaca9 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 4, 2025, at 3:00 AM, the main witness (T1) began ascending Mount Piton de la Fournaise (Réunion Island) from the volcano refuge, accompanied by three people, including a second witness (T2), to watch the sunrise from the summit. Approximately two-thirds of the way up, under a clear sky, T1 briefly distanced himself from the group and spotted a glowing object moving across the sky. The phenomenon was observed for 2 to 3 minutes. The object followed a horizontal trajectory, from left to right, before disappearing on the horizon. Its initial brightness, white-yellow, progressed to a more intense yellow, without flickering, and left a visible trail behind. T1 estimated its apparent speed as faster than a plane but slower than a shooting star. Near the end of the observation, he distinguished two very close light sources, suggesting a possible separation into two elements progressing on the same trajectory. T1 and T2 photographed the phenomenon, capturing six usable images on which the object is clearly visible. Consistency is good: although only T1 reported the observation to the GEIPAN, the photographs collected could be analyzed and provided additional information, allowing, in particular, to reconstruct with great precision the trajectory of the PAN.

According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The perceived strangeness by T1 is low: he believes the observation likely corresponds to a rational and identifiable phenomenon, possibly listed in an astronomical or space database, given the duration and visual amplitude of the object. However, unable to identify it, he reported it to the GEIPAN. The strangeness remains low after analysis, as the phenomenon was identified at the end of the investigation. The hypothesis of a "bolide" (meteorite type) is highly improbable, as the observation duration and the apparent speed of the PAN are incompatible with this type of phenomenon. Conversely, the characteristics described (observation duration of the order of two minutes or more, intermediate speed, regular trajectory, white/yellow light trail, separation into two close sources, dawn observation towards the sunrise zone) are typical of an artificial re-entry into the atmosphere that would be visible from Réunion. Data obtained from the CNES Operational Space Surveillance Center (COSE) indicate that a satellite, in the process of entering the atmosphere, corresponds to the orbitography data, Starlink® 2437 (Annex 1). However, the trajectory reconstructed using the data does not exactly match that reconstructed from the witness's photographs (Annex 2). For a Starlink® at an altitude of approximately 160 km, just before final desorbitation, atmospheric friction is already very strong, causing the trajectory to bend from the Kepler prediction model + average braking. A small deviation in the atmospheric density used by simulation tools and reality can result in errors of dozens of kilometers along the trajectory and, therefore, dozens of seconds and a few degrees of apparent deviation seen from the ground. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B", probable atmospheric re-entry of a Starlink® satellite.
