Summary: Observation during a flight of a moving light point in the sky: observation of Starlink® satellite flashes.
--- seg-911a302a52178a0399f102b7 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: During a flight over the Atlantic on 01/09/2023, the airline pilot (T1) and co-pilot (T2) observed 2 luminous objects in motion in the sky, directly ahead with a low angular height (0 to 2 degrees). T1 initially thinks he is observing aircraft headlights performing maneuvers, however the distance to the objects seems to be increasing. Indeed, during the observation, the altitude increases to reach approximately 12°, giving the impression that the objects are at a much higher altitude. Their brightness gradually decreases until making their observation impossible. The observation lasted more than half an hour. Of weak strangeness and average consistency (multiple witnesses, but only one testimony and no photo of PAN), this case turns out to be an observation of Starlink® satellite flashes. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The description of the PAN is entirely consistent with this type of observation: rapid changes in brightness reaching that of Venus, apparent movements of a few degrees, always located in the same area of the sky. A check in Stellarium confirms that the conditions for observing Starlink® satellites in orbit are met: observation vertical to the Sun, when it is between 30 and 45° below the horizon, as explained in a note from the GEIPAN available on the GEIPAN website in the news: New phenomenon explained: Starlink satellite flashes. Only the azimuth of 82° reported by the witness does not match (offset of about twenty degrees), but the fixed stars used as references on the sketch provided to us, are likely Castor and Pollux of the constellation Gemini. Or, these two stars are perfectly vertical to the apparent position of the Sun (azimuth 60°). The case is classified "A", observation of Starlink® satellite flashes.
