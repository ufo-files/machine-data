Description: On July 28, 2014, at 9 PM, a witness observed and photographed above the sea two dark objects moving in the sky. When they reached his vertical, he went back inside his apartment, then came back out to record a video. The observation of straight-line flying objects suggests balloons, such as helium-filled children's balloons, often shiny (mylar) balloons commonly found at fairs. The witness saw brown objects, but the witness was backlit: any object would appear very dark under these conditions. The object's doubling could correspond to a perspective effect of two balloons one behind the other. The fleeting bright point could correspond to the sun reflecting off the balloon, visible only in certain positions of the balloon. An analysis of the photo indicates an angular size of around 0.2°, which could correspond to a 70 cm object seen from 200m, compatible with a child's balloon carried by the wind. Météo-France indicates a weak, very unstable wind (see record), and the wind is also variable along the coast (see map): it is impossible to confirm that these objects were indeed moving with the wind, even though their movement (see witness's video) seems consistent with this hypothesis. Without this information, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "C", but the balloon hypothesis aligns with the observation.
