Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named CIOTAT (LA) (13) 21.04.1982 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. On April 21, 1982, around 9 PM, a driver spotted above the LA CIOTAT golf course a formation of at least six white glowing balls in the shape of a V. For 15 seconds, he observes these balls which seem fixed. No noise is heard. The entire formation disappears instantly. No other testimony about this phenomenon has been collected. Despite the gendarmes' investigation, who contacted the air surveillance manager in Salon de Provence at the time, stating that "no aircraft from the Salon base was in flight at the time of the incident," all evidence points towards the hypothesis of confusion with the French Patrol (particularly their powerful nose light, the only visible element from a great distance) flying in formation towards the witness for a few moments (see the investigation report). Perhaps this manager was not the right person to judge the French Patrol's activity (he did not directly answer this question) or he did not have permission to share the information, even with military gendarmes. It is also attested that the French Patrol was in La Ciotat the day after the witness's observation. The French Patrol (PAF), contacted and informed of these elements in favor of and against the hypothesis (air traffic advice during the observation), assesses a 60% probability of the French Patrol's presence at that location and time, but no longer has archives to prove it. In addition to the above-mentioned temporal and spatial plausibility elements, there are very strong similarities for the "French Patrol" hypothesis, particularly:

- "V" formation: There are at least three possible formations of the French Patrol that take the form of a "V" more or less elaborate.
- Number: Presence of at least six balls. The French Patrol aircraft can number up to 8 or 9, but they sometimes fly in smaller formations.
- Color: White lights. The landing lights are located on the nose of the Alphajet, a configuration since 1981. The color is white or white-yellow.
- No noise perceived: It is entirely possible that the aircraft were several dozen kilometers away, and the lack of perceived noise is therefore not surprising.
- Trajectory: PAN stationary. Observed moving towards the witness, they will indeed appear stationary over a short observation duration of 15 seconds.
- Duration: Approximately 15 seconds of observation and instant disappearance. The aircraft changed direction after this time. Not being visible except by their nose light, which has a limited visibility angle, they became invisible to the naked eye due to the great distance separating them from the witness. The consolidated plausibility elements, reinforced by the strong similarities, lead us to accept this hypothesis as very probable. The consistency is rather weak, but nevertheless sufficient in light of the chosen hypothesis. The angular measurement data, such as the dimensions of PAN, individually and for the whole formation, and the height on the horizon, are missing. We do, however, have a drawing. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies it as A: very probable observation of an aircraft squadron.
