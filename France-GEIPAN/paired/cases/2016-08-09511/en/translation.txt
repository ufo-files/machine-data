Summary: Observation of movements in the night sky covered with two white lights: probable misidentification with a skytracer.
--- seg-07441035379455ab3701aa73 [needs-review] ---
Description: On August 9, 2016, around 0:30 (not 8 as indicated in the questionnaire), a witness in the commune of Wissembourg (not Riedseltz) observed in the sky two white lights that seemed to follow each other above the clouds along circular and linear trajectories. The lights appeared to come from above the cloud. They disappeared instantly. Only one testimony was collected. A hypothesis is favored: the observation of a skytracer (a bright spot coming from the ground and reflected by the atmosphere. See the investigation report). Many details of the observation strongly suggest this type of misperception: pursuit lights, rapid movements, circular pursuit, repetitive movements, etc... (the witness said "circular chase and linear pursuit, repeating like a 'game' without being really in the same place, there was a slight offset at each passage"). It's also worth noting that the weather was cloudy, which is favorable to this type of misperception. There can be various configurations: visibility of only the impacts, only the beams, or both. Weather conditions determine visibility and visibility distance, which explains the random and fleeting nature of the phenomena. The witness's objection to this hypothesis is of two orders: 
- The type of movement. These can vary according to equipment and context. A witness cannot know all possibilities.
- The light seemed to come from above the cloud, not from the ground. It's likely that the witness only has experience with impacts with beam traces rising, giving the certainty that the lighting comes from the ground. When the rising beams are not visible, it's indeed possible to think that the light comes from above the clouds. The witness's expression ("those being illuminated from above, I mean we saw a part of the clouds below, unlike the famous disco projectors where we can clearly see that the clouds are illuminated from below") indirectly expresses the somewhat diffuse nature of the spot ("a part of the clouds is below") typical of these impacts without clear beam traces on clouds. 
A search for events held in the observation area during the night of August 8-9, 2016 reveals the presence of the Acropole Open Air event in Surbourg (67), a fiesta held in the parking lot of the Acropole hall on the evenings of August 6, 7, and 8, 2016, located 14 km southwest of the observation site, in the direction of the PAN observations. The last festive evening was accompanied by an orchestra. It was not possible to determine if a skytracer was used on this occasion. Therefore, the hypothesis of observing a skytracer remains only probable, due to the characteristic description, but not certain. The case is classified B, probable misperception with a skytracer.
