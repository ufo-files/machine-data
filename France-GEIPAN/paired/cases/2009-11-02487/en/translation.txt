Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time and now being re-examined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as 'B' in 2011 by the GEIPAN. On December 17, 2009, the GEIPAN received direct testimony from a person who reported observing a moving light from west to east on November 19, 2009 at 5:30 PM. The PAN was silent, and the observation lasted approximately 3 minutes. The case falls within a wave of local observations. By comparing it with other cases, the GEIPAN initially concludes that it is likely an observation of a nightclub projector. However, the described phenomenon shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color, trajectory) with a perfectly known astronautic object: the ISS (International Space Station). Furthermore, the ISS was well present in the observed sky area while the witness does not mention it. Finally, the description provided by the witness from Buironfosse matches another observation recorded at the same time, perfectly identified as a passage of the ISS. It is not the witness's visual perception that is in question, but the interpretation the witness makes of his observation through his feelings (surprise, fatigue). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this weak strangeness case is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception with the ISS.
