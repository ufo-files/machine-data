**Description:** On Tuesday, December 11, 2018, a witness, after watching a movie on TV and listening to music while staring at the stars in a clear sky, was intrigued by a light, then by three PAN, two of which were oval and white, and one triangular and black. The passage was quick, and the PAN disappeared, hidden by a nearby building. The observation lasted between 30 seconds and one minute. Only one testimony was collected. The case analysis (see the investigation report) did not allow for any explanatory hypotheses, at least at this stage, as the witness's statements are vague. The drawings of the observed PAN do not provide any indication of a known particular phenomenon: the strangeness remains strong. As with the impossibility of confirming the presence of actual nebulae on the day of the observation, it is worth noting that the witness contradicted himself regarding the date of taking the ambient photos, as he initially stated he took them the day after the observation, then after December 18, 2018. Furthermore, despite reminders, the witness did not answer several questions that would have helped better define the observation, particularly on the following points:

- What movie was watched on TV before the observation to clarify the observation date: according to the testimony, the observation took place during the night of December 10 to 11, but it could also have taken place during the night of December 11 to 12, as the witness mentions "Tuesday" while talking about what he did during the evening. It is also worth noting that during his first email contact with GEIPAN, the witness mentions "Tuesday evening": "I saw three UFOs on Tuesday evening at 1:30." However, it should be noted that W9 broadcast an evening dedicated to UFOs in its "Au cœur de l'étrange" (At the Heart of the Strange) show on December 11, according to Toutelatele's program guide. This broadcast could have influenced the witness;

- The witness's statement "The Shepherd's Star was very bright, it reflected the black triangle, otherwise I wouldn't have seen it" is ambiguous: in fact, the Shepherd's Star (more likely Sirius in this case) cannot directly reflect on a distant star like the black triangle, but it could make its visibility easier. When questioned on this point to clarify his thoughts, the witness never answered. The description of the PAN could evoke a formation of flying lanterns, as the PAN follow each other in a line. However, the shape of the PAN and their trajectory relative to the wind are not consistent with flying lanterns at all. Since the observation took place through a window, the hypothesis of a reflection of electrical appliances can also be considered. However, it is worth noting that the witness opened the window during the observation, which seems very inconsistent with the reflection hypothesis. On February 12, 2019, the witness contacted GEIPAN again to talk about an observation of Irish pilots that he considers identical to his own. This is the observation of a PAN by several airline pilots in the Irish sky early in the morning of November 9, 2018, reported by La Dépêche, among others. However, this observation by the pilots, which strongly evokes a bolide observation due to its speed and description, has little resemblance to this case. Therefore, we cannot establish that the two observations are similar. Quite strange but of low consistency (single witness, no photos, lack of precision in the testimony), this case turns out to be unidentifiable due to the lack of reliable data. Despite the apparent sincerity of the witness, the case cannot be studied in more detail because, despite several reminders, questions about specific details of the observation remain unanswered. Furthermore, the testimony has date imprecisions. No explanatory hypothesis can therefore be favored with the provided elements. The case is classified as C, unexplained case due to the lack of reliable data.
