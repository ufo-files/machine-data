**Description:** On the night of April 28, 2013, at midnight, a driver observes repetitive, sinuous movements of a pale white light in low-altitude sky. Her passenger also witnesses the phenomenon. Only one questionnaire is collected. The observation occurs in three stages:

1-a) Around midnight, on the Saturday to Sunday night, a passenger in a vehicle on the highway spots a luminous phenomenon.

1-b) This prompts the driver to stop at the next rest area, 500 to 800 meters after the initial observation. The observation while stopped lasts for one hour, with both witnesses remaining in the vehicle. The phenomenon is seen above the trees, at an elevation estimated by the witness to be 30°.

1-c) Since the phenomenon doesn't disappear, the witness resumes driving. Weather conditions, as reported in Béziers (about 50 kilometers northeast) on April 27, 2013 at 11 PM on Météociel, as well as in the early hours of April 28, indicate an overcast sky (8/8), no precipitation (very weak the previous day at 2 PM), visibility of 60 kilometers, west wind of about 10 km/h with occasional gusts. This weather is identical to that in Carcassonne, 60 kilometers to the west, diametrically opposite.

The phenomenon is unique, luminous (pale white glow), without a geometric shape, moving through the sky (at the level of the treetops), following the same trajectory with sinuosity ("like a bird flying"), without sound or trail, and difficult to estimate in size as the witness struggles to gauge the distance.

During the stop (1-b), the witness mentions the trajectory is narrow, passing alternately behind two trees about 20 meters apart. During the initial observation on the highway (1-a), the witness states, "it felt like it was right next to us." Similarly, during the departure (1-c), the passenger says, "it was following us." This latter comment, instead, indicates a significant distance from the phenomenon since the direction remains the same while the witness is moving at high speed.

The date is notable: it's the night of Saturday to Sunday (April 27 to 28, 2013), the eve of the Labor Day holiday (May 1), during the Easter school vacation for the Montpellier academic zone (from April 20 to May 5, 2013). The environment is also particular: the highway a few kilometers west of Narbonne crosses hilly, undervillaged limestone terrain with a Mediterranean landscape (garrigue and pine forest), dotted with vineyards (see map). There are also "châteaux" with vineyards, some with guest rooms in the middle of nature, featuring pools and tennis courts.

The witness suggests projecting a beam of light onto the base of the clouds. However, they question the luminous rendering of such a phenomenon (regularity of the trajectory, characteristics of the glow), as well as its origin, stating, "There's only pine forest here." All these elements lead us to favor the hypothesis of a beam of light projected onto the base of the clouds as suggested by the witness.

Analysis of photos provided by the witness allows us to determine the location of the viewing from the Narbonne-Jonquières rest area (1-b), based on the witness's statement, refined by the view of pines standing out from the back of the sky. This enables us to estimate the azimuth angle of the views (document:  Azimut_serre.jpg). Extending this field of vision (document:  Azimut_moyen.jpg), we can determine the origin zone of these light animations. Inside this zone is at least the "Château de Jonquières," 700 meters from the witness. Other considered hypotheses (events at the Abbey of Fonfroide, lighting of the Château de Saint-Martin) are ruled out as they are outside this field of vision.

To confirm this hypothesis through triangulation, we use the witness's statement regarding the first observation (1-a), the vehicle moving on the highway, between 500 and 800 meters before the stop at the rest area. By transferring this distance along the highway upstream of the rest area, we find that before this point, the highway is uphill, widened to three lanes, in a cut in the hills with slopes on both sides obscuring the horizon. At 800 meters upstream, the highway is on a plateau, the horizon clear in front of the witness, with a view of the Château de Jonquières, located 1.35 kilometers away. This phenomenon at a 45-degree angle to the right of the vehicle cannot go unnoticed!

Verification element: from the rest area in 1-b, the phenomenon is seen at an elevation estimated by the witness to be 30°. At a distance of 700 meters according to our hypothesis, the phenomenon's height is: 700 * tan(30°) ~ 400 meters, which corresponds to the altitude of the cloud cover.

All these elements reinforce the hypothesis of a luminous animation (very likely from the Château de Jonquières or one of its dependencies. Unfortunately, as of today, the owner is not reachable by phone to confirm this). The distance of 1.3 kilometers (1-a) to 700 meters (1-b) explains several aspects noted by the witness:

- the low luminosity,
- the non-geometric shape, as well as the irregular trajectory, a projection seen in profile onto the undulating base of moving clouds,
- the perceived closeness and following of the witness, due to the low evolution of the azimuth of the PAN relative to the witness, this low evolution despite the vehicle's movement results from its significant distance from the PAN relative to the witness.

Consequently, the case is classified by GEIPAN as A: luminous animation.
