Summary: Observation of the movement of a white-light glow in a cloudy night sky: observation of a light display.
--- seg-9a9e93596a000d091f4ad8ae [needs-review] ---
Description: On April 28, 2013, at midnight, a driver observes the repeated, winding movements of a whitish glow at low altitude in the sky. Her passenger also observes the phenomenon. Only one questionnaire is collected. The observation has 3 stages: 1-a) Around 00:00, during the night from Saturday to Sunday, a luminous phenomenon is seen by the passenger in a vehicle travelling on the motorway. 1-b) This prompts the driver to stop at the next rest area, 500 to 800 metres after the first observation. The stationary observation continues for 1 hour, with the 2 witnesses remaining in the vehicle. The phenomenon is seen above the trees, at an elevation estimated by the witness at 30°. 1-c) As the phenomenon does not disappear, the witness resumes driving. The weather recorded at Béziers (about fifty kilometres to the northeast) on April 27, 2013, at 23:00 on Météociel, and in the early hours of April 28, also on Météociel, indicates cloud cover of 8/8, no precipitation (the last, very light precipitation was at 14:00 the previous day), visibility of 60 km and a westerly wind of around ten kilometres per hour, with some gusts. This weather is identical to that at Carcassonne, 60 km to the west, in the opposite direction. The phenomenon is single and luminous (a pale whitish glow), without a geometric shape, moving in the sky at treetop level, always following the same winding path ("like a bird flying"), without noise or a trail. Its size is difficult to estimate because the witness has difficulty judging its distance. While stopped (in 1-b), the witness mentions that the path is small, passing alternately behind 2 trees about twenty metres apart. During the first observation on the motorway (in 1-a), the witness says, "we had the impression it was next to us". Similarly, when leaving (in 1-c), the passenger says, "it is following us". This last comment instead indicates a considerable distance to the phenomenon, since its bearing remains the same while the witness is moving at high speed. The date is noteworthy: it is the night from Saturday to Sunday (April 27 to 28, 2013), just before the extended Labour Day holiday (May 1), during the Montpellier education district's Easter school holidays, from April 20 to May 5, 2013. The surroundings are also noteworthy: the motorway, a few kilometres west of Narbonne, crosses undeveloped, rolling limestone hills with a Mediterranean landscape of scrubland and pine woods interspersed with vineyards (see the map. There are also wine-estate "châteaux" with cellars, some offering guest rooms in natural surroundings with a swimming pool and tennis court. The witness suggests a beam of light projected onto the cloud base, but questions the luminous appearance of such a phenomenon (regularity of the path and characteristics of the glow), and its origin, stating that "there is only pine woodland at that location". All these elements favour the witness's hypothesis of a beam of light projected onto the cloud base. Analysis of the photographs supplied by the witness makes it possible to locate the camera positions at the Narbonne-Jonquières rest area (in 1_b), based on the witness's statement and refined by the view of the pines silhouetted against the sky. This allows an azimuth angle to be estimated for the photographs (document: Azimut_serre.jpg). Extending this viewing sector (document: Azimut_moyen.jpg) identifies the area from which these light displays originated. It contains at least the "Château de Jonquières", 700 m from the witness. Other hypotheses considered (events at the Abbaye de Fonfroide or lighting at the Château de Saint-Martin) are excluded because they lie outside this viewing sector. To confirm the hypothesis by triangulation, we use the witness's statement about the first observation (in 1-a), with the vehicle travelling on the motorway 500 to 800 metres before stopping at the rest area. Plotting this distance backwards along the motorway, we find that before this point the motorway climbs uphill, widens to 3 lanes and passes through a cutting in the hills, with slopes on either side hiding the horizon. Going back 800 metres, the motorway is on the plateau, with a clear horizon ahead of the witness and a view of the Château de Jonquières, 1.35 kilometre away. This phenomenon, 45° to the right of the vehicle, could not go unnoticed! Verification point: from the rest area in 1-b, the witness estimates the phenomenon's elevation at 30°. At a distance of 700 metres according to our hypothesis, its height is 700 * tan (30°) ~ 400 metres, corresponding to the altitude of the cloud cover. All these elements support the hypothesis of a light display (very probably from the Château de Jonquières or its outbuildings. Unfortunately, to date the owner has not been reachable by telephone to confirm this). The distance, from 1.3 km (in 1-a) to 700 m (in 1-b), explains several aspects noted by the witness: - The faint luminosity; - The non-geometric shape and irregular path, a projection seen from the side onto the undulating base of moving clouds; - The perceived impression of proximity and of following the witness, due to the small change in the PAN's azimuth relative to the witness. This small change despite the vehicle's movement results from the considerable distance between the PAN and the witness. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: light display.
