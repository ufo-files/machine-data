Description: On April 1, between 9:00 PM and 9:27 PM, a witness observed from their apartment in Vitrolles (13), a luminous phenomenon they described as being slightly larger than a star, with an intensity greater than that of ordinary aircraft, varying from a pure white to an orange tone during the observation. Unlike aircraft lights, the light remains perfectly stationary; however, during intermittent observations, the witness notices that the PAN is descending towards the horizon. One single witness statement collected. Of weak strangeness but good consistency* (single witness who provided a complete QT, usable videos and photos of PAN), this observation case turns out to be a misperception with Venus. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. This hypothesis is supported by a reconstruction of the celestial situation using Stellarium.fr (see annex) at the time of the observation, which shows that Venus was indeed present according to the azimuts provided by the witness and that this planet was setting below the horizon between the observation times. The video allows for a re-evaluation of the witness's reported decrease in elevation, in accordance with the celestial situation. The provided images reveal the orange color, which is caused by the planet's position becoming lower and lower on the horizon. The variation of its brightness towards red is related to the thickness of the atmosphere crossed on its descending trajectory, an atmosphere that is increasingly dense compared to the observer. The witness will himself suggest his mistake with this planet in a later email. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A", Venus observation.
