Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case, named GRAND GALLARGUES (30) 1984, is part of a set of cases recently reviewed. On January 5, 1984, around 02:30, two witnesses are traveling in a VSL vehicle on the A9 highway towards Montpellier (34). The witnesses notice a PAN following them along the highway for a distance of about twenty kilometers. A second PAN is observed solely by the second, very frightened witness. No particular noise was heard during the observations. Two phenomena, PAN 1 and PAN 2, should be distinguished (see the investigation report). The physical characteristics and behavior of PAN 1 observed by the two witnesses do not correspond to any known phenomenon. However, upon further studying the testimonies, it becomes apparent that practically all the elements of the testimonies, in addition to the drawings, diverge in their description of the appearance and behavior of PAN 1, and their quantitative and qualitative weakness does not allow for further verifications and cross-references that could either explain, confirm, or refute the strange and inexplicable nature of the phenomenon. This inconsistency prevents the case from maintaining its initial classification D. As a result, GEIPAN classifies PAN 1 in C: lack of reliable information. Regarding UFO PAN 2 only observed by T2 (see case [A9] of NIMES (30) towards MONTPELLIER (34) 01/05/1984 T2), it is an astronomical confusion, with the star Aldébaran, observed in a geographically favorable configuration at that time. A portion of the strangeness experienced by T2 results from the illusion of the trailing sphere, created by the witness's own movement: the PAN (here, the star) appears to follow the witness's movement as it moves relative to the road, while it is actually the road that is moving relative to the star's fixed direction. As a result, the GEIPAN categorizes the PAN 2 in A: observation of Aldbaran.
