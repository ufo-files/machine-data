Description: On September 13, 2022, around 9:00 PM, the witness, in their garden in Ruffec (16), observes to the west a white glowing point moving in a circular arc, in a cyclic and repetitive manner at the same location in the sky. Each appearance is interrupted by a period of disappearance, with each cycle lasting approximately 6 seconds. After a slight downward and west-southwestward movement, the PAN disappears completely after about 5 minutes of observation. The consistency* of the case is average, with a single witness who did not take a photo or video of the PAN. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Four hypotheses were explored (see the investigation report):

- The observation of planes
- Reflections from the Sun on Starlink® satellites (called "starlink flares")
- Light displays due to a "skytracer" (a projector aimed at the sky)
- Reflections on ice crystals suspended in the atmosphere

The last two hypotheses were quickly dismissed for the following reasons: the presence of mid-level clouds that could serve as a support for projected lights, which cannot be formally confirmed for a phenomenon due to a "skytracer"; the strong wind at altitude preventing the formation and maintenance of ice crystals at approximately the same location during the entire observation period.

Regarding the first two hypotheses, the description of the PAN suggests an aeronautical observation, as the witness describes it resembling the position lights of an airplane. A reconstruction of air traffic at the time of the observation shows that numerous planes passed along the trajectory indicated by the witness. However, the number of appearances of the PAN and their rate seem to be significantly higher than the number of planes that actually passed. The hypothesis of a blend between a single PAN making rotations and several plane passages is therefore unlikely without formal verification.

Finally, regarding the hypothesis of observing "flares" from Starlink® satellites, the investigation showed that even if the appearance of the PAN resembles these phenomena of the Sun's reflection on these satellites, this object is below -29° (in a limit condition of the required position) (between -30° and -45°) according to a northwest azimuth (~312°) while the observation was oriented to the west. Additionally, the PAN are located too high on the horizon: the witness estimates the elevation between 45° and 60°, while the maximum required elevation is a few degrees (max 5°). Thus, this hypothesis is incompatible with the observation conditions. The investigation work did not allow to associate the PAN with a known phenomenon, so the case maintains a strong residual strangeness, mainly due to its repetitive behavior. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies this case as "D", an unidentified phenomenon after investigation.
