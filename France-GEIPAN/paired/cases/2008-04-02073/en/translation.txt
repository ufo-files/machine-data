Description: On April 12, 2008, around 10 PM, two witnesses were intrigued by a luminous phenomenon in the sky: a glow with strong, multicolored twinkling, predominantly blue, and round in shape, stationary in the southwest in a clear sky. The witnesses had time to retrieve binoculars to better observe this larger and brighter glow than a star. The observation lasted a total of about ten minutes. The sky map for that day (see attached) indicates that the brightest star in the sky, Sirius (-1.44 magnitude), was in the southwest sky at that time, 18° above the horizon: when this star is visible low on the horizon, it often surprises witnesses, especially on clear days. It will have disappeared, hidden behind the hill. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of the star Sirius. Since the direction of observation is not indicated very precisely, there remains a slight doubt about this hypothesis.
