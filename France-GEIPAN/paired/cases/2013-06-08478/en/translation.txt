Summary: An automobilist observed two luminous phenomena in the sky: Insufficient information.
--- seg-6e0db6ccde038d28c3ba7b42 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On June 6, 2013, at 10:10 PM, the attention of a driver is drawn to two lights in the sky that appear stationary. Continuing on his route, he stops and observes two distinct forms. No unusual noises are noticed during the observation, and the lights disappear towards the N-W. Two photographs are taken by the witness with a mobile phone. No other testimonies have been collected. An investigation reveals that the Sun is 8° below the horizon (the observation took place approximately 30 minutes after sunset). To the East, the star VEGA is noticed, very bright (Azimuth = 65° Altitude = 32°) and according to Meteociel and the record at Souillac: the sky was clear, and the wind almost nil. For this observation, the GEIPAN considered several hypotheses, such as illuminated planes by the Sun, but 30 minutes after sunset, planes at 10,000 meters altitude are no longer illuminated by the Sun, as well as the observation of stars or planets, observed through a cloudy veil, but no pairs of stars corresponding to the observation are found. The passage of luminous balloons or Thai lanterns remains possible, although unlikely on a weekday, and the detailed description of the witness does not well correspond to the usual accounts of Thai lanterns. This peculiar case of distant lights seemingly moving slightly remains unexplained due to a lack of information or other reported testimonies: the case is classified C. The low strangeness of the case does not justify a more in-depth investigation.
