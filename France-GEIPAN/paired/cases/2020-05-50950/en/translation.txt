Summary: Sequential observations in a stormy sky of red light phenomena with slow extinctions: observation of a ball lightning phenomenon, category globular lightning.
--- seg-138953e2fd62b367d8a8ad21 [needs-review] ---
Description: During a thunderstorm on May 4, 2020, at 10:15 PM, a witness observed a first phenomenon in the northwest, a bright red rounded shape that disappeared due to a progressive decrease in light intensity, lasting 30 seconds. A few minutes later, the same shape reappeared at the same location, followed by the appearance of two other identical glowing points, but separated from each other. The four appearances lasted a total of 20 to 30 seconds and then disappeared as if slowly extinguished. No movement was observed, and the objects appeared stationary. The observations stopped as soon as a rain shower passed. Only one testimony was collected. The witness's description of the observation corresponds to a case of ball lightning of the "globular lightning" type. The testimony was submitted to the Lightning Research Laboratory, which unambiguously recognized a ball lightning phenomenon, categorizing it as globular lightning. Indeed, the Saint-Savin (33) area, where the observation took place, was experiencing a strong thunderstorm episode (see the attached maps of lightning strikes 10 and 50 km around the commune). There were numerous lightning strikes, and the intra- and inter-cloud activity was intense. The visual aspects, duration, and movements are highly compatible with globular lightning of the type. The colorimetry of the photo provided by the witness is also compatible. There is therefore a very high probability that these are globular lightning spheres resulting from the widespread thunderstorm activity at the time, particularly the intra- and inter-cloud activity. This type of observation is not rare during similar thunderstorm configurations. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: observation of a ball lightning phenomenon, globular lightning category.
