Description: On Wednesday, June 14, 2017, at 5:00 AM, a witness is intrigued by the presence in the sky of a very bright stationary phenomenon. The witness describes a white, double-lobed PAN with a red dot in the middle and notes that, aside from the Moon, no stars are visible in the sky. Only one testimony was collected. What the witness describes is likely an observation of Venus. It is normal that between 5:00 and 6:00 AM in June, Venus is the only visible star in the dark sky. Venus is in the east, the witness mentions "SE" without conviction, but states that the Moon is clearly to the right of Venus and according to a "vertical line at 45°", which is consistent with their respective positions. It is also classic for Venus to produce a non-point light (here, a red lobe effect) and multi-colored. This results from the atmospheric effect (diffraction of light rays). It is not possible to firmly confirm this hypothesis. Indeed, the testimony is very imprecise, given that it is from 6 months after the observation. The witness did not feel the need to note the end of the observation (stopped observing after 5 minutes) or even take a photo, which, along with the delay in testifying, indicates a relatively low perception of strangeness during the observation. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Venus.
