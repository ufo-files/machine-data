Summary: Observation of a straight-line passage of an orange ball with a slight smoke trail: observation of an atmospheric reentry of a Starlink® satellite.
--- seg-b8a975a05e9ef9d072260195 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The sole witness of this case, preparing to leave for work, observes at 6:45 a.m. from the porch of his residence in Julos (65), a large red-orange ball leaving a thin trail of smoke behind it. The phenomenon moves in a straight line, from northwest to southeast, passing directly overhead. According to the witness, the apparent size of the phenomenon is estimated at 2 cm, measured using a ruled gradation held at arm's length. After approximately 30 seconds of observation, he loses sight of the phenomenon as it is obstructed by landscape elements. He later consults the "Misperce du Ciel" website, recommended by the GEIPAN, and considers the possibility of an atmospheric reentry, which he quickly rejects, stating: "There has never been a trail like the one I observed this time." Of average consistency (single witness and absence of photo or video of the PAN) and with a strangeness primarily due to the presence of a trail, this observation case is determined to be a reentry of a Starlink® satellite.

According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. This conclusion is based on the following:

- The FIREBALL IMO (International Meteor Organization) website, specialized in recording atmospheric reentries, both natural and artificial, recorded nine witness reports at the time of the phenomenon's observation, from witnesses in the same geographical area as the observer (south of France, Pyrenees foothills). See the event on the IMO website.

- These witnesses describe a similar observation to that of the witness, specifically an object primarily described as red-orange, moving on a straight trajectory for a duration estimated between 7.5 and 45 seconds, within the range of the witness's observation time. The assessment of the trajectory's orientation differs among witnesses. However, for two witnesses located on the Basque coast, it corresponds to that reported by the observer of the PAN, i.e., a direction from northwest to southeast. The trajectory determined by the IMO (Annex 1, blue arrow on the map from the website) closely matches that reported by the witness, with a variation of only a few degrees.

Given the observation duration of this atmospheric reentry, it is likely to be an artificial object, such as a satellite, space debris, or a rocket stage, rather than a natural phenomenon like a meteoroid. This hypothesis is supported by IMO data, which attributes the origin of these observations to the Starlink® 5144 satellite. The GEIPAN classifies this observation case as "A": atmospheric reentry of a Starlink®.
