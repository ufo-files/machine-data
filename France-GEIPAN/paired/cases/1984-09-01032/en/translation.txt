Summary: Observations of an orange glow shaped like a rugby ball: mistaking for the Moon.
--- seg-ca52870b31214929d3599fa0 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time and now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as 'D' in 1984 by the GEPAN. On September 28, 1984, between 8:30 PM and 8:50 PM, five witnesses saw in the sky the slow movement of an orange glow, roughly the size and shape of a rugby ball. During the observation, this glow split into two oval parts and moved in an intriguing way for the witnesses. The described phenomenon has many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the setting Moon. Moreover, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, while the witnesses do not mention it (see investigation notes). It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation based on their feelings (surprise, fatigue, night observation). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of average strangeness is classified as PAN A: observation related to a misperception of the Moon.
