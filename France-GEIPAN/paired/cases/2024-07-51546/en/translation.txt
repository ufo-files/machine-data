Description: On Saturday, July 20, 2024, at 11:50 PM, while driving on a rural road, the witness sees a light in the sky to their right, heading towards Montauban. The sky is cloudy, and a storm is approaching. The scene is quickly obscured by trees. Continuing on, the witness approaches an intersection and just before the stop sign, they observe a light in the shape of a lasso, changing form like an elastic with rapid changes in direction and altitude. The witness films the PAN with their smartphone for 11 seconds, but not looking directly at it, as they were focused on driving. The video shows a dim light performing various movements near the windshield. The witness then takes the road bifurcation and notices only the city lights of Montauban, approximately 10 kilometers away, which they are heading towards. The observation lasted a total of about 2 to 3 minutes. The case is consistent* with the presence of a technical questionnaire completed by the witness and a video that could be analyzed (see the investigation report). Regarding the strangeness felt by the witness, it is primarily due to the appearance of the PAN, in the shape of a "lasso," and its movements in all directions.

*According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Remote investigation and video analysis have led to conclude that the PAN is likely a sky-tracer used either by a Montauban nightclub, or during the "Fireflies" festival taking place at the same time in Montauban's downtown, or by a private individual in a private setting on that Saturday evening. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Appearance of the PAN (shape of a "lasso," pale white color), described by the witness and visible on the video, consistent with the impact on low clouds of a light source from a ground-based projector.

- Behavior of the PAN, described by the witness and visible on the video, consisting of back-and-forth movements, perfectly normal for a sky-tracer.

- The observation took place on a Saturday evening, the ideal time for using such a projector in a festive setting. The witness themselves later concluded they had seen a sky-tracer ("laser") after conducting their own investigation and observing a similar phenomenon again. Unfortunately, it was not possible to determine the actual use of a sky-tracer in Montauban and its vicinity at the time and date of the observation. However, we cannot rule out the use of such a projector in a private setting or during the "Fireflies" festival taking place in Montauban at the same time.

GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a sky-tracer.
