Description: The sole witness of this event was alone in the garden of a house in Bayeux on October 22, 2023, at 00:05, when he observed a PAN. Initially, it moved rapidly in a straight line from southeast to east-southeast, appearing as a fixed red light. The PAN then stopped, and two lights, one red and one green, became visible, alternating at a one-second interval. Subsequently, a pale yellow conical beam emerged from the PAN, directed towards the ground, with a sweeping motion. This phase lasted between 6 and 7 seconds, before the PAN instantly resumed its eastward trajectory, disappearing after a "zigzag" movement lasting approximately two seconds. The consistency* of this case is average with a single witness and the absence of photos or videos of the PAN. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Two hypotheses were examined (see the investigation report): the observation of a helicopter or a drone. The lights observed correspond to standard regulatory lighting, red and green, which can be fixed or flashing, except for the green light for a helicopter, which should be fixed (right navigation light). Similarly, the rapid movement and stationary flight remain common characteristics of these two types of craft. However, the instant stop in flight and the restart at maximum speed without perceptible acceleration are behaviors more characteristic of a drone. Furthermore, the description of zigzag movements during the departure of the PAN is also compatible with the maneuver capabilities of a drone. The pale yellow projector described can be used on both types of aircraft mentioned. In the case of a helicopter, it can be used for search operations or signaling. For a drone, its use can also extend to recreational applications. It should be noted that these projectors, due to their power, are now easily accessible to the general public. No radar trace was reported by the National Center for Air Operations (CNOA) at the time and location of the observation. The investigation also failed to find evidence of the use of a helicopter that night by the military, civil security, Le Havre airport, or a private hotel establishment. The witness's lack of noise perception is not a determining factor since the wind was not carrying at the time of the observation. Distance can also hinder the perception of surrounding noise around the PAN. It is also worth noting that, in the context of the drone hypothesis, its owner is violating several prohibitions, including those related to nighttime use, operation in an urban area, and flight in an area subject to overflight restrictions as defined by Géoportaill. Of the two hypotheses, the drone hypothesis appears more plausible due to certain details related to the movements of the PAN (zigzags, sudden stops and departures) that are more in line with the piloting capabilities offered by drones. The GEIPAN classifies as B: probable observation of a drone.
