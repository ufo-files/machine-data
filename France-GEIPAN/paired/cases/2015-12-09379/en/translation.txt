Summary: Observation of the silent passage of an orange glow across the night sky on a holiday evening: probable observation of a Thai lantern.
--- seg-be4c7a35658159715b6f4e64 [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 24, 2015, at approximately 7:40 PM, a witness in their garden observed a very bright orange glow silently crossing the clear sky from East to West. The glow shrunk and disappeared. Only one testimony was collected. The witness's information points the investigation towards the observation of a lantern, with all its characteristics: orange glow, moving pushed by the wind, without noise, and then disappearing as it went out. We will confirm this hypothesis: First, the witness reports inconsistencies in their account. They initially state that the phenomenon "crossed the sky from full East to full West." Then, in the detailed description of the phenomenon, they indicate a trajectory "West/East." To resolve the ambiguity, we will consider the phenomenon's position relative to the moon, which the witness describes as "to the right and below the Moon." The lunar calendar ephemeris indicates a phase one day before a full moon, with a rise at 3:39 PM (UTC) on this date. The Moon is positioned to the East (rising three hours ago), to the left of the witness who is observing towards the South. The phenomenon's position to the right of the Moon (to the West when observing towards the South) leads to a West/East trajectory, which corroborates its detailed description. The weather at the Belfort-Dorans station, less than 10 km to the South, as recorded on Météociel, indicates "clear skies, visibility 20 km" with a "West wind of 10/20 km/h at its peak." A floating object would thus be pushed from West to East, corresponding to the phenomenon's trajectory. The witness describes an orange glow, about 1/4 to 1/3 the size of a full moon, then shrinking to a bright point before disappearing by extinction. Note that it is the night of Christmas Eve, conducive to this type of festive manifestation. In conclusion, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a Thai lantern.
