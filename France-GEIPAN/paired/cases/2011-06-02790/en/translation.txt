Summary: Misidentification due to an optical phenomenon: reflection in the camera lens.
--- seg-43e8decae6f82f9aefc3eba4 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: A witness sends us a series of photographs in which they discover "strange elements." No observation was made during the taking of the photos. Determined to understand the phenomenon, the witness takes another photograph under the same conditions the following day. The same phenomenon reappears. This case very well illustrates a category of technical explanations for a priori very strange photographs: it is a matter of reflections in the camera's optics, appearing as "caustics" (a family of surfaces in optics). The conditions under which the 5 photographs taken in Ariège were taken are perfect for the appearance of such optical effects in certain cameras (as it depends on the configuration of the optical blocks): a dark landscape with a few bright light sources, creating a strong contrast, further enhanced by a long exposure time (between 23 and 35 seconds). The quality of the provided photos allows the GEIPAN to open this case in order to concretely illustrate a case of misidentification linked to an optical phenomenon essentially due to the use of a digital camera under particular conditions (see the photographic analysis). The case is classified as A.
