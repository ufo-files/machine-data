--- seg-227af7f03025e190b4e6f7fa [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 30, 2015, around 2:00 PM, a witness observed parachute-dropping planes in the sky and noticed an object between two of them descending in a zigzag pattern with rapid, jerky movements. The object moved away at a phenomenal speed. The GEIPAN contacted the "Abeille Parachutisme" club in Honfleur, who confirmed repeated rotations that day to drop parachutists. A club official stated that "During these rotations, it happens that objects escape from the dropping planes, and these objects never follow a straight trajectory, but rather arabesques linked to air currents." Indeed, an object dropped from an airplane at flight speed is subject to: - gravity, which causes it to fall, - aerodynamic forces that slow it down and make it spin, - the dominant wind, which pushes it in its own direction, - and local air currents, sometimes upward. The sum of these forces could explain the observed trajectory, which is completely chaotic because the object in question is certainly very low-density. The shape described by the witness (flat, rectangular) could be that of a cardboard plate. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B, as a probable observation of an object falling from a parachute-dropping plane.
