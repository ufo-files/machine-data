Summary: Successive appearance of 8 red and yellow spheres silently passing through the sky: observation of a group of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-1ed1dce8d6a7a72936575231 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 2, 2019, between 6:40 and 6:50 PM, while walking on the sidewalk, the main witness reported observing a multicolored, round sphere, with a bright red exterior, a sparkling yellow interior, and a dark gray oval at its center, moving "not very high in the sky and coming from nowhere." A second sphere, followed by a third, and finally eight in total, emerged from the same point. The witness did not observe their distant approach; they appeared and remained in their aerial trajectory. The last sphere remained stationary for approximately 15 to 20 seconds before departing. On her way home, the witness contacted a friend to share her observation. This friend then put her in touch with another witness (T2), who also reported seeing four "red circles" at the same time, one of which stopped and then followed the same direction as those observed by the main witness (T1). Only one technical questionnaire was completed for this observation (see the investigation report). The hypothesis of observing a series of Thai lanterns in this case is supported by the following elements:

- Shape: round
- Colors red and orange (commonly described in this type of observation) as well as gray, the latter corresponding to the upper part of the rice paper envelope not illuminated (or very weakly) by the candle flame
- Confirmed movement of all Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (PAN) in the direction of the wind, at a speed less than 9 km/h, in accordance with the maximum wind speed and implying a distance between lanterns of T1 less than 150 meters. This modest distance explains the witness (T1) being able to describe the lanterns precisely; - Number: the lanterns are often released in groups of three or more; - No noise; - Duration of observation: 2 minutes, corresponding to the maximum possible duration of life of a lantern, that is to say approximately 20 minutes. One of the lanterns seems to have stopped. It should be noted that wind speeds can vary from 0 to 9 km/h, so it is plausible that this lantern, slightly off from the others, temporarily encountered a windless zone or one with a weaker wind. It is also noted that the witness (T1) provided a fairly precise estimate of the trajectory, which he perceived as parallel to the Salins Street. This visual trajectory, not real, allowed to correctly establish the direction of the wind (from the left to the right of the witness). The case is classified "A", observation of a group of Thai lanterns.
