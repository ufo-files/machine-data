Summary: Observations by two witnesses of the silent movement across the night sky of 7 reddish-light phenomena: probable observations of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-76f124e8c4e017f0bdd95ea3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Friday, March 30, 2018, around 10 PM, a witness (T1) comes out of their house and sees a reddish light moving in the sky and then disappearing. He then sees two other light phenomena of the same type and takes a photograph with his mobile phone. One of the two lights initiates a turn to come towards the witness who is heading home. He is observed with his companion (T2), and together they witness up to 7 luminous phenomena moving silently through the night sky. Only these two testimonies were collected. One of the witnesses disputes the initial explanation of GEIPAN, however, the observation is likely related to the movement of Thai lanterns: - the color, the passage of multiple PAN one after the other, the occurrence on a weekend night, and the movement in the direction of the wind measured that evening (from SE to NW, see attached file) are all elements supporting this hypothesis. T1 mainly objects that at one point a PAN turned towards them before resuming its normal course. This does not contradict the hypothesis because: - it could be an interpretation error. The PAN are moving at an average altitude in the sky, far from a vertical position. If the lantern at some point shone brighter, it is classic to interpret this as a horizontal approach of the PAN towards the witness. Similarly, a decrease in intensity would have been perceived as a distance; it is quite possible that a generally well-oriented wind is locally accompanied by disturbances (trees, urban planning, river) and follows a non-straight path. A lantern or a formation of lanterns can follow this wind current. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observations of Thai lanterns.
