Summary: Observation of two unusual smoke trails in the sky: likely observation of military aircraft contrails.
--- seg-f5c636ecaacdbce00a1bb4b3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On January 18, 2017, at approximately 10:10 a.m., a witness observed for 15 seconds from their window two intermittent gray smoke streaks in a clear sky. The witness was intrigued by these unusual tracks, which did not resemble typical airplane contrails. Only one testimony was collected. The observation was somewhat peculiar. The witness did not provide strong elements to reject their own hypothesis of an airplane (intermittent contrails, absence of noise). Intermittent contrails are quite characteristic of military aircraft. The absence of noise observed can be explained in several ways. The aircraft could have been flying at high altitude and not in post-combustion (a classic case that generates significant noise). Additionally, any noise from the aircraft could have been masked by external noises from the city of Saint-Ambroix. Although the witness stated that there was no external noise during the observation, it is worth noting that the observation took place in the heart of a city of over 3,000 inhabitants, with a major traffic axis located nearby, namely the D904, an old national road. Furthermore, the observation occurred on a weekday, a Wednesday morning. The hypothesis of a misidentification with a diurnal meteor fragmenting and leaving residual contrails is excluded, as the two smoke streaks were too far apart from each other, and accelerations were observed by the witness. Moreover, this type of astronomical phenomenon is generally witnessed by numerous observers. The accelerations reminded of two aircraft in maneuver. Although the PAN was not formally identified, the description provided by the witness leaves little doubt about the misidentification. The too-late consultation of military radars did not allow to validate or not this hypothesis. The case is classified as B: probable observation of military aircraft contrails.
