**Case Summary: Red Unidentified Lights Observed at Airbase**

On January 9, 2020, four military personnel from the surveillance team at Airbase BA 123 in Orléans-Bricy reported observing unidentified red lights to the north of the base. A Gendarmerie patrol was dispatched but found no unusual activity. All four personnel were interviewed. Additionally, three independent witnesses also reported similar observations. This document details the seven witness accounts.

The consistency of the accounts is notable, with seven detailed testimonies, including three independent witnesses. Two videos recorded by T1 were also analyzed as part of the investigation. The conclusion reached was that the observed PAN were likely distress rockets launched from an unpopulated area, probably a field (as per the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following factors:

- The red color, characteristic of distress rockets
- The shape, varying between a point and a sphere depending on the distance from the witness
- High brightness
- Movement and trajectories, with rapid ascents followed by slower descents (due to the rocket's parachute), following an arc or curved path
- Brief individual observation times of each PAN (a few seconds at most), confirmed by analysis of the two videos taken by T1
- A peculiarity noted by three witnesses: the presence of a trail, described as either bright and the same color as the PAN or white, similar to smoke.

The high speeds observed, which surprised witnesses T5 and T7, are consistent with the distress rocket hypothesis. The apparent horizontal movement of the PAN observed by T1 and T5 can be explained differently for each witness:

For T1, it was demonstrated that the PAN do not only move horizontally but also have a vertical component, as evidenced by the first video. Additionally, the distance and brief appearance of the PAN in the second video do not allow for a precise, unaided eye assessment of purely horizontal movement.

For T5, the observation area is characterized by vast fields with no nearby obstacles blocking vision. Only a few trees and houses in nearby hamlets and villages can be seen in the distance to the south/southeast. Under these conditions, an object with little or no intrinsic horizontal movement (compared to the observer) and located between the witness and these distant objects, tends to experience the effects of parallax, the visual effect that occurs when the observation of an object changes based on the observer's point of view. In this case, T5's observation area is characterized by open fields without nearby obstacles. When T5 moves horizontally, distant objects like trees, houses, or villages to the south/southeast appear to move in the opposite direction to T5, creating the illusion of horizontal movement of the PAN in the opposite direction of the observer's movement.

Gendarmes conducted an exhaustive on-site investigation but were unable to identify the person responsible for launching the rockets. However, it is not ruled out that an individual with rockets who wanted to dispose of them without constraints launched them in an open area free of any housing. Triangulation based on the witnesses' observation directions indeed points to an entirely unpopulated area in the middle of the fields.

Based on these findings, the GEIPAN classifies the case as 'B': probable observation of distress rockets.

*According to the GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony.
