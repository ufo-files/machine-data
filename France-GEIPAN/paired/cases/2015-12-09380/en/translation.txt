Description: On December 25, 2015 at 18:40, two witnesses observed the passage of a red-orange light in the clear sky. The trajectory is irregular and no particular noise is heard. The observation lasts 1 to 2 minutes before the phenomenon disappears towards the North. Only one testimony is collected, along with the video made from a mobile phone. The witness is intrigued because it does not resemble a meteor, a star, or an airplane or helicopter. 1) The observation is made from the outside by a stationary witness. A video is recorded in close-up, which does not allow visualization of the environment. 2) The phenomenon is unique, luminous, with a red-orange color, circular or star-shaped, small in size, moving across the sky from East to West, without noise or trail, then disappearing towards the North. 3) The weather conditions recorded at the Tarbes-Lourdes-Pyrénées airport (about ten kilometers North) on December 25, 2015 at 6:00 and 7:00 PM: the weather report by Météociel indicates a light south wind of 6 km/h on average, no precipitation, and an unknown cloud cover. 4) The PAN occurs on a Friday evening, the night of Christmas on December 25, 2015. 5) The observation is made from an urbanized, scattered neighborhood, bordering the Southeast of Lourdes, looking towards the North which presents a similar type of environment. All these classic elements lead us to favor the hypothesis of a Thai lantern release. Appearance: The video and description indicate a bright phenomenon, of high intensity comparable to that of stars, of red-orange color, moving across the sky, then disappearing, all perfectly compatible with a lantern before its extinction. Date and location: This occurs during a festive evening, on Christmas night, which is therefore conducive to such a playful event. Trajectory: It is described by the witness as from East to West, then North. The wind measured 10 km away is 6 km/h on average and oriented from South to North. We cannot access the knowledge of the wind at the assumed level of PAN, but we know that in a rugged terrain, as in this case, the orientation of the wind can vary at a small scale. The site configuration even allows us to establish a probable mechanism for wind evolution and good compatibility between the wind measured at 10 km and the trajectory of PAN. Indeed, the Pyrenees mountains show a relief with hills. The attached map (relief.pdf) is more explicit. The observer's position is pointed out, the terrain features are circled in green, the south wind is indicated in red with an arrow, and the phenomenon's trajectory is reported in blue. It is understood that wind can cause an aspiration in the Lézignan valley (to the right), which produces a right to left (east to west) trajectory before resuming wind in the Adé valley from south to north. The trajectory can thus be perfectly explained. The attached file (trajectoire_Capture.pdf) reconstructs the probable trajectory of the phenomenon seen by the witness. All these elements confirm our hypothesis of a Thai lantern released during a festive Christmas evening in 2015. The consistency of the testimony is average, the video is poorly exploitable, the sky position information is incomplete, but sufficient given the good probability of the retained hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a Thai lantern.
