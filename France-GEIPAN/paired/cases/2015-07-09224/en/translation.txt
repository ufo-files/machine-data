Summary: Observation in the sky of a group of lights that separate and move independently but in pairs: satellite observation.
--- seg-6446174544c82635bb76c8f4 [needs-review] ---
Description: On July 03, 2015, around 1:10 AM, a witness observed in the pitch black night four "stars" aligned vertically and tilted to the right, evenly spaced. Then the group dissociates and the points move differently by two. The lights eventually turn off. A verification of satellite passes on the Calsky site (see attached log) indicates at this precise moment the passage of two groups of two notable satellites: - the doublet of satellites USA 62/NOSS 2-1C and USA 62/NOSS 2-1B - the satellite ADEOS 2 H2A Rocket and the "debris" Yaogan 1 LM Rocket (the brightest of the four) which appear to "glide" together, but are in fact at very different altitudes. The rare spectacle described by the witness is absolutely consistent with the model Calsky generates from the orbit parameters published by NORAD (USA). Due to the halt in public lighting, the witness particularly noticed this coordinated passage, which is quite exceptional; we even note that another pair of satellites was visible on a nearby trajectory a few minutes earlier: Cosmos 1689 Rocket and Landsat 5. This time of year is very conducive to observing a maximum number of satellites on axes close to North-South or South-North. The GEIPAN categorizes this case as A: satellite observation.
