Summary: Long observations in the night sky of the slow, one after the other, movement of many red-tinted lights that eventually disappear: probable observations of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-6d9310edb99cab6ec65a4083 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On Saturday, October 24, 2015, at 8:30 PM, several people observed in the sky the slow, one-after-the-other movement of numerous red-orange lights appearing at low altitude near Mollèges (13) and eventually disappearing vertically between Saint-Sixte (13) and Le Contras (13). The witness took photos and videos. Other people, contacted by phone, also observed the lights. Two testimonies were collected. The observation of red-orange lights crossing the sky on a weekend evening strongly suggests Thai lanterns, likely released nearby after a private party. The observed movement was an angular elevation in the North-Northwest direction: it could be a movement towards the witnesses and/or a vertical ascent. Local weather conditions recorded in Salon, Marignane, and Avignon indicate a weak, very unstable wind: it is not possible to determine the wind direction at the exact time and location of the observation. The two analyzed photos reveal surprisingly clear light points, white on one photo and colored on the other. We notice that these points are exactly in the same position in the image: these two characteristics (extreme sharpness, fixed position) are those of what is called "dead pixels" or "hot pixels"; these are common defects of digital cameras, only noticeable on night photos. Therefore, these two photos provide no information other than the observed phenomena were dim. Given the witnesses' account compatible in every way with the observation of Thai lanterns, and despite the impossibility of validating the hypothesis with the local wind record, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of Thai lanterns.
