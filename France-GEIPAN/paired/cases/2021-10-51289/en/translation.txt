Summary: Daytime observation of silent movement of a bright shape in the sky: observation of a fantasy balloon of "Mylar" type.
--- seg-eddd07ce00fb1efcbd14a697 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 24, 2021, at 5:40 PM, a witness was having a coffee in their garden when their attention was drawn to a silver and black object. It moved across the sky in a straight line and occasionally spun. The witness observed the PAN for two minutes before it disappeared. The witness was able to record the event with their smartphone. Only one statement was collected. The consistency is very good: despite knowing there was only one witness for this observation, they were able to capture video of the PAN, which was used for analysis (see the investigation report). The investigation revealed that the physical and dynamic data of the PAN were consistent with the hypothesis of a Mylar-type fantasy balloon. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Spherical shape, common for a fantasy balloon.
- Mixed color, silver and black, which can be identified with certain types of balloons made partly from a highly reflective, aluminized Mylar envelope and partly from a more conventional envelope (like latex) that can be a duller or darker color.
- The movement is slow but regular and straight, in the direction of the dominant wind, which is very light and generally blows from the west or northwest. This wind, which can vary locally, creates minimal turbulence and, as a result, causes the balloon to spin on itself. The witness noticed the balloon primarily due to the strong reflectivity of its Mylar surface to sunlight. Because of its small angular size, it was quickly lost from view. Note that the direction from which the balloon originated is the Aquitaine coast, and it is possible that such balloons are sold during the first weekend of the Toussaint school holidays. It may have been misplaced by its owner before slowly moving to Mérignac in the hours or days following, as this type of balloon has a very long lifespan.

Classification: "A": Observation of a Mylar-type fantasy balloon.
