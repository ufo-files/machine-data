Description: On February 17, between 6:00 and 6:30 a.m., while taking a break, the witness observed a pale yellow/blue PAN in their north-facing garden moving from west to east along a slightly descending trajectory. This observation, lasting two seconds, occurred when the PAN was visually located behind an airliner in landing phase. After accelerating and passing the plane, the PAN disappears at the level of the plane. Only one witness statement is collected for this event. According to Geipan's criteria, this case has average consistency (consistency refers to the amount of reliable information gathered from a testimony), namely a single witness, no photos or videos of the PAN, and a very short observation duration. The analysis (see the investigation report) indicates that the witness likely observed an LED balloon. This hypothesis is supported by the following elements:

- The round shape of the PAN
- The reported characteristics: a bright object with a yellow/blue color
- Linear movement in the direction of the dominant wind, from west to east
- Absence of a trail and noise The size of the PAN cannot be precisely assessed due to the lack of exploitable data. The witness describes the object's size as being "1/15th that of the aircraft," without specifying the method used for this estimate (direct observation, retrospective assessment, etc.). Furthermore, due to the inability to identify the aircraft in question, the distance between that aircraft and the witness, as well as the distance of the PAN from the witness, remain undetermined. It is plausible that the PAN appears optically aligned with the aircraft, but in reality, it is not at the same distance, particularly due to atmospheric turbulence. In this configuration, the PAN could have been deviated from its straight-line trajectory. Therefore, it is just as plausible that the PAN is located at a distance greater or less than that of the plane from the witness, making it impossible to determine its relative position with certainty. The perceived strangeness by the witness consists of the acceleration of PAN followed by its disappearance, visually noted at the level of the left wing of the aircraft. This strangeness can be attributed to a brief gust of wind that caused its acceleration, while its sudden disappearance may result from its potential disintegration once it reached, according to the witness' observation, the level of the aircraft. In conclusion, despite the relatively significant lack of exploitable data, particularly regarding the dimensions and distance of the PAN from the witness, its physical characteristics (round shape, color, and brightness) and dynamic characteristics (moving with the wind) suggest the probable observation of a LED balloon carried by the wind. The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'B': probable observation of a LED balloon.
