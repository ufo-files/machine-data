Summary: Photograph trace and intermittent furtive ground lighting concurrent with photo capture: observation of an Airbus A321 aircraft.
--- seg-f342d1dcbba891b2bae8cc09 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On April 17, 2018, at 9:00 PM, a witness was taking a long-exposure photo of the sky pointed at the zenith between Ursa Major and Leo when they noticed a trace they initially thought was a routine airplane trail. At the same time, they noticed the ground flickering intermittently for two seconds within their field of vision. Only one testimony was collected for this phenomenon. We demonstrate (see the investigation report) that the witness photographed an Airbus A321 on its regular Munich-Lisbon route during flight LH1792/DLH05A. Using an online stellar calibration tool, we first identified the field of stars on the photo, through which the plane was passing. Second, overlaying this field with the correctly oriented sky area allowed us to determine the observation azimuth and the plane's trajectory axis. Finally, matching this trajectory with those of planes passing over the observation area allowed us to definitively identify the involved aircraft. Note, however, that the sequence and synchronization of the plane's end-of-wing white anti-collision lights are abnormal; Airbus aircraft usually flash twice very closely at one hertz, not four times or randomly as seen in the photo. The overall consistency leads to conclude that this is a malfunction of the lights. The witness's noted oddity ("at the same time, I noticed that the ground was flickering intermittently for two seconds in my field of vision...") is described differently by the witness after being questioned by GEIPAN ("my surprise came when I saw the detail on the photo. It was then that the lighting impression on the ground became clear to me"). The second version is very likely the correct one. The phenomenon (called triggering) is classic in PAN observations: a first oddity creates a comprehensible awakening situation (which can go up to panic) for the witness, who can then associate other oddities or information (which might not even have been perceived, or at least not worried about) in the absence of the initial oddity. The furtive nature of this lighting and the lack of direct perception (observation in the field of vision, not along the line of sight) already suggest it was caused by a local, punctual light source: a passing car pointing its headlights in the witness's direction, a neighbor using a flashlight, etc.. The additional testimony reinforces this explanation. The consistency is low: - although a photo was taken, the original was not provided. The investigator had to reconstruct the angular data from the provided elements, in the absence of precise witness reports (angular heights, azimuths, angular distance traveled, etc.); - no data exists regarding the witness's surroundings (precise location, presence of lights, neighbors, roads, etc.) that could have further explored the source of the ground lighting (the false second oddity); - also note that the witness described the second oddity differently in the initial testimony (and in fact, more strangely) than the actual awareness they later said they had during observation. This low consistency, however, is not enough to prevent the explanation, given the overwhelming evidence. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": Observation of an Airbus A321 aircraft, flight LH1792/DLH05A en route to Lisbon. Post-photo perception of oddity.
