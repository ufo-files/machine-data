Description: On November 14, 2016, at 6:14 PM, a driver observed the movement of a white light in the sky for approximately 10 seconds. Having already seen shooting stars, he remains astonished that this one does not burn up and continues on its path towards the West. No other testimony was collected. The description provided corresponds perfectly to the observation of a slow-moving small meteor coming from the East and crossing the sky in front of the witness in a direction toward the West or Southwest. It is common for a shooting star not to be accompanied by a well-defined light trail (or tail), especially when its brightness is modest and the retinal persistence is consequently significantly reduced. In these cases, the possible colors and their variations are often difficult to perceive. At the time of observation, the radiant of the Taurid swarm (Taurid South then Taurid North) (constellation of Taurus), quite close to the star Aldabaran, is rising, just on the East-Northeast horizon (azimuth 70°/N). The meteors from this swarm (associated with comet 2P/Encke) are slow (~29 km/s), making it very possible that an observation duration of about ten seconds is consistent with the testimony. This swarm provides few meteors (<5/h) but spans from 09/10 to 12/10, with observation occurring only two days, the maximum (12/11 for the Northern Taurids) falling within a favorable period. It appears that this swarm may also produce bolides (fireballs) periodically. Based on its descriptive, temporal, and spatial characteristics, the observation reported is very likely that of a meteor from this swarm. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies this case as A: meteor.
