Summary: Observation of the movement of an orange-colored light phenomenon, then a second identical one: observation of two celestial lanterns.
--- seg-1ff55dd6da667f31e09bb0de [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 8, 2023, between 1:30 and 1:36 AM, a witness observed in front of his home an orange-colored light phenomenon pointing towards west-northwest, above the Vercors. He described this phenomenon as consisting of a central point surrounded by a bright orange glow of comparable intensity, according to the witness, to that of "a distant bright flame." Its movement was slow, following a horizontal trajectory. The estimated orientation was south/north or southwest-northeast. The elevation was estimated at 30-35° at the beginning of the observation and 30-40° at the end. After being lost from view after 10-15 seconds of observation, obscured by buildings, a second PAN was observed by the witness approximately 6 minutes later, and was identical in every way to the first. The witness was able to briefly film it before it passed through the clouds, slightly illuminating them as it went. Only one testimony was collected. Of average consistency and low strangeness after analysis, this observation is very likely that of two sky lanterns (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The described shape could be compared to that of a lantern
- The orange color and bright glow similar to that of a flame
- Movement in the direction of the wind
- A straight horizontal trajectory at the end
- The observation period, during the night between Saturday and Sunday, is ideal for the release of sky lanterns on the occasion of local or private festivities.

Finally, it should be noted that the observation likely occurred while the lanterns had already been released for some time, as they had time to ascend to the height of the clouds (located at 2100 meters altitude for the lowest layer), the second one even being obscured by them at the end of the observation. A lantern has a maximum lifespan of about 20 minutes, which gives it time to make this ascent once released. The witness mentioned a possible vertical upward trajectory at the beginning of the observation. It is probable that these lanterns have been in flight for some time already, as they reach the cloud layer and that the actual trajectory is difficult to appreciate due to the observation distance and a possible perspective effect. The video sent by the witness turns out to be unusable, as the PAN is not visible, even after improvement. Classification as "B": Very probable observation of two sky lanterns.
