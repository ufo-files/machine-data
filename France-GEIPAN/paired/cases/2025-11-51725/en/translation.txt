Summary: Observation of the movements of "7 white shapes" in the sky and gradual disappearances: observation of condensation trails from commercial aircraft.

**Description:** On November 3, 2025, at 5:35 PM, a single witness from La Grande-Motte beach observed seven white, elongated, horizontal PANs. Shortly after sunset, these PANs moved slowly to the west, some traveling from left to right and others in the opposite direction. After a few minutes of movement, they gradually disappeared, shrinking in size due to distance. The witness took five photographs with his smartphone, providing visual data used for expertise. No other witnesses came forward to the GEIPAN. The consistency of this case is good, despite a single testimony, as the analysis was supported by the five photographs taken by the observer. The strangeness experienced by the witness is significant, as the PANs had unusual characteristics for him. The hypothesis of observing condensation trails of planes seen from a great distance is corroborated by the following:

- Elongated shape and white/yellow color, typical of such trails observed during sunrise or sunset (other examples handled by GEIPAN are in Annex 1).
- In this specific context, at 5:35 PM facing full west, the setting sun provides a flat lighting that makes condensation trails at cruising altitude particularly visible. As the sky is already turning into twilight, these trails remain brightly lit, enhancing their contrast and allowing them to be distinguished hundreds of kilometers away.
- The aeronautical situation clearly shows the presence of two heavily trafficked air corridors oriented SO-NE, located between approximately 220 and 265 km (projected on the ground) over the Toulouse region (see Annex 2).
- Meteorological records from Montpellier (given in Annex 3) indicate exceptionally clear air at the time of observation, with maximum horizontal visibility (60 km indicated). This favors the visibility of condensation trails.
- The precise identification of the aircraft is complex due to the dense traffic in the affected air corridors at this time of day. However, analysis of the witness's photographs using the IPACO image processing software (Annex 4) allows for precise technical data to be established. Calculations determine that these aircraft are flying at cruising altitudes between 8 and 9 km, with speeds ranging from 800 to 916 km/h. At the time of observation, they were estimated to be between 275 and 314 km away from the witness. The hypothesis developed by the investigation reduces the strangeness of this observation, thus, the GEIPAN classifies the case as "A," observation of condensation trails of commercial aircraft.
