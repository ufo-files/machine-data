Summary: Observation of a white glowing object in the sky: observation of a commercial aircraft.
--- seg-ba5f47d08905c068fdee6881 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 12, 2022, between 6:00 PM and 6:10 PM, the witness, on a private terrace, was interrupted by the presence in the sky of an object they did not recognize, neither with the naked eye nor with binoculars. He first thinks of an aircraft, but the object has no wings or tail (stabilizer). It is a very bright, very dazzling white. The witness then thinks he may have observed a dirigible. Two other witnesses present also observe the PAN. During the observation, the witness takes a photograph with his/her mobile phone. The witness notes the presence of a commercial aircraft higher up at an axis almost perpendicular to the trajectory of the PAN about 2 to 3 minutes later. This aircraft leaves a condensation trail. Only one testimony will be collected. The consistency is correct: unique testimony but multiple witnesses and a photo of the PAN (without metadata). The residual strangeness after investigation is low (see the investigation report). The analysis showed that the witness had observed a commercial aircraft, the Mitsubishi CRJ-900LR, flight DLH3AJ, operated by Lufthansa. The description and visual aspect of the PAN in the photo: a very bright white cylinder, evoke the observation of an aircraft, and more particularly its fuselage, seen in broad daylight and contrasting against a blue sky. The mention of the observation of the second aircraft 2 to 3 minutes later on a trajectory perpendicular to that of PAN allowed the two aircraft to be identified: PAN and the well-identified aircraft by the witness. The observation conditions, in particular, the moderate observation angle, the distance, the high light reflectivity of the white aircraft fuselage, and atmospheric effects prevent the witnesses from recognizing the aircraft. Regarding the visibility of the tail, the color of the involved aircraft was azure blue, which could go unnoticed against a blue sky background. The straight trajectory at constant speed confirms the hypothesis. The two planes were not flying at the same altitude (approximately 600 meters apart), so it is likely that the pressure, temperature, and humidity conditions were different, explaining why only one of the planes generated a condensation trail. Due to the lack of more precise data (the closest radiosonde occurring approximately 200 km from the observation site and one hour earlier), this point cannot be formally confirmed, but remains entirely plausible. Similar situations have already been observed and studied by the GEIPAN. The case is classified A: observation of a commercial aircraft.
