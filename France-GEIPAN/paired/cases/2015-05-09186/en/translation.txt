Description: On May 26, 2015, from 9:45 PM to 10:00 PM, a witness observed a phenomenon similar to one seen a few weeks prior, which intrigued them. It was a glowing point, "less bright than Venus," static, with changing colors (green, red, yellow), visible to the naked eye, appearing to make small circles around a fixed point. Only one testimony was collected. This testimony is not very precise: the time is a priori incorrect, as the witness described and photographed at night, while the sun does not set until 10:07 PM. We will assume the observation took place around 11:00 PM. The direction of observation indicated is quite imprecise: the witness's diagram best shows the height on the horizon, estimated to be around 20°. The weather report for Brest indicates a mostly clear sky, and a north wind (see Météociel report). Observing a seemingly fixed glowing point in the sky naturally makes one think of a star (probably Capella, see the attached sky map), but the witness reports a spinning red, green, yellow light: this characteristic seems strange but can be explained by the twinkling phenomenon (see the article "Is it a Star?"). Twinkling and faster or slower variations in color and appearance are often observed and can lead to misunderstandings; these variations are particularly notable when the star is low in the sky and the atmosphere is both clear but disturbed (hot temperatures, wind), which was the case that day. The photograph, taken by hand with a camera equipped with a powerful zoom, shows a reddish-typical meander from camera shake. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of the star Capella.
