Summary: Low-altitude observation of lights followed by a dark V-shaped PAN that appears stationary: observation of a night tourist plane (pivot effect).
--- seg-b9636b64f691ef09874ec7e0 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 31, 2015, around 0:50-0:55 a.m., two witnesses (T1 driver, T2 passenger to his right) were on duty in a van located in the commune of Silly-Le-Long (60) and entered the National Highway 2 in the northeast direction (towards Nanteuil-Le-Haudouin (60)). The night is clear and the Moon is behind them. While driving, they notice at low altitude white (fixed) and red (flashing) lights that intrigue them. In fact, being familiar with the area and close to Roissy Airport, they are used to seeing planes, but what they see appears far too low and seems stationary, thus abnormal. Slowing down, they notice on their left a massive and dark PAN in the shape of a V. Just after crossing the bridge known as "de Silly," only T1 will be able to see the PAN. The observation while driving will be through the windshield, window, or rearview mirror; it will last between 1 and less than 4 minutes before the PAN is hidden by the road's terrain (descent). Witnesses reported their observation in a gendarmerie protocol, in a GEIPAN questionnaire. No other testimony was collected. This observation case was very interesting and required a lot of work for the GEIPAN: it ultimately represents a case study from several perspectives detailed below and in the attached investigation report. The witnesses observe in front of their vehicle, white and red lights arranged horizontally. These appear stationary. Upon reaching the height of the phenomenon, the witnesses observe a massive object, in the shape of a very flattened V. The main element that makes the observation strange (and motivates the testimony) is the "stationary levitation" aspect of an object with a triangular shape (not an airplane because it's stationary, and not the appearance of a helicopter). The investigation highlights that a tourist plane was flying at low altitude (Touch and Go maneuver) in the opposite direction of the witnesses' vehicle's movement. Considerable investigative work, including on-site reconstruction with witnesses and 3D simulations, based on the GPS navigation data of the plane and the trajectory of the witnesses' vehicle, highlights a pivot phenomenon, meaning the axis connecting the witnesses' position to that of the plane "pivots" essentially on itself, and can thus give the impression to witnesses who saw the plane that it is fixed at the pivot point. The pitch effect of this aircraft is especially proven and consistent with the visual observation of the PAN by the witnesses as the witnesses come closest to the PAN. The pivot is located at the level of a bridge spanning the road, and it is at this level that the PAN stationary object is observed. The reconstructed position of the plane and those testified to by the PAN are angularly close for much of the observation. The angular deviation can reach 20 to 30°, especially immediately after the bridge, but this remains compatible with a perception or memory error, especially since a deviation of the same order appears between the two testimonies. Here is the translation of the provided GEIPAN text:

This is a key element in favor of a misunderstanding by the witnesses with the aircraft. Furthermore, the witnesses do not distinguish the plane from the PAN, while the plane (if it is not the PAN) would only deviate by 20° or 30° and emits its lights at night on a few kilometers distance (even less at the pivot). Similarly, the pilot of the aircraft (interrogated) does not notice a large object (as described by the witnesses) emitting lights (strong enough to be seen several kilometers away by the witnesses) and located immediately near his landing trajectory. These elements taken alone would allow this case to be classified as a misidentification with the identified aircraft. Nevertheless, this case has been subject to more in-depth analysis due to significant discrepancies between the witnessed observation and the appearance of the plane. - Among the lights witnessed, some (color, flashing or not) cannot be explained by those of the plane. But the most significant deviation in the lights comes from one of the witnesses (T1) and misperception can be invoked. In particular, T1 perceives (after crossing the bridge) slowly flickering white rectangular lights that should have been point flashes (avion flash codes). - The main deviation from the airplane hypothesis lies in the angular size: that of the airplane, at the observed point, is 0.7° while the observed size ranges from 14 to 27°, according to witnesses and restitution methods. It is common to have an overestimation of the angular sizes of a PAN, but the difference here is very significant. However, it is noted that these angular returns of the size of PAN are obtained absolutely or without reliable targeting with ground objects. It is also noted that the most compelling angular discrepancies (the white lights differ from the aircraft not only by their rectangular appearance, but also by the angular gap between them) come solely from witness T1 while he was after the bridge and observing under at least uncomfortable and unfavorable conditions for precision (at night, looking backward while driving, relying mostly on the rearview mirror). Due to these discrepancies, this case was submitted in its entirety to the panel of experts of the GEIPAN. The elements in favor of misidentification with an airplane prevailed, and the majority of experts validated the hypothesis of a significant perception illusion, an illusion considered explicable by nighttime driving, observation in the rearview mirror, as well as fatigue or stress. These deviations are, however, significant and justify their inclusion in the GEIPAN database to support ongoing or future work on issues of perception or memory (whether specific to or unrelated to PAN). As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Misidentification with an airplane.
