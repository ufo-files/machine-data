Summary: Nighttime observation in the sky of the silent movement of a "white bar" passing through the constellation Ursa Major: observation of the satellite train Starlink® group 11.1 resulting from the launch carried out by SpaceX that morning from the Vandenberg base in the United States.
--- seg-743459ac8cc8a87472768c01 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 4, 2024, around 11 PM, from the terrace of their home located in Morey (Saône-et-Loire, 71), witnesses observed in the sky an unidentified aerospacial phenomenon (PAN) described as "a huge bright bar composed of white bulbs." This PAN moves silently along a curved trajectory from northwest to northeast at an elevation of approximately 45°, crossing the constellation Ursa Major. The witnesses estimate its apparent length at 30-40 cm, measured at arm's length using a graduated rule. The phenomenon disappears after 1 to 1 minute 30 of observation. Although the witnesses attempted to record a video confirming the observation time, it could not be used for analysis. The case consistency is good, with two concordant witness statements submitted to GEIPAN and one partially usable video (see investigation report)." *According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation confirms that witnesses observed the passage of Starlink® satellites from the launch of "Starlink group 11.1" by SpaceX on August 4 at 07:32 UTC. The 23 satellites, aligned in a linear formation (called a "satellite train"), were visible shortly after their ejection from the launcher's fairing, before dispersing into the target orbit. This visibility is explained by solar lighting, while the Sun was set for the observer. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Highly consistent appearance with that of a chain of satellites, a few hours after launch: punctual shape of illuminated elements, alignment of the whole chain, white color and high brightness.
- Confirmed launch on the same morning of the observation of such a chain from the Vandenberg base in the USA.
- Curved trajectory following the roundness of the Earth and consistent with that of a satellite train.
- Initial azimuth (northwest) consistent with that of the satellite train, with a passage through the constellation Ursa Major.
- Final azimuth at northeast consistent with that of the satellite train.
- Disappearance by entry of the train into the Earth's shadow cone. This passage could indeed be photographed and filmed by other people in France and Switzerland, confirming that the satellites passed through the Ursa Major constellation. The term "imaged" here refers to the visual capture (photographs or videos) of the phenomenon. The strangeness of the observation is reduced when it is compared to the proven passage of Starlink® satellites from group 11.1. The case is classified "A", observation of the Starlink® satellite train group 11.1.
