Summary: Observation of a black patch in stationary black fog in a clear sky: unexplained phenomenon of average strangeness (D1).
--- seg-23eb325369544e48ff42ca83 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 23, 2015, in the late afternoon, a witness goes to the Fumel aerodrome (in the commune of Montayral, 47) by car to "get some air with his dog." He walks along the hangars and parks his vehicle in a small space near the surveyor's markers. He opens the door, lets his dog out, and when he turns around, he sees "a black patch in a black fog" in the sky. The PAN appears with "some sort of handle" in the form of a semicircular arc at the bottom left and "high and far away": it remains perfectly motionless and makes no noise. The sky is blue, there is no mist. The witness does not have binoculars or a camera to observe the PAN. At one point, he turns his eyes away from the phenomenon for a few seconds, then tries to see the PAN again but in vain, it's gone. Amazed by this observation, the witness thinks it will be mentioned in the press the next day by other witnesses, which is unlikely. No other testimony will be collected. Three factors (immobility for 20 minutes, sudden disappearance, distant round shape with protuberance) stand in opposition, either separately or in combination, to the various hypotheses considered: star (appearance and disappearance), plane (immobility), helicopter (appearance, noise, and not detected by radar), the witness's ophthalmic problem (immobility). The investigation focuses on a balloon, which cannot be a rubber balloon (too small) or a solar balloon (cause of disappearance? little realism at this location), then on a hot air balloon hypothesis. The hypothesis is realistic at this location and in the late afternoon sun. The size (10 to 20 meters) provides the minimum distance (a few kilometers) for a perception of distance and blur around the phenomenon. Nevertheless, this hypothesis faces two difficulties: - the description of the protuberance, which is difficult to explain by the gondola (protuberance not oriented downwards). However, this element is not the most clearly detected by the witness (only after 10 minutes with a significant concentration and focus effort), - and especially the rapid disappearance. The witness diverted his gaze and did not find the phenomenon again. It is difficult to assess the duration of non-observation and its compatibility with a hot air balloon descent during this duration. This would be compatible if several unknowns (hot air balloon size) or error margins (the witness's angular data on the size and elevation of the PAN) are taken into account in a favorable sense, in addition, of course, to the temporal conjunction (with the gaze diversion). In total, the hot air balloon hypothesis is the strongest but its probability is well below 50% and cannot therefore be retained. The various hot air balloon pilots in the department, contacted for this investigation, did not respond to the requests, which in itself does not constitute an argument against the hypothesis. This is a unique testimony, without photography. It is quite possible (rural area) that the phenomenon was not seen by others. The witness is precise and credible, the consistency (level of information and credibility) of the testimony is just sufficient (given the level of strangeness) to validate the unexplained character of the observation (see the investigation report). Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as D1: Explained phenomenon of average strangeness. Note: this case was submitted to the panel of experts of the GEIPAN.
