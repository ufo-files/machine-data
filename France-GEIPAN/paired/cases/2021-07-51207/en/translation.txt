Description: On the night of July 2 to 3, 2021, the witness is in his courtyard observing the sky, an activity he does daily. Suddenly, he spots a flat "V" shaped object, dark gray to black in color, moving from west to east, passing vertically over his position. Equipped with a laser, he directs the beam at the PAN and notices that the light "flicks" upon contact, revealing a surface covered in honeycomb-like structures reminiscent of a bee's nest. He then follows the object towards the east, noting that it maintains a constant altitude and a regular speed. After traveling approximately 300 or 400 meters, the object suddenly makes a half-turn in a smooth and controlled movement, returning in its direction. The witness, still pointing their laser at the object, feels a strong sense of dread, even experiencing weakness in their legs. The object slowed down above him, seeming to almost hover, then suddenly accelerated to the west until it disappeared. The witness also specifies that during the previous three nights, he observed to the south a luminous phenomenon: an object that seems to grow progressively while making slight movements. He finally mentions a persistent feeling of being watched. Only one witness statement is collected in the context of this observation. Some elements of the testimony led to the hypothesis of a drone observation, particularly due to the "V" shape, characteristic of certain fixed-wing drones of the "flying wing" type (see the investigation report). The absence of angular data is a major obstacle in evaluating the hypothesis, as it prevents any precise estimation of the distances and dimensions of the observed object. Furthermore, it should be noted that the observation zone is strictly prohibited for drone overflights, particularly at night. Several highly probable elements suggest that the witness observed a real phenomenon and correctly identified its form: - The interaction of the handheld laser with the surface of the PAN, although not allowing for an estimate of its distance due to the high range of the device (5,000 to 8,000 meters). - The presence of a halo around the PAN, facilitating the perception of its silhouette and thus excluding a shape illusion, despite the lack of contrast between the object's dark hue and that of the night sky. The consistency* of this case is low: a single witness, absence of angular data elements, and no photo or video of the PAN. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Thus, although it is likely that the witness observed a real phenomenon, it seems particularly difficult in this context of lack of data for us to study it further. The GEIPAN classifies the case as C: the absence of consolidated data does not allow for certain conclusions about the observed phenomenon.
