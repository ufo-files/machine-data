Summary: Observation from a moving train of a moving light point in the sky: observation of Venus and illusion of movement of the phenomenon.
--- seg-e2160c7dc641a71fdbe52896 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On February 15, 2022, at 07:30, a passenger on TGV INOUI 8483 traveling from Paris to Poitiers observed a very bright white point in the sky. Despite several plausible explanation hypotheses put forward by the witness, the latter considers that none of them correspond to the trajectory and movement of the PAN (presumed UFO) observed. Only one testimony was collected. According to Geipan criteria, the consistency of this case is correct (consistency is the amount of reliable information gathered for a testimony), meaning that although this case is based on only one testimony, it is supported by photos and a short but exploitable video (refer to the investigation report). The overall appearance of the PAN in the photographs and video, as well as the description provided by the witness, perfectly match the characteristics of Venus (shape, color, brightness, point-like aspect). The description of the PAN, the images (photos and videos), and the PAN identified in the drawing provided by the witness, are consistent with the appearance of Venus. An astronomical verification using Stellarium indicates that the celestial body was at an azimuth of 141°, in perfect correlation with the azimuth of the PAN visible in the video. Furthermore, the witness claims there was only one bright point in the sky. Since Venus was clearly visible at that time and in the direction indicated by the witness, the latter would have seen and filmed two bright points instead of one. The main peculiarity perceived by the witness is the unusual movement and speed of the phenomenon, which can be attributed to what is commonly called the "illusion of the following sphere". Being on board a TGV traveling up to 300 km/h, the passing of the landscape, clouds, and the train's direction changes can create an impression of movement of the observed phenomenon. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: Observation of Venus.
