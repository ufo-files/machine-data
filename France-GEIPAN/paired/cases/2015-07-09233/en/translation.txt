Summary: Observation of the silent passage of 15 to 20 orange-colored light points in the night sky: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-e17e131d84b5b9ee53f44db4 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 6, 2015, at 10:45 PM, two individuals observed multiple glowing points in the sky (between 15 and 20). These pink-orange points were in a line and moved silently towards Aix-les-Bains in a clear sky. The observation lasted approximately 10 to 15 seconds. Only one testimony was collected. The observation of orange balls crossing the sky in less than a minute, on a summer night, strongly resembles Thai lanterns, likely released nearby after a private party. The movement was observed from south to north, which is consistent with the reported south wind in Chambéry-Aix-les-Bains. The Meteociel report for the observation day indicates a light south wind of 2-9 km/h recorded in Chambéry. The distance estimate made by the witness cannot be accurate as they had never seen such an object before. Since Thai lanterns are inherently dim (large candle flame), they are always perceived as farther away and therefore larger and faster than they actually are. See the article "Estimation of size and distance of an unknown phenomenon" on the GEIPAN website. The very regular movement of the objects in the sky is perfectly normal for an object carried by a light wind; if the objects fly over industrial areas with strong ventilation or intense heat sources, the trajectory will of course be irregular. As lanterns are often released one after the other, after the same delay corresponding to the time of the burner ignition, the result is a very organized line of flying lights. The videos mentioned by the witness seem to correspond entirely to Thai lantern releases.
