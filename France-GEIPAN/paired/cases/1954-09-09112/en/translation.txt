Description: On September 27, 1954, at 20:30, three children (aged 12, 9, and 8) observed near their home a strange aluminum-colored, rectangular craft that was partially split down the middle in the vertical direction. The craft has two angled supports on each side at its base. It measures approximately two meters high by one meter wide. One of the children throws a stone, then an arrow from a toy he is holding at the advancing craft, which slowly tips over the child. The last frightened one returns home and goes to bed. Two sisters will also see the craft and, frightened, will go hide. No witness will see the craft disappear. The two sisters state that they also saw a red glow swaying close to the ground near their residence. The parents will not give any credence to the children's statements. Their teacher will report the observation to the gendarmerie. During the gendarmerie investigation at the time, two reports were drawn up. The captain does not question the credibility of the children. These individuals live on a remote farm, do not read newspapers, and do not go to the cinema. Ground tracks are found in the direction of the red glow's appearance: it is a large crown in the laid-down grass oriented clockwise. These tracks are not recognized as being the trampling of people or animals. Four holes are also found in this crown. The GEIPAN was aware of the investigation by private ufologist Yves Bosson who met with the witnesses in the 1980s, who confirmed having pulled a prank whose consequences they did not anticipate. His report was presented at the Lyon Congress in 1992 ("Actes des sixièmes Rencontres européennes de Lyon, 1-2-3 mai 1992", SOS OVNI, Aix-en-Provence 1992, pp. 4-16), and then published in the chapter "Prémanon or Innocence: An Investigation into a Case Above All Suspicion" in the book "OVNI, Vers une Anthropologie d'un Mythe Contemporain" by Thierry Pinvidic et al., Heimdal éditeur, France, pp. 123-145, 1993. Pending verification of these seemingly reliable claims, the GEIPAN classifies this case as 'C': lack of confirmed information.
