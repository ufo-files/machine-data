Summary: Sequential observations of a bright PAN fixed in the night sky: mistaken for Venus.
--- seg-091c463c84fef459fd8505fa [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 8, 2020, between 4:30 AM and 5:10 AM, a witness is intrigued by the presence in the sky of a bright PAN seen intermittently several times. The witness estimates he observed the PAN for 5 minutes, spread across 3 observation phases. He takes photographs with his smartphone. The PAN remains stationary from 4:30 AM to 5:10 AM. Upon returning home, intrigued by what he sees in his photos, he steps outside again one to two minutes later but the PAN is gone. Only one testimony is collected. With a low strangeness rating and good consistency (single witness, but quite detailed testimony, photos of the PAN), the case turns out to be a misidentification with Venus (see the investigation report). The description of the PAN and its appearance in the photos are characteristic of an astronomical misidentification. However, it turns out that the position of the PAN corresponds to that of Venus. The only seemingly strange aspect is the sudden disappearance of the PAN, one to two minutes after the witness last saw it, but it should be noted that the movement of clouds at that observation time explains the disappearance of Venus, temporarily obscured by one of them. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": misidentification with Venus.
