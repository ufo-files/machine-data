Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named CHARLEVILLE-MEZIERES (08) 09.01.1994 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On January 9, 1994, around 7:45 PM, the front passenger (T1) of a vehicle observed a bright phenomenon in the sky that intrigued her. At several different locations, the witness observed a rectangular shape, then a diamond shape, and finally an oval shape with lights of various colors. No particular noise was heard. The witness suddenly felt afraid as the shape seemed to be moving towards her. She went to the gendarmerie, and the driver (T2), who was unable to make the same observations as his passenger, confirmed her account orally. No other testimonies or information were collected during the gendarmerie investigation. Despite a gendarmerie investigation, many basic elements necessary for this type of analysis are missing (see the investigation report). For each of the phases: - the azimuth or a directional reference point where the PAN was located; - the elevation above the horizon; - the duration of observation; - the angular dimensions of the PAN. We have a second witness T2 who says "confirm the account of T1" but at the same time indicates that he only saw the PAN at point #3 and never again, without making or signing any statement. We do not know if the vision of the PAN at point 3 matches that of T1. The fact that T1 did not share her second observation at point #5 with T2, and that T2 did not testify, also weakens the consistency of the entire testimony, which is already heavily marred by a lack of precision. The consistency (level and reliability of the information) of the testimony is too low to build a hypothesis or to formulate any conclusion. In this context, the strange and inexplicable character that may have prevailed during the initial investigation cannot be retained. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as C: lack of reliable information.
