Description: The witness and his nephew were in the garden of their home when they observed an unusual phenomenon. Around 3:00 a.m. local time on August 13, 2021, the witness noticed a bright point in the sky moving very slowly. By the time he alerted his nephew, the point had stopped. A few moments later, the nephew also noticed a point that, in his words, "moved strangely." The point would advance a little, then retreat, moving back and forth, always returning to the same position. These movements were nearly imperceptible in the sky. The witness attempted to film the event but was unsuccessful. Around 5:00 a.m., they went to bed. The observation lasted for two hours, intermittently, as the witnesses also watched meteors and other satellites during that time. Contacted by phone on November 15, 2021, the witness described the object as a small point that seemed very far away, no larger than a star. Remote investigation determined that the light point was located at an azimuth close to 90° (East). Consistency was average. The phone conversation helped clarify the azimuth but not the elevation precisely. The duration of the phenomenon (2 hours) and its description point towards an astronomical misperception. In the Eastern time frame, the Stellarium software highlights a possible candidate: Aldébaran (a double variable star) with an azimuth range of 79° to 100° and an elevation of 13° to 35° during the 3:00 a.m. to 5:00 a.m. period. Since the witnesses did not observe the PAN continuously, they may not have noticed the changes in azimuth. The magnitude of 0.85 makes Aldébaran less visible, which is consistent with the witness's description of "this bright point being weaker than a star." The perceived strangeness by the witness is due to the movements of the PAN that he observed and described as "it moves strangely." He explained that it would advance slightly, then retreat, moving back and forth but always returning to the same position. This is the autokinetic effect, a well-known optical illusion among astronomers. It makes a fixed light phenomenon appear to be moving when placed in a medium with few reference points, such as a dark night sky. See link https://scilogs.fr/raisonetpsychologie/leffet-autocinetique/. The GEIPAN concludes that the observation was of the star Aldébaran and classifies it as A.
