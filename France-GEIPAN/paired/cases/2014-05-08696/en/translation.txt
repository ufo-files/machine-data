Description: Above the English Channel on May 13, 2014, at 5:35 pm (local time), a pilot reports to air traffic control an observation of a puzzling light phenomenon. To the left of the aircraft, he sees one or two silver balls. No other witnesses have been found for this date. The very brief testimony simply states "one or two silver balls on the left" without specifying the duration or direction of movement of the phenomenon. The sun is at an azimuth of 255° (SW) at an altitude of 36°, to the right of the plane. Weather conditions in Caen indicate significant cloud cover. The reported phenomenon (silver ball) is to the left of the plane. If the phenomenon were moving at the plane's speed, in the same direction, it could be a glory effect (Brocken specter, pilot's cocard) in the direction opposite the sun, projected onto the cloud layer. It could also be the atmospheric reentry of space debris or a meteoroid. Due to the lack of details in the testimony, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" for lack of information.
