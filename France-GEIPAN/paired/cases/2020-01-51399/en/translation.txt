**Description:** On January 27, 2020, a single witness was driving on D96 within the commune of Theizé when they briefly spotted, to their right and at a distance, an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon (PAN). Approaching it, the witness decided to stop on the side of the road to observe the anomaly for approximately twenty seconds. At that moment, the PAN appeared as an elongated, stationary object composed of multiple cubes of varying sizes, with hues ranging from "gray-white to black." A perceptible heat emission seemed to be occurring, altering its visual appearance. The witness then attempted to capture an image of the phenomenon using their phone. However, they momentarily lost sight of it for about two seconds. Upon resuming observation, the object's appearance had significantly changed: it now resembled a small tourist airplane, characterized by a white-gray livery with black stripes. The object then moved at a reduced speed before disappearing behind the roof of a residence.

The consistency* of this case is low: it relies on a single witness, without visual support (photo or video) of the PAN, and the testimony was reported more than two years after the observation. According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Three hypotheses were considered to explain this observation: a tourist airplane, a wine spraying aircraft, and a radio-controlled model aircraft. The appearance and behavior of the PAN—especially during the final phase of observation—correspond exactly to those of a small airplane (see the investigation report). The wine spraying aircraft hypothesis is deemed unlikely due to the specific operational constraints of this activity, including property size, altitude restrictions, and potential nuisance to neighbors. Furthermore, the radio-controlled model aircraft hypothesis was ruled out as the observation area was too far from regulated spaces for such an aircraft. The small airplane or glider hypothesis is supported by the proximity to an airport. A check with the local flying club confirmed that no flights had been recorded on the day of the observation. However, the significant delay between the event and the testimony does not allow for additional verifications with the National Center for Air Operations (CNOA) or private specialized platforms.

Moreover, this hypothesis does not explain the atypical aspect of the PAN during the second phase of observation. The witness then mentioned a possible "perceptual error" that remains to be proven. The over two-year delay before the witness reported their observation presents a major obstacle to analyzing this case, greatly limiting the possibility of verifying the hypotheses. Such a latency can lead to memory alteration, whose impact on the reliability of the testimony is difficult to quantify. However, this uncertainty does not invalidate the witness's reported sense of strangeness. The case is classified as "C," an unexplained phenomenon due to a lack of information and technical elements necessary for a thorough analysis of the observation.

* *consistance* - in this context, referring to the reliability and completeness of the evidence supporting the testimony.
