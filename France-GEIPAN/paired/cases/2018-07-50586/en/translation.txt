Summary: Observations by two witnesses of the silent NE-SO passage of 4 rapidly blinking white light points: probable misidentification with LED balloons.
--- seg-fb0ec77a30089ec60a00b8de [needs-review] ---
Description: On Tuesday, July 31, 2018, between 11:00 PM and 11:15 PM, two individuals in an alleyway of a village were intrigued by the silent passage of 4 blinking white lights in the clear sky. The points, which seemed grouped, moved towards the southwest: the observation lasted about a minute. The witnesses provided a video reconstruction of their observation. Of average strangeness and good consistency (two witnesses, one reconstruction but no photo), this case appears to be a probable misidentification with LED balloons (see the investigation report). The testimony provided by the witnesses is quite precise and allows us to conclude that the indicated time is accurate, as the observation window could only have occurred between 11:00 PM (public lighting extinction) and 11:15 PM (passing of a commercial airliner). The description of the PAN is typical of a formation flight of flying balloons: joint movement of light points, complete silence, trajectory consistent with wind direction. However, the description presents two peculiarities compared to classic misidentifications with Thai lanterns: the color of the points and their blinking. These apparent peculiarities rule out the hypothesis of a misidentification with Thai lanterns. On the other hand, LED balloons can explain these peculiarities, as they are white in color and can exhibit a slow blinking mode. Being less common than traditional Thai lanterns, it is understandable that the witnesses were unable to identify LED balloons. Without precise confirmation of a release of LED balloons, the hypothesis is not certain but probable. The case is classified as B, probable misidentification with LED balloons.
