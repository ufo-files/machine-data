Summary: Observations by 3 witnesses of a silent, gray-silver half-sphere passing through the sky with "tentacles" below: likely observations of a Mylar balloon.
--- seg-089a3d40ab3b39f7b0a26b4a [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named MARDIE (45) on 02.05.1990 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. It concerns the observation by three witnesses of a PAN from their garden, then from the street in front of their home in BOU (45), at 21:00 on 02.05.1990. The witnesses describe the silent passage of an oval or half-spherical gray-silver shape with appendages visible beneath the PAN, disappearing into the horizon. The PAN is described as oval (though the drawings rather depict a half-sphere), gray/silver in color, with a "polished" appearance. The three witnesses also note the presence beneath the PAN of what they describe as appendages, stalks, or "tentacles," as well as light reflection phenomena on its surface, identified by witness 1 as likely caused by the impact of solar rays on that surface (see the investigation report). The PAN is finally described as silent and moving at a constant speed. Its movement is in the direction of the light breeze. Estimates of the duration of the observation vary, ranging from one minute to five minutes. All these data are compatible with the hypothesis of a "Mylar" fantasy balloon (reflecting sunlight) moving straight and regularly in the wind, the "appendages" being then the strings holding it in place on the ground or a component of the fantasy balloon (such as "legs" for an animal-shaped balloon). Such balloons already existed in 1990. This is an urbanized area and still during the day. Such events tend to occur more often on weekends. However, a birthday party can also be held on the actual day, or there may have been some kind of activity in the town or further upstream along the wind. The fact that nothing can be found near 30 years later cannot disprove this hypothesis, which can therefore be considered probable given the strong consistency on other points. During the observation and initial investigation, the GEIPAN had little or no experience of misidentifications caused by Mylar balloons (see on the website www.geipan.fr, the cases recorded since, search for cases with keywords Mylar or balloon). The consistency is average, mainly based on the good concordance of the descriptions and sketches of the PAN made by the witnesses, as well as on the plans of the site with the trajectory of the PAN established by the Gendarmes. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a Mylar balloon.
