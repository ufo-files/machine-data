--- seg-2d93e07b816f04a6d416bcdb [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On January 5, 2026, at Maulde (Nord, France), a witness observed a luminous phenomenon in the sky from his home between 03:12 and 03:45 UTC (04:12-04:45 local time) for approximately 33 minutes. He makes this observation from his rural home, through an open window, with the naked eye and then using Leica 8×56 binoculars. Five videos of the phenomenon were taken using a smartphone iPhone 13 placed behind the binoculars. Another witness was present, but did not wish to complete the questionnaire GEIPAN. The original videos were transmitted and analyzed as part of the investigation, confirming the solid consistency of the case and enabling a direct analysis of the observed phenomenon (see the two most legible videos published). The strangeness perceived by the main witness lies in his inability to recognize the phenomenon, despite the investigations he conducted. The examination of the testimony and videos allows for the description of the PAN as a succession of bright points appearing in a sector of the sky located to the east (azimuth approximately 80°/90°), at a maximum height estimated around 20° above the horizon. The objects appeared primarily as orange light points, accompanied by light variations. The PAN appear to move slowly and regularly, mainly according to a descending vertical trajectory, some seemingly crossing in the observed area. The phenomenon concerns a portion of the sky estimated by the witness to be approximately 10 to 20 degrees in apparent diameter. No noise was perceived and no thermal signature was detected during an observation attempt with thermal binoculars. Video analysis reveals two distinct trajectories: a steep descending leftward trajectory and a very slight descending rightward trajectory, respectively oriented roughly SSO/SO towards NNE/NE and O towards E (see attached reconstruction document). The appearance of the PAN in both cases is strictly identical, so it is likely the same phenomenon. A first hypothesis explores the observation of Starlink® flares (sunlight reflection on satellites), the direction and azimuth of the phenomenon to the east, corresponding to those of a setting sun. However, the duration of these flares as well as the too high appearance elevation strongly invalidate the hypothesis. The second hypothesis explored, more consistent with the observation, is that of celestial lanterns. It relies on the following elements:

- Pointed or spherical shape, discernible according to distance, consistent.
- Orange color and strong brightness, consistent with the camera settings (high sensitivity).
- Fluctuating brightness caused by the lantern's flame, subject to local aerodynamic currents, as the lantern is open at the bottom.
- Number: a total of 13 PAN were identified in the video, consistent with a release in numbers.
- Straight and regular trajectory, consistent with objects carried by the wind.
- Observation duration for each individual varying between 9 and 41 seconds, consistent with the maximum lifespan of lanterns, which can last up to 10/15 minutes.
- Some PAN disappear in the videos, corresponding to the lantern's extinction due to depleted fuel. The residual oddity resides in the two different trajectories taken by the PAN. However, we were able to demonstrate through the analysis of meteorological elements, and in particular the AROME modeling data provided by Météo-France, that these two trajectories were oriented in the direction of the winds which varied with altitude. Thus, at relatively low altitude (near the ground up to about 100 m), the winds were directed SSO, pushing the lanterns located at these altitudes in that direction, while at higher altitudes, around 750/1000 m, the winds were directed OSO/O, pushing the lanterns located at these altitudes in the other direction. The totality of the case elements - orange color, point or spherical shape, flickering brightness, multiplicity of luminous points, observation duration of each PAN, absence of noise, slow and regular movement according to straight-line trajectories oriented in the direction of the winds, absence of thermal signature, and presence of coherent videos consistent with the witness's description, converge towards the identification of the phenomenon as wind-carried sky lanterns, released successively in a regular manner. Based on these information, the case is classified as "A": observation of celestial lanterns. *CONSISTENCY: According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.
