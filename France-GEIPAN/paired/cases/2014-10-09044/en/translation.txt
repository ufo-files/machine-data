Description: The observation of orange light shapes crossing the sky in less than a minute, on a weekend night, strongly resembles a Thai lantern, likely released nearby after a private party, possibly related to Halloween. The movement is observed from south to northeast. The wind at Orly is light (4-7 km/h) from the south (southeast reported at Toussus and Pontoise). The witness describes a movement towards the north and east, which could be consistent with local wind, though difficult to estimate precisely due to the proximity of the Seine. But the rapid movement (12 seconds to traverse a large part of the sky) is poorly compatible with a light wind. The instabilities observed could have been caused by non-uniform local winds. The dynamic of the phenomenon described by the witness does not well fit this Thai lantern hypothesis. (See the witness's reconstruction video). Due to the lack of precise meteorological knowledge and cross-references, the GEIPAN case is classified as "C".
