**Report:**

On February 15, 2018, around 9:30 PM, a witness driving on the A19 highway between Beaune-la-Rolande (45) and Chilleurs-aux-Bois (45) observed, on their right, a PAN in the shape of a triangle with two red lights. Initially, the witness thought it was an airplane, but they noticed the object making a right turn across the highway in front of them, with the red lights turning into light blue. They also reported seeing two white lights located below and in front of the craft. The object then made a second right turn to follow the highway. The witness attempted without success to photograph the PAN and opened their vehicle window, hearing no noise from the object. The observation, estimated to last between 2 and 5 minutes, ended when the PAN disappeared into a cloud. The *consistency* of the case is average, with a single witness who was unable to take a photo of the PAN.

According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. In this investigation (see the investigation report), we examined the hypothesis that the witness observed a military aircraft, which appears probable for the following reasons:

- The presence approximately 30-40 km from Airbase 123 in Orléans, towards which the PAN was heading at the end of the observation. This base houses military transport aircraft, such as the C-130 Hercules.
- The observed colors, red and white, correspond to standard aeronautical lights. The shade described by the witness as "light blue" is actually slightly bluish white. The absence of reporting the green light, usually positioned on the right wing, can be explained by the object's successive positions relative to the witness, limiting visibility.
- The observation conditions were particularly unfavorable: it was nighttime, in a vehicle traveling at approximately 130 km/h, under active rain with windshield wipers in use, while the witness remained focused on driving, performing a pass as the PAN crossed the highway.

These factors very likely contributed to preventing the witness from recognizing the craft, despite their repeated statements describing an airplane. Consequently, the precise perception of the craft's shape and engine sounds could not be assured. Note that a military aircraft, the C-130 Hercules, has a navigation light configuration similar to the lights described by the witness (presence of white and red lights), although we cannot explain why the witness mentions two red lights when this aircraft has only one. The C-130 also has white and red anti-collision lights that a pilot can turn off for training needs, which could explain why the witness did not mention seeing flashing lights.

Finally, the behavior of the PAN, described by the witness as "unusual," contributes to the uniqueness of the observation. This phenomenon can be explained by the craft's relatively low speed and significant variations in its apparent speed, as perceived by the witness, resulting from both the two successive turns made by the aircraft and the witness's travel speed. However, the significant delay between the observation and the investigation did not allow for formal confirmation of the presence of such an aircraft at the location and time of the observation.

The case is classified as "B," a probable observation of a military aircraft.
