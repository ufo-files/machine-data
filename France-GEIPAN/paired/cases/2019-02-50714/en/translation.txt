Summary: Multiple and lengthy night-time observations of a luminous phenomenon appearing static at a low angular height in the sky: astronomical misidentification with the prominent star Sirius.
--- seg-e14dfb3152582618c5a3f4b6 [needs-review] ---
**Description:** A witness during the nights of February 15-16 and 18-19, 2019, observed from the first floor of their home a luminous phenomenon, appearing static in the sky and at a low angular height. The MUFON Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon (PAN) report is observed intermittently for approximately one hour each time. The witness took two photos allowing for the identification of the phenomenon. They describe the PAN as a large, brighter-than-surrounding star that appears to follow a curved trajectory to the right and descending. Only one testimony was collected. The witness noted the presence of blue light at times, which is a chromatic aberration (see investigation report). Several photos were taken, showing reference points that identify the phenomenon as Sirius (Alpha Canis Majoris), the primary star in the constellation Canis Major. Note that this star is the brightest star in the sky after the Sun. All available and analyzable data confirms the confusion with the star Sirius. This case is to be classified as "A": Astronomical confusion with the notable star Sirius.
