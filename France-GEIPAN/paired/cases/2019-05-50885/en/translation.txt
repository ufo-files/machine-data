Summary: Nighttime observation and photography of the movement of a bright point in the sky: mistaken for the International Space Station (ISS).
--- seg-076d13009868af0b830a105a [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: During a night walk on May 31, 2019 at 11:30 PM, a witness was surprised to catch sight in the sky, at low altitude, of a very bright PAN spherical object moving. They had time to take photographs before the PAN disappeared from view due to distance. Only one testimony was collected. The best photograph is addressed to GEIPAN. An email from the witness regarding the direction of movement of the PAN provides important detail. Of low strangeness and average consistency (single witness, poor quality photograph), the case turns out to be a misidentification with the International Space Station (ISS) (see the investigation report). The description of the PAN strongly resembles the ISS, the only satellite bright enough to be easily photographed. The slow and regular movement, as well as the round shape of the PAN, are very consistent with the ISS, which had just passed overhead at an angular height consistent with the description of the PAN. Only the trajectory of the ISS appears reversed compared to that of the PAN, but the witness very likely reversed the cardinal directions (the PAN's movement from right to left indicated in one of their emails). This case should be compared to another observation of PAN made at a consistent time, which was also found to be a misidentification with the ISS. The case is classified A, misidentification with the ISS.
