Description: On September 10, 2017, between 9:45 PM and 10:00 PM, while caring for her child, the witness noticed a brief, bright light passing by. The object was described as having a round, fiery, yellow-orange shape that descended silently. The witness quickly moved towards the window but the light was gone. Thinking the object might have fallen nearby, she searched the next morning but found nothing. The witness reported seeing a very bright light (dazzling) for 1 to 2 seconds from her window, giving the impression that the round shape was on fire. This description aligns with numerous other reports (at least 25) of a meteoroid entering Earth's atmosphere on September 10 at 7:29 PM Coordinated Universal Time (21:29 local French time), visible from Meurthe-et-Moselle to Bouches-du-Rhône, passing through the Pyrenees-Orientales, Isere, and Switzerland. It appears the meteoroid fragmented into multiple pieces at the very end of its luminous trajectory (more details on the website link: REFORME. This meteoroid was also detected by 14 cameras in the FRIPON network. The time indicated by the witness does not exactly match the meteoroid's passage, but the provided timing is not very precise as the witness placed her observation between 9:45 PM and 10:00 PM, stating her alarm clock was the correct time, but she may not have checked her alarm clock immediately prior to the observation, which could explain the discrepancy with other meteoroid reports. The altitude estimate (4 meters) was intuitive. Although the witness perceived the phenomenon as very close, without an objective reference point, meteoroid events, often perceived as very close by witnesses, actually occur at altitudes of 100 to 50 kilometers, and are thus visible from the ground over an area approximately 200 kilometers in diameter. Despite the time difference with the witness's provided timeframe, the appearance and trajectory described leave little doubt about its correspondence with this widely observed meteoroid. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": very probable observation of a meteoroid.
