Summary: Discontinuous observation of a white and flat saucer-shaped phenomenon at low altitude: lack of information.

**Description:** On June 11, 2014, around 7:45 PM, a solo driver is surprised to see a white, "relatively large" phenomenon at low altitude along the road axis (East or South-East). He brings the observed shape closer to the image of a flat flying saucer underneath. Continuing his journey, he loses sight of the phenomenon, hidden by trees, but spots it again a few kilometers further on as a very small point in the sky. Only one testimony about this phenomenon was collected. The PAN (Unidentified Flying Object) was visible in the road axis, that is, towards the East or South-East. Several hypotheses have been put forward (see investigation notes). The hypothesis of a misperception with the Moon at its rising was examined: fair weather, PAN very low on the horizon and constantly moving away from the witness, time close to that indicated for the observation. Moreover, it should be noted that with an azimuth of 116°, the Moon was visible in the axis of D25 at the level of the roundabout where the witness spotted the PAN. However, the lack of precision (absence of direction during the last phase of observation) prevents the formal validation of the hypothesis of a misperception with the Moon. With average strangeness and weak consistency (single witness, no photo, late testimony), this case proves to be too imprecise to be identified. It is not the visual perception of the witness that is in question, but the interpretation the witness makes of his observation through his feeling (surprise, excitement, observation during automobile driving). This case is classified C due to lack of precision.
