Summary: Observations by two independent witnesses of rapid movements of a colored light phenomenon towards Mont Cimon: probable observations of a TLP (Transient Lightning-like Phenomenon).
--- seg-6d113d3eeedf7404d2cb219b [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named LA MURE (38) 21.11.1978 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed (see the investigation report). On November 21, 1978, a witness (T1) on his way to work noticed the presence of a PAN at half the height of Mont Cimon from his car. The witness followed the rapid movements with trajectory changes of the PAN, then it circled the left side of Mont Cimon and disappeared. The observation lasted about 3 minutes. During his testimony at the Gendarmerie, the witness stated that one of his neighbors had seen a similar phenomenon in the direction of Mont Cimon on November 18 and 19, 1978, around 21:45. One hypothesis is consistent with all the descriptions made by the two independent witnesses. It is an unknown phenomenon at the time of the observation, whose discovery is recent and whose study has developed significantly in recent years. This phenomenon, referred to by the acronym "P.L.O.T." for "Transitory Lightning-like Luminescent Phenomenon," appears during anticyclonic conditions and/or in the presence of electrical installations (two points consistent with the observation environment) capable of generating electromagnetic loops, facilitating the emergence of these luminescent phenomena. The GEIPAN has, among its partners, an expert from the Lightning Research Laboratory who was consulted for this case. P.L.O.T.s are rare phenomena but frequent enough to be characterized by this laboratory (testimony collection, field observation). The appearance of the PAN, as described by the witnesses (a sphere surrounded by a halo), is found in a large number of P.L.O.T. observations. This phenomenon can be visible for several minutes and can remain static, consistent with the description of the PAN by the witnesses. Its repetition over several days is possible and has already been observed, the conditions prevailing for its formation having possibly varied very little over the considered time period. The presence of a forest on the side of a hill, in the observation area with the presence of electrical currents above the trees, favors the rapid movement of the P.L.O.T., consistent with what the witnesses reported. It is not possible to verify the presence of certain local meteorological conditions that could favor the phenomenon and its reproduction over several days, even though the existence of such conditions is highly plausible. However, this is not enough to invalidate the hypothesis given the total consistency of the phenomenon with what is described. The testimony is highly consistent with two independent witnesses and has strong overlaps. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a Transitory Lightning-like Luminescent Phenomenon ("P.L.O.T.").
