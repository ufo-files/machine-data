Description: During a gathering of friends in Bourges (18), at 23:47, several individuals observed the silent movement across the sky of a PAN composed of several "luminescent points." The shape initially appeared triangular, then circular, but no structure was visible. The object passed over a building and was then lost from view. Only one person from the group provided a statement. The witnesses very likely observed a passage of Thai lanterns:

- These are potentially isolated lights as no shape linking them was discerned.
- The lights are bright and appear consistent with Thai lanterns.
- The movement from east to west (as seen from northeast to north) aligns with the prevailing wind direction and was measured in Bourges at that time on the Météociel website.
- It was a weekend night during the "Printemps de Bourges," all conditions being favorable for festive events such as lantern releases.

The witnesses were surprised by the change in the lights' configuration, which they might have mistaken for a shape change if they imagined a form between the lights, while they did not distinguish any. Lanterns can fly in close formation and have non-rigidly identical trajectories due to slight size differences, wind trails, and small-scale wind variations (especially in urban areas at low altitudes of 100 to 300 meters for lantern flight). Thus, the formation viewed from the ground can evolve; in this case, it changed from something resembling a triangle to something resembling a circle. Consequently, GEIPAN classified the case as A: Thai lanterns.
