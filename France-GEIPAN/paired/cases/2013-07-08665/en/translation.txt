Summary: Observation of a stationary golden-white glowing phenomenon in the sky: Lack of information.
--- seg-ead7827f60f66074c33683df [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On July 17, 2013, around 11:50 PM, a witness observed from their home a stationary, bright white phenomenon. The observation lasted one minute before the phenomenon faded then relit for 30 seconds before disappearing permanently. No other testimonies were collected. The GEIPAN considered several hypotheses for this case:

- Astronomical hypothesis: A planet, temporarily hidden by clouds, could have given this impression, but the sky map reveals no bright star in the southeast.

- Hypothesis of a sun reflection on a satellite: The effect would have been similar, but shorter, and not fixed for one minute. A check with the Calsky site shows several satellites passing through the witness's field of view at the indicated time, as well as two Iridium satellite flashes at 11:45 PM and 11:54 PM, but in a direction opposite to that indicated by the witness.

- Aeronautical hypothesis: A plane seen face-on, with its landing light on, can certainly give the impression of a fixed point: it appears to disappear if it changes course, while the witness is no longer in the beam of the spotlight. This hypothesis could not be confirmed in time with air traffic.

This peculiar observation case, of average consistency due to the uniqueness of the testimony and the absence of a photo, is classified as "C" due to a lack of information overlaps.
