--- seg-f9691b790bb16b3243bab01f [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On January 26, 2015, at 6:55 PM, a witness observed in the sky an orange ball, much brighter than Venus, and twice its size. Intrigued, the witness attempted to take a photo 10 minutes later, but the phenomenon had disappeared. The witness, looking at the moon, observed a star that he mistook for Venus (see his very clear diagram on page 9 of his questionnaire), and in alignment, another large, much brighter, and red point, which seemed strange to him. The sky map shows that the star slightly below the moon is not Venus but Mars (with a magnitude of 1.36, slightly brighter than the stars of Ursa Major). Below, on the same axis, we find Venus, with a magnitude of 3.34 on that day, about 4 times brighter; as Venus is setting, it appears redder, like all stars in this position: 15 minutes later, setting Venus will have become invisible. The GEIPAN categorizes this well-documented case in category A: certain observation of the planet Venus at its setting.
