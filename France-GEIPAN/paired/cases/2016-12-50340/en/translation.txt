Description: On December 27, 2016, at midnight, the sole witness of this observation case is in his bedroom on the 4th floor of an apartment building in Fontaine (38). He observes, at a northeast elevation of approximately 75 degrees, a PAN of round shape "like a star," orange in color and with a brightness comparable to that of a star. This PAN moves silently at a moderate speed along a trajectory generally oriented towards east/north, before disappearing from view to the northeast at an elevation of around 60 degrees after 1 minute and 30 seconds of observation. The witness emphasizes the absence of noise, the orange color of the light source, and a trajectory that appears "practically at a right angle," these elements constituting the main perceived oddity. The case consistency remains average due to the uniqueness of the witness and the absence of photos or videos of the phenomenon. The oddity of this case, for the witness, essentially lies in the orange color of the PAN—as he initially thinks of a gendarmerie or civil security helicopter, but notices the absence of red and green flashing diodes—and in his perception of a trajectory "practically at a right angle."

The explored hypothesis, the observation of a sky lantern, is corroborated by the following elements:

- Spherical shape and orange color, typical of a lantern;
- Silent and moderate speed movement, compatible with the drift of a lantern carried by local weak aerological currents;
- Short observation duration, consistent with the usual visibility duration of this type of object.

The wind analysis does not contradict this hypothesis. Indeed, the available meteorological data suggest not a strong and homogeneous wind, but rather a situation of weak and locally variable winds at the scale of the Grenoble agglomeration. In an environment of basin and relief like that of Fontaine (38), such winds can translate into weak local aerological currents, sometimes different between ground level, the vicinity of buildings, and slightly higher layers at altitude.

Therefore, the absence of wind sensation by the witness at his observation point does not rule out the presence of an air current sufficient to ensure the slow drift of a passive object carried by the wind. Furthermore, the description of a movement "from right to left" or a "turn" does not, in itself, allow for the certain determination of an absolute direction of the phenomenon in a night environment without structured references. Thus, the impression of a "practically at a right angle" trajectory can be explained either by a slight variation in the local aerological current encountered by the lantern during its ascent, or by a simple perspective effect affecting the perception of its movement.

The whole remains compatible with the expected behavior of a sky lantern observed at night. The sky lantern hypothesis (shape, color, trajectory, silence) reduces the perceived oddity by the witness, even though we have found no trace of a release during the observation. In this case, the GEIPAN classification is "B": probable observation of a sky lantern.
