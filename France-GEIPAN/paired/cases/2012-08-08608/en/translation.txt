Description: On August 17, 2012, at 11:35 PM, an individual, while observing the sky with binoculars, saw a bright point of light moving erratically in a zigzag pattern and flickering irregularly. The testimony was collected in November 2013. An astronautical verification indicates two satellites that followed a trajectory close to the observed path: Cosmos 2322 and 2360; however, they are of low luminosity. The flickering does indeed evoke an airplane, but the zigzag trajectory seems inconsistent with this hypothesis. This area is frequently overflown by commercial airplanes on a north-south axis: the likelihood of an airplane on this trajectory is very high. The zigzag movement is certainly surprising, but this illusion is not uncommon: for airplanes, if collision lights are synchronized in opposition, the witness interprets the alternative lighting of two distant lights as a zigzag. Furthermore, the auto-kinetic illusion (see: Perception of the movement of a bright point in the sky) could contribute to this effect. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of an airplane with a perceptual illusion (flickering lights and auto-kinetic illusion).
