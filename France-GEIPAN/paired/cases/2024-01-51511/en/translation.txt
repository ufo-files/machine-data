Description: On January 27, 2024, between 11:25 PM and 11:30 PM, while at his home in Gisors (27), the witness is drawn to an intense, intermittent red light coming from the outside. As he walks towards his window, he observes a shape moving at an extremely high speed, without any perceptible noise, even though the window is open. Continuing his observation, he notices several (Unidentified Aerial Phenomena - UAP) of similar appearance, seemingly drawing a geometric configuration, all moving in the same direction before disappearing. A second series of identical UAPs then appears, and the witness decides to record three videos. These follow the same trajectory as the first ones and disappear at the same location. All UAPs move from southeast to northeast, following a curved trajectory, and are observed at an elevation of approximately 45°. The witness describes them as red in color, with very intense brightness, and having shapes resembling fractured lines. He also notes that the videos do not accurately capture the brightness or color of the UAPs, which appear pale yellow in the recordings. The observation lasted about 5 minutes. Only one testimony was collected. The consistency* of this case is good, with the use of three video documents allowing for a thorough analysis, even though only one testimony was collected.

*According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The explored hypothesis, that of observing sky lanterns, is supported by the following elements:

- The circular shape of the objects is clearly identifiable on the video recordings. A shape illusion can be assumed: the apparent uniformity of the UAPs and their synchronized movement in the same direction may have led the witness to perceive a single object incorrectly.

- The red color is compatible with that of some sky lanterns, although the orange shade is more commonly observed. It is worth noting that color perception can vary among observers, and as the witness indicates, smartphone-recorded videos do not reliably reproduce the actual shade of the observed phenomenon.

- The slow and simultaneous movement of all UAPs in the same direction, clearly observable on the video recordings, corresponds to the typical behavior of wind-driven sky lanterns. The available meteorological data (see Annexes 1 and 2) indicate a weak surface wind blowing from southeast (azimuth between 120° and 135°) with a speed estimated between 10 and 27 km/h. These conditions are consistent with the trajectory described by the witness, whose observation spans from an initial southeast azimuth to a final northeast-northeast azimuth. A light wind is also a favorable configuration for releasing lanterns, minimizing the risks associated with uncontrolled drift or fire outbreaks.

- The observation lasted 5 minutes, which aligns with the lifespan of lanterns, which does not exceed 20 minutes.

- The high brightness is possible, by contrast against a black sky, and indicates a certain proximity of the lanterns.

- The observation day, a Saturday, is typical for observing such lanterns, which are released in groups (explaining the "waves" observed by the witness) on special occasions (weddings, anniversaries, etc.). However, we were unable to identify the event behind the release.

- The distance and subsequent disappearance of the first UAP at "very high speed" can be explained by the extinction of the first observed lantern, reaching the end of its fuel, giving the false impression of this distance, followed by its very rapid disappearance. Indeed, the witness's initial mention of "intermittent" light clearly indicates that this first lantern is nearing the end of its life, the flame flickering on its support due to lack of fuel.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": Observation of sky lanterns.
