Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type 'C' cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as 'C' in 1991 by the SEPRA and previously named JEGUN (32) after the gendarmerie brigade. On August 6, 1991 at 12:15 AM, the JEGUN (32) gendarmerie was notified by a couple of witnesses living in Saint-Lary (32) of a peculiar light in the sky. The permanent staff also spotted the phenomenon from 12:35 AM and observed it carefully, using binoculars, until 1:29 AM. This case is consistent (three witnesses, including a gendarme), and the description of the PAN observed is fairly precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The phenomenon described shares many common characteristics (observation duration, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: Capella. Furthermore, this star has a perfectly consistent color with that of the PAN. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (surprise, fatigue, night observation). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this weak strangeness case is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception with the star Capella.
