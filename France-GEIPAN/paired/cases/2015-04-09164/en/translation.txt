Summary: Observation of the silent movement of two colored light phenomena in the night sky: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-092de98584db9ab44892e40b [needs-review] ---
Description: On Saturday, April 18, 2015, at 11:45 PM, a witness observed during two minutes two luminous objects, white and red, moving across the night sky from their property. The two objects appeared to be flying one behind the other. No unusual noises were heard during the observation, which ended at 11:53 PM. The witness reported the observation to the gendarmerie by letter. The investigation revealed no significant meteorological phenomena, no fireworks displays by local municipalities, or military training flights that evening. Local inquiry failed to gather other witness accounts. The observation of white and red lights crossing the sky in a few minutes, on a weekend night, strongly suggests Thai lanterns, likely released nearby after a private party. The movement was observed from Northwest (Bailleval) to West (see map), which is consistent with a North-East wind of 20-30 km/h recorded in Creil (see the day's Meteo France report). However, some elements of the observation report do not align well with the hypothesis of objects carried by the North-East wind:

- The objects stop above the marsh: This fact implies that the wind weakens or changes direction at that point, which is possible given the unique topography of this particularly humid valley.

- One of the objects ascends towards Ars, full West: This description could correspond to the gradual dimming of the lantern; witnesses often interpret this decrease in brightness as distance.

- The second object exits the witness's visual field at very high speed towards Bailleval (i.e., North, against the wind): This point is absolutely contradictory to an object carried by the wind, unless it is also a perception illusion during the lantern's extinction.

- The return of the second object would then be a third lantern launched from the same location, towards Bailleval, shortly after the first two.

In conclusion, after considering the possibility of perception illusions during the lights' extinction, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of Thai lanterns.
