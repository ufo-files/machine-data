Description: On February 11, 2016 at 21:06, a witness observed through the window of their living room a very sparkling orange light. He takes a video in which the witness later observes the presence of a small white object spinning rapidly around the ball. His wife, present, also observes the PAN in the sky. Only one witness statement is collected on this phenomenon. The consistency of the observation is good: two witnesses (even though only one testified) and a video. The video, its landscape elements, its dating, the viewing of PAN and the presence of clouds are consistent with the testimony (see the investigation report). A release of a Thai lantern is often, more frequently observed during a weekend. However, this observation made on a Thursday, given the witness's statements, corroborates the hypothesis of a Thai lantern. The wind speed: 6 km/h is compatible with a lantern release. The disappearance of the PAN, as mentioned by the witness, is consistent with the PAN moving away due to wind. The bright appearance, the orange hue, the fact that the PAN is below the clouds (without rain), the variations in brightness, contribute to reinforcing the hypothesis of a lantern with the observation of a flickering candle flame. Finally, there is a suitable field for a release at 300 m in the line of sight of the witnesses. The strangeness of the phenomenon is essentially due to the a posteriori observation, through a zoom on the video, "of a small white object spinning around the ball at high speed." This phenomenon is explained by the coupling between the flickering flame, the possible focus variation, and the movement (iPhone held by hand) of the witness filming. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of a Thai lantern.
