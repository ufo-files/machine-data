Description: On the evening of December 12, 2024, a resident of Entremont-le-Vieux (73) observed, for the first time, a bright white line about 1.5 cm across her screen or in her field of vision (to be specified). Since that date, each clear evening, she has regularly observed the re-appearance of this phenomenon. Gradually, she identifies the appearance of a second and then a third bright line, seemingly aligned and regularly spaced. On December 31, 2024 at 7 a.m., from another point in her home, she observed a "bright line" at an approximate elevation of 30°. To obtain a more precise image, she used the zoom on her phone to take a photograph. With the naked eye, only one bright line is visible, but upon enlarging the image, she distinguishes a shape resembling a "craft." The investigation reveals that the photo was taken on December 31 at 7:22 a.m., not on December 30 as initially indicated in the Technical Questionnaire (QT) provided by the witness. Additionally, the witness submits to GEIPAN a new photograph of an unidentified aerial phenomenon (PAN), taken on January 13, 2025 at 5:45 p.m. This case, of low strangeness and good consistency (a unique testimony but multiple witnesses, photos of PAN), turns out to be an astronomical observation, specifically of the planets Mars and Jupiter (as detailed in the investigation report). The witness's description of the unidentified aerial phenomenon (PAN) closely matches an astronomical observation: recurring at the same times over several hours, only visible on clear days, very slowly moving from east to west, and disappearing at sunrise. The perceived strangeness lies in characterizing the PAN as "bright lines." Analysis of the submitted photographs reveals a motion blur at the time of capture, preventing a precise evaluation of the extent of the line as perceived. However, the information provided by the witness, along with data from the Stellarium software, reveals that the PAN photographed on December 31, 2024 and January 13, 2025, correspond respectively to the positions of the planets Mars and Jupiter, whose negative magnitudes make them particularly visible celestial landmarks. The case is classified as "A," astronomical observations of the planets Mars and Jupiter.
