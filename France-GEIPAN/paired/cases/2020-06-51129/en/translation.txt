--- seg-a246b7f4e24dc597f411906f [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 03, 2020, the GEIPAN received a questionnaire and a photographic file of a PAN observed by the witness on June 21, 2020 at 00:02 in the Bordeaux sky. Only one testimony will be collected on this phenomenon. The witness, while waiting for his friend in a car (passenger side, vehicle stopped), is interrupted by a movement in his direct angle. He observes a first movement of a PAN over a short segment (approximately 5 cm), the PAN quickly becomes stationary and disappears. He takes a photo through the windshield (see witness photo). By observing the photo, four months later, he performs analyses by zooming in, then an image processing with color inversion and notices a shape that intrigues him. That is when he testifies to the GEIPAN. Regarding dating, the witness states that he relied on the information associated with the photo indicating the date of June 21, 2020 at 00:02. Or the sky is still clear in the photo sent, which does not seem compatible with the schedule indicated by the witness. An IPACO analysis of the photo (see the attached IPACO document) allows for its dating to June 20, 2020, at 10:02 PM, which, in this case, seems more compatible with the sky appearance in the photo, given that the sun was at 16°35 (see the Stellarium document). In the witness's description, it is difficult to distinguish between what he observed with the naked eye and what he later noticed in the photo, particularly through analyses conducted using software on his phone. In his free testimony, the witness mentions a point, however in the questionnaire description, he refers to an object in the shape of a goblet. The witness's description, along with the date (summer Saturday evening), suggests an object carried by the wind, similar to a Thai lantern, a festive object. The appearance on the photo is uncommon for a lantern; the brightness suggests a reflecting object rather than one that is inherently luminous. The witness mentions (4 months later) an initial movement from left to right; the observation was made in a northern direction, which is equivalent to a west-east movement. The Canejean weather station, located 5km away, reports a wind at 332 degrees, that is, N/SE. Nearby stations; Canejean, Villenave d’Ornon, and Cadaujac report wind speeds between 2 and 7 km/h. The direction, sense, and value of the wind are compatible with the observation of an object carried by the wind, in the mentioned direction of movement. The fact that the wind is light can also explain the impression of a halt in the movement (see weather document). The witness also mentions that once stationary, the PAN exhibited a NE/SO imbalance movement, which can again be explained by the wind, or in the case of a lantern, by the flame's movement. The photo shows an object that appears to be doubling; as the witness mentioned observing a point, it is very likely that the image of PAN found in the photo is a doubling effect due to the windshield. The GEIPAN classifies this observation as B: probable misidentification with a wind-carried object (party balloon, lantern).
