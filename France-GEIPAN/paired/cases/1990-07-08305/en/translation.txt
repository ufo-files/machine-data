Description: A witness reports in 2012 an observation made between July and August 1990 (the date of July 1st, 1990 is arbitrarily assigned, as the witness cannot recall the exact date). This person also links this first observation to a particular event that occurred in 2005. As a teenager, the witness observed the stars with his brother from the family home's bedroom around midnight and a half. Both noticed a bright point approaching and a gigantic, black, and silent craft stabilizing in front of the house. The children heard voices and, frightened, hid under a desk until the craft departed. It was 2:30 when they emerged from their hiding place, surprised to have stayed hidden for so long. During a night in 2005, the main witness, waking up very agitated, remembered that during the very long time her brother and she were hidden, she had boarded the craft. She found herself surrounded by six beings talking among themselves about her and was completely terrified. The witness suddenly found herself under the desk in the bedroom of the house. The witness reported an observation of PAN in 1990, followed by a strange encounter in 2005. The account of both cases was provided for expert analysis to a researcher from the University of Toulouse, a member of a CNRS laboratory, who specializes in eyewitness testimony. The result of this analysis concludes that the beginning of the first observation was indeed experienced by the witness (the beginning of the testimony resembles a satellite passage); however, the antiquity of the observation and the lack of information (date, time) do not allow for a clear conclusion. The second experience is more recent, but the conclusions highlight strong indicators of non-lived accounts, known as "false memories," meaning memories adopted by the witness in good faith but resulting from an involuntary construction on their part. The appearance of false memories can occur in witnesses who have experienced post-traumatic stress (in this case, the witness retains a traumatic memory of the first experience with repercussions on their health (memory disorder)). This case is classified as "C": non-exploitable due to a lack of precise information.
