Description: On June 11, 2017, around 11:30 PM, a witness was intrigued by the presence in the sky of an object spinning on itself, then a red glow, and the sudden disappearance of PAN. Only one witness statement is collected regarding this phenomenon. The apparent immobility of the PAN, for several minutes a priori (see: "stationary object then turning on itself several times before departing"), can evoke an astronomical misperception, all the more so since the PAN's location in the sky corresponds to that of the planet Saturn (10° altitude and 144° to 23:30 azimuth). It should be noted, however, that clouds present on the horizon were to obscure Saturn (cf: the investigation report). The description of the PAN could also correspond to a skytracer, given that the observation took place on a Sunday evening, with the presence of clouds (which the witness refuted). The rotation of PAN on itself is, indeed, quite consistent with a skytracer. Of average strangeness and weak consistency (single witness, no photo of PAN, contradictions or inconsistencies in the testimony), this case proves to be non-identifiable due to the lack of reliable data. The description of the PAN appears to be influenced by the witness's strong expectations, and it is difficult to distinguish between what they actually saw and what they believed they saw. Additionally, the witness states that the sky was perfectly clear at the location where the PAN was located, while satellite images show the presence of clouds. Finally, the duration of the observation appears contradictory between the free narrative and the rest of the testimony. The long delay between the observation and the analysis does not allow for a deeper testimony. The case is classified C, unidentifiable due to lack of data.
