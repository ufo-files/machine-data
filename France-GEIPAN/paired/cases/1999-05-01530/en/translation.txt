Summary: Night sky observations of a bright mass followed by a triangular cloud: very probable observations of a M45 or M4 missile launch from the Gulf of Gascogne off the coast of Brittany.
--- seg-79b9a104cf7ca9575600be5d [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The observation case previously classified as D and named (DPT) HAUTES-PYRENEES (65) 05.05.1999 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. Its examination allowed for a connection with another observation case [AERO LOC] MORTIERS (17) 04.05.1999 (observation from an airplane) that was initially unusable (classified as C). On May 5, 1999, around midnight, nine witnesses located in different communes of the Hautes-Pyrénées (65) observed in the night sky a phenomenon consisting of a very bright light followed by a triangular or conical cloud. No particular noise was heard. During the observations, of varying duration, the PAN appears to rotate on itself. The light disappears and the cloud gradually fades away. This phenomenon is also reported by a pilot (whose airplane was located south of Cognac). The air traffic controller also noted that two other pilots reported seeing the same phenomenon. With very good consistency (three groups of independent witnesses in the Hautes-Pyrénées, fairly precise information), this case appears to be a very probable misperception with a missile test launch. Thanks to the internet, access to missile launch annals is easier 20 years after the launches than it was at the time (see the investigation report). The description of the PAN strongly resembles a missile launch, and several witnesses initially thought it was a rocket launch. If no rocket launch corresponds to the observation, it is worth noting that two French missile launches took place on May 4, 1999. The observation of the PAN, in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), corresponds well to the date of May 4, 1999. Many elements are perfectly consistent with the hypothesis of a misperception with a missile launch: the directions of observation indicated by the witnesses point towards the Gulf of Gascogne and off the coast of Brittany. Furthermore, the fact that the three geographically distant groups of witnesses saw the PAN under similar conditions shows that it was at a high altitude. Additionally, the crew of a commercial airplane (case [AERO LOC] MORTIERS (17) 04.05.1999 flying over the Charente) made the same type of observation at the same time. All of this establishes the hypothesis of a misperception with one of these two missiles, perfectly visible (with distinction of the body and the ejected gases) in the night sky of the witnesses because the missiles are still illuminated by the sun due to their high altitude. It is known that there was a launch that day, and it is indeed at this precise time that the following launches took place. Under these conditions, the fact that we do not have the confirmation of the launch time that day is not likely to harm this hypothesis. It is not reasonable to initiate a procedure (heavy in formal terms 20 years later) to inquire at test centers for a precise hourly confirmation of the launch on May 4, 1999. Consequently, the GEIPAN combines the two observation cases into one case, classifying it as A: very probable observation of a M45 or M4 defense missile launch in the Gulf of Gascogne from off the coast of Brittany.
