Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. The observation case previously classified as D and named JAUSIERS (04) 1993 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed (see the investigation report). On June 29, 1993, around 9:00 a.m., two witnesses on a surveillance tour for the O.N.F. observed in the sky the silent movement of a dark, circular shape with "two tubes hanging underneath in the shape of an upside-down V." No smoke or trail was noticed, either with the naked eye or through binoculars. The PAN had a horizontal trajectory before suddenly changing direction and taking an upward trajectory. The PAN disappeared from view behind a cloud. The witnesses likely observed a solar balloon or a fantasy balloon. The weather conditions were ideal for using such an object (clear skies, little wind). The perception of an upside-down "V" under the PAN contributed to its strangeness and probably led the witnesses to reject their initial hypothesis of a weather balloon. This "V" can be explained by a single balloon with a complex shape (a fantasy object or animal) or by the assembly of three classic solar balloons (two of which were elongated). This balloon would have risen up the Parpaillon valley with the warm air currents coming from the bottom of the valley before ascending vertically on the strength of an upward current near the valley's relief. The balloon was observed at 9:00 a.m., but it could have been launched the previous day from the valley. With the evening coolness, it could have returned to the ground, and the morning warmth caused it to resume its course. The witnesses briefly considered a balloon and also a helicopter. This second hypothesis is ruled out since the valley is narrow, and the PAN was by definition close to the witnesses and thus identifiable through binoculars (used), if not by its noise. The case is classified as B: probable observation of a balloon.
