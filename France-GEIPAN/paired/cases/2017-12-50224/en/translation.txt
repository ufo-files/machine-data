Summary: Short, filmed observation of the passage of a white, luminous object in the night; double clicking sound heard: probable observation of an insect attracted into its path by the flash light.
--- seg-11dab9f1c245b7e227fe9b77 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 3, 2017 at 12:16 a.m., while taking a picture outdoors with a smartphone in camera mode, a witness suddenly saw a bright light appear to his right and heard a double clicking sound from his left. The whole event lasted two seconds. Upon returning home, the witness noticed that a PAN had been captured on film, and the sound was much clearer than he could have heard it. No other testimonies were collected. Through analysis (see the investigation report), we demonstrated that the PAN seen and filmed by the witness was merely a small object moving quickly very close to the lens, likely an insect. This conclusion is based on the following points:

- The rapid movement of the PAN, combined with the exposure time of each frame, results in an artificially elongated shape that is not representative of the true form of the PAN;

- At the same time, this movement is likely not parallel to the focal plane of the lens, which causes the apparent length of the PAN to decrease as it moves;

- The automatic flash in night conditions brightly illuminates the PAN, giving it its characteristic white color, and already suggests that it is not far from the lens, as the flash range is very limited;

- The identification of two blurs, combined in the longitudinal plane: an anisotropic blur of movement, horizontal, and an isotropic blur of focus, both vertical and horizontal. The presence of the latter blur confirms the impression from the previous point, that the PAN was very close to the lens, and therefore small in size. The proximity to the lens and thus the flash explains why, despite its small size, the object appeared as a bright light in the witness's eyes. The witness perceived a noise, the device recorded it, and reproduces it as a synchronized puff as the PAN passes, and therefore presumably created by it. The sound level is comparable to the witness's breathing and could correspond to the puff of an insect passing by, without necessarily being large or very close. The puff is harder to explain for an object other than an insect because the wind creating the movement would have been recorded.

Therefore, GEIPAN is classified as B: probable observation of an insect attracted to its path by the flash light.
