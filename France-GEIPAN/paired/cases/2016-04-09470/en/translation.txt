Summary: Observations at various times during the same night of the movements of a luminous phenomenon: very likely misidentification with a satellite and the planet Jupiter.
--- seg-c67b300da75f313acfe34f4b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On April 28, 2016, between 21:06 and 21:30, and then around midnight, two people were camping at the top of Puy-de-Tourettes. The witnesses, nestled in their sleeping bags in the bottom of a doline, observed a bright light source the size of a large star. They are intrigued by the movements of this point. The witness provided two photographs, unfortunately very little exploitable as they have a strong shake (photos taken with raised hands). Of strong strangeness and good consistency (two witnesses, photos, but lack of precision in the testimony), this case turns out to be a very probable misperception with the planet Jupiter, associated with a prior observation of a satellite in twilight skies, the witnesses' confusion having blended the two observations into that of a single object. (see the investigation report). Many details allow for a misidentification with Jupiter: similar description, shift in the sky during the night, apparent immobility during observation for nearly 30 minutes, and most importantly, the positioning of the PAN similar to that of Jupiter. The witness indeed indicated that the disappearance of the PAN may have revealed the position of the planet Jupiter, previously obscured by the PAN. However, it should be noted that the witness makes an interpretive error in his observation, as he states that Jupiter was not visible to the naked eye before 9:30 PM, while the visibility conditions were perfect: entirely clear sky, and a more transparent atmosphere since the observation location is in the mountains. In any case, Jupiter was therefore perfectly visible at the beginning of the observation phase. This misinterpretation is primarily due to the witness using the appearance of Jupiter in the sky the following day, in a flat sky with weather conditions worse than the previous day, as a reference point. The first phase of the observation strongly suggests the passage of a bright satellite, which is very difficult to identify due to the lack of precision in the testimony, but there have been several satellite passes at this time and in this region of the sky near Jupiter that could have caused such a misunderstanding. The various jerky movements observed of the PAN are quite typical of an optical illusion caused by uncontrolled eye movements (nystagmus). The case is classified B, very probable misidentification with a satellite and the planet Jupiter.
