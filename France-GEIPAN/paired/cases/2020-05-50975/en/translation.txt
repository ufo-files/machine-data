Description: Following a day during which his family heard gunshots in their residential area, the witness around 9 PM decides to go into fields with binoculars to observe the surroundings. The witness suddenly sees "a cloud" explode in the sky "literally." It resembled an atomic mushroom, like Hiroshima but smaller, and translucent and visible due to the cloud it hit. He runs to get his brother, and together they hear regular gunshots again and note that "the clouds were writhing, and splitting into three horizontal layers quite quickly, but especially, very geometrically. A bit like lenticular clouds, but perfectly geometric." His brother sees a projectile rising into the sky. The witness then describes the clouds with peculiar shapes and colors, as well as the formation of a storm. The witness and his brother state they then saw an unknown craft hover and move among the clouds. The witness estimates the observation duration at 30 minutes. The available elements leave little doubt about our explanatory hypothesis (see the investigation report). We have interrelated elements that prove perfectly explicable. The good availability of the witnesses, the information obtained during the investigation highlight:

- Gunshots were heard throughout the day on February 19, 2020, including the evening of the observation between 9 PM and 10 PM.
- Gunshots were scheduled for May 19 as part of a night military exercise.
- The witnesses state the detonations were different from usual shots. However, the perception of sounds in the evening or night differs from daytime perception, as ambient noise varies.
- Crop protection shots, particularly against pigeons, were also conducted that evening.
- The clouds resulted from favorable conditions for the creation of lenticular clouds, so-called layered clouds that dissipate as night falls. The sunset enhancing the contrast effects described by both witnesses.
- Through the binoculars (which were not perfectly adjusted), a black mass with various jerks, resembling a large fan, was observed, which could correspond to the description of a paramotor ULM flying in the evening.
- The observation of the black mass described as a large fan strongly resembles a paramotor, parachutes being one of the elements of these craft that enable aerial acrobatics.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as B: primary misidentification with an aircraft, but also multiple misidentifications with:

- Day and night shooting exercises,
- Shots to scare away undesirable cultural animals (pigeons and wild boars, etc.),
- Lenticular cloud formations,
- Paramotor ULM. (Highly probable hypothesis).
