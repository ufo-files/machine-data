Description: On August 12, 2016, around 10:30 PM, a witness was stargazing and became intrigued by the passage of a bright white light of intense intensity that seemed to zigzag slightly. The witness called their spouse, who made the same observation. The phenomenon crossed the sky from West to East and then disappeared. The witness saw the International Space Station (ISS). Their indications in the sky closely matched the ISS's trajectory, except for the disappearance, which was at a lower elevation than indicated, unless it was a cloud that ended the observation. The end of the ISS's visibility (the end of its sunlight illumination) might seem strange, due to its speed, or conversely, here, due to its slowness ("a candle flame dying in 2 or 3 seconds"). The impressions of oscillations around the trajectory, which also confer strangeness as the trajectory of a satellite is regular, are well-known phenomena of autocinematic perception. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: observation of the ISS's passage.
