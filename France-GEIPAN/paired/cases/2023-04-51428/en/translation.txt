Summary: Multiple light phenomenon observations by a pilot during a flight from Abidjan to Paris: observation of Starlink satellite flashes in operational orbit.
--- seg-9b2c9ae244bc6353aa032091 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the night of April 7 to 8, 2023, one of the pilots of flight AFR ABIDJAN-PARIS, accompanied by a portion of the crew, observed from 00:40 and for approximately one hour, four white glowing phenomena whose intensity varied. These objects display rapid trajectories with apparent direction changes, located at a considerable distance from the witness. Their angular position relative to the aircraft axis (AFR ABIDJAN-CDG) varies between 20 and 30 degrees, while their angle relative to the aircraft pitch oscillates between +10 and +20 degrees. Only the flight commander films and testifies. The sky is perfectly clear, with low lighting on the ground, until approaching Algiers, where the brightness of the moving points gradually diminishes, until disappearing. On June 27, 2023, the witness provides additional information about the observation to better describe the movements of the four PAN observed. The astronomical hypothesis is examined and validated for one of the four PAN (PAN 2), which remains stationary and whose position (azimuth and elevation) corresponds to that of the star Vega (see the investigation report). The satellite hypothesis is then considered due to the rapid luminosity variations of the PAN over relatively short periods (around 20 seconds), suggesting solar flashes reflecting off the solar panels or metallic surfaces of satellites at a specific angle. Online research reveals correlations with numerous observations associated with low Earth orbit Starlink satellites in operation. These observations are made under specific conditions: at approximately 45° vertically from the Sun when it is between 30° and 45° below the horizon. This context generates the appearance of a clustered flash phenomenon, named "Starlink cluster flares," observed as Starlink satellites, evolving on different orbital planes, pass through the affected area. This may give the impression of various trajectories as illustrated in the Web article starlink-satellites-flaring-in-cassiopeia. The observation for PAN 1, 3, and 4 (Sun position below the horizon, observation area, description of PAN) corresponds in every way to Starlink satellite flashes in operational orbit. Of low strangeness and good consistency (several witnesses though one unique but quite complete testimony, with an associated video), this case turns out to be an observation of Starlink satellite flashes. On November 29, 2023, the witness reported a similar observation to that of the PAN on April 5, 2023, during the AFR flight from Cayenne to Paris, overflying the Gulf of Gascogne. Solar conditions were also favorable for observing "Starlink cluster flares." The case is classified A, observation of Starlink satellite flashes in operational orbit. Furthermore, this observation presents very great similarities with other cases investigated by the GEIPAN and published on the GEIPAN website. [AERO AFR] from KINSHASA (CD.KN) to PARIS (75) on 10.04.2023 [AERO TVF] from AMMAN (JO.AM) to ORLY (94) on 31.01.2023 [AERO TVF] from EREVAN (AM.ER) to ORLY (94) on 13.12.2023 These four cases support a technical investigation note and the publication of an Update.
