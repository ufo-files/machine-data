Summary: Long observation by several witnesses, at two different locations, of lights with twinkling and slow movement in the sky: misidentification with very bright stars (Jupiter, Sirius, Vega, Rigel).
--- seg-28ee866e1939145b01f99261 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: During the night of November 6 to 7, 2013, a resident (T1) of Tallard (05) observed and filmed several lights in the sky starting at 11:55 PM. He contacts the gendarmerie and civil aviation services by phone to report the incident. A patrol is sent to the witness and all make new observations between 0:30 and 0:54 from Tallard (05). At 1 a.m., a gendarme in Gap (T2) spots a glowing, shimmering ball heading towards the SOUTHEAST. At 01:40, T1 and the gendarmes' patrol stop their observation. Around 2:00 AM, T2 also stops observing. The video recording made by T1 is of very poor quality: the images are blurry and zoomed in. The observation took place from two different observation points (see investigation report) in a relatively clear sky. The observations of the various PAN all present the characteristics of astronomical observations, given the description of the PAN (flashing lights) and the length of the observation times (over two hours from Tallard (05), approximately one hour from Gap (05)). The twinkling of the PAN is perfectly consistent with the twinkling of stars, a natural phenomenon caused by wind at high altitudes. The twinkling of stars is all the more intense as the stars are low on the horizon, which is indeed the case with the various PAN observed that night. In addition, the presence of a very important relief (Alps) in the immediate vicinity of the observation locations only reinforces the altitude winds responsible for this twinkling. The very great consistency between the true positions of the brightest stars then present in the sky and those of the PAN observed leaves no doubt about the misperception. Of low strangeness and high consistency (multiple witnesses, video recording), this case appears to be a very probable misidentification with stars: Rigel, Jupiter, Vega, Sirius. Furthermore, these various stars are visible directly in the directions indicated by the witnesses, and the sky was clear. The twinkling of the PAN and the movement of PAN 4 are, in fact, perfectly consistent with stars. This case is classified A, mistaking it for very bright stars (Jupiter, Sirius, Vega, Rigel).
