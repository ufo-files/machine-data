Summary: Multiple observations across southern France of the rapid passage of an elongated glowing phenomenon: likely observations of a meteoroid reentry.
--- seg-d7f27a13b0d2c68dc08740f6 [needs-review] ---
Description: On June 6, 1983, around 11 PM, numerous witnesses across southern France, from the Alps to the Eastern Pyrenees, observed a rapidly moving light in the sky at the same time (11 PM) and for a brief duration (usually less than 20 seconds, and at most 1 or 2 minutes). Twenty-nine testimonies have been reported. The shape is often described as elongated. The summary table (included in the investigation materials) of observation locations and times is striking for its geographical scope and temporal correspondence. This suggests a single phenomenon at high altitude (>50 km) with visibility over a wide area, a key characteristic of the well-known atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid. This is further supported by another key characteristic: the elongated light and its short duration. A longer duration could have led to considering the nearby hypothesis of atmospheric reentry of a spacecraft. Today, many sites record data collected on the ground (most often by automatic cameras) during these reentries, so testimonies regarding GEIPAN are often resolved without specific investigation. The 2016 example, with over 20 recordings (included in the IMO supplementary analysis), illustrates the analogy with this old case from June 6, 1983, at 11 PM. The supplementary analysis includes a collection of witness descriptions that clearly convey the impression of rapid movement with an elongated shape. Therefore, GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observations of atmospheric reentry of a meteoroid. A new testimony has been added today: it's an observation made in Gémenos (13) around 11 PM of the rapid passage and disappearance of a yellow-light, elongated, low-altitude phenomenon. No noise was heard by the witness. Two press articles are attached to the completed questionnaire by the witness's spouse.
