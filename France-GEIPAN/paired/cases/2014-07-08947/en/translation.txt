Summary: Observation of the movement of white lights in the sky: satellite observation.
--- seg-ced96fdd3c31466fb4e39be6 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The witness, sitting in their garden around 11:15 PM, observed a very bright white light, oval in shape, surrounded by a blue halo, moving without flickering at the speed of an airplane. The object gained altitude and then disappeared. Other bright points were seen. The observation for a few seconds of a very bright white light moving at the angular speed of an airplane suggests the observation of a satellite during a portion of its trajectory. The Calsky log (see attached) indicates at that time multiple satellites flying overhead, more or less visible (the "magnitude" indicator being smaller as brightness increases), specifically at 11:16 PM mentions a relatively rare phenomenon for a given location, a "Iridium flare": a temporary reflection of the sun on a highly reflective part of the satellite. It is likely this phenomenon was what the witnesses observed (the questionnaire leaves ambiguity regarding the phenomenon's position in the sky). As this sun reflection gradually diminishes as the satellite progresses in its orbit and changes its attitude relative to the sun, observers often interpret this decrease in brightness as a receding object, which was the case here. The second part of the observation could correspond to the observation of the two NOSS 3 satellites (4A and 4C) flying in formation, with a magnitude of around 4, meaning they are barely visible to the naked eye. Their apparent trajectory from the ground is a great circle. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of satellites.
