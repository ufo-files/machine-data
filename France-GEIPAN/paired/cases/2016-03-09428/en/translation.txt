Summary: Observation of a white glowing point in the sky with color flashes in the night sky: observation of Sirius at low altitude.
--- seg-f7172a529c8d7746aadedf88 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On March 5, 2016, around 11 PM, a witness is intrigued by a stationary bright point in the sky. He observes the phenomenon through a telescope and distinguishes blue, purple, and white beams. His wife also thinks she sees a sparkling star or planet. The witness reports the point at an azimuth of 236° (indirectly measured on Geoportail) and an elevation below 15°. Finally, the witness's wife considers she saw a star, but with a purple color. However, between 11 PM and 11:30 PM, Sirius moves from an azimuth of 215° (SSO) at an elevation of 15° to an azimuth of 225° (SO) at 13° elevation (see attached sky map with Stellarium). Therefore, the PAN can only be Sirius; otherwise, the witness would have also reported Sirius (or a very bright star in a clear sky, especially when viewed through a telescope!). The main witness saw Sirius and was misled, which is classic, by Sirius' changing lights and colors at low elevation, and this could be even more so due to observing through glass. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Observation of Sirius.
