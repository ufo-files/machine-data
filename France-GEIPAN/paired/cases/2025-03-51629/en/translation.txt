Description: While at her home, located in the commune of Saint-Pryvé-Saint-Mesmin (Loiret, 45), the witness observes, during the night of March 8 to 9, 2025, shortly after midnight, in a northern direction, a group of ten to twelve luminous PANs (Unidentified Flying Objects) of white color. These objects move silently along a straight and regular trajectory oriented from east to west. The observation lasted approximately fifteen minutes during which the witness took two pictures of the phenomenon. The consistency* of this case is good, despite a single witness, with the availability of two photographs utilized in the investigation. *According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of information considered reliable and objective, gathered for a testimony. The hypothesis explored is that of the observation of several luminous balloons (see the investigation report with the photographic analysis). It was concluded through the investigation for the following reasons:

- The observation took place during the night from Saturday to Sunday, a time period highly suitable for the use of such balloons in a festive context.
- The white color is frequently observed for this type of balloon.
- The movement of the PANs was found to be with the wind.
- The trajectory, straight and regular, is entirely consistent with that of balloons carried by the wind. The witness, questioned by the investigator regarding the size of the PANs, indicated that they were all the same size, contrary to what appears in the photographs. This observation confirms that this distortion results from an artifact related to the movement of objects during the exposure time, which also explains the variation by a factor of two between the two images. The analysis of the photographs indicates that, if the PANs are indeed wind-carried objects, their distance from the witness is estimated between 400 and 500 meters. Their size is estimated to be approximately 1 meter to 1.40 meters in diameter if they are spherical, or in height if they have a vertically oval shape. Considering a possible margin of error mainly related to local wind that can be lower than that recorded in meteorological stations, these results are compatible with balloons, rather large in size. The investigations conducted show that such balloons exist, with dimensions that can reach up to 90 cm in diameter. Light and inflated with helium, they can be easily released or lost. We finally attempted without success to determine if a local festive event could have used such balloons. Using it in a festive setting by an individual remains entirely possible. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of luminous balloons.
