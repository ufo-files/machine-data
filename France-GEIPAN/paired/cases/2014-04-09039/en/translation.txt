Summary: Observations of the silent movement of a dozen yellow-orange light points in the night sky: probable observation of the passage of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-59912071b45809fe188d0504 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On April 28, 2014, at 10 PM, a couple in a car was intrigued by a dozen glowing points in the sky. Yellow-orange orbs moved silently towards the south. The observation lasted 3 minutes before the orbs disappeared in the distance. Several photographs were taken with a mobile phone. Two testimonies about this phenomenon were collected. The gendarmerie investigation did not reveal any unusual events or other witnesses in the area that night. The observation of orange orbs crossing the sky in a few minutes strongly resembles Thai lanterns, probably released nearby after a private party. The movement was observed from North to South, then South-West, which is consistent with the North-North-East wind reported in Bourg-les-Valence. The Infoclimat record in Bourg-les-Valence indicates a light wind from NNE (3 to 5 km/h). The steep relief of the Crussol Castle caused wind disturbances at low altitude, which could explain the change in direction. The witness took several photos: they turned out to be out of focus (focus blur, very common in night photos under these conditions) or blurry. However, they prove the reality of the phenomenon, and the color of the phenomenon is entirely consistent with that of Thai lanterns. The distance estimate made by the witness cannot be accurate since he had never seen this type of object before. Due to the fact that Thai lanterns are inherently dim (large candle flame), they are always perceived as farther away and therefore larger and faster than they actually are. See the explanation of estimating the size and distance of an unknown phenomenon. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B", very probable observation of Thai lanterns.
