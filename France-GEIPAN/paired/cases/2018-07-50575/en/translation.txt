Summary: Observation of 7 very close bright lights forming a line; elevation in the sky and disappearance: possible misperception with a flight in military aircraft configuration.
--- seg-c5c8c42d444cfdd87d540044 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the morning of July 26, 2018, around 4:00 AM, two people by Lake Annecy were intrigued by white lights above Mont Veyrier. Six or seven bright points lit up one after the other, forming a line. After a few seconds, the points ascended into the sky and disappeared. Only one testimony was collected regarding this phenomenon. All elements of the testimony point towards the hypothesis of confusion with a formation of aircraft (particularly their powerful landing lights, the only visible elements at a great distance) flying towards the witness for a few moments. It could possibly have been the French Patrol, in nighttime training in the area (as detailed in the investigation report). These details are as follows:

- Flight type: formation.
- Number: presence of at least 6 PAN. The French Patrol aircraft can number up to 8 or 9, but sometimes fly in smaller formations.
- Color: white light. The landing lights (or beacons) are located on the nose and undercarriage of the Alphajet, in use since 1981. They are white or white-yellow.
- Noise: absence. It is entirely possible that the aircraft were several dozen kilometers away, hence the lack of perceived noise is not surprising.
- Observation duration of approximately 10 seconds, instant appearance and disappearance. The aircraft changed their direction so that their landing lights were initially visible to the witness and maintained this trajectory for about 10 seconds. After this time, they changed direction again and disappeared, only being observable by their landing lights, which are only visible within a limited cone of view; they thus became invisible to the naked eye, as neither their structure nor other lights were visible due to the great distance separating them from the witness. However, it was not possible to formally demonstrate the presence of the French Patrol in the area at the time of observation. The GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": possible misidentification with a military aircraft formation.
