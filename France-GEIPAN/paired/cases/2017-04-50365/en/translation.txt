Description: On Wednesday, April 12, 2017 at 12:15 (TU), a pilot flying a Cessna saw a very bright PAN above the Etampes airport. Rapidly, the PAN crosses the witness's plane: the pilot describes a non-spherical bubble, the front slightly elongated, but with no visible propulsion means. His wife, as a passenger, also saw the PAN. The observation lasted between 5 and 15 seconds. The witness thinks among other things of a helicopter, then a drone. Only one testimony will be collected. The only hypothesis considered (see the investigation report) is the confusion with a drone, but it could not be evaluated precisely due to a lack of data, and the strangeness remains quite strong (0.6). The consistency is lower (0.5), with some data missing and/or unusable. Furthermore, a second witness did not complete the questionnaire despite the request of GEIPAN. In conclusion, the drone hypothesis was interesting, and the GEIPAN was unable to further study and retain it. Indeed, this hypothesis, although credible given the behavior of PAN (straight-line movement and an altitude technically possible to reach, though not regulated as it is well above the maximum allowed for drones at 150m), is more difficult to exploit and validate on the following points: - Radars do not allow for the detection of drones; - It is impossible to obtain from the witness more precise observation information about the PAN, as the latter is focused on providing its final reconstruction; - Inconsistency between the witness's testimony and the drone hypothesis formulated by the witness: the difference between his testimony (very bright ball) and what is known about the appearance of drones is striking. - If it is indeed a drone, it is in violation (potentially even endangering the safety of regular air users above the 150m limit) and under these conditions, it is difficult to hope to find its owner to gather their testimony; - No testimony from his wife, who was present. The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'C' due to a lack of reliable data.
