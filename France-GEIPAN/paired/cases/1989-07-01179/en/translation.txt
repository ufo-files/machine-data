Summary: Mountain observations of a sparkling object moving from a lake towards a ridge before disappearing into the sky: likely observations of a red Mylar fantasy balloon.
--- seg-69a215a19b567c24aabd208a [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named LE LAC D'OPALE (65) 1989 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined (see the investigation report). On July 18, 1989, around 4:00 p.m., two witnesses are at the top of a pass overlooking the Grand Lac d'Opale in the commune ofEstaing (65). They spot a PAN slowly moving from the middle of the lake towards one of its edges. The PAN continues its movement over the pebbles of the shore and a snowbank before ascending through an area of scree until it reaches the crest. The witnesses lose sight of it in the clear sky. Despite the distance, the witnesses describe an elongated object with a red shimmer and "white lights," which then became bright and shiny in the sun. No particular noise was heard during the long observation (approximately 25 minutes). Intrigued, the witnesses return to the site the next day but find no traces on the ground (especially on the snowbank). Only these two testimonies were recorded (gendarmerie protocol and questionnaire). The case is remarkable, both for the strong strangeness that emerges from the description of the phenomenon (appearance and behavior) and for its strong consistency, with two witnesses (especially T1) having described their observation in detail, as well as two subsequent investigations, one of which was conducted on-site by gendarmes. The strangeness is characterized by the PAN's features: elongated shape, relatively small, red-lit, and silently moving for 25 minutes at water level, over pebbles and snowbank, then in the air, in broad daylight in a rather desolate mountainous area. In addition, its movements in "jumps" over pebbles and the intermittent observation of two white lights described by the witnesses as "eyes" contribute to this. The few hypotheses put forward by the witnesses or gendarmes, after examination, have a reliability of zero to low in light of what characterizes this strangeness. Several other hypotheses were explored in this new investigation. The hypothesis of a backpack on a "super trailer" would be acceptable in many respects but is not, considering the disappearance of the PAN into the air. On the other hand, a thorough analysis allows us to retain the Mylar balloon as the most probable hypothesis at over 50%. The absence of contact with the witnesses (at the time) is regrettable, but the consistency of the testimony is sufficient in light of the hypothesis retained. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a red Mylar fantasy balloon.
