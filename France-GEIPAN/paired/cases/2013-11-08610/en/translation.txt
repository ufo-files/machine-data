Summary: Observation of a sparkling white light in the sky: likely observation of Venus.
--- seg-91fa4761f02b1d7ff658ccd7 [needs-review] ---
Description: A witness reports a long observation of a white light in a clear sky that seems to twinkle at times in a blue color. No noise is heard. No further testimony will be collected. This long and repeated observation of a very bright light point close to the southwest at the beginning of the evening has all the characteristics of an observation of the planet Venus, particularly visible at this time (Magnitude -4.6). The variability of the brightness comes from the variability of the atmosphere, probably due to the proximity of the ocean. The given observation directions correspond to the planet's position (see witness map and GEIPAN map) with some inconsistencies: the witness indicates an elevation of around 45° (while Venus was around 10°), and the witness observes a significant movement of the phenomenon in the sky. These inconsistencies are likely due to the lack of precise references during the observation and the multitude of observation locations. (The witness mentions the moon, absent at that time on November 22, but clarifies by separate message that it was Tuesday, November 19, that he saw the Moon). The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of the planet Venus.
