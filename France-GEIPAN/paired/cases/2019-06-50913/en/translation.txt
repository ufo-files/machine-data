Description: On June 30, 2019 at 12:05 AM, from his home in Angers, the witness observed the stars after opening the window, his attention was drawn to one of them which disappeared and reappeared on the side, in a random manner. The following day, the minor witness reported his observation to his parents. The testimony was brought to the attention of the GEIPAN on February 16, 2020. The witness mentioned that the observation was made in a southerly direction, that the Moon was visible in the east with a clear and unobstructed sky. Only one testimony was collected regarding this phenomenon. The witness mentions that the Moon was visible in the east. However, on that day, the Moon rose at 2:04 AM and was only 6.8% illuminated, making it impossible to see at the time of the observation. Furthermore, the witness states that the sky was clear and unobstructed, while meteorological sites reported an 8/8 octas overcast, indicating a completely overcast sky. Thus, the observation data is not compatible with the astronomical and meteorological reality of the place and time of the observation. It is therefore highly probable that the observation did not take place on June 30, 2019 at 12:05 AM. The witness's father was contacted on March 29, 2021, to verify with his son the accuracy of the date and time data. His response was: "In answer to your questions and having verified the dates of the video and its camera: the dates we have specified are correct. My son clearly remembers having seen the moon that night." In this case, if the time is not exact, we are certain of the month (June). The response does not allow us to conclude on the day and time of the observation. One thing is certain, this observation could not have taken place on June 30 at 12:05 AM. It may have taken place later that same day, or at the indicated time, but on a different date in June. It is therefore impossible to treat this observation in a relevant manner. The GEIPAN classifies this case as C due to a lack of exploitable information.
