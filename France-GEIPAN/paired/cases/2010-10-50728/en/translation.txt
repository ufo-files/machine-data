Description: Recent (March 17, 2019) testimony of an old observation: on October 7, 2010, at 10:45 PM, the witness, on a beach with a friend, suddenly sees a huge triangle delineated by white lights at each corner in the cloudy sky. The PAN appears on the horizon in the direction of the sea (West) and moves towards the witnesses, flying silently over them. Surprised, the witnesses do not have the reflex to take a picture or video; the observation lasts a short time and the PAN continues its route inland. The witness loses sight of it. Only one testimony is collected. Because of its strong strangeness and relatively weak consistency (two witnesses, but a unique and late testimony), this case lacks precise and strong data (see the investigation report). Note that the observation date, initially imprecise, was found with a high degree of confidence thanks to the elements provided by the witness. However, although the case is very strange, it is also noted that the late testimony makes any verification impossible, in particular regarding air traffic at Berck-sur-Mer (62) airport, as some observation elements suggest a misidentification with a tourist plane in landing phase. It is also impossible to verify to what extent the witness may have been influenced during his testimony, since during the very long period between the observation and the sending of the testimony, the witness often discussed the subject with the second witness and conducted extensive research on the internet, comparing his observation to famous PAN cases, such as Phoenix (Arizona) and Golfech (82). It is surprising that no other witness saw the PAN flying over a populated agglomeration, even though it was very bright and huge in angular size. In the GEIPAN methodology, strong strangeness requires high consistency to be able to make a classification. The GEIPAN classifies this case as C, unexploitable due to lack of reliable information.
