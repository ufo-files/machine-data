Summary: Sequential observations of distinct light phenomena: Venus and electric phenomena (EDF disturbance).

Description: On March 3rd and 7th, 1980, several individuals witnessed peculiar and distinct light phenomena. On March 3rd at 10:15 PM, a driver and her daughter were suddenly blinded by a red light emanating from a sphere moving along the horizon. This sphere, about the size of a soccer ball, moved silently from southeast to northwest. Its movement appeared to be in increments at high speed. This phenomenon was observed by others, including gendarmes, who at 10:25 PM saw a bright red point 5 degrees above the horizon and watched it slowly disappear to the northwest at 10:35 PM. The gendarmes returned to the site the next day and observed through binoculars a glowing orange object producing blue flashes. It disappeared completely at 10:45 PM on the horizon. Notably, at this time of year, Venus was particularly visible (magnitude -4.10) and very low on the horizon (-5 degrees from the horizon), disappearing completely around 10:38 PM. The speed and stepwise descent described by the witnesses is an impression given by the fact that the celestial body is very low and far on the horizon. The witnesses likely made an astronomical observation of Venus that evening.

On March 7th, 1980, around 10:10 PM, the same individuals and others witnessed another peculiar light phenomenon. Returning home and getting out of her car, a witness observed a glowing sphere composed of several colored circles. A flash of light struck the ground at the feet of her child. The sphere, estimated to be between 8 and 10 inches in diameter, moved from west to northwest before disappearing completely. No sound or electrical discharge was heard or felt; however, many people in their homes or in public lighting observed a drop in voltage on the EDF power line. Notably, one witness reported seeing an electric arc between an electrical line and the ground. These individuals likely witnessed an electrical phenomenon.
