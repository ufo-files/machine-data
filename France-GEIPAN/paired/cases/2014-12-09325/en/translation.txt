Summary: Observation of the passage of a dark triangular shape bordered by lights: probable observation of an aircraft preparing for landing.
--- seg-107923b2bd61eab5bce9509d [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 28, 2014, at 6:20 PM, a passenger in a car is intrigued by the presence of two fixed, parallel lights in the sky. As the lights fly over the car, the witness, through the panoramic roof, observes that it is a dark equilateral triangle, pointed south. The triangle is formed by a light at each corner, and a less bright one in the center. The trajectory is straight. The witness's description of two, then three low-altitude glowing points flying in a straight line towards the south, the mention of two flashing lights, point towards the hypothesis of a low-flying plane coming from the North (from Great Britain?), preparing to land at Nantes-Bouguenais from the south, into the wind (see the north wind report at Nantes Météociel). Of course, the fact that the witness describes a triangle rather than an airplane seems contradictory with this hypothesis; but the witness mainly sees the powerful landing lights, and, blinded,  ne distingue pas le fuselage (voir un exemple sur photo DSC_6916_avion.jpg). This is a common perception illusion, called a contour illusion or shape illusion, which gives the impression of a triangle encompassing the glowing points. Due to the significant delay after the observation, the GEIPAN was unable to access aeronautical information to determine which plane was seen. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of an airplane preparing for landing.
