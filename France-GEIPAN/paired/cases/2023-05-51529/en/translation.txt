Description: On May 31, 2023, around 11 PM, a witness and his spouse, located in Anor (59), observed an aerial phenomenon coming from the north, appearing as a "tube" or "parallelepiped" with several white lights. The witness counted 32 lights arranged in pairs, separated by dark spaces. Although he considered the possibility of a Starlink satellite train, recently launched by a Falcon 9 rocket from California, he expressed doubts due to the trajectory and altitude, which were deemed unusual for such an observation. Only one testimony was collected. Although the testimony is late (April 2024), the case's consistency is good, with the presence of a second witness (but who did not complete a QT form) and the existence of a blurry photo that still allows for the identification of the PAN. The perceived oddity by the witness mainly resides in the appearance of the observed phenomenon, which he interprets as a single, large object moving at an altitude significantly lower than expected for Starlink satellites. The hypothesis of the Starlink satellite train is the one we explored and validated, based on the following elements (see the investigation note):

- Elongated shape, identical to that of all satellites, aligned.
- White color, consistent with that of the satellites.
- Proven presence of a satellite train above the witnesses at the time of observation.
- Conforming movement, generally from north to south.
- Straight trajectory, consistent.

Several factors contributed to the witness not recognizing the Starlink satellite train:

- Expected trajectory towards the east, while the launch was on a polar orbit oriented at 70° with, subsequently, a curved trajectory over France directed towards south-southeast. The visual trajectory as reported by the witness has a certain angular deviation from the actual trajectory, likely due to difficulties in precisely orienting at night.
- Difficulties for the witness to accurately assess the altitude and actual size of the PAN.
- Shape illusion: the witness interprets the phenomenon as a single object rather than a succession of distinct entities. This type of illusion is typical during the observation of homogeneous objects following a converging trajectory.
- The final element to consider is the "close call" felt by the witness immediately after the passage of the unidentified aerial phenomenon (PAN) over his garden. This phenomenon can be attributed to a possible wind gust, which the witness instinctively associated with the PAN. A photograph was taken by the witness, showing the satellite train, albeit with some blur. Additionally, a sketch was made by the witness, illustratively representing the appearance of the Starlink satellite train as observed from Earth. Finally, note that the number of "lights" (32) mentioned by T1 for the PAN is very close to the number of satellites in this train, counted at 40 by an external observer. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Starlink satellite train.
