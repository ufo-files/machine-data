Summary: An automobilist observed on the Toulouse bypass a PAN that at first appears stationary but then quickly disappears into the sky: misidentification with an airplane taking off.
--- seg-8b75201976fb13693951c769 [needs-review] ---
Description: On February 21, 2016, around noon, a driver was traveling on the Toulouse bypass, a two-lane road that then becomes three lanes, with heavy traffic. He observes in the direction of Toulouse, just above the roofs of a large department store, a saucer-shaped object, appearing to be brown in color. He thinks it might be an advertising balloon, but he cannot make out any logos. The object, which appears to remain very still for a long time, eventually disappears at high speed. The phenomenon is observed starting at 11:55. Only one testimony is collected. The easterly wind conditions suggest an aircraft taking off from Blagnac on runway 014 (into the wind) and making a turn to the south. During the analysis over a comparable period, we note the trace of flight AF6135 (TLS-ORY) with identical wind conditions. The attached file named "trace AF6135 du 05.07.2017.pdf" shows the trace of this flight consistent with the aircraft's position at the 3 key moments of the witness's observation: A - stationary object, corresponding to the head-on view of the aircraft heading towards the witness. Under these conditions (altitude 1,400 m, distance 5,500 m), the aircraft appears at an elevation of 14°. B - An object crossing A64 (alt 1,500 m, dist 2,300 m) appears at 30° elevation. C - An object taking a heading to the northwest (alt 2,200 m, dist 8,000 m) shows a decreasing elevation of 15°. During the southerly turn, the aircraft, moving face to the witness, appears stationary in the sky, then regains apparent speed when it takes a trajectory perpendicular to the witness. This is confirmed by the dating of the positions. The time interval between positions A and B is less than a minute. The one between B and C is approximately one and a half minutes. The observation likely corresponds to an airplane, facing the witness, making a loop, under conditions comparable to the flight record of AF6135, creating the illusion of a stationary station, then quickly accelerating before disappearing as it moves away. The main strangeness for the witness is not to distinguish wings if airplane. The disk-shaped form can resemble a plane seen from the front, such as a Beluga, with the thin edges corresponding to the wings. Then from the side, the wings are not always visible, the observation having not been continuous, this may explain their lack of visibility. During broad daylight and amidst the flow of vehicles with other potential witnesses, he was the only one to emerge. This weakens the strangeness of his testimony. On the other hand, the consistency of the testimony is good, and the witness provided a photo of the surroundings (but not in real-time) and gave a statement at the Gendarmerie. The description of the phenomenon perfectly resembles the trajectory of an aircraft after takeoff from Blagnac towards the southeast (wind conditions), then making a loop towards the south to set its course north. Standard trajectory by easterly wind to avoid overflying the urban area. The GEIPAN classifies this phenomenon as B, likely identified as a misperception with an airplane taking off.
