Summary: Observation of the silent passage of an orange triangular object in the night sky: observation of a Thai lantern.
--- seg-54a5f56618a987c3e7b0964b [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On September 10, 2017, at 10:26 PM, a witness on their terrace spotted a low-altitude light moving in a western direction. Very quickly, the witness realized the light was heading towards them and observed a triangular shape. They then rushed inside to retrieve a pair of binoculars. Through this observation, the witness described a triangular object, orange in color but with brighter ends leaning towards white. The movement was silent on a straight trajectory from west to east. The observation lasted approximately fifteen seconds before the witness lost sight of the object without being able to explain its sudden disappearance (possibly hidden by a cloud). No other witnesses were reported. The witness likely observed a Thai lantern. Indeed, all these characteristics: - the orange color, - the eastward movement direction, consistent with wind measured at all nearby weather stations (unfortunately, none have data available within 25 kilometers, such as the one in Gourdon at 25km from Infoclimat), which always has a strong eastward component at that time, - the date, a summer Sunday evening, conducive to festive events, are all elements supporting this hypothesis. The witness's objections to this hypothesis do not withstand experience with GEIPAN lantern sightings: - a lantern can be unique, - speed can be regular and sustained, depending on wind at the flying altitude (100 to 300 meters), - flames are not always visible, and not better observed directly below the lantern as they can be obscured by the burner, - lanterns can have various shapes or cross-sections, particularly in their upper part, including triangular, square, or even heart-shaped (see a merchant site), - the lantern's color is not uniform, as paper can have color variations, especially if the shape is irregular. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Thai lantern.
