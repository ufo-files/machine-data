Summary: Observation of a curved orange streak in the sky: probable release of jet fuel from a military aircraft, performed at high altitude (a priori to limit environmental and population impacts).
--- seg-61be5cac637ad67d8e7378b3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 22, 2017, at approximately 9:45 PM, a witness observed an orange streak in the sky towards Tarbes from their window. Initially thinking it was an airplane trail, the witness was perplexed by its thickness and especially the curve it described, leading them to consider a struggling airplane. The witness took a series of photographs and contacted two gendarmerie stations. No other testimonies were collected.

The analysis of the photograph and the peculiarities noted by the witness (width and duration of the streak's dissipation) led to the hypothesis of an airplane conducting a fuel dump (see the investigation report). These same analyses enabled the establishment of scenarios for the plane's position and speed. The hypothesis of a commercial airliner could not be maintained as such an aircraft would have been found on public aircraft tracking sites, and most importantly, because such a fuel dump operation would have left a trace, which was not the case.

In contrast, regarding a military airplane, it is understandable that it does not appear in public records or in the CNOA radar record, as the latter was made over a too-short distance and cannot be retrieved after a two-month delay. The CNOA was unable to provide an operational record of the fuel dump, however, the search was too late (after the necessary investigation time to orient towards the hypothesis) to conduct it on all squadrons in France. Furthermore, such an operation may not have been recorded as it is not always a critical operation for a military airplane.

In this context, the absence of a military airplane and fuel dump operation on record does not allow us to invalidate the hypothesis, which should therefore be retained, given the strong visual agreement (witness and photo) and the consistency of possible trajectory reconstructions (photo analysis). The consistency is good, with a precise testimony and photos that could be exploited. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of a fuel dump from a military airplane at high altitude (a priori to limit environmental impacts on the population).
