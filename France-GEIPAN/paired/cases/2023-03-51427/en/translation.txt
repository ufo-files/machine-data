Description: On March 18, 2023, between 8:12 PM and 8:40 PM, the witness observed from their home in ROSHEIM (67) a bright light in the sky. After a few seconds, the light dimmed slightly, then intensified again, dimmed a second time, and ultimately grew 4-5 times its original size with a blue reflection, but without any sparkle this time. The shape became slightly oval. The light began to move, tracing small circles after initially moving in a zigzag pattern. The PAN changed color and appeared to move away from the witness towards the west. The witness then grabbed their smartphone and recorded a video and several photos. Due to having to leave for an appointment, they stopped observing the PAN. The case's consistency is good: it is well-documented by the witness's testimony and the video and photographs (see the investigation report). Analysis of the witness's testimony and the documents they provided led to the conclusion that it was an observation of Venus. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Location: The witness observed a bright, glowing light in the sky that they estimated to be at an azimuth of ~270° and an angular height of ~15° at the beginning of the observation. At 8:12 PM, Venus is located at an azimuth of 272° and a height of 14°55’. This is entirely consistent.

- Movement: The witness believed the PAN was heading towards northwest, which is a general direction and not an exact indication of the PAN's position since they mentioned "towards the west, deeper." Venus moves from an azimuth of 272° to 277°, which is consistent with a movement towards northwest (a left-to-right movement for the witness). Additionally, Venus descends in angular height from 14°55’ to 10°, which could give the witness an impression of receding.

- Magnitude in the sky is -3.96 for Venus, with no equivalent in this part of the sky, which aligns well with the witness's mention of a "bright light."

- The observation duration of approximately 20 minutes is typical of an astronomical observation.

Despite comparing the brightness of the PAN to Venus, the witness was unable to recognize the star due to several factors:

- The sky was almost completely overcast, temporarily partially obscuring the planet.

- There was no other light source as bright in this part of the sky.

- Atmospheric turbulence distorted Venus's shape and caused color variations.

- The effect of autocinematics gave the witness the impression of aberrant movements.

- Using a dedicated smartphone app to search the sky, but it was poorly framed as Venus was more to the right.

- Distortion induced by the photos, showing a defocused object while Venus is pinpoint, as the camera used was unable to focus during the zooms.

Finally, it seems surprising that the witness did not report the presence of Venus, which was in close proximity to the PAN and simultaneously, in the event that the PAN was not Venus.

GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Observation of Venus.
