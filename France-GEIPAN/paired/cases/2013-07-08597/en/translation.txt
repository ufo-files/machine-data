Description: On July 31, 2013, around 11 PM, a family observed the silent movements of a pair of bright yellow-orange lights in the sky. The lights suddenly took a straight upward trajectory. The witness observed from a small village near the Quiberon peninsula on the coast, looking southeast to south, just above the peninsula's entrance on the Carnac side. The weather report indicates that day a south wind up to 15 PM, then weakening and very variable until 11 PM (switching from south to north), then northeast at 11 PM, and then shifting to the east after midnight. (Source: weather) The time and location are very conducive to the release of Thai lanterns, often launched in groups. The wind reported in Vannes is consistent with the direction of movement of the observed phenomenon, which appears to be heading west as it ascends. In the witness's observation axis, there is the Quiberon tourist airport. The phenomenon described by the witness could also correspond to a plane seen from the front; however, it would be highly unexpected for there to have been night activity at this small tourist airport. Once the plane's headlights were turned off, the witness should have been able to make out the plane's flashing lights. The lantern hypothesis is much more likely. The phenomenon's phase of immobility could correspond to a movement towards the witness according to a very local breeze. The rapid ascent of lanterns into the sky is normal, the dimming of light is often interpreted as a receding, and the observation duration of a few minutes is typical for a lantern. Their flight path at takeoff might have been obscured by buildings or vegetation. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of Thai lanterns.
