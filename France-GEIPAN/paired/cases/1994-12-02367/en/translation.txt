**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more accurate in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the past years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named PLERIN (22) 29.12.1994 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. This case consisted of multiple observations made between December 28 and 31, 1994, in the evening or early morning in Côtes-d'Armor: four witnesses (T1, T2, T3, T4) reported their observations to the gendarmerie, all documented in the same police report. The attached investigation note details all the observations and phenomena witnessed. The entire observation set is inconsistent, with imprecise descriptions. Reassessment leads to the identification of three distinct phenomena:

- PAN 1 observed by T1 and T2 on December 29, 1994, between 7:00 and 7:30 PM.
- PAN 2, 3, and 4 seen by T3 on December 29, 30, and 31, between 6:00 and 6:45 AM (with T4 on December 31).
- PAN 5 observed by T3 and T4 on December 31, and PAN 6 by T4 on December 28.

PAN1 exhibits all the characteristics of an orage observation. The lights forming PAN are indeed compared to one or several lightning bolts: "It looked like explosions but miles apart from each other and always one at a time," "There were lightning-like flashes in the sky," "These bolts were flat and very long. I can't tell you the size of these bolts," "It looked like flashes but much larger," "A new lightning bolt appeared," "When I told you I saw lightning, there was only one that seemed to move." The hypothesis of a misidentification with an orage is strengthened by the fact that PAN is observed towards the sea. This direction is indeed confirmed by the witnesses: "I drove for a little while towards the sea," "I saw another bright blue light, immense, in the direction of BINIC, maybe above the sea." However, the presence of a storm at sea, visible from the coast, is confirmed by a gendarmerie investigation at the beginning of the evening on December 29, 1994, which is consistent with the observation time. Therefore, GEIPAN concludes for PAN1: B, probable observation of an orage.

PAN #2, 3, and 4 exhibit very similar characteristics. They are bright white lights observed at the same times over three consecutive days, for extended durations (over 40 minutes for PAN #3 and 4), without movement, and in the same direction from one day to the next. These data are entirely characteristic of an astronomical misidentification. A reconstruction shows the presence of Venus (very bright magnitude -4.24), in a southeastern direction (~130°) and at a height of about ten degrees in the sky. The hypothesis of a misidentification with Venus is strengthened by the fact that PAN is observed at a low angular height, through a window (the kitchen window of T3 and T4) oriented southeast. Consequently, GEIPAN concludes for PAN #2, 3, and 4: B, probable observation of Venus.

The descriptions of PAN #5 and 6 are too imprecise (short duration, limited information) and inconsistent (inconsistent testimonies) to be used. One can thus imagine confusions with any lights: car headlights, urban lights, in a context emotionally charged by previous observations. Consequently, GEIPAN concludes for PAN #5 and 6: C, lack of reliable information.
