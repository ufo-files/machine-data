Description: On April 10, 2022, at 5:23 PM, a witness observing from their garden saw in the sky the slow movement of an object that intrigued them. The last one spins erratically on itself, reflecting sunlight while maintaining a straight trajectory SE-NO. The observation lasts for more than 5 minutes, during which the witness takes photos with his mobile phone. The PAN is then lost from view. Only one witness statement is collected for this phenomenon. The photos and description by the witness point towards the very probable observation of a mylar balloon (see the following links: what did I see? and fantasy balloon). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- "Metal, aluminum" color: Mylar is a polyester film that reflects 95% of light and gives a metallic appearance.
- "Occasionally reflects light."
- The movement is slow, regular, and straight-line, with rotations of the PAN around itself. The movement is generally in the direction of the dominant wind (114°), the wind is very light (see the weather document): 5km/h and may vary locally, generating slight turbulences and therefore causing the balloon to rotate on itself. - "parallelepiped shape": Mylar balloons can have very diverse shapes. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: very probable observation of a mylar balloon.
