Summary: Long observation of a fixed multicolored light point in the night sky: astronomical misidentification with the star Sirius.
--- seg-f8ae7d9ab237f4bb30ac7b0c [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On February 24, 2019, a witness was watching television and took advantage of a commercial break to close the blinds in their living room. After a 1st observation in 2016, he got into the habit of watching the sky. He looks up at the sky around 6:43 PM and observes a glowing point that changes color while remaining stationary. He is taking pictures and thinks there is something abnormal. At 7:19 PM, he stops his observation, judging that he has enough evidence (31 photos and 5 videos are taken). No other testimony is collected. This case turns out to be a certain misidentification with the star Sirius (see the investigation report). The PAN does indeed present all aspects of a misidentification with Sirius: long observation duration on a clear day, the PAN's stellar appearance at normal magnification, a bright star, and colored twinkling. In addition, the position of Sirius corresponds perfectly to that of the PAN, both in azimuth (only a 1° difference according to the testimony) and in angular height. It is noted that for this last one, the witness estimates it at 30°, but his sketch shows that it was likely around fifteen degrees. In addition, the star Sirius is the only one to display a great variety of colors, as can be seen in videos and photos of the PAN during its twinkling. The low position of Sirius relative to the horizon enhances its twinkling. The case is classified A, mockery with the star Sirius.
