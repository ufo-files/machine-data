Summary: Long direct observation of a green streak in the night sky, followed by the identification of "blue or white orbs" through photographs: observation of the IPRAL laser from the SIRTA observatory (École Polytechnique), as well as stars.
--- seg-fe2d90b2387e403e209413d2 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the evening of August 29, 2017, several witnesses were intrigued by a piece of green light beam in the cloudy sky, disappearing and reappearing a few moments later, always at the same location. The witness took photographs. The observation lasted approximately one hour before witnesses went to sleep. Upon reviewing his photos of the PAN, the witness "discovered balls" that were white or blue. Only one testimony was collected. Of low strangeness and good consistency (3 witnesses, but a single testimony, photos of the PAN), the case turned out to be an observation of the IPRAL laser beam from the École Polytechnique, as well as stars (see the investigation report). The description and visual aspect of the main PAN seem very consistent with a laser. Notably, the green color and the fact that it was vertical strongly evoke the laser used by the École Polytechnique observatory. The observation direction of the PAN also corresponds to that of the SITRA observatory, managing the IPRAL laser. Contacted, the SITRA observatory confirmed the operation of the IPRAL laser at the time of the observation. The bright points ("balls") discovered after the observation on the photos of the PAN are very likely stars. Their relative position does not change on images taken at intervals of several minutes. In this investigation, double bright points may be an effect related to movement during the exposure time. The case is classified as A, observation of the IPRAL laser from the SITRA observatory (École Polytechnique), as well as stars.
