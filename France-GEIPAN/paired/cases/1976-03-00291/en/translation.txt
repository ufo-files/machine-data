Summary: Observation of two half-spheres partially buried on a beach.
--- seg-f3bce23f3f1f0c635244bf5c [needs-review] ---
Description: On March 24, 1976, the manager of a restaurant informs the gendarmerie that two bright spheres with an approximate diameter of one meter have just been discovered on the beach of La Feuillère (Marie-Galante Island, Guadeloupe). The gendarmerie indeed observes two spheres buried in the sand at the limit of the breaking wave and partially covered by seawater. They are 14 meters apart. A thin metallic coating (about 5/100 of a millimeter) covers both spheres: on one of them, the coating appears to have melted, revealing a harder and more resistant metal that does not scratch with the point of a knife. The next day at 6:30 AM, the objects are cleared by the gendarmerie, and it appears they are two hemispheres belonging to the same craft, perfectly fitting together. An inscription H 476 AC is readable on one of the hemispheres. These objects were to be returned to the brigade for cleaning and potential identification. However, at 10:00 AM on the same day, the mayor of Capesterre de Marie-Galante, despite the gendarmerie's prohibition, had the hemispheres removed to display them on the town hall square. This case is not an observation of PAN. It concerns the collection of two metallic hemispheres on a beach. The origin of these hemispheres might perhaps be determined using the nomenclature found inside one of them.
