Description: On the night of July 31, 2013, starting at midnight, a witness on the plateau of Madonne d'Utelle was intrigued by the slow, high-altitude movement of a glowing ball emitting regular flashes. Among the many satellites visible in the summer evenings, it appears that the third stage of the rocket that launched the American satellite LAGEOS in 1963 could correspond to this observation. In the Calsky document, the sky map oriented northwest, with the Big Dipper to the right, shows a white trace that corresponds to the object's path in the sky for 30 minutes. This type of object is almost always rotating: a reflective element of this object may periodically reflect the sun intensely and create a flash. However, the imprecision regarding the phenomenon's position in the sky does not allow us to confirm this hypothesis. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a high-altitude satellite (debris) whose rotation creates periodic flashes.
