Summary: Discontinuous observation of a white rectangular PAN with a zigzag trajectory: probable observation of aircraft (a Dash Milan 80 aircraft on a surveillance mission for fire risk over the Toulon mountains (phases 1b and 3), and regular flight of a commercial aircraft (for phase 2)).
--- seg-0a51f7c82deb35d355c589ae [needs-review] ---
Description: The main witness (T1) takes the boat, line 18, with his mother (T2) to cross the Toulon bay towards Les Sablettes (which they reach 20 minutes later). They observe a "craft," which T1 locates above the bay and which is performing zigzags in the sky. This PAN is oblong, white or silver and sometimes appears as two (according to T1) or three points (according to T2). Just before arriving at the Sablettes landing strip at 14:15, T1 takes two photos and a video of the PAN. He gets off the boat, walks towards his car parked in a nearby lot, and does not see the phenomenon again during the 15-minute drive home. It was only 5 minutes later, once he arrived home, that he saw the PAN low on the horizon to the east, visually passing behind a nearby house. The observation lasted 15 minutes during the crossing of the bay and less than a minute from the home of T1. The case is consistent due to the presence of a video and two photographs that could be analyzed by IPACO, the photo/video analysis software of the GEIPAN, on several parameters allowing the calculation of an angular height and an azimuth, enabling the determination of the direction of movement of the PAN during the second phase of observation. T2 reported his observation to the GEIPAN, but in an incomplete manner. According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. In this case, the observation of several aircraft and their maneuvers naturally results in a curved or zigzag trajectory since T1 is convinced of observing throughout a single and unique PAN despite various discontinuities; this forms for him the essence of the strangeness of the observation. This impression is reinforced by the similar appearance of the aircraft, seen from a distance (white color, "tic-tac" shape). However, note that T1 was very precise and accurate in its estimates of azimuths and angular elevations. Certain indices from T1's testimony (in particular a discontinuity in the observed observation and the differences between various elevation estimates) led us to divide this observation into four phases, which corroborated the hypothesis of multiple observations of several aircraft (see the investigation report). The elongated shape and white color of the PAN is consistent with those of aircraft observed from a distance. However, we note that for the initial phase of the observation, even if these characteristics could be consistent with other phenomena (bird, balloon...), we were unable to clearly identify the phenomenon in question. For all other phases, the file study, consisting of the operation and use of various aeronautical tools, and in particular the CAPCODA* map and the dedicated website FlightRadar24, has shown that a surveillance aircraft for forest fire risk over the Toulon mountains and a regular aircraft, flight EWG6838 that T1 photographs and films around 14:15, were the origin of the observation. In fact, the reconstructed trajectories are consistent in azimuthal positions and elevations with that of PAN in the observation time slices. The GEIPAN is classified as 'B', probable observation of a Dash Milan 80 aircraft on a surveillance mission for fire risk over the Toulon mountains (phases 1b and 3), regular flight of a commercial aircraft for phase 2. Phase 1 is classified "C": Unidentified phenomenon due to lack of consolidated information. *CAPCODA: Air Center for Planning and Conducting Air Operations and Air Defense (Air and Space Army). *IPACO: Image analysis and processing software for GEIPAN (IPACO.fr).
