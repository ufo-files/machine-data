Summary: Observation of the silent north-south movement of a group of twenty white light points: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-d0e4c1fc938dff64531da346 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 19, 2017, between 10:00 PM and 10:10 PM, a witness and his wife, observing from their apartment's terrace on the second floor, witnessed the movement of approximately twenty white glowing points forming a formation on a straight trajectory in a clear sky. No noise or trails were noticed during their high-speed movement. The observation lasted about 3 to 4 minutes, with the phenomenon moving towards the South. The witness contacted Avignon Airport and the gendarmerie but received no response. The witness dismissed hypotheses of drones, Thai lanterns, planes, or space debris. Only one testimony was collected regarding this phenomenon. The description aligns with that of a flight of Thai lanterns. The witness's objection to this hypothesis mainly revolves around the speed they estimate to be comparable to that of a plane. A witness only perceives an angular velocity. In this case, the movement was from North to South at a 60° vertical angle in 4 minutes. For a Thai lantern flying at 100 meters, this translates to a movement of approximately 200 meters, or roughly 3 km/h. Doubling these values for a lantern flying at 200 meters altitude, which seems more likely given the witness's distinction of more glowing points than shapes, aligns with wind measurements at a nearby station in Les Cabannes on Infoclimat, which shows a North-South oriented wind with a speed between 3 and 6 km/h. The station also records gusts up to 12 km/h, consistent with the hypothesis of a flight at a higher altitude (up to 500 meters), where the appearance of lanterns indeed resembles stars. To further strengthen the hypothesis, we note that it was a summer weekend evening, a time conducive to festive events like lantern releases. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Thai lanterns.
