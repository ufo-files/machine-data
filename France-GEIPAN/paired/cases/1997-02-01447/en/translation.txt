Summary: Observation of the movements of two amber-colored light streaks in the sky with zigzags and variable speed: probable observation of a skytracer.
--- seg-04df2afc9323e1792d840d5a [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This previously classified D observation case is part of a recent set of cases that have been re-examined. On February 13, 1997, a witness observed in the sky a bright amber-colored streak that moved slowly. The movement then became very fast and zigzag. A second bright streak appeared and joined the first. The entire formation moved in unison before disappearing towards the south. The witness stated that this "maneuver" lasted between 30 and 40 seconds. Only one testimony was collected. It is likely an observation of a skytracer (see the investigation report). The physical and dynamic characteristics of the PAN as reported by the witness are consistent. We can particularly cite: - the shape: it is a partial beam created by the presence of precipitation just below the cloud base, not the direct projection of the beam onto that base, which is why the witness mentioned observing it against a backdrop of sky; - the color, amber, likely refers to a pale yellow, a frequently used color; - the random movements are entirely consistent with the festive use of skytracers; - this type of projector already existed at the time and has caused other observations with the GEIPAN (see the www.geipan.fr website and case search with the keywords "skytracer" or "laser"). The effect can occur at several dozen kilometers away. Ground projectors could have been located in a local amusement park present at the time, or further away in the Marseille metropolitan area, in a nightclub, a commercial event, or during a fair, for example. The Gendarmerie's search area was too limited. The main uncertainty for this hypothesis results from its duration (too short for an event) and its occurrence on a non-festive evening (not during the weekend). This double peculiarity allows us to maintain the hypothesis, considering it could be a short functional test (test run) just before the weekend of the event. Another nearby hypothesis based on portable lasers (already existing at the time) better accounts for a short weekday use since anyone can use them any day of the week and for any duration. However, this other hypothesis must be rejected as the light (amber) and the low luminosity of the PAN do not match, and there is too much doubt about the range capability to reach clouds or condensation zones under the clouds. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable skytracer (laser) observation.
