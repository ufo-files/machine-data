**Case Description:**

On May 31, 2018, at 10:00 PM, a teenager was intrigued by the very yellow color of a stormy sky and filmed the sky with her smartphone from the balcony. A few minutes later, she told her mother she had filmed "something strange." Her mother corroborated her observation. No other testimony was collected. This is a case of strangeness created by the camera/video and thus not seen with the witness's eyes. The GEIPAN no longer handles these cases to dedicate its resources to strangenesses seen in the sky, rather than those created by devices or elements immediately adjacent to the device (e.g., insects, reflections). When we have strong doubt, we send a standard message (see analysis document GEIPAN) and the witness generally clarifies the situation, allowing us to either close or usefully handle the actually seen strangeness, where the photo/video is, of course, very helpful.

This case was studied and published, as it well illustrates the difficulty of handling the testimony of a child, either independently or relayed through their parents. This type of testimony is not isolated, and we sometimes struggle to obtain the vigilance or critical thinking of parents following our questions or requests for clarification. It is well known that a parent is not always the best positioned for a critical analysis of their child's statements!

In this instance:
- The parent of the minor witness, in response to our standard message about a probable photo/video strangeness, confirmed that their child first saw it with their eyes and then filmed it. We objected to the very strong difficulty for a PAN (presumably a fast-moving UFO) to be detected with the eye and to start the video recording, and the parent responded that their child filmed while looking directly at the sky and rotating the device in their hand.
- The unusual filming method, the changing description of an observation condition that remains contradictory to the initial testimony where the child alerts their parents because they FILMED (not SAW) something strange, are all elements reinforcing our doubts about the reality of the strangeness vision with the eyes.
- The parent continued to refute our opinion and insisted that the strangeness had been seen, while finding expeditious the GEIPAN's case classification.

We are unable to publish the testimony (the questionnaire and email exchanges beyond the initial standard response from GEIPAN) as the parent refused. We conducted a thorough analysis. We do this when the witness's argumentation (on the truly visual character of the observation) is strong, as was the case here for illustrative purposes. And once again, this confirmed our experience and the need for vigilance with strangenesses linked to photos and videos. We made several captures of the video (see attached document: 4 instances at 922, 2050, 2707).

Using the specific IPACO tool, we demonstrate that at each instance, the "strange" light point is in symmetry (regarding the center of the image) with the sun. This is indeed a classic optical reflection effect: a very strong light (here, the sun) is duplicated (of course, diminished) by the lens in symmetry with the optical center of the lens, which is, in principle, confused with that of the image (or not far off, which explains the slight offset). Of course, when the aim is scanning, this reflection moves twice as fast.

We confirm that this is a perceived strangeness without direct visual observation but on a device screen, and that this strangeness results from a photographic artifact. It is possible that the child saw a strangeness with their own eyes, but this was certainly not the subject of the testimony and what is noticed on the video.

**Conclusion:**
The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: photographic artifact.
