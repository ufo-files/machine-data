Description: The observation of the phenomenon took place from the witness's garden during an early summer night, around 23:00 on August 7, 2016, while stargazing. Only one testimony was collected. The witness reported a "first bright red/orange light," "very vivid" and "oval in shape," flying at a 45-degree elevation, but described by the witness as "quite low." A "second light followed." These "two lights moved together slowly," "quietly," "without a trail," appearing as "4 cm at arm's length." These "two lights got smaller," "turned towards southwest." The "scene lasted at most 30 seconds." The witness drew a diagram of the PAN's movement. At the end of the regular movement, the "lights got smaller," and the witness mentioned a "change in direction at the end of the path" towards southwest, "to the right of the two-story house." The witness specified "quietly," "without a trail." The last-mentioned direction towards southwest is contradictory to the witness's drawing, which states a direction towards northwest. We prioritize the drawing as more reliable. The weather conditions recorded in Saint-Étienne-Saint-Geoirs (38), approximately 30 km east of the observation location, on August 7, 2016, indicate a light wind, less than 10 km/h, from the south starting at 23:00 (see Météociel record). The witness "was concerned" by the lack of "other testimonies read or heard," even after "internet searches for this night of August 7." This testimony reports characteristic elements of Thai lanterns. The red/orange color, bright light, moving together slowly while following each other in a wavy pattern, quietly, without a trail. The direction of movement (towards northwest) is consistent with the measured wind direction (towards north) considering the latter is measured at 30 km. The final phase of lights getting smaller could correspond to the end of candlelight. Finally, it was a summer weekend evening, conducive to festive events such as releasing lanterns. The hypothesis of observing lanterns is consistent with the observation and is the most plausible. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: Observation of Thai lanterns.
