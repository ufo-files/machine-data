Description: On March 5, 2025, around 11:45/12:00, a single witness observed from an electric all-terrain vehicle, in the Vernier Marsh area in the commune of Saint-Aubin-sur-Quillebeuf (27), a PAN briefly above the coppices. The witness describes a round or "rather spherical" silent object, without halo or trail, of apparent size comparable to a handball, heading south towards the sun before disappearing behind the bushes after an observation of less than fifteen seconds. The file also contains two photographs of the PAN taken by a nearby photographic trap on March 5, 2025, at 10:50, and then on March 6, 2025, at 12:37, showing a generally similar silhouette in both images. The case consistency is good. Although coming from a single witness, the visual observation is well documented thanks to two exploitable photographs, a detailed technical questionnaire, a plan reconstruction, as well as a field investigation completed by environmental and meteorological checks. This set of data constitutes a clearly more rigorous case than a single, isolated testimony, despite the absence of direct instrumental measurements regarding the time of the photographic trap deployment (see the investigation report). The strangeness felt by the witness essentially stems from the unusual appearance of the PAN — perceived as a small, round, silent, and non-glowing form — and the discovery of that same silhouette in two photographs taken by his camera trap. The witness states that the appearance was too fleeting to provide a detailed description, initially leading him to think it was a balloon. It was the subsequent visualization of the images that transformed this mundane event into a report. Two main hypotheses were considered: one of a drone or light artificial object, and one of a bird observed and photographed under particular conditions. The drone hypothesis has some descriptive power, the silhouette evoking a discreet small utility craft. It remains speculative: no technical element supports it, no local operator has been identified, and the constraints related to propulsion, the absence of noise, and the context weaken it. The most coherent hypothesis favors a type of anatid bird seen from behind, or almost from behind, in powered flight. Technical analysis demonstrates that such a configuration generates a generally rounded silhouette, with the head and neck obscured by the thoracic volume, featuring a consistent tone contrast between a dark back and a lighter belly. Moreover, the shooting conditions can blur the wing motion to the point of making them indistinguishable, explaining the compact and unreadable aspect of the phenomenon. The dimensions estimated from the images are compatible with a medium to large-sized bird. Among the local species, the Canada goose is a credible candidate, although the quality of the images does not allow for formal identification. The initial visual observation, though non-discriminating, does not contradict this reading. A residual strangeness persists, however, mainly due to the unusual appearance of the silhouette in both images and the object's immediate unrecognizability to the witness and those consulted. This residual oddity, however, is greatly diminished by the analysis, which shows that a rear or near-rear view of an anatid, combined with an imperfect representation of the wings by the photographic trap, can produce this type of appearance without the need to invoke an unknown phenomenon. The fact that two photographs taken one day apart show a generally comparable silhouette does not appear to be incompatible with this hypothesis, provided the flight geometry and photography conditions remain similar. After the investigation, the most plausible hypothesis remains that of the observation of an anatid bird, likely a Canada goose, captured under unfavorable optical conditions that prevent a standard morphological return. The case can thus be classified as 'B': phenomenon likely identified, probably corresponding to the observation and imaging of an anatid under particular geometric and imaging conditions. *CONSISTENCY: According to the criteria of GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.
