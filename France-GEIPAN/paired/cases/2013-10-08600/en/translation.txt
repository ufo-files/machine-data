Description: On October 30, 2013, at 23:38, a witness observes three luminous objects flying at the same height, silently and at high speed. The objects disappear above the rooftops. The observation of luminous points moving very rapidly in a straight line for a few seconds suggests shooting stars or fireballs. This observation is remarkable because 3 objects are moving in parallel. It could be a meteoroid that fragmented into 3 during its entry. The small sideways movement of one of them could be a perceptual illusion called autokinesis (see The perception of the movement of a luminous point in the sky). The BOAM network (see: BOAM recorded numerous fireballs that night, including one precisely at 22:38 TU, but they were observed to the east, from eastern France; it is therefore not the same event. This network has no camera close to Sablé-sur-Sarthe (72). However, the frequency of events that night indicates only an appreciable probability of observing fireballs. Nevertheless, given the brevity of the observation, the fact that there is only one witness account, and the fact that there are 3 objects, this fireball-observation hypothesis is very tenuous. http://www.cnes-geipan.fr/index.php?id=382 GEIPAN classifies this case as "C" pending possible corroboration.
