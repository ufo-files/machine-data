Summary: Observations of a stationary and silent light phenomenon towards the West: astronomical observations of Venus.
--- seg-c973e009f007935b44059afa [needs-review] ---
Description: This "C" classified observation case by the GEPAN is now part of a re-examination (see the Investigation Notes document). On February 5, 1977, between 9:00 and 9:30 PM, two people in Reims (51) observed a bright ball of light moving towards the West that appeared stationary in the sky. No noise was heard during the observation of this phenomenon, which completely disappeared around 9:45 PM. This case is weak (two witnesses, but with brief testimonies), and the description of the PAN observed is fairly accurate. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The phenomenon described shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: Venus. Furthermore, Venus was indeed present in the observed area of the sky, with particularly notable brightness. The issue is not the witnesses' visual perception, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, night observation). In the current GEIPAN classification, this weak strangeness case is classified as "A," an observation related to a misperception of Venus.
