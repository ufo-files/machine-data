Summary: Motorists' observations of a luminous phenomenon seemingly blocking the road: misidentification of the Moon.
--- seg-0d172d7c505c4e8d8488a323 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time and now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified as 'D' in 1988 by the SEPRA and previously named LE VIGAN (30). In the night of December 17 to 18, 1988, around 2:45 a.m., four witnesses driving on D113 in two vehicles observed a luminous object consisting of a double rectangle separated by a black line and appearing to block the road. The object was 7 to 8 meters above the ground on the road's axis and retreated as the cars advanced. The phenomenon was silent. This case is consistent (three witnesses), and the description of the PAN observed is accurate. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The phenomenon described shares many common characteristics (shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the Moon at sunset. Moreover, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky while the witnesses made no mention of it (see investigation notes). It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (surprise, fatigue, fear, driving at night). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of average strangeness is classified as PAN A: observation related to a misperception with the Moon.
