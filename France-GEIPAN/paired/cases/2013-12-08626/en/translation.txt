--- seg-982d7fd292020fd9e81d7043 [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 29, 2013, around 5:45 a.m., a witness on the terrace of his home was intrigued by a glow in the sky that appeared between clouds, followed by a sparkling disk. The stationary object quickly disappeared behind the clouds. No other witnesses have come forward. The witness describes a "slightly inclined, sparkling, light yellow disk" observed for 2 seconds through a gap in the clouds; he indicates an observation direction "above Cernay, seen from Steinbach towards Mulhouse, therefore south": the map shows that the direction from Cernay to Mulhouse is southeast (approximate azimuth: 125°). The sky map indicates the presence of the Moon at 129° azimuth and 9° elevation, in its last quarter (very inclined thin crescent). It is highly likely that the witness did not recognize the Moon due to the clouds and the brevity of the observation. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B", a probable observation of the Moon. Given the difference in elevation estimation between the witness's report (20°) and that of the Moon (9°), there remains a slight doubt about the presented hypothesis.
