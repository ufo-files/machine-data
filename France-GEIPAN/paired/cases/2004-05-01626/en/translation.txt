Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named SAINT-HAON LE CHATEL (42) 2004 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. Two witnesses reported their observation to a UFOological association and the press before being summoned to the gendarmerie. They stated that on their way back from an evening out, around 01:30 or 02:00 AM on May 21, 2004, T1 observed in the sky a flickering bright point that was advancing at the same time. Then, the two witnesses observed this bright point following varied trajectories (ascending, descending). The PAN approached, and the witnesses then distinguished several bright points of various colors, all framing a black triangular mass. They heard a deep engine noise. The phenomenon flew over them at low altitude and then moved away towards the mountains. Although some data are missing (angle measurements, especially height on the horizon and dimensions of the PAN, and the exact location of the colored lights on the PAN), and others seem contradictory, key parameters allow for the hypothesis of an aeronautical confusion, probably with a small tourist plane in night VFR flight (see the investigation report). These parameters are: - The presence nearby and along the observation axis of the Roanne airport, approved for night VFR flight. - The direction of movement of the PAN towards other airports on the other side of the mountain. - The witnesses' perception of a similar engine noise to that of an airplane or helicopter. - As well as three colors (red, green, yellow) in accordance with aviation regulations. The elements that could be put against this hypothesis are: - Insufficient to invalidate the hypothesis: the plane could also have had a blue light, followed an irregular trajectory at the beginning, or the perception of irregularity could have resulted from a physiological natural phenomenon of micro-ocular mobility (micro-nystagmus) that occurs when fixating a bright point, especially at night, and gives the false impression that point-like bright objects are affected by small aberrant movements. - And/or lacking reliability (e.g., blue light, changing lights) due first to observation conditions (strong emotion, generated from the beginning of the observation by an extreme hypothesis of an object in contact with the witnesses, even an extraterrestrial object, and potentially altering perception or memory of some factors observed later) and second to testimony conditions (summoned by the gendarmerie, after initial revelation to a journalist). The consistency of the testimonies is low: - Lack of information. No angular measurements were made, nor were the exact locations of the colored lights observed by the witnesses specified. - Reliability is compromised by the conditions (as mentioned above) of observation as well as by the conditions of testimony. Nevertheless, the case can be classified as B: probable observation of an airplane. At the limit of a case C: unusable testimony.
