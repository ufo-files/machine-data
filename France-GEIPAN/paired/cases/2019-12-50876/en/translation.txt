Description: The witness observed on December 08, 2019, around 4:30 PM, for approximately thirty minutes, two black streaks that appeared vertical and moved slowly. He believed in airplane trails with a light effect. The witness took several photos of the phenomenon by zooming in. The strangeness noted by the witness is due to the absence of an airplane in the zoomed photos and the impression of a fall. The witness then thought of a plane crash, a meteorite, a rocket debris. Only one testimony was collected. The hypothesis considered is that of confusion with an airplane condensation trail (initial supposition of the witness), oriented along the witness's line of sight, and observed shortly after sunset. Observing aircraft condensation trails under certain meteorological and astronomical conditions can be particularly spectacular and lead to speculation about their origin. They can persist for a very long time after the passage of the aircraft responsible for their creation. The arguments in favor of this hypothesis are as follows: - The color, black but still well visible, is explained by the fact that we are at twilight. According to the position of the terminator in the sky (terminator: imaginary line separating the illuminated side from the side in shadow of the Earth; when, for an observer on the ground, the sun is set, the terminator will be at a greater or lesser height depending on the time elapsed since sunset), the condensation trail is more or less lit; - the shape, in reality consisting of two broken parallel lines, the double line deforms, as visible on the photos, according to the different winds at altitude and converges at the location where the aircraft is located. The strangeness is due to the apparent absence of an airplane and the impression that the object is heading towards the ground, which can be explained as follows:

- The airplane responsible for creating these trails may not be visible or may no longer be visible, often because it is no longer illuminated by the setting sun, or because it is already much farther away, leaving behind its long-lasting condensation trail;

- The perspective effect when the airplane responsible for creating the trail moves along the observation axis of the witness, either "moving away from" or "heading towards" the horizon. In this case, given the downward evolution of the trail, the aircraft is heading towards the horizon, creating a false impression that it is an object performing a vertical downward trajectory, when in fact it is not (the aircraft maintains the same altitude). It was not possible to positively identify the aircraft responsible for the phenomenon, as it was not possible to determine the exact time of the observation. Indeed, the witness mentions "around 4:30 PM," "the sun setting," and we have a timestamp for photos at 6:21 PM and a verified sunset time from Stellarium of 6:54 PM (local time). Given that, the consultation of Flightradar shows several possible candidates within a time slot from 4:30 PM to 7:00 PM, in the direction of the observation, that is, to the South West. The GEIPAN classifies the case as A: very high probability of condensation trails, multiple possible aircraft.
