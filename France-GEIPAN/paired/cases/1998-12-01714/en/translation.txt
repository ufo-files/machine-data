Summary: Observations of a bright red bar at the eastern horizon; impression of forward and backward movement towards the witnesses: misperception with a moonrise.
--- seg-d4ad66e9a337ad1dd76181c8 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This previously classified observation case D, named SAINT-POURCAIN SUR SIOULE (03) 1998, is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On December 5, 1998, at 7:45 PM, a family of three people observed in the overcast sky a long horizontal bright line at the height of the eastern horizon. This line appears to be composed of very bright red globes. After 30 seconds of observation, the PAN seemed to move back and forth before suddenly disappearing. No sound was heard during the observation. Two testimonies were collected. It was found that the observation axis was that of the driveway in front of the house. The driveway is relatively long (15 meters) and narrow (2.5 meters), providing a good reference for determining the azimuth of the PAN, approximately 65°. Additionally, the drawing of the PAN above the hedge at the back of the garden allows for an estimation of its elevation at around 4 degrees, assuming the observation was made from the middle of the driveway (as the drawing suggests). Coincidentally, the Moon is precisely at that location in the sky (Stellarium) at 7:45 PM (4 degrees and 66 degrees East). The mistake with the Moon is classic and can be explained by the cloud cover (present that evening) which almost completely hides the Moon, except for small gaps that evolve due to the wind, gaps that can reproduce any type of figure with one or more lights, usually red when the Moon is low below the horizon, as was the case that evening. In this instance, the Moon must have been just above or below the cloud layer as the witness sees the lights outside of the cloud mass. The other strange description is of the PAN moving towards the witnesses and then away from them. This is also a classic mistake, as the gaps vary in size or opacity, the lights vary, and the usual interpretation is to see a change in distance of a light of constant intensity. The witness could be contacted by phone. They confirmed the exact position of their observation relative to the driveway and hedges, as well as the height of the hedges at the time. They were able to understand the mechanisms of the mistake as described and, therefore, the conclusions of the GEIPAN. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Moonrise.
