Summary: Cloudy sky observation, with recurring reflections of light followed by a long stop to this "light dance": very probable observation of impact spots on the cloud layer from mono-beam projectors located on the ground.
--- seg-58dd5ff1c68a44cec8fe4fb4 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On November 5, 2022, at 10:42 PM, the witness is at their home in Clamart. He observes, in the sky, an intriguing light activity: types of round-oval light reflections, positioned in a triangle, making ping-pong like back-and-forth movements, in a regular, continuous, and moderate speed manner, before joining together into a single central circle. This phenomenon occurs for approximately 2 minutes, after which the light shapes remain fixed for at least one hour. Only one testimony will be collected. The consistency is good: only one witness completed the questionnaire and provided high-quality photos and a video (see the investigation report). The investigation concludes that the witness very likely observed bright light spots, the result of mono-beam projectors ("skyrose") impacting a low cloud layer. The range of such projectors is generally over 10 km; they can be commanded automatically or manually and produce fixed or moving (and independent of each other or not) light beams up to approximately 240,000 lumens of flux, producing a "sweeping" effect exactly as described by the witness (see images on the JMSkytracers site). This conclusion is based on the following elements: - "round - oval" shapes, - white, blue color, - absence of noise, - movements in "ping-pong sweep" type, - low cloud layer, - Saturday night. The witness found this observation strange for two reasons: The first is the absence of a beam. This point was explained by the fact that at the time of the observation there were no precipitation and therefore a beam could not form. The second is the presence of this phenomenon for at least one hour. The light display could have been temporarily stopped for various reasons (device settings, end of animation before shutdown, etc.). THE GEIPAN classifies the case as A: very probable observation of impact spots on the cloud layer from mono-beam projectors located on the ground.
