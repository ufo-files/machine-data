Description: On the night of August 20, 2012, between 10:00 PM and 11:00 PM, two witnesses observed a very bright, orange-colored object silently moving from north to south in the sky. Through binoculars, the witnesses could discern a halo and a faint trail from the object. The observation lasted less than a minute before the object's brightness dimmed and it was obscured by a nearby house. Only one witness reported the observation. The light wind in Basel was blowing slightly from the south to the west, which makes it incompatible with an object carried by the wind (e.g., a Thai lantern). The observation duration (one minute), the trail observed through binoculars, the dimming, and the transition to white are consistent with the observation of a satellite debris re-entry. Space-Track lists three debris re-entries on that day, but the orbits do not match the observation. Estimating the distance of such an event is impossible for a witness. Given the accuracy of the testimony, despite the lack of evidence, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": unlikely oddity, probable atmospheric re-entry observation: natural meteorite or more likely satellite debris.
