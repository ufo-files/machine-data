Summary: Observations of a red glowing phenomenon at the horizon: mistaken for the setting Sun.
--- seg-9015a3303b533b465f60988f [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time as Type "C" cases, which are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of the Type "C" cases that have been reassessed (see the document Investigation Notes). On October 24, 1977, between 5:15 p.m. and 6:00 p.m. (the times vary according to the witnesses), three residents of GUISE (02) observed on the horizon a red or fiery ball from which flames were dispersing. The observation lasted from five to ten minutes, after which the PAN gradually disappeared into the horizon. The witnesses filed their statements with the gendarmerie on October 25 and 27. After several days of patrolling, the gendarmes did not observe any abnormal events and did not collect any other testimonies. The case was the subject of a press article published in the newspaper L'Union on October 26, 1977 (see the document Investigation Notes). This case is consistent (three witnesses), and the description of the PAN observed is fairly precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The phenomenon described has many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the sunset Sun. In fact, the Sun was indeed setting in the axis indicated by the witnesses. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feeling (surprise). In the current classification of the GEIPAN, this case of weak strangeness is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misunderstanding with the sunset Sun.
