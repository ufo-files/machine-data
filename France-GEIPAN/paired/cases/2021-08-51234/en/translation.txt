Description: During the night of August 25 to 26, 2021, a driver traveling on D137 heading south reported observing an unusual light phenomenon at the exit of the commune of SAINTE-GEMME-LA-PLAINE (Vendée). He describes an intense flash, comparable to a lightning bolt at altitude, although the sky was clear and the Moon clearly visible. This flash illuminated a large portion of the sky in front of him, immediately followed by the appearance of a stationary, blue-tinted glowing ball located near the horizon. This light remained visible between one and five seconds. The witness estimates that the apparent size of this ball was slightly smaller than that of the Moon that evening, with slightly blurred contours. The driver of the vehicle that was ahead also perceived a flash and a blue coloration, without however distinguishing the ball. This second witness did not wish to give official testimony. The consistency* of this case is judged to be average: two witnesses, but a single testimony, no photo or video of the PAN. *According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The investigation led to digging into the hypotheses of lightning or meteor. The description of PAN, particularly the brevity of the observation, is compatible with the hypothesis of observing a bolide, the light flash potentially corresponding to the atmospheric entry of a meteoroid (see investigation report). It cannot be lightning, even distant, as no storm activity was detected at the time of observation. The fact that the PAN was observed on the horizon suggests it was likely above the Gulf of Lion. In addition, a bolide was detected from Rome (Italy) at a time consistent with the observation, by a sensor of the FRIPON network. However, the absence of trajectory data does not allow for the precise determination of the direction of its movement. The strangeness perceived by the witness is due to two elements: - a flash "like a lightning bolt at altitude" in a non-thunderstorm sky - a blue ball. The flash can be explained by the hypothesis retained. The blue ball aspect itself could be due to a retinal persistence. The case is classified "B", probable observation of a meteoroid.
