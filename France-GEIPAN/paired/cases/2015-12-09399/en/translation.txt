Summary: Observation of the silent movement of a red light in the sky, followed by the descent of a black object that then blends with the mountain: probable observation of a Thai lantern.
--- seg-762bd6d9907160e573319b6a [needs-review] ---
Description: On December 24, 2015, at 5:50 PM, two people on a walk observed the silent movement of an intense red light in the slightly cloudy sky for 8 minutes. This light has an ascending trajectory and takes a more orange color before disappearing into the clouds. The intrigued witnesses move and then see a black shape detach from the clouds and silently follow first a straight trajectory then a descending one. Unfortunately, they lose sight of the shape whose dark color blends in with that of the mountain. Only one testimony was collected. The witness conducted extensive research to try and find other testimonies and an explanation for what she saw. Based solely on color, shape, and brightness, what the witness describes is already typical of a Thai lantern observation. Other elements that support this hypothesis include a "holiday" date (December 24th) and, even more so, the final phase of the trajectory and observation. Indeed, it is described an object becoming dark that descends as if to land, which is indeed what happens at the end of a lantern's combustion, the lantern remaining nevertheless visible as a gray or black shape (not always round, here the witness speaks of an alveole) when the night is not too dark (the witness indicates that the moon was illuminating in twilight). The witness indicates no wind. However, the ground wind is often not felt by witnesses, even though there is often wind at 100 or 200 meters altitude. A light wind of 6 km/h can already cause a 1 km displacement in 10 minutes, thus creating a significant angular movement in the sky for a lantern flying at 100 m altitude. Unfortunately, it is not possible to correlate the trajectory with the wind, as there is no local weather recording available. The absence of noise from an unknown object adds to the strangeness of the observation, but remains perfectly explicable for an object carried by the wind. The witness thinks of a large object (twice the size of a glider). This estimation is explained by the fact that when one does not recognize an object and does not know the distance separating us from it, it is very difficult for any witness to estimate its size. Many indications given by the witness corroborate the hypothesis of observing the passage of a Thai lantern released on a private occasion. As a result, the GEIPAN classifies the observation as B: probable observation of a Thai lantern.
