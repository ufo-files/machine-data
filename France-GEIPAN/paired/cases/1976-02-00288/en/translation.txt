Summary: Observations of a moving light mass: astronomical observations related to a mistake with the Moon.
--- seg-8e43dc443d30057b64e03cf3 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On February 9, 1976, at 2:05 AM, a motorist and his passengers were first intrigued and then frightened by the presence of a luminous mass in the slightly overcast sky. Their observation begins at the exit of Luzon (85). After parking their vehicle at the exit of Beugne-L'Abbé (in the commune of Magnils-Reigniers (85)), but remaining inside, the witnesses observe the luminous phenomenon for 2 to 3 minutes. They describe the shape as two concave plates one on top of the other, each about twenty meters in diameter. At first stationary at tree level, this mass then slowly gains altitude. No particular noise is heard during the observation. The witnesses then continue on their way towards Les Sables-d'Olonne (85) and can still visually track the phenomenon and see it disappear to the West. The gendarmerie, notified immediately, found no trace in the indicated area. No other testimony regarding the observation of a particular phenomenon at this time will be collected. No electrical disturbances in the area will be recorded by E.D.F. Another witness will report having seen, on Sunday, February 8, 1976, at 8:00 p.m., two silent and unusual craft passing through the sky. The GEIPAN continues to publish all its archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time as Type "D" cases and which are now subject to a re-examination. This is not intended to diminish or increase any particular category of classification, but to be more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. This observation case is part of cases classified "D" in 1978 by the GEPAN and formerly named LUCON (85). It was published in 2011 under the name Les Sables d'Olonne (85) 09.02.1976 and reclassified as A: Moon Misidentification. The new publication in 2018 only impacts the case name. This case is consistent (3 witnesses), the description of the observation location and the PAN observed is precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been doubted. The described phenomenon has many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, color, size, etc.) with a known astronomical phenomenon: the Moon. Additionally, the Moon was well within the observed sky area, yet the witnesses make no mention of it. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation that the witnesses make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, anxiety, fear, and belief in the OVNI phenomenon). In the current GEIPAN classification, this case of weak strangeness, now explained as a misperception of the Moon, is categorized as PAN A.
