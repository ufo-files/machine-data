Description: On February 9, 1976, at 2:05 a.m., a motorist and his passengers were initially intrigued and then frightened by the presence of a bright light in the slightly overcast sky. Their observation began at the exit of Luçon (85). After parking their vehicle at the exit of Beugne-L'Abbé (in the commune of Magnils-Reigniers (85)), but remaining inside, the witnesses observed the luminous phenomenon for 2 to 3 minutes. They described its shape as two concave plates stacked on top of each other, approximately 20 meters in diameter. Initially stationary at the level of the trees, this mass then slowly gained altitude. No unusual noises were heard during the observation. The witnesses then resumed their journey towards Les Sables-d'Olonne (85) and could still visually track the phenomenon as it disappeared to the west. The gendarmerie, immediately notified, found no traces in the indicated area. No other testimony regarding the observation of a peculiar phenomenon at that time was collected. There was no record of electrical disruptions on the network in the area by EDF. Another witness reported seeing, on February 8, 1976, at 8:00 p.m., two silent and unusual craft passing through the sky.

The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation group) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type "D" cases, which are now being reassessed. This reassessment is not intended to diminish or enhance any particular classification category, but rather to make the conclusions more relevant. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or explicit changes in classification noted in an investigation report. This observation case is part of cases classified as "D" in 1978 by the GEIPAN and formerly known as LUCON (85). It was published in 2011 under the name Les Sables d'Olonne (85) 09.02.1976 and reclassified as A: Moon Misidentification. The new publication in 2018 only affects the case name. This case is consistent (3 witnesses), with precise descriptions of the observation location and the UFO observed. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The phenomenon described shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, color, size, etc.) with a known astronomical phenomenon: the Moon. Furthermore, the Moon was indeed in the observed sky area, something the witnesses did not mention. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, anxiety, fear, and belief in the UFO phenomenon). In the current GEIPAN classification, this weakly strange, explained case is classified as UFO A, related to a Moon misidentification.
