Summary: Observation of the silent movement of two orange-fire light phenomena that extinguish simultaneously: probable observation of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-64c1dbc3af4129cf7d46d037 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On April 15, 2012, around midnight, a witness observed the silent movement of a round, orange ball that seemed on fire. The object is moving on a descending trajectory towards the East and then turns to a southerly heading. A second phenomenon appearing identical (on fire and orange) is observed and seems to be joined by the first: the two PANs move together following the same course. The light intensity of both PANs gradually diminishes and then they both go out at the same time. Only one questionnaire is collected. Based on the descriptions, we can conclude that it's two wind-carried objects as they follow the same trajectory; the witness also reaches this conclusion. ("The appearance and behavior of the phenomenon led me to believe it was very light and 'carried by the wind'.") The measured wind (at ground level) at the airport is N-N0 (15 km/h), which does not exclude, at higher altitudes (with the influence of the city, hills, or forests), winding wind currents that could explain a change in direction from west to north. The two PANs follow the same trajectory with a gap between them; the feeling that one PAN is being approached by the other is subjective, as it occurs when the PANs are seen in the same direction and thus with distance differences difficult to assess. The orange color and final (in flight) extinction of the two PANs are characteristic of Thai lanterns, and a weekend night is conducive to such festive events. The description of flames at the rear of the PAN is unusual for Thai lanterns, which can indeed catch fire but quickly fall afterward. However, the perception of flames is often subjective and can be induced by the flickering light of a lantern for someone unfamiliar with all the luminous aspects of a lantern, or even more here by atmospheric turbulences and conditions of mist or fine rain. This does not detract from the witness's accuracy, as they are a commercial pilot. The investigation benefits from a careful reconstruction of the trajectory that the witness had to establish faithfully based on their angular perception (as a witness only has that). Or he locates on his drawing the appearance of the 2nd PAN 5 to 6 times farther than the closest pass of the first and therefore from an a priori angular perception 5 or 6 times smaller. Nevertheless, the same perception of fire comes from this PAN that is 5 or 6 times smaller and not larger than a 1/10 of the moon (according to the angular references given by the witness), which reinforces the subjective nature of the "flame" appearance. The "trailing flames" aspect is the only strange element in a set where everything points to wind-carried objects, and in particular Thai lanterns. But it is quite possible that this aspect results from a difficulty of perception (or interpretation error) favored by atmospheric conditions far from optimal. As a result, the GEIPAN concludes that the observation was likely of Thai lanterns.
