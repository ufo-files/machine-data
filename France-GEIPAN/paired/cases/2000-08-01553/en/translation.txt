Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named LILLE (59) 2000 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. On Sunday, August 20, 2000, starting at 11:30 PM, a driver observed in the sky an object in motion, shaped like a large circle with "windows" of light. The witness followed the object for approximately 8 km before it disappeared. Further along, when he stopped, the witness observed on his car's hood a red point that was moving erratically. When he raised his head, he noticed the same circular stationary phenomenon above his car. After a few seconds, the phenomenon disappeared, along with the red point. Only one testimony was collected. The description might have seemed strange during the initial analysis of the case in 2000. With the experience gained by the GEIPAN, there is little doubt today. The description is consistent with reflections in the sky from a set of projectors (commonly known as "skytrackers") used for advertising purposes (see the investigation report). The beams rising from the projectors are not visible here; only the impacts on a sky screen are. The pattern formed by these impacts is typical of the scanning and rotation capabilities of the devices used at that time in discotheques, as evidenced by other reports to the GEIPAN where the investigation led to the recovery of equipment producing such effects (see the ILE BOUCHARD case). In the GEIPAN experience, various configurations can be observed: visibility of only the impacts, only the beams, or both. This depends on weather conditions. Here, the presence of clouds that could create a screen in the sky, as well as recent rain that may have cleared the atmosphere of any water droplets that could make the rising beams visible (it is the presence of particles in the air that allows this visibility) is verified. It is a weekend evening in the summer, in a resort area (Normandy coast: Deauville, Trouville...). This is conducive to the opening of night establishments using such projectors (discotheques, etc.) as they were already widely used at that time. As for the second PAN, it resembles in every way the effect of a portable red laser activated by a prankster from nearby houses or buildings: this is plausible, though unverifiable. It can be understood that the witness, already facing the strangeness of the first phenomenon, might have associated it with the second. Without the first PAN and the alert, he might not have noticed the second or been intrigued enough to give a testimony. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case according to the first PAN. The consistency of the testimony is low, with particular gaps in angular dimensions and sky positions. Nevertheless, the consistency of the aspect in the sky and weather conditions allows for a confident conclusion (although the projector responsible could not be identified): very probable observation of a skytracer or lasers.
