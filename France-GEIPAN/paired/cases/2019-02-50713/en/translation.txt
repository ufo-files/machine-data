Summary: Independent witness observations of slow and silent passage of bright circular objects in the sky: hot air balloon observations.
--- seg-3da9df75e81dc2a2dd59eb58 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On the morning of February 19, 2019, several independent witnesses were intrigued by the presence in the sky of PAN circular and bright objects moving slowly and silently. The witnesses take photos or film the PAN. Two testimonies are collected at GEIPAN but other people living in northeast France seem to have seen the PAN. The idea of a wind-carried phenomenon seems to be the most likely explanation, as the descriptions agree in describing (sometimes two) white and bright balls that appear stationary or moving very slowly and regularly (see the investigation report). The "Ballon Concept Challenge 2019" is a hot air balloon competition starting from Switzerland and requiring the aerostats to travel as far away from the takeoff location as possible. The 2019 edition thus saw the fleet disperse towards the north to overfly Franche-Comté then Lorraine before reaching Germany (for the best). The consistency is maximum as two totally independent testimonies are available (not to mention all those known outside GEIPAN), concerning observations of respective durations of over a quarter of an hour. The phenomenon was observed by several witnesses for approximately twenty minutes under good visibility conditions. It was photographed and filmed several times. The absence of a gondola and the shape described as "ball" may have seemed strange, but all the evidence collected points to an aerostat. With the origin perfectly identified by the (expanded) investigation, there are no residual oddities left. It is also noteworthy that it is probably the particularly unusual altitude of the hot air balloons that created the strangeness felt, as the witnesses could hardly equate what they were observing with aerostats, especially since their metallic appearance reflected the Sun strongly. This double observation relates to perfectly identified stimuli, in this case, a specific type of hot air balloon (double-walled). They were participating in a competition, namely the third edition of the "Ballon Concept Challenge." The two witnesses who initiated this investigation had the excellent reflex to both photograph/film what they were observing and immediately testify to the Gendarmerie for one, and to GEIPAN for the other. The GEIPAN classifies the observation in category A (perfectly identified phenomenon): hot air balloon observations.
