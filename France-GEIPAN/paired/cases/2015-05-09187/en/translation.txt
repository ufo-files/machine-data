Description: On May 30, 2015, around 11:55 PM, a couple outside was intrigued by the straight and slow passage of an orange-light object through a cloudy sky. No particular noise was heard. The object disappeared into the clouds. Only one testimony was collected. The GEIPAN analyzed several hypotheses for this observation:

- A flare (reflection) on an Iridium satellite, visible at that exact moment (see Calsky record), but it was visible in the southwest and moving towards the northwest, plus the brightness should have been perceived as variable: this hypothesis does not fit well.

- A flare on another unlisted satellite: very unlikely.

- A bolide (meteorite re-entry into the atmosphere): the brightness, duration, and trajectory might fit, but the witness would likely have perceived variable brightness. No other observations were reported, nor camera recordings (but the sky was cloudy!).

- Thai lantern: the color, time, and day of the observation are consistent with this hypothesis, but the strong north wind is a priori incompatible with the observed movement towards the east (see Météociel).

Ultimately, the GEIPAN does not adopt any of these hypotheses due to lack of verifications or cross-checks. Additional information may eventually help determine the origin of the observed phenomenon.
