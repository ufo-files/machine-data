Description: On August 21, 2021, at 3:12 PM, a witness driving a car spotted a peculiar object in the sky heading northeast: a kind of long, linear, white object resembling a "long stick," apparently stationary. Although he initially thinks it's a glider or an airplane, the witness cannot discern any wings or tail. He managed to capture two photos in the space of two seconds, before the object disappeared, hidden behind buildings. The witness turns around, attempting to locate the Pan, but to no avail. According to Geipan's criteria, the testimony is of good consistency (consistency is the amount of estimated reliable information gathered for a testimony), meaning that there is one witness, notably precise, and photos support the testimony. Several hypotheses are considered (see the investigation report):

- A stationary airship, perhaps for advertising purposes, whose shape and color seem to correspond.
- A drone flying above a stadium or nearby parking lot in the observation area.
- An aircraft flying at very low altitude, such as a small tourist plane or helicopter. The National Air Operations Center (CNOA) map confirms the presence of air traffic in the observation area at the time of the incident. The indicator F-GSLT on the CNOA map identifies a helicopter from the company Héli-Travaux®, flying at low altitude with a speed between 77 and 99 knots. Initially, the witness thought it could be a plane or a glider, the perceived strangeness being due solely to the fact that he saw no wings or fin. This can be explained by a possible partial reflection of the sun on the cockpit or the rear of the aircraft. In fact, with the Sun located near Southwest (Azimuth 218°/NG) and at an angular height of approximately 48°, it is directly ahead and slightly behind the witness moving towards Northeast. This configuration favors intense specular reflection on metallic surfaces or well-oriented reflective strips. The CNOS report confirms that the observed phenomenon corresponds to a small helicopter belonging to a construction company. The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'A': observation of a brightly lit helicopter by the Sun.
