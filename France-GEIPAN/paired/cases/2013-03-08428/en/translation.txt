Description: On March 3, 2013, around 11:00 PM, a driver observes in the sky a kind of white cross among the stars, then notices, a little further, the movements of a luminous phenomenon with color changes. His observation lasts one and a half hours. The gendarmes, called to the scene, will observe and film the oscillations of the phenomenon. Testimonies and video show a roughly fixed bright point, but changing color and oscillating slightly and randomly. The star Sirius (magnitude -1.44) is in the direction of the witness's observation (at an azimuth of 225°, approximately 212° as recorded by the gendarmes), only 15° above the theoretical horizon. The weather conditions (see the Meteociel report) can favor extreme scintillation: the atmospheric pressure, in decline and persisting the next day, indicates strong winds at high altitude, which cause intense scintillation of the star. Low temperature can also cause particular optical atmospheric phenomena if there are ice crystals in the air: halos, crosses. The autokinetic illusion (see autokinetic illusion) may also have played a role. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A": observation of the star Sirius with intense scintillation due to particular weather conditions.
