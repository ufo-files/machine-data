Summary: Observation in the sky of a very bright yellow point: astronomical observation of Venus.
--- seg-05e070095d30e4683c22b296 [needs-review] ---
Description: On Monday, May 24, 2010, at 10:30 PM, a witness observed a very bright, static, yellow point from the first floor of a residence. He alerted his parents, who observed the same thing. Two photographs were taken using a digital camera. No unusual noises were heard during the observation, which lasted 45 minutes before the witness decided to go to bed. The witness was intrigued as he also reported other light phenomenon observations from this residence, without providing specific dates and in a different geographical observation area. No other testimony about this phenomenon was collected. The observation on May 24, 2010, is certainly of Venus. The other repetitive observations are likely observations of other celestial bodies (Mars and Sirius), very visible in April to the southwest (see the April 2010 celestial map). The three bright points observed with binoculars are probably an optical aberration or an effect of atmospheric turbulence: this effect is very common for observations of very bright celestial bodies. The witnesses may also have observed weather balloons regularly released from the Météo France center in Trappes (12 km southeast of the observation location) during the day or early night. These balloons are sometimes released in "trains" and are likely to "disappear" suddenly when, at high altitude, the sun sets behind the horizon. This May 24, 2010 case is classified "A": astronomical observation of Venus.
