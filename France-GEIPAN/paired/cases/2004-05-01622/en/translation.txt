Summary: Observations over two consecutive nights with the naked eye, then with binoculars and a camcorder, of bright yellow and green balls in the night sky, perceived as moving: likely observations of Venus.
--- seg-3cd96d55d83f5f88a4bad7e3 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D and named case DIJON (21) 2004 is part of a set of cases recently re-examined. On May 02, 2004, around 23:30, and on May 03 at 20:30, a witness and his son observed, for a very long time, with the naked eye, then with binoculars, and subsequently by taking a video, a luminous phenomenon that intrigued them. Witnesses describe the presence in the sky of bright, spherical lights that are much larger than the surrounding stars. These balls seem to move away and then approach before quickly disappearing. The light intensity is very high. The description includes typical characteristics of an astre observation: - the observation duration is long (> 15 or 30 minutes) and the PAN does not move or changes little in position in the sky. - The PAN appears for two consecutive nights and in an aspect and position sufficiently identical for the witness to report it as the same phenomenon. (T1: "The following evening around 10:30 PM, I filmed this phenomenon again"). The indication of the second night (Moon opposite the direction) fixes the observation direction in NO. Or, in the NO, a star significantly brighter than the others stands out, VENUS. - T1 states, "I went to the window and there I saw in the sky a large glowing ball that was 10 times larger than a star, it was green." - T2 states, "My father called me, saying come see, there's a star that's bigger than the others." I looked and saw a large yellow and green star. It is clear that, despite the weather report stating cloudy skies, the witnesses observed a group of stars in that direction of the sky, as T2 states, "I would like to specify that that evening there were several stars, but this ball was larger than the others." All of the above is characteristic of a misidentification with VENUS, which is indeed much brighter (or "larger") than other stars, and especially can appear in various changing colors due to atmospheric effects, which can also create sensations of motion (T2 says "This phenomenon also made zigzags from right to left and up and down"). The elements of strangeness described by the witnesses initially boil down to seeing a particular star due to its intensity and yellow or green color. Then, there is a clear increase in strangeness perceived with these other balls accompanying the main ball; the main ball also makes forward-backward movements (T1: "this sphere looks like a tennis ball always in motion, quickly approaching and quickly receding," "other glowing balls formed a 'V' above and quickly descended to the level of the village lights."). Then, these balls took off again as quickly as they had appeared.") That evening, Venus, along with Saturn and Mars on one side, and Elnath and Capella on the other, formed a V-shaped formation pointing downwards towards Venus (see the reconstruction document). The entire object (as described) does indeed descend towards the city lights, as it goes from an elevation of 10° at 23:30 to crossing the horizon to the right at 00:40 on the first day. T2 states ("Then later there was a ball that returned with a smaller one," this could correspond (see reconstruction) to Pollux and Castor taking the initial place of Venus. The backward-forward motion perception for T1, or for T2 of "leaving and returning" or yet another "rapid movement towards the EAST" (meaning closer to T2 since he is looking towards northwest) can correspond to a well-known optical illusion of a GEIPAN: variations in intensity of a PAN, here caused by atmospheric opacity changes, are interpreted as distance changes of the PAN. It is noted that this increase in perceived strangeness (beyond the initial aspect of the yellow star) begins with the observation made with binoculars and especially on the video. From there, it becomes difficult to distinguish what results from naked-eye observation and what results from observation through a device. And it is probably this surplus of strangeness that led the witnesses to testify, and also what may have led the GEIPAN to an initial classification as Unexplained D. Or, we know from experience that the peculiarities observed in videos, photos, or telescopes, which are not already present to the naked eye, are in the vast majority of cases artifacts from these devices, artifacts that generate a very strong perception of oddness. Moreover, the GEIPAN no longer takes into account these cases of testimonies. The GEIPAN does not have the video transmitted to the gendarmerie to proceed with confirmation. The V explained above by the astronomical configuration has a span of 30°, and would correspond more to a naked-eye sighting, but it is possible that the V is much smaller and was only perceived on the video and is a pure video artifact related to the zoom (maximum here). It is also possible that the video artifacts created a strong sense of strangeness, leading the observer to perceive as strange, with the naked eye, an astronomical configuration that was only moderately strange at first (a large yellow or green star). Although we cannot precisely determine the exact origin of the peculiarity between the two plausible causes (astronomical configuration or man-made artifact), it is evident that the point of origin of the observation is very likely Venus, and without it, the reference to devices, the peculiarity would have remained with that star, larger than the others and yellow in color. As a result, GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable Venus observations.
