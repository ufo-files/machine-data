Summary: Observation of the successive passage of two luminous phenomena in the sky on a straight trajectory: probable observation of the passage of Thai lanterns.
--- seg-d9360dcb6c4a5ced0653af67 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On June 14, 2013, at 11 PM, a witness in their courtyard was intrigued by the successive passage of two orange glowing phenomena. The description of slowly and silently moving red points in the sky on a weekend evening evokes Thai lanterns. The Blagnac Météociel report indicates a light northwest wind: as the witness sees them appear to the north, they are logically carried towards the east. The Calsky report of visible satellites indicates the passage of the ISS station just before 11 PM, on a trajectory very close to that reported by the witness, but the time does not seem to match a priori. The ISS is generally perceived as a white or yellow point, rarely orange. Furthermore, there would be no explanation for the second similar object just after. One could also think of planes taking off from Toulouse-Blagnac (landings are made to the south of Toulouse by northwest wind): but the flashing lights would have been noticed. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of Thai lanterns.
