Description: On Wednesday, October 3, 2018, at 11:00 PM, a witness observing from their home's doorstep saw and photographed three glowing points of light that moved slowly and silently across a clear, starry sky. The points formed a triangle and disappeared rapidly on an upward trajectory. Only one testimony was collected. Analysis of the testimony and images reveals a low-importance, strange phenomenon, barely visible to the witness and quite fleeting (see the investigation report, with the three points labeled PAN A, PAN B, and PAN C). The PAN are visible low in the sky, apparently close to the ground. They appear to move generally from east to west, rising only slightly into the sky. An effect of distance from the witness is evident in the photos. The dimness suggests a low-intensity phenomenon. We consider the possibility of released lanterns, even though the day is not typical for this type of release. It's Wednesday, not Friday or Saturday night, or the eve of a holiday, often conducive to such activities. However, a release for a birthday, for instance, is not impossible. After analyzing the photos, we find that the PAN follow a progression compatible with the direction and speed of the wind, which is oriented SSE (200°). The effect of distance evident in the photos indicates a "pushing" wind relative to the observer looking south, reinforcing our hypothesis. The low speed of PAN B and C, at a low angular height, strongly suggests a recent release close to the witness. The observed color is not disqualifying, even though lanterns generally have an orange hue. We also note on image 225920 a clear orange tint on PAN (B), previously white on the image just before. The IPACO image processing software indicates: possible lantern (see annex). We repeated with PAN C from image 225929. The movement of the PAN in the direction of the wind's movement is clearly visible in one photo. The strong contrast enhancement of image 225920 shows a bright light coming from below the PAN, reminiscent of a small candle producing light-heat under a paper envelope. The witness's perception of rapid disappearance is explained by the distance in the direction of the SSE wind, creating the illusion of rapid escape. In light winds, Thai lanterns begin with an ascending rise, seem to stabilize (an impression of immobility), then catch the wind and move away. Consequently, the GEIPAN is classified as A: Thai lanterns.
