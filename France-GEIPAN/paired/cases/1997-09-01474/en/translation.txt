**Description:** The GEIPAN (French UFO investigation body) continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions' relevance. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named PARIS (75) 1997 is part of a recent batch of cases re-examined (see the investigation report). On Sunday, August 24, 1997, at 8:01 PM, a witness observed and photographed two UFOs (unidentified flying objects) over PARIS (75). He contacted a scientific journal, which advised him to inform the DGAC (French Civil Aviation Directorate). The DGAC contacted the Gendarmerie's Air Transport Staff, which instructed the Paris BGTA (air transport gendarmerie unit) to conduct an investigation. On September 4, 1997, gendarmes visited the witness's home to conduct an interview and review nine slides he had had processed at a private lab. Eight of these nine slides were subsequently sent to the Gendarmerie's Central Photographic Laboratory, then to the Criminal Research Institute of the Gendarmerie. An additional witness interview was conducted by the gendarmes on October 11, 1997, to gather more information. At this time, the witness also drew a sketch of the UFO, which was handed over to the gendarmes. In May 1998, a study was conducted by GEIPAN, followed by an exchange of letters and faxes between the witness, GEIPAN, and the Fleximage company; the latter received a detailed account of the witness's observation in June 1998. The case was subsequently reclassified as D by GEIPAN due to the difficulty in discerning and explaining the various strange elements between:

1. What the witness saw
2. What was on the slides presented to the gendarmes
3. What was found on a new paper print after cleaning the slides of development residue
4. What the witness described about the UFO after seeing the paper print

It is now established that:

(2) The UFO witnessed by the witness is not depicted on the slides presented by the gendarmes. The latter were alerted to a point on the photos (boomerang shape) that turned out to be a development residue.

(3) A paper print derived from one of the cleaned slides contains, by chance, an artifact (as nothing equivalent exists on the slide) in the form of a hollow pentahedron.

(4) The paper print was shown to the witness without revealing the artifact nature of the object. The witness, when asked to comment on this object, did not make the connection with the UFO initially described (and sketched) as resembling "a greasy paper carried by the wind" with "shattered angles." Later, however, the witness provided new accounts of his testimony, where the UFO initially described (and drawn) as having "five sides, a pentahedron" shape. Thus, it is known that the UFO witnessed by the witness is not depicted on the slides, and also that the witness was subsequently influenced, consciously or unconsciously (false memory phenomenon), by what he saw on the paper print.

(1) The absence of the UFO on the slide does not allow the initial witness description to be doubted. However, no information is available (or exploitable without ambiguity) regarding the angular dimensions, the angular height on the horizon of the UFO. For example, "5000 meters altitude" means nothing since a witness cannot estimate the distance of an unknown object. During their visit to the witness, the investigators focused on viewing the slides without conducting further investigations (photographing the shooting locations, measuring angular distances from the observation window...). Nevertheless, the limited information available is consistent with the observation of a light object (debris, paper, balloon...) carried by the wind, as the wind (meteorological data) was blowing in the direction of the observed UFO's movement.

In conclusion, we have a series of exploitable photographs, but their meticulous analysis does not highlight the presence of any UFO, and we do not have sufficient information to identify the UFO described by the witness. However, what was described does not convey extreme strangeness as it is compatible with a light object carried by the wind. Accordingly, GEIPAN reclassifies the case as "C": Unexploitable testimony due to lack of reliable information.
