Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case named PARIS (75) 1997 is part of a set of cases recently re-examined (see the investigation report). On Sunday, August 24, 1997 at 20:01, a witness observed and photographed two UFOs (PANS) above PARIS (75). He contacts a scientific journal which advises him to inform the DGAC. The latter contacts the General Staff of the Gendarmerie of Air Transport, which instructs the BGTA in Paris to conduct an investigation. On September 4, 1997, the Gendarmerie investigators went to the witness's home to take his statement and view the nine slides he had had made at a private laboratory. Eight of these nine slides are then successively entrusted to the central photographic laboratory of the Gendarmerie, and then to the criminal research institute of the Gendarmerie. A complementary hearing of the witness is conducted by the gendarmes on October 11, 1997, to gather further information. The witness also makes a sketch of the PAN at that time, which will be handed over to the gendarmes. Finally, a study is conducted by the SEPRA in May 1998, followed by an exchange of letters and faxes between the witness, the SEPRA, and the company Fleximage; the latter receiving a detailed account of the observation by the witness in June 1998. The case was subsequently classified as D by the SEPRA due to the difficulty at the time of distinguishing and elucidating the various strange elements between: 1) What the witness saw 2) What was on the slides presented to the gendarmes 3) What was found on a new paper print once the slides were cleaned of developing residue 4) What the witness said about the Pan after becoming aware of what was on the paper print. It is now established that: Point 2) The PAN seen by the witness is not in the slides presented by the gendarmes. These last ones are alerted by a point on the photos (boomerang shape) which turns out to be a residue of development product. Point 3): A printout from one of the cleaned slides accidentally contains an artifact (as nothing equivalent exists on the slide) in the form of a hollow pentahedron. Point 4): The paper printout is shown to the witness without revealing the artifact nature of the object. The witness, called to provide an opinion on this object, does not associate it with the PAN observed. However, later the witness provides new accounts of his testimony where the PAN initially described (and drawn) as resembling "a thick piece of paper carried away by the wind" with "shattered angles" becomes "Its remarkable shape cutting through the blue background of the sky was extraordinary: five sides, a pentahedron." So it is known that the PAN observed by the witness is not present in the slides, and also that the witness is subsequently influenced, consciously or unconsciously (false memory phenomenon), by what he saw in the paper printout. Point 1): The absence of visual evidence on the slide does not allow us to doubt the witness's initial description. But no information is available (or exploitable without ambiguity) regarding the angular dimensions, the angular height above the horizon of PAN. For example, "5000m altitude" means nothing since a witness cannot judge the distance of an unknown object. During their visit to the witness, the investigators focused on reviewing the slides without conducting further investigations (photographs of the shooting locations, angular measurements from the observation window, etc.). However, the limited information available is consistent with the observation of a light object (debris, paper, balloon, etc.) carried by the wind, as the wind (weather data) is blowing in the direction of the observed movement of the PAN. In conclusion, we have a series of usable photographs, but their careful analysis does not reveal the presence of any PAN, and we do not have sufficient information to identify the PAN described by the witness. As such, what is described does not translate to great strangeness as it is compatible with a light object carried by the wind. As a result, the GEIPAN reclassifies the case as 'C': Unusable testimony due to lack of reliable information.
