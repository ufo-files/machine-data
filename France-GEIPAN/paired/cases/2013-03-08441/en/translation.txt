Description: On March 14, 2013, at 7:16 PM, a witness is intrigued by a sight of a yellow-orange luminescent phenomenon in the sky and films it for a few seconds. Turning for a moment, the phenomenon is gone when the witness looks back at the sky. Photo and video analysis (viewed online) leaves little doubt about the origin of the phenomenon. This observation is similar to those made by numerous witnesses in Martinique on February 15 (see case observation Sainte-Luce (972) 02.15.2013). Airplane contrails at sunset (or sunrise) are often orange, very bright as they are lit by the setting sun, and contrasted against the dim twilight sky. Certain weather conditions produce spectacular contrails. The plane is coming from the north horizon towards the witness, so the contrail is oriented towards the horizon and gives the impression of being vertical. The contrail is actually parallel to the Earth, but seen from below, with a low angle of incidence, it appears vertical. The object seems nearly stationary as it advances towards the witness, while being very far away (at least 30 km). Furthermore, there is no fixed reference point in the sky to accurately judge its movement. When the plane, advancing towards southeast, enters the Earth's shadow, the contrail will no longer be illuminated and will become less bright than the blue-white of the sky, thus becoming invisible. The witness should be able to witness the same "show" from time to time, but it requires particular weather conditions: a clear sky, of course, but also specific humidity and temperature conditions at the plane's altitude. Note that many commercial airplanes fly in this direction (London-Barcelona, London-Toulouse), the precise identification of the present plane would have little interest. This case is classified "A": observation of an airplane contrail illuminated by the setting sun.
