**Description:** The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined to enhance the conclusions' relevance. Thanks to new technical tools (software) and investigative experience gained over the years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named SAINT-HILAIRE (91) 04.10.1981 is part of a recent batch of re-examined cases. It concerns the observation of a PAN by a couple of motorists at a crossroads known as "la cabane à Pierrot" in the commune of SAINT-HILAIRE (91) during the night of October 3rd to 4th, 1981, around 2:30 AM. The witnesses were surprised to see ground flashes followed by three red lights still at ground level that seemed to be approaching them. Fearing for their safety, they quickly left the area. No noise was heard. The Gendarmerie was notified on October 9th and visited the site without finding any traces. Only one testimony was recorded by the Gendarmerie on November 3rd, 1981.

**Preliminary note regarding a case where testimonies and traces relate to a phenomenon explicitly described or perceived on the ground:**

- Without evidence that the phenomenon on the ground could only have arrived and/or departed by air, the search for an explanation goes beyond the GEIPAN's sole competence, and a GEIPAN conclusion cannot be formulated unless proof or near-proof is established. Otherwise, the GEIPAN must conclude C: Unexploitable as it exceeds its sole competence.

- Conversely, the absence of testimonies or traces of the phenomenon's phases off the ground may prevent the GEIPAN from reaching a conclusion due to lack of reliable information (classic C GEIPAN classification). We are in the first case, as nothing rules out the possibility that the phenomenon arrived or departed by the ground, as the absence of ground traces proves nothing in either direction. The case is therefore classified as C: Unexploitable as it exceeds its sole competence.

Despite this, the GEIPAN provides, without further conclusion than the above, an analysis according to its methods of certain aspects of the case (see the investigation report). The well-observed lights as coming from the ground are hard to explain in terms of vehicles:

- The successive vision of white and red lights, as well as the evolution of the lights' configuration, could correspond to changes in viewing angles or orientation of a vehicle.
- An agricultural vehicle in operation could thus have particular lighting with a single headlight at the front (perceived as successive flashes due to pointing variations) and three red lights at the rear. However, an agricultural or other practice by the owner or operator is unlikely at 2:30 AM on a Sunday. Nevertheless, the identified owner was not questioned.
- The intrusion of one or more third parties onto the property cannot be ruled out, as a Saturday night party context could favor incongruous initiatives or accidents.
- Particular lighting could only be explained by vehicles (several motorcycles?) or particular vehicle configurations.
- No trace was visible in the field, though this does not rule out the presence of a vehicle at the time of the incident, especially if the ground was dry.
- The witnesses' perception at the end of the observation of lights approaching them could correspond to either the lights' actual movement or a known phenomenon to the GEIPAN: the witnesses interpret an increase in light intensity (which could be caused here by better alignment of the lights in the witnesses' direction) as the lights moving towards them. The hypothesis of a moving vehicle raises the question of its plausibility at that location and time, without being able to rule it out.

On the other hand, the case's consistency in information and reliability is poor:

- The witnesses did not make spontaneous statements. Rumor led to the intervention of a GEPAN correspondent and then the witnesses' hearing by gendarmes. Under these conditions, a witness may feel constrained in their statements by previous descriptions made on the spot and by the (potentially more strange) understanding that may have been made of them and spread;
- There is only one testimony. The identical description (to the point that the Gendarmerie only considered one testimony) may indicate strong coordination and influence between witnesses prior to their convocation by the gendarmes;
- There is no description of the PAN's disappearance, nor any angular measurements;
- The owner of the property where the phenomenon occurred was not questioned.
