Summary: Multiple observations of peculiar moving phenomena in the sky on February 2 and 4, 1985:

Observations on February 2: Observations of the star Arcturus.

Observations on February 4: Possible observations of an airplane.

Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time as Type 'D' cases and now being reassessed, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in its conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an investigation note. The case of observation formerly named ALDUDES (64) 1985 is part of cases classified 'D' by the GEPAN and recently reassessed. In fact, it's two completely different observations, on February 2nd and February 4th (see investigation notes). The PAN on February 2nd corresponds to an observation of the star Arcturus. - This star, seen at the horizon, regularly causes misunderstandings due to effects of twinkling, color explosion, and movement: effects generated by the passage of light rays through the atmosphere. - There is perfect correspondence in the azimuth directions. - The observation is accompanied by a rarer atmospheric refraction phenomenon (superior mirage) that explains the star being seen beyond the horizon. This phenomenon also amplifies the classic misunderstanding effects related to the passage through the atmosphere. It is not possible 32 years later to quantitatively confirm this atmospheric refraction. - The same observation is reported by the press (a group of night skiers at the Somport station, see PV). The repetition of a nearly identical observation of PAN by two independent groups of witnesses, with the same coincidence (1 or 2 degrees apart) in azimuth with Arcturus, and with the same nature of looking beyond the horizon, validates the Arcturus hypothesis and the particular atmospheric refraction phenomenon accompanying it. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: Observation of Arcturus. The PAN on February 4th likely corresponds to an airplane observation. - The hypothesis was rejected by the witnesses due to the absence of noise from the PAN. The wind blowing from the northwest acted in the opposite direction of the noise and thus may have greatly diminished it. - The observation was made on the evening of February 4th, while the press had published the same day an article "____ in the sky" related to the February 2nd observation. It is therefore possible that the witnesses on February 4th were in an alert state and perceived strangeness in an observation that would have remained more mundane without the first observation and its media coverage. The GEIPAN classifies this case as B: Probable observation of an airplane.
