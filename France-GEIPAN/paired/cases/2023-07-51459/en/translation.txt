Description: The main witness of this observation (T1) observed through his bathroom window a PAN of orange color, trapezoidal in shape, moving horizontally. Intrigued, he went outside and alerted his companion (T2). Together, they spotted a second PAN with a similar shape, located at a higher altitude in the sky. T1 then captured a brief one-second video of the PAN before noticing, along with T2, that both PAN were moving away towards the southeast while gaining altitude. The witness specified that from a distance, the PAN appeared spherical and made small circular movements. No noise was perceived by the witness. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, the case consistency is good, with two witnesses to the observation, one of whom completed a technical questionnaire along with a sketch and exploitable video. Consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. Only one hypothesis is explored, that of observing sky lanterns (see the investigation report). The witness's description of the phenomenon (PAN), including the orange color, trapezoidal then round shape, comparison with a flame, and absence of noise, remains characteristic. The witness did not feel any particular strangeness, as he repeatedly compared the PAN to a lantern in his testimony. The apparent trajectory of the PAN, moving towards the southeast, is consistent with the wind direction, which was west-northwest. Its low intensity is also a factor supporting the hypothesis, as sky lanterns cannot be released except in calm weather. The witness's description of the first PAN is very precise, mentioning a particular shape, "trapezoidal," "feet" that could be likened to the suspenders of a lantern, and also "small circular movements"; all of these elements indicating that it must have initially been at a low distance from the witnesses, to the east, before moving away towards the southeast. The municipalities of Pralong (42) and Champdieu (42) were not aware of a sky lantern release on the night of the observation, however, the good description of the PAN and the confirmed aerological conditions support this hypothesis. The case is classified as "A," observation of sky lanterns.
