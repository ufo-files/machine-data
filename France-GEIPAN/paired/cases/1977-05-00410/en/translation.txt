Summary: Observations of the presence of a red-orange glowing ball in the sky: Astronomical observation with mistaking of the Moon.
--- seg-74390994b45f3fca45091d48 [needs-review] ---
Description: On May 30, 1977, at 3:30 a.m., two people in a car observed a bright red-orange ball above the Vitteaux (21) urban area. This ball was at low altitude and moved slowly in a north-south direction. No noise was heard during the observation, which lasted 5 to 6 minutes. This old case was reviewed. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, the review sometimes leads to new observations or an explicit change in classification in an attached investigation note. This case is consistent (two witnesses, including a police officer), and the description of the PAN observed is very precise. The sincerity and credibility of the witnesses have never been questioned. The phenomenon described shares many common characteristics (observation duration, shape, size, color) with a perfectly known astronomical object: the Moon at sunset. Moreover, the Moon was clearly present in the observed area of the sky, while the witnesses made no mention of it. It is not the visual perception of the witnesses that is in question, but the interpretation they make of their observation through their feelings (fatigue, night driving, Moon seen intermittently through clouds). In the current classification of GEIPAN, this case of low strangeness is classified as PAN A, an observation related to a misperception of the Moon.
