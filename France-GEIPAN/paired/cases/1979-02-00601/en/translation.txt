Summary: Observation of an orange-colored half-sphere object: mistaking it for the Moon at sunset.
--- seg-f8c14c33dba8c0f2b48eec8a [needs-review] ---
Description: On January 4, 1979, at approximately 1:15 AM, a witness observed a bright object in the sky for 30 seconds from their vehicle. The object had the shape of a half-sphere, orange in color, moving from southwest to northeast on a horizontal trajectory. The object gradually faded away as it dimmed. This observation report was initially published by the GEIPAN in category C due to lack of information. An internet user, an astronomy enthusiast, identified this case as potentially a misidentification with the Moon at sunset; to verify this hypothesis, they returned to the observation site in February 2015, 36 years later.

The orbits of Earth and the Moon are such that the period when Earth and the Moon share the same relative positions with respect to the Sun corresponds to 223 lunar months, or 6,585.32 days: this period is known as a Saros; it is used to calculate the interval between lunar or solar eclipses. Note that after this period, which is not a whole number of days, the solar time is not the same. In our case, between the date of the observation and the date of the recreation, we needed to count this period twice, close to 18 years. The exact date falls on February 25, 2015, at 4:36 PM, significantly different from the observation time in local time.

To choose the best recreation date, we need to find moments near this date when the azimuth and angular height (elevation) of the Moon are closest to those of the observation. Two dates prove suitable:

- February 23, 2015, at 11:55 PM, with the Moon at 1.1° angular height and an azimuth of 288°, a lunar month of 5 days, and a phase two days before the First Quarter.
- February 25, 2015, at 12:00 AM (so the night of February 24-25), with the Moon at 1.5° angular height and an azimuth of 292°, a lunar month of 6 days, and a phase the day before the First Quarter.

For comparison, the Moon's position during the observation in Boulaysur-Moselle was 1.4° angular height and an azimuth of 286° (the recreation was thus done with a Moon position perfectly similar to 1979), with a lunar month of 6.7 days and a phase at the First Quarter.

The GEIPAN publishes in full the observation report of this amateur astronomer, a very well-documented report whose conclusion is: "The testimony from that time corresponds in many ways to the observation made during the recreation: the shape is indeed a half-sphere of reddish-orange color, its tilt is almost horizontal. The observation duration could have been brief, as the Moon was low and close to the horizon. Additionally, we can assume the presence of clouds that could obscure it at any moment. The observation location depicted on the plan attached to the gendarmerie protocol has a topographical peculiarity that might surprise the witness. Indeed, this location has a slope where one goes from a point with an altitude of 324m to a point with an altitude of 340m, over a nearly straight distance of about 250m. The slope is thus approximately 6%. This relief configuration can, for example, in the case of driving a motor vehicle, create an illusion of a burst of elements at the horizon that were previously masked by the slope."

This highly convincing recreation allows us to classify this case as "A": observation of the Moon at sunset.
