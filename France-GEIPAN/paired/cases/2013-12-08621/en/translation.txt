Description: On Sunday, December 15, 2013, at 6:30 PM, a witness is intrigued by an orange glowing point larger than an airplane, silent, and stationary in the clear sky. After a few minutes, the object moves away and quickly disappears on an ascending trajectory. The GEIPAN analyzed two hypotheses for this slowly moving orange light near the horizon: -A plane seen head-on, at low altitude, or approaching an airport (Bordeaux Mérignac, in this case). The witness reportedly saw the aircraft approach and noticed its flashing lights. The radar traces from the military air traffic control show high air activity in the area at that time (see record, unfortunately very illegible, which does not allow for a clear correlation with the observation). In the sector bounded to the northwest by Taillan via Sainte-Hélène and to the northeast by Cubzac, the following flight paths corresponding to civilian aircraft can be observed:

- GE 751 heading 200, FL 380 (altitude 12,000m)
- GE 962 climbing, passing FL110 (altitude 3,300m) at heading 020 after a turn to the south of St. Jean d’ILLAC and an initial course of 220.
- GA474 at heading 020, FL380 (altitude 12,000m).
- Two overlapping trajectories corresponding to an aircraft at heading 220 descending, passing 3,500' (altitude 1,200m) at 5:13 PM and a craft at heading 040 at 5:46 PM climbing, passing 2,000'.
- GE 743 at heading 160, 3,800' (altitude 1,300m).
- A Thai lantern: the color, appearance, duration, and elevation match well; however, the light easterly wind should have pushed the lantern to the left of the observer, which the witness does not mention. One can also think of a local wind, a little different from that recorded in Mérignac. Given the time of observation, at dusk, it could also be a reflection of the sun on a weather balloon, launched from Mérignac. It is also noted on that evening at very close moments two phenomena of powerful bright points in the sky:

- the passage of the international space station ISS from 6:07 PM to 6:16 PM, from SO to NE (the direction of observation does not correspond well to that indicated by the witness).

- and a flash of a few seconds from an Iridium satellite at 6:25 PM directly south, little above the horizon. Multiple hypotheses can explain this very unusual observation, but none perfectly aligns with the testimony. The GEIPAN classifies this case as C due to a lack of information and corroboration.
