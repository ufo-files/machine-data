Summary: Observation and photographs of a dark PAN in motion in the sky: probable observation of a fantasy balloon.
--- seg-57c937b2572372aef46ea7f1 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On May 10, 2025, around 8:56 PM, the witness, in their apartment in Metz (57), was observing the sunset when they noticed a black PAN in the northwest sky at an elevation of approximately 30°. The object moved in a straight line at a constant speed along an east-west axis. The witness had enough time to take two photographs, including one with maximum zoom, before losing sight of the object to the west at an elevation of 15° after about two minutes of observation. No noise was heard during its passage, and no other witness reported this observation to GEIPAN. The case's consistency is good: despite only one witness, two photographs of the PAN were taken and analyzed for the hypothesis study (see the investigation report).

According to GEIPAN's criteria, consistency refers to the amount of reliable and objective information gathered from a testimony. This analysis indicates that the observed data is consistent with a passive object carried by the wind, such as a helium balloon (a hypothesis proposed by the witness). The object, possibly partially deflated, would move in a straight line at a distance estimated between 190 and 480 meters from the witness and at a relatively constant speed between 2 and 6.5 km/h. The direction of its movement and the speed of the PAN are generally consistent with the wind conditions measured near the observation site. The black color is entirely possible for a fancy balloon, and this aspect could also be caused by an observation in contra-daylight.

The observation took place on a Saturday evening, a time when using fancy balloons in a festive setting is common, for example, to celebrate a birthday or a wedding. The variation in the shape of the PAN between the two photographs can be explained if the object is a partially deflated balloon and/or if it is rotating on itself during its wind-carried movement, revealing a dissymmetry in its shape.

The main strangeness reported by the witness is the presence of a reddish light in the second photograph (not observed with the naked eye). Such light can be briefly produced by the reflection of the setting sun on a highly reflective part of the balloon. It could, therefore, be a Mylar-type balloon, composed of a partially or completely aluminized envelope. The envelope's shape presents a dissymmetry or is possibly partially deflated.

GEIPAN classifies the case as "B": probable observation of a fancy balloon.
