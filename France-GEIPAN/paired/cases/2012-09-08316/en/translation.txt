--- seg-0621b3a49e9519c619426764 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On September 9, 2012, around midnight, a group of three friends observing meteors are intrigued by a very bright star that seems to move quite quickly in all directions, followed by another light of lower intensity. Only one statement is collected. The sky map shows the planet Jupiter and the star Aldébaran at the position described by the witnesses. The movements of these lights described by the witnesses are obviously not consistent with the hypothesis of observing fixed stars. The hypothesis of an autocinetic illusion is advanced to explain this statement: it can be seen that this area of the sky is not densely populated with other bright stars, making this hypothesis plausible. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": observation of Jupiter and Aldebaran, probably observed with an autocinetic illusion.
