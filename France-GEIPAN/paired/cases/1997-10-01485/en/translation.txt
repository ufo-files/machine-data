Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more accurate in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named LYON (69) 1997 is part of a recent batch of cases that have been reassessed. On August 27, 1997, around 9:45 PM, three witnesses returning from work observed the movement of a triangular object (or rather, a round object according to the testimonies) that moved silently, at a low altitude, and in complete silence. The craft was equipped with two large headlights. As it approached the witnesses, one of the headlights briefly shone directly at them. Two silhouettes inside a glass cockpit were seen by one of the witnesses. The gendarmerie received a letter from T1, cosigned by T2 and T3, and interviewed the three witnesses. The report states:

- T1: The object was above the area where helicopters from the hospital were operating. The oddity for T1 was the momentary pointing of the spotlight or headlight directly at her, the absence of noise, and the inability to see the rotor and gas exhaust system.

- T2 repeats the first two elements of strangeness from T1 but saw green and red lights, characteristic of air navigation lights.

- T3 does not specify any particular oddities ("I didn't pay much attention to the phenomenon and I got back into my vehicle"). This occurs at a location where helicopters are present and landing. Everything described is consistent with a helicopter flight. The witnesses are, a priori, familiar with helicopters as they work at the hospital. The absence of noise led the GEIPAN at the time to dismiss this hypothesis. However, since then, there have been numerous other cases where witnesses do not perceive the noise of a helicopter at 100 or 200 meters, as their perceptive attention is focused on the visual or due to emotion. It is also known that noise depends on wind conditions. See on www.geipan.fr the cases MESSINCOURT (08) 10.03.1994 or [D417] of MUNSTER (68) to COLMAR (68) 13.11.2015.

What triggered the strangeness for the witnesses, especially for T1, who may have influenced T2 (T3 does not seem affected), was the pointing of the spotlight at them. Helicopters do point spotlights at the ground quite often. The other reported elements of strangeness, such as the inability to see rotors or gas exhaust systems, are not considered given the night observation conditions. On the other hand, the green and red lights and the low speed of movement strengthen the helicopter hypothesis. The overall testimonies are inconsistent. It seems that T1 may have influenced T2 and T3, who do not convey the same perception of strangeness. This is imprecise and leads to inconsistencies (for example, regarding the lights). The helicopter hypothesis leaves little doubt, mainly due to the location, the green and red lights, the references to this hypothesis by the witnesses, and the explanation of the phenomena triggering the strangeness (the spotlight pointed at them and the absence of noise). Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as A: helicopter.
