Summary: Observation of successive appearances of 5 glowing orbs in low altitude moving across the night sky: very probable misidentification with Thai lanterns.
--- seg-80881a3e3d5eeaebf8dee021 [needs-review] ---
Description: On Wednesday evening, August 5, 2020, between 10:45 PM and 10:55 PM, the witness observed five luminous phenomena successively in an azimuth of 358° (towards the North) at an elevation of approximately 3°. They all have identical shapes, same luminosities, same sizes, same directions and same trajectories, same angular velocities. The only difference is that phenomena 3 and 4 follow each other at an angle of approximately 10°. The trajectory is straight towards southwest. The angular velocity is constant at 16.57°/min and becomes virtually zero. The witness also reports intermittent disappearances and variations in brightness as if the PANs were penetrating a cloud or fog. The disappearance of PANs is noted at 329° azimuth. No particular noise is heard. The witness takes a video with his mobile phone. Only one witness statement is collected on this phenomenon. The case file is consistent, the witness's data is very precise, and the video adds value to the testimony. The witness initially did not consider a release of lanterns and that is why he contacted the GEIPAN. On October 19, 2020, the witness recontacted the GEIPAN. He confirms to us that after watching videos of misidentifications with lanterns, his observation (particularly the extinction) corresponds exactly to this phenomenon. The trajectory and apparent speed are also consistent with the wind data for that evening. The investigation confirms this second analysis of the witness. The observation is indeed compatible with Thai lanterns: 
- in appearance: bright, glowing points that are fairly bright, with an apparent size of about 5', seemingly slightly twinkling, with hues ranging from yellow to red/pink, passing through orange. Here is the translation of the provided GEIPAN text:

Typical colors and brightness of lanterns are observed here. - In trajectory: the PAN are following each other, heading south-west, which is typical of lanterns being carried by the same wind current and corresponding to the wind direction given by the Tavaux weather station (7km) (see the weather document). - In duration and disappearance mode: the observation duration (5 minutes) is compatible with the flight duration of lanterns, and the disappearance of these lanterns is likely caused by the gradual extinction of the burners. For a witness, this may be in error corresponding to the entry of the PAN into the clouds, while the latter are generally at an altitude higher than the maximum flight altitude of the lanterns. - the speed of 16.57°/min, corresponding to approximately 87 km/h, was overestimated by the witness as he positioned the PAN at a distance of about 5 km. If one considers that the PAN were likely several hundred meters away, one finds a speed of the order of 8 to 9 km/h, compatible with the speed of lanterns carried by the wind (see Google Earth documents and distance and speed calculations). A release of lanterns is not usual on an evening during the week. But given that it's August and there has been a long period of confinement, it's possible that a festive family gathering took place on a weeknight. The Jura Prefecture having notified that between the period from April 24, 2020 to October 6, 2020, no release of lanterns was authorized, as the department was subject to successive decrees due to drought, if a release of lanterns was carried out, it was done without authorization, making it impossible to trace its origin. The GEIPAN classifies this observation as B: very probable misidentification with Thai lanterns.
