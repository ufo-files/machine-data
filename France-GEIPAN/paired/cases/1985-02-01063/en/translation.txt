Summary: Fall of an object preceded by a whistle: Fall of a part of a German war machine (ground-based "smoke launcher") that spontaneously caught fire.
--- seg-24cad42366418f35b1dc199d [needs-review] ---
Description: On February 25, 1985, two farmers were working on their respective plots when an object fell into a field less than 50 meters from one of them, bouncing several times before coming to a stop. The object was hot and could not be touched for an hour. It was a cylinder approximately 50 cm long and 15 cm in diameter. Initial investigations by the Rochefort Air Base did not identify the object, which did not correspond to any type of French or foreign missile. Additionally, no radioactivity was detected on the recovered object. The GEPAN conducted a search but no military activity could explain the fall of such an object. The search then focused on space objects that may have re-entered the atmosphere that day. NASA and NORAD responses indicated that a piece of the COSMOS 1629 satellite's propulsion stage, launched on February 21 and referenced 15.575, likely re-entered around February 25. This information led the GEPAN to the provisional conclusion of the atmospheric re-entry of a piece of propulsion stage from the fourth stage of the COSMOS 1629 launch. However, the object was returned to the GEPAN by the air base for further in-depth analysis. These in-depth analyses, later launched and conducted by the Arcueil Research and Study Center, revealed indications and markings proving the object was of German origin and bore the marks of the Third Reich. The GEPAN's initial conclusion was thus abandoned in favor of the hypothesis of an unknown origin propulsion element abandoned since the last war and which spontaneously caught fire. Additional expertises identified the device as the rear part of a land-to-land "fog launcher" and showed that the point of origin of this device, which could have been ignited by thermal effect or following a slight shock, could be estimated between 3300 and 3600 meters northwest of the impact, which corresponds to a marsh area where the German army, at the end of World War II, submerged many munitions while being encircled in the Royan pocket. This investigation, which spanned nearly 5 years, is a model of its kind by its evolution and successive twists.
