**Report:**

On May 14, 2022, at approximately 03:10, from the balcony of his apartment in Montluel (Ain, 01), a single witness observed a PAN. Heading south, he noticed for about 8 seconds the passage from west to east of a gray anthracite rectangular object. This PAN, without lights or trail, appeared to the witness as roughly 20x40 mm at arm's length. It emerged silently behind a southwest hill at a 15° elevation, followed a straight horizontal trajectory, then disappeared behind another southeast hill at the same elevation. The witness described two notable peculiarities: cloud distortion during its passage, comparable to effects observed around black holes, and a thin white halo surrounding it. The oddness of the observation was particularly significant to the witness. Initially deemed average, the *consistency* of the case was reevaluated as good following a field investigation. This reevaluation is due to the witness's perfect collaboration with investigators, despite the absence of visual documentation (photos or videos) of the PAN and the unique nature of the observation. The Technical Questionnaire (QT) was completed by the witness only three days after the observation, and the data collected in the field (13 months later) are very complete and appear reliable (see investigation report).

*According to GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of considered reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. The measurements taken during the field investigation, supplemented by analysis calculations (taking into account the witness's overestimation of the PAN's apparent size), demonstrate that the PAN, disappearing behind a hill at the end of the observation, must have been at least 15 meters long and moved at a minimum speed of 130 km/h.*

The witness also considered several explanatory hypotheses, ranging from a natural phenomenon evoking a rectangular moving black hole to a military aircraft with camouflage capabilities, a dirigible with heat emission, a swarm of insects, a low cloud, or a possible mental projection. However, none of these hypotheses can adequately explain the characteristics of the PAN observed due to significant discrepancies between their properties and those described in the phenomenon. Furthermore, the aircraft hypothesis, although the calculated data (observation distance, possible sizes and speeds of the PAN ) are compatible with an aircraft, seems unlikely. This low probability is explained by the significant difference between the appearance of the PAN and that of an airplane or drone. Additionally, no aircraft was recorded by radars nor appears on CAPCODA (Air and Space Army Air Defense Planning and Conduct Center) return maps.

The investigator formulated two additional hypotheses to explain the observation:

- **Hypnagogic or Hypnopompic Dream:** Although several causes can explain this phenomenon, we will focus on the one related to insomnia or fragmented sleep, in connection with the witness's nocturnal activity. The witness indeed indicated having adopted a shifted schedule between December 2021 and December 2022, going to bed around 6 a.m., without sleep episodes during his nocturnal wakefulness, and with rapid adaptation to this schedule. Given these elements, it seems unlikely that the witness experienced this type of dream once.

- **Negative Retinal Persistency:** This phenomenon, resulting from prolonged exposure to a light source, can generate a persistent negative image on the retina. Given that the witness spends a significant portion of the night in front of screens, facing rectangular windows, it is possible that he experienced this phenomenon during his breaks. The sudden transition from intense brightness to a darker ambiance could theoretically create an optical illusion of a dark rectangular shape, similar to the description of the PAN.

The reconstructed timeline during the field investigation shows that approximately 30 seconds elapsed between the moment the witness left his workstation and the observation. However, the duration of such an illusion's persistence remains uncertain, as it requires sustained concentration and fixed gaze, and is favored when eyes remain closed. Several solid arguments challenge this hypothesis:

- **Consistency of observation conditions:** The witness has been working under the same conditions for several months and follows an identical ritual during his breaks. It is therefore unlikely that he observed this phenomenon only once without questioning it.

- **Absence of observation in darkness:** If the phenomenon had been due to negative retinal persistency, the witness would also have observed it during his trip in the dark to the balcony, which did not happen.

- **Unexplained characteristic:** The distortion of clouds in the background during the PAN's passage, a central element of the oddness, cannot be explained by a simple optical illusion. These observations lead to conclude that the negative retinal persistency hypothesis does not correspond to the phenomenon's characteristics.

Consequently, no known phenomenon hypothesis can reduce the perceived strangeness by the witness, and given the considered satisfactory consistency of the testimony, GEIPAN classifies this case as "D," that is, an unidentified phenomenon after investigation.
