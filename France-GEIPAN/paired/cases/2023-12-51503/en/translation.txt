Description: On December 15, 2023, at 07:10, a witness driving on N106 towards Nîmes (30), observed a PAN at the height of La Calmette (30), in a clear night sky. The PAN was described as an intense white light, visible at an elevation of 30°, moving from north to south. No trail was observed and no noise heard. The PAN traveled in a straight line at high speed before changing its trajectory, appearing to circle around a hill, then disappeared after a duration of 3 seconds. The portion of the sky traversed by the PAN was very brief. Only one testimony was recorded. According to the criteria of GEIPAN, this case presents an average consistency due to the following: the testimony comes from a single person, without photographic or video evidence of the PAN, and the available data are incomplete, particularly regarding the exact direction of the witness's observation. The following support the hypothesis of a bolide, defined as a luminous phenomenon resulting from the entry into the atmosphere of a natural celestial body:

- White light in rapid motion,
- Confirmation recording of the presence of a bolide at 07:07 by three cameras of the FRIPON (Fireball Recovery and InterPlanetary Observation Network) network located in Mantet (66), Le Bleymard (48), and Aubenas (07),
- Short observation duration (3 seconds),
- Initial straight-line movement.

Although the precise trajectory of the bolide was not reconstructed by the FRIPON network, the directions captured by two of the three cameras closely match the witness's position. The absence of a visible trail is surprising but not necessarily improbable. It could be the result of a combined effect of the observation conditions: from a moving vehicle, through a window, and during a very brief period. Furthermore, a perspective effect, linked to the entry angle of the bolide into the atmosphere and the witness's position, could explain this apparent absence. The trail might have been present but not discernible under these circumstances. Similarly, the observed change in direction could be only apparent and a result of the witness's vehicle changing direction.

GEIPAN classifies the case as A: bolide.
