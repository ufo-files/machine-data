Description: On December 27, 2015, between 5:05 and 5:15 PM, a driver was astonished to see in the sky a fluorescent orange object in the shape of a "upturned soup plate." The stationary object was surrounded by an orange halo. The object moved in a southerly direction, and no noise was heard during its movement. It disappeared over the horizon. Several drivers, stopped along the road, also reported observing the phenomenon. Only one testimony was recorded. The observation of an orange ball in the sky for several minutes on a weekend night strongly resembles a Thai lantern, likely released from Saint-Vallier after a private party during the end-of-year holidays. The observed movement was slight, heading towards the southwest. The wind at the Auribeau-sur-Siagne meteorological station, 12 km away, was zero (see the Infoclimat record for the observation date). At this time, in mountainous areas at sunset, there are very local winds, according to valleys, very light and hard to predict. This weather report does not provide decisive information to confirm the Thai lantern hypothesis. The lack of results on the photo is not surprising: mobile phone cameras are very poor in low light. Nevertheless, despite the lack of elements confirming this hypothesis, given the description of the phenomenon and the high frequency of lantern releases during the end-of-year period, the GEIPAN classifies this case as "B": probable observation of a Thai lantern.
