Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over the years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named LA BAULE (44) 1979 is part of a recent batch of cases reassessed. Two individuals (T1 and T2) observed, from their homes, a luminous phenomenon with a red-orange and yellow halo in the night sky above Pornichet. This very large phenomenon was initially perceived as stationary, then moved in the direction of Saint-Nazaire, with the halo gradually disappearing. No other testimony was collected despite announcements by the gendarmerie in the press and on the radio. Both witnesses very likely observed the Moon. The Moon (El 4.6°, Az 90.5°) is indeed very low over Pornichet, as seen from the tip of Penchateau. All descriptions are consistent with a misperception with the Moon rising at the horizon:

- Shapes: Clouds were present that night, a partial and variable (gap) presence of clouds in front of the Moon, which could make it appear in all sorts of shapes. The mention of a halo is also consistent with the effect of the Moon's light being scattered by fogs and clouds near the gap. T2 clearly distinguished a central shape, well-defined (the gap) within a halo. T2 reported, "Indeed, I observed for about a minute, an UFO in the shape of a dome, clearly detached from a red halo."

- Colors: Depending on the thickness of the fog or cloud, the color can indeed vary from red or "violent yellow."

- Size: The Moon with the halo surrounding it can indeed correspond to the width of Pornichet as seen from the tip of Penchateau (3 or 5 degrees), as mentioned by T2.

- Perception of movement: This comes towards the end of the observation, with the PAN described as stationary up to that point. T1 mentions movement towards Saint-Nazaire. This is the classic illusion produced by the decrease in the angular size and/or brightness of an PAN (here, the progressive closure of the cloudy gap over the Moon), which the witness very logically interprets as a distance increase. The geography of the location allows for describing a distance increase beyond Pornichet, as seen from the tip of Penchateau, as a movement towards Saint-Nazaire. T2, in fact, provides a very precise description of this apparent movement phenomenon: "When I saw this UFO move, it gradually moved at a fairly regular speed at first, then faster afterward, but always with the same progression. It didn't disappear suddenly. I always saw this halo decreasing as the UFO moved away."

Note T2's astonishment: "What struck me the most was that this halo didn't reflect either on the water or in the sky." T2 imagines the PAN over Pornichet, therefore just on the other side of the bay from the observation point. It is logical to seek a mirror effect (specular reflection) on the water at a midpoint between the PAN and T2. However, the Moon, like the clouds scattering its light, are much farther away. Reflection on water cannot be specular but only by diffusion effect, which is much less intense and depends on the water surface roughness (height and orientation of ripples) probably not strong enough that night. Consequently, the GEIPAN reclassifies the case as A: observation of the red Moon.
