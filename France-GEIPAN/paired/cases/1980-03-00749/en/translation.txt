Description: On February 17, 1980, around 8:45 PM, a witness observing the sky spots a very bright object that intrigues them. This round point is stationary, larger and yellower than the other stars. The observation lasted over 10 minutes without the point appearing to move. The gendarmerie was unable to find any additional witnesses to provide further information. However, local press reports that other witnesses in the area made the same observation. After verifying the sky map, we find that in the indicated direction, Venus is particularly visible (magnitude -4.01) at this time of year. Venus's altitude above the horizon is 8° instead of the 20° reported by the witness. Considering a possible margin of error in the object's altitude measurement, the favored hypothesis is an observation of Venus. In fact, many observation reports are recorded during this period.
