Summary: Sky observation and photography of a moving object; rapid and silent disappearance: hoax.
--- seg-d703a757c74fd3f3afca5438 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: A witness reports an observation made on August 20, 2011 at 20:15 from the terrace of their 4th-floor apartment. While looking towards Montjuzet (63), he observes a dark gray, oval, flat object in motion at cloud level. No particular noise is heard. The witness takes a photograph with a camera. The PAN disappears very quickly. The observation lasted 10 seconds. No further testimony will be collected. The witness's photograph is the key piece of evidence in their testimony, the one that bears all the strangeness (see the investigation report). The analysis of this photo reveals unusual elements leading to the conclusion that a very probable hoax is involved, at a minimum with the photo itself, and most likely with the entire observation. In fact, three hypotheses can be formulated: - the photo is truly authentic, the unusual effects are camera defects: the defects observed, in particular the 2° offset of the grid from vertical, are not conceivable defects for this type of camera, especially as these defects do not persist on subsequent photos. The shadow under the object is not compatible with the declared time of the observation. The technical parameters (ISO) of the PAN photo are too different from other photos of the same device showing a view of the sky. This hypothesis is rejected. - The photo is truly authentic, the unusual effects are a result of the camera's exposure to an unknown phenomenon: for this hypothesis, one would have to consider that electromagnetic perturbations are such that they add this grid effect to the photo, but without significantly disturbing the image itself. The type of radiation that could produce this effect is unimaginable. The shadow under the object is not compatible with the declared time of the observation. The technical parameters (ISO) of the PAN photo are too different from other photos of the same device showing a view of the sky. This hypothesis is rejected. - The witness did indeed observe a flying object in the sky, and then fabricated the photo to make his testimony more credible: in this case, the witness would have had to act quickly enough to find a photo that somewhat resembled his observation, set up the shooting procedure, and quickly provide the photo to the press. This hypothesis remains plausible, but greatly weakens the reliability of the visual observation, especially since the witness does not recognize the hoax when these arguments are presented to them. It's a hoax; the witness fabricated the observation scenario from a photo extracted from a video documentary. The observed defects are not compatible with an authentic photo of a cloudy sky; the grid effect can be very well explained if the witness photographed a document bearing this grid or if he photographed a video screen. The shadow under the object is not compatible with the declared time of the observation. The technical parameters (ISO) of the PAN photo are too different from other photos of the same device showing a view of the sky. The witness was unable to provide photos preceding and following the reference photo. A hoax typically involves multiple attempts to select the "best rendition." The failure to respond to the GEIPAN's request to provide these photos supports the hoax hypothesis, even though this point is less decisive than the previous ones. These elements give the hoax hypothesis a very high probability, leading to the conclusion that it is the only one to consider. As a result, the GEIPAN concludes in A: very high probability of hoax. The photo is a photo or screen shot depicting a PAN. Why this late publication? The GEIPAN (in 2011) manager informed the witness by phone of his conclusions suggesting a probable hoax. The witness assured that his photo was authentic. It was agreed at the time with the witness not to publish on the GEIPAN site the (unmodified by the exchange) conclusions of the GEIPAN out of consideration for the witness. In exchange, the witness was to refrain from publicly disclosing this observation by stating an absence of conclusion or a conclusion different from GEIPAN, which would harm the work of GEIPAN. That is what the witness did, however, in 2012 on a forum by stating that the GEIPAN found no explanation, but did not publish this finding of no explanation. The witness's attitude therefore leads to the publication of the GEIPAN's conclusions today.
