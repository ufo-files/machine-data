Summary: Observations of a spark spray preceded by a detonation. Detection of a hiss and a hum receding to the NE: lack of information and cross-checking.
--- seg-39d517435ca99f0b41865e48 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 27, 1954, returning from a night shift, two gendarmes riding bicycles at 5:15 a.m. discussed in loud voices a white patch seen in the grass on the right side of the road. They were then surprised to hear a detonation and immediately saw, on the left side of the road about 150 meters away, a spray of sparks 1 meter or 5 meters long at 1 meter or 1.5 meters above the ground, accompanied by a hissing and then a humming sound. The noise quickly moved off towards the northeast. No shape was seen by the gendarmes. They approached but found nothing but frightened cows mooing. The gendarmerie investigation at the time reported that the brigade commander went to the scene with one of the witnesses at 7:45 a.m., and the section commander did the same with the other witness at 1:30 p.m.; the two accounts from the two witnesses, independently obtained, "are in no way contradictory." At the scene, the gendarmes noted that the grass was flattened over an area of 1.6 by 1.3 meters, and a fence 3.5 meters from this location was torn over a length of 25 meters (four posts were damaged, two broken at ground level). Neither the pasture owner nor the gendarmes thought these damages were caused by livestock. The grass could have been knocked down by cows before or after the observation. It is not certain that the ground traces are related to the heard phenomenon. A detonation followed by a spray of sparks and a hissing sound obviously suggests the explosion of a pyrotechnic device. The observation took place in a geographical area traversed by various armies during the different wars. Could the auto-ignition of an abandoned military explosive have produced this kind of phenomenon without creating a crater? It is surprising that the gendarmes considered the ground traces of this phenomenon as the landing of an unidentified craft when they observed no shape. Was this idea prompted by the particularly intense media hype about flying saucers in October 1954? The GEIPAN classifies this case as C due to lack of information and cross-checking.
