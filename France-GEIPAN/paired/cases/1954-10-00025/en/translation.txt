Summary: Observations of a spark spray preceded by a detonation. Detection of a hiss and a hum receding to the NE: lack of information and cross-corroboration.
--- seg-39d517435ca99f0b41865e48 [needs-review] ---
Description: On October 27, 1954, returning from a night shift, two gendarmes riding bicycles with all lights off around 5:15 a.m. exchanged a few loud words about a white patch seen in the grass on the right side of the road. They were then surprised to hear a detonation and immediately see, on the left side of the road at about 150m, a spray of sparks 1m or 5m long at 1m or 1.5m from the ground accompanied by a hissing then a purring sound. The noise quickly moves away towards the North-East. No shape was seen by the gendarmes. They approach but find nothing more than frightened cows mooing. The gendarmerie investigation at the time reports that the brigade commander went to the scene with one of the witnesses at 7:45 a.m., and the section commander did the same with the other witness at 1:30 p.m.; the two accounts from the two witnesses, collected independently, "are in no way contradictory." At the scene, the gendarmes observe that the grass is flattened over an area of 1.6 by 1.3 meters, and a fence located 3.5 meters from this spot is torn over a length of 25 meters (4 posts are damaged, with 2 broken at ground level). Neither the pasture owner nor the gendarmes believe that these damages are caused by livestock. The grass may have been trampled by cows before or after the observation. It is not certain that the ground tracks are related to the phenomenon heard. A detonation followed by a spray of sparks and a hiss clearly suggests the explosion of a pyrotechnic device. The observation takes place in a geographical area traversed by various armies during the different wars. Could the self-ignition of an abandoned military explosive have produced this type of phenomenon without creating a crater? It is surprising that the gendarmes considered the ground traces of this phenomenon as the landing of an unidentified craft, when they observed no shape. Could this idea have been sparked by the particularly intense media frenzy about flying saucers in that October of 1954? The GEIPAN classifies this case as C due to a lack of information and overlap.
