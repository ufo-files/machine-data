Description: On March 9, 2019, at 01:50 in the morning, a driver on the A75 observed for 2 to 3 seconds a luminous phenomenon above Clermont-Ferrand. He clearly saw a relatively powerful orange halo. This halo had the diameter of a 1 euro coin held at arm's length, and it was in motion. It quickly rose in a circular motion to dissipate behind a cloud. The movement resembled an inverted comma moving from bottom to top, starting from the left. Observation angle: 50 degrees. Observation distance: approximately 2km. There was no trail. The halo did not change color and glowed constantly except when passing behind a cloud. The comma movement was very rapid, and the circular motion very tight. Only one testimony was collected. The consistency was relatively low given the brevity of the observation (see the investigation report). The testimony provided a good description of the PAN and its environment, but the phenomenon was very brief, and there was only one witness, reducing the reliability of the testimony. The globular lightning hypothesis is compatible with the characteristics of the PAN and the testimony, especially since the witness mentioned and recognized this phenomenon in a video about the subject. However, the weather conditions that night were not favorable for the appearance of such a phenomenon. This last information was confirmed by the Lightning Research Laboratory. The brevity of the observation and its context do not allow for enough information and elements to emit a satisfactory hypothesis. The strangeness remains average, and the consistency low. This case is classified as "C": lack of exploitable information.
