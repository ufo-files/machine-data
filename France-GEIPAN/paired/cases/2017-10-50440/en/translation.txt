Summary: Observation of the silent passage across the night sky of points regularly aligned on a curved line ahead of an opaque rounded shape: observation of a group of migratory birds, probably greylag geese.
--- seg-22be7d5329f3bfbe839ee1da [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On October 14, 2017, at 21:00, a witness looking up at the sky was intrigued by a dozen white points arranged regularly and forming a curved shape moving silently. Just behind these points, the witness distinguished an opaque rounded shape. Only one testimony was collected. Analysis (see the investigation report) revealed that the PAN observed by the witness were very likely a group of migratory birds, probably greylag geese, flying at high altitude. This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- Observation period compatible with the autumn migration period;
- Observation area geographically located in a migration corridor;
- Direction of movement of the PAN compatible with the direction usually taken by migratory birds at this time of year;
- Number of PAN compatible with the possible number of birds (with a gregarious behavior during migration) in a formation;
- Classic "V" or arc-shaped formation;
- Slow, regular, straight-line progression, according to estimated altitudes and speeds compatible with those known for greylag geese;
- Color of the individuals, a muted white, very dim, consistent with the visual rendering of the ventral white plumage of geese reflecting the residual ambient light.

Furthermore, we were able to show that the main strangeness of the case, namely the witness' perception at the center of the group of PAN of a "dark mass," was caused by a physiological phenomenon related to nocturnal observation: temporary visual disappearance of stars in central vision during observation of the PAN in peripheral vision.

The GEIPAN classified the case as "A": observation of a group of migratory birds, probably greylag geese.
