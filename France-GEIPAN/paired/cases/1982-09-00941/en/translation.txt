Summary: Nighttime observations of an orange-colored, saucer-shaped light phenomenon low on the horizon: misperceptions with the Moon close to sunrise.
--- seg-ce4203e29caca3bebe9ca3a5 [needs-review] ---
Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reexamined with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reexamination sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named PALAISEAU (91) 1982 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reexamined. On September 6, 1982, at 10:30 PM, a couple on their balcony on the third floor saw a bright, orange-colored phenomenon in the direction of Orly's runways. The witnesses compared the object to a saucer or a dirigible. The observation lasted two minutes during which the phenomenon gradually disappeared. No sound was heard during the observation. A gendarmerie investigation with various air surveillance services or fire stations in the area yielded no additional information. Because of its strange yet average consistency (two dependent witnesses, no photo), this case turns out to be a misperception with the Moon close to its rising (see the investigation report). The description by PAN and the details of the gendarmerie investigation strongly suggest a misperception with the Moon: PAN oval with a large diameter, orange color, bright light, with the hypothesis of a fire. The witnesses, although having an excellent view from their balcony towards the East, said they did not see the Moon in the clear sky they described, while it was indeed present in the direction they indicated, at a low angular height that could explain its orange color. The fact that a plane passed between PAN and the witnesses shows that PAN was slightly to the right of Orly Airport's runways, which perfectly corresponds to the Moon's position. The presence of clouds on the eastern horizon, not mentioned by the witnesses but inferred from meteorological data, explains (for T2) the disappearance of PAN by shrinking due to the gradual obscuration of a cloud, as it explains (for T1) "an eastward departure," because T1 interprets the shrinking as a distance escape that is indeed towards the East. During the first investigation, GEIPAN did not have the same means to assess the presence of clouds and had not yet gained experience with the illusions of movement that clouds can create in front of the Moon or a star. The case is classified as A: observation of the Moon.
