Summary: Observation of the movement of a white light: probable observation of an aircraft.
--- seg-5e5eabdc3d1750343a015bcf [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: A witness reports their observation made on May 25, 2011 at 2:09 AM: through a window, the witness observes a strong, white light to the North. The phenomenon follows a straight trajectory heading South before turning towards East-South-East. The observation lasts for five seconds before the light suddenly disappears. No noise was heard during the passage of the phenomenon. The favored hypothesis is the face-on observation of an airplane coming from the North, preparing to land in Orléans or Orly, with its landing lights on. When the observer faces the airplane, it appears as a nearly immobile point (one cannot see the flashing lights because the forward beacon blinds), from which one cannot estimate the distance. These landing lights are visible over 10km in their axis. If the airplane changes its course, the bright point moves laterally, changes in brightness, and then appears to disappear because the observer is no longer in the axis of the beacons. If the airplane is not too far away, one can then see the flashing lights. The phenomenon must recur from time to time when weather conditions are similar. The absence of noise is not surprising if one assumes that the witness's distance estimation is incorrect, an estimation impossible to make at night from a bright point. This case is classified "B": probable observation of an airplane.
