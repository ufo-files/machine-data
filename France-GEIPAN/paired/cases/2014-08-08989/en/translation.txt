Description: The witness was waiting for the fireworks when she observed 7 bright points moving in a cross formation. The phenomenon disappears into the clouds. The observation of bright points crossing the sky in formation in less than a minute, on a festive night, clearly suggests Thai lanterns or wind-carried glowing balloons, likely released nearby as part of the local celebration; one might even think of simple inflated rubber ducks or mylar balloons illuminated only by the lights of the small town. The movement is observed towards the southwest (direction of the ocean). The wind in Biarritz (37 km South) is light and from the South: weather. The wind at Biscarrosse (70 km North) is from the North West meteorological sector. The local wind in Vieux Boucou proves to be quite difficult to determine. In the summer evenings, the local wind on the east coast is more dependent on marine or thermal breezes than on the general wind pattern, if the winds are light; therefore, it is not possible to verify the compliance of the movement of the bright points with the wind. The fact that, despite the large number of potential witnesses, there was only one testimony indicates that the phenomenon was not considered strange by a large part of the present population. The GEIPAN classifies this case as a "B" observation, likely Thai lanterns or balloons.
