Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D and named COULOMMIERS (77) 12.05.1992 is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On May 12, 1992, around 10:30 PM, a witness from the first floor of his home observed "a yellow bright spot" in the sky. Joined by a second witness (T2), they observed the PAN, which seemed to stretch out and slowly followed a rising trajectory. In a final phase, the PAN changed direction, leaving behind a yellow trail. No noise was heard during the observation. The PAN gradually moved away. These two testimonies are recorded at the gendarmerie, which conducts an investigation (see PV). T1 reports at the beginning of the hearing protocol two other previous observations. The PAN observed by the two witnesses resembles a simple aircraft, observed in a first phase face-on, landing lights on, appearing stationary or moving slowly as it was directed towards the witnesses; then, in a second and third phase, having made a turn while gaining altitude until it encountered an atmospheric layer more conducive to the formation of a condensation trail, and appearing to move quickly by "brutally accelerating" after its turn, as it was now observed more transversally (see the investigation report). This condensation trail even started to be visible, probably, at the end of the first phase, as very short, but as the witnesses could not distinguish the plane from its trail, they initially considered it as a single object "stretching out," and then, when the trail stretches further after the turn, they distinguished the two. This plane could have taken off in a VFRn (night flight) from the Auxerre-Branches airport and headed north, facing the witnesses, before making a turn towards northeast, towards its final destination. The appearance of the PAN described by the witnesses in phase 1 ("yellow bright spot" shining with a "very strong glow") is entirely compatible with this hypothesis, which they only reject due to the absence of perceived noise. However, at a great distance and depending on the wind (carrier or not), the noise of an aircraft can very well not be perceived. In the second phase, the description given by the witnesses ("very long bright trail of the same color as him") perfectly matches the observation of a condensation trail produced by this plane, which was at that moment at an altitude conducive to the formation of such a trail. As the observation took place at dusk, we attempted to determine if it was possible that this trail was still illuminated by the sun, already at 10° below the horizon at the time of the observation. Unfortunately, too many uncertainties about the existing data and sensitive variables to measurements do not allow for a certain conclusion on this point. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as B: probable observation of an aircraft.
