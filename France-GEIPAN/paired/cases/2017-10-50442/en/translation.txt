Description: On Tuesday, October 3, 2017, between 2:00 p.m. and 2:15 p.m., a witness operates a drone near a railroad track. Suddenly, the witness sees a "flash" of light. Upon returning home, the witness downloads the images captured by his drone and then watches two furtive "light flashes" on the drone's recording. Only one testimony was collected. Two "flash" observations were made and recorded on the drone's video, and a "light flash" was also directly perceived by the witness. In conclusion, and after a quick analysis of the video, the PAN filmed by the witness's drone could be nothing more than small debris carried by the wind and passing in close proximity to the camera. Furthermore, the witness's perception from the ground of a very intense light flash is most certainly not related to the two objects visible on the video, but would also be a ground reflection. A classification as "B," probable confusion with small debris carried by the wind and passing near the camera, could be considered, but the lack of exploitable data, due to the very fleeting nature of the PAN visible in only one image of the video, does not allow for any other classification than "C."
