Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archive on its public website www.geipan.fr. Among its publications are old cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being reassessed with the sole purpose of being more relevant in their conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over many years, this reassessment sometimes leads to new observations or a change in classification. This observation case previously classified as D is part of a recent set of cases that have been reassessed. On October 14, 1981, between 2:30 and 2:45 PM, two witnesses observed the silent movement in the sky of an object with a cylindrical shape for one minute. No wings or ailerons were noticed. Of moderate strangeness and good consistency (two dependent witnesses), this case appears to be a probable misidentification with an airplane (as detailed in the investigation report). Several details in the testimonies strongly suggest the hypothesis of a misidentification with an airplane, particularly the appearance of a fuselage without wings, well-known today to the GEIPAN: the geometry between the witness, the airplane, and the sun causes the sun's rays to be reflected only on the fuselage and not on the wings (which are either masked or have a limited reflecting surface). An air corridor leading to Marseille Airport passes just north of the observation location, with an orientation perfectly consistent with the trajectory of the PAN. The strangeness of the case mainly rests on the fact that the witnesses did not see any wings or ailerons on the PAN, and that it was estimated to be close to the witnesses. However, the witnesses, a classic error when an object or phenomenon is not recognized, were unable to accurately estimate the actual distance between them and the PAN. The hypothesis of a misidentification with a weather balloon can also be advanced based on the testimonies, but it turns out that local radiosonde results show that the winds were contrary to the direction of movement of the PAN. The case is classified as B, probable misidentification with an airplane.
