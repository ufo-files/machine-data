Summary: Observation of the rapid and silent passage of a luminous phenomenon of color composed of 5 lights in V: probable misperception with a fragmented meteor.
--- seg-24625dd1bd6b8b9b1321b9f4 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On August 25, 2016, a family observed the starry sky with a telescope. At 10:30 PM, they observed with the naked eye the very quick passage of a phenomenon composed of 5 lights of a yellow-orange color trending towards reddish. No particular noise was heard during the observation, which lasted less than 5 seconds. The only testimony collected reported that during its passage, the PAN occluded the stars. Of average strangeness and good consistency (multiple witnesses, but a unique and fairly precise testimony), the case appears to be a probable misidentification with a fragmented meteor (see the investigation report). The description provided by the witness can evoke several explanatory hypotheses:

• Thai lanterns: The shape of the PAN and the fact that they do not move in the direction of the wind eliminate the first hypothesis;

• A group of birds illuminated by streetlights could potentially explain the observation, but the very high speed of the PAN and the fact that they follow each other in a line present inconsistencies with this explanatory hypothesis;

• That of a fragmented meteor, too weak to be detected by the FRIPON network, can instead explain all elements of the observation: shape of the PAN, very quick movement in a line, color, and the fact that the stars were occluded. The case is classified B, probable misidentification with a fragmented meteor.
