Summary: Observation of the passage of a luminous phenomenon: observation of the passage of a meteor caused by the atmospheric reentry of a natural meteorite that fragments.
--- seg-be86e2925826e3667627c5a8 [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On March 15, 2015, around 8:45 PM, numerous people were able to observe the passage in the sky of a luminous phenomenon of color. The GEIPAN has received several witness accounts, including one from a pilot. This phenomenon observed by hundreds of people in Northeast France and especially in Germany and Switzerland, is clearly a meteor. A BOLIDE is a phenomenon corresponding to the entry into the atmosphere of a "large" object (over 100 g, and from a few centimeters to 1 meter in diameter). This produces a large trail of light capable of illuminating the landscape for several seconds. This bolide was also recorded by several cameras (see, among others, BOAM), which allows to perfectly characterize its trajectory, and therefore its natural origin: it is a meteoroid, a small rocky body that crossed the Earth's orbit. Determining the size of this object at the entrance is very difficult, between 100g and 10kg. This bolide had the particularity of being observable for almost 10 seconds due to its relatively low trajectory. The meteor's trajectory went from northeast to southeast, approximately vertical to Stuttgart to Zurich. The GEIPAN received 5 ground witness testimonies and two in-flight pilot testimonies (see table and geographic maps). It should be noted that one of the pilots, flying towards the North, states that the phenomenon 'scared' the plane, while it was more than 200 km away. This proximity illusion is a constant in all meteor witness accounts, it does not spare pilots; moreover, at high altitude, the atmospheric veil is nonexistent, making it even more impossible to estimate the distance of a powerful, unknown light phenomenon. An excellent analysis of all observations of this event, compiled by the American Meteor Association, is available at: U-Sphere. The GEIPAN classifies this case as 'A': phenomenon perfectly identified as a meteor, caused by the fall of a natural meteoroid.
