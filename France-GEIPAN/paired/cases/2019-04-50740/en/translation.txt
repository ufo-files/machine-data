Summary: Brief observation during a car trip of a PAN "enormous" and stationary object near the top of trees at approximately 7km: observation of a commercial aircraft.
--- seg-e262129ff14c4d2d72e22420 [needs-review] ---
Description: On April 19, 2019, in early afternoon, the driver of a car carrying 4 other passengers, was driving at approximately 70/80 km/h on D217 towards MOLLKIRCH (67). To the right of the road there is a forest and to the left a large open meadow. While driving, the driver observed for several seconds a "flying stationary object" in the clear sky. This PAN was seen "as a plane from behind" quite close to the top of the trees, beyond the meadow on the left, at about 7 kilometers in the direction of ROSENWILLER (67). The PAN was very quickly hidden by vegetation. Only one testimony was collected. The testimony is of average consistency: several witnesses, but a unique testimony, no photos or videos. This case appears to be the probable observation of a commercial airliner (see the investigation report). A check on Flightradar24 shows that two planes flew over the observation location at low altitude, at times that could correspond to the observation. However, only the TVS3443 flight, a Boeing 737, landing at Strasbourg Airport, has the characteristics of the PAN: passing a few kilometers to the southeast and a low angular height relative to the landscape. The perceived oddity is due to the size of the PAN ("the object was enormous, the size of a typical B777 airliner") and its apparent immobility. In fact, a Boeing 737 is shorter than the B777 cited as a reference by the witness, but size estimation depends heavily on associated distance estimation, so it can be considered that the B737 remains compatible with the mention "enormous object type B777" made by the witness. The immobility of the PAN can be explained by a pivot effect, relative to the landscape serving as a reference, as the witnesses were driving. Additionally, the plane's speed was reduced, as it was in the landing phase (its speed was thus approximately 150 knots, or 278 km/h approximately) and the observation was made over a relatively short time (30 seconds). The case is classified A, observation of a commercial airliner.
