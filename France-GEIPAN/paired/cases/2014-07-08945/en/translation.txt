Summary: Multiple reports of luminous phenomena in the night sky: likely observations of an atmospheric reentry (shooting star), planes, and satellites.
--- seg-2f7d4d6b559c637c07465fa9 [needs-review] ---
Description: Between July 9, 2014, and July 11, 2014, a witness is intrigued by several observations of light phenomena in the sky. First observation on July 9 at 23:54: The witness describes a glowing sphere crossing the sky in less than a minute, coming from the southwest. Between 11:45 PM and 12:00 AM, a patrol of two gendarmes, located between Le Pouliguen and Guérande, observed for 2 to 3 seconds a "phenomenon similar to a shooting star" crossing the sky from south to north, eventually splitting into two parts before fading away. The description from all witnesses suggests a meteoroid (entering the Earth's atmosphere, an event also known as a meteorite fall or meteor, similar to a very large shooting star, lasting longer and brighter). The BOAM site (see attached document) does not mention any recorded events of this type, but systematic coverage across France of this type of phenomenon is not guaranteed. One could also think of an atmospheric reentry of a space debris, but these phenomena usually last at least one minute. In addition, no significant reports are listed: see: Aerospace. The Space-Track site (see attached document) indicates several reentries of small debris on very inclined orbits that day, which might possibly correspond to the observation (further detailed study to be pursued). If airplane pilots observed the same phenomenon, they would likely have found it banal. In any case, the GEIPAN has not received any reports. For this first observation, given that it is very likely that the witness and the gendarmes saw the same phenomenon, the GEIPAN favors the hypothesis of a meteoroid's atmospheric reentry. Second observation at 0:15: A blinking white light point advancing in a straight line through the sky clearly resembles a high-altitude aircraft: these very bright flash lights remain the only ones visible from a distance: the position lights, quite weak, are not visible, and the eye is blinded by the flash lights. The GEIPAN received the observation report more than a month after the events, which did not allow it to carry out checks with air traffic control. Third observation: July 10, 23:18: The witness describes a very powerful, progressive light clearance lasting less than 2 seconds. This description clearly evokes a satellite flash or flare, which reflects sunlight like a flat mirror. The satellite's rotation induces this progressive and temporary glow. Many satellites can produce this effect, in particular Iridium satellites. The Calsky site (see attached document) lists several satellites passing overhead of Pouliguen around 23:08: Helios 1A Rocket, Lacrosse 5. We also note an Iridium flare at 23:23. Observation at 11:15 PM: This observation of a fast-moving bright sphere between clouds could also correspond to the observation of a satellite, many of which were present at that time. Observation at 23:55: This observation of a bright sphere from West to East could also correspond to the observation of a satellite (Echostar 16 may be the source, see the Calsky log). The apparent disappearance of the phenomenon corresponds to the satellite entering the Earth's shadow while moving towards the East. Observation at 00:02: White light with unequal flickering, from southeast to northwest. The time and trajectory correspond approximately to the satellite Yaogan 12 (see Calsky log). But the witness's description could also correspond to an airplane. Observation at 12:13 AM: The movement of the light phenomenon is described in the witness's textual report as from East to West, which would be incompatible with that of a satellite. But on the drawing, it is noted "Northwest," making it compatible with the passage of the 3rd stage of the SJ 11-03 Rocket. 00:32 observation: Two bright, lightning-like streaks and a glowing sphere to the west: This could well correspond to two shooting stars, and the large satellite TERRA (see Calsky log).

00:36 observation: Flash vertical: Unexplained. Observation at 0:45: 3 flashes with 5 or 6-second intervals: possible reflections on Cosmos 1300 Rocket. In conclusion, almost all of these observations, which are ultimately not that strange, can be explained by various phenomena that can be observed at night: meteors, planes, and satellites. This very observant witness had the patience and precision to note everything down and share it. The inevitable coincidences between the numerous planes and satellites added to the strangeness of the spectacle. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "B", probable observations of bolides, planes, and satellites.
