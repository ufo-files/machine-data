Description: On July 7, 2018, at 11:39 PM, a witness observes and films the silent movement of a round, glowing object of orange color. The PAN disappears behind a cloud after flashing several times. The witness considers several hypotheses but remains intrigued. Only one testimony was collected on this phenomenon. During our investigation, we were able to show that the PAN observed by the witness was in every respect consistent with the explored hypothesis, namely the confusion with a Thai lantern carried by the wind and launched that Saturday evening, possibly for a celebration (see the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements: - spherical shape corresponding to the center of the lantern illuminated and observed over a certain distance,
- yellow-orange color with a brighter halo around the perimeter (corresponding to the lantern envelope illuminated by the flame),
- silent movement,
- brief observation duration, two minutes. A lantern can remain lit for over ten minutes,
- disappearance by extinction (clearly visible on the video), typical of this type of confusion; the witness states on this point: "the object disappeared after flashing 3 times". In the end of life, the flame of a lantern, due to lack of fuel, will flicker and diminish before completely going out,
- favorable period for lantern releases: summer and a Saturday evening, is a propitious time for the organization of festivities that could involve such releases,
- the observation distance was greatly overestimated by the witness. In the context of the hypothesis, it could be verified in two different ways: using the IPACO software on the photograph and through the angular data provided by the witness. These methods yield the same result, namely a distance between approximately 150 and 200 meters, perfectly compatible with the hypothesis. We were finally able to explain the two elements that formed the strangeness for the witness, namely:
- the absence of wind, according to the witness, and the too high speed of the PAN. The felt absence of wind is explained by the witness's position, sheltered on his terrace. The "too high" speed of the PAN is only a subjective impression, caused by the witness's idea that the PAN is much farther away than it actually is.
- the change in trajectory of the PAN, which the witness states is curved. This curve is only apparent; indeed, unable to accurately assess the distance to the lantern, the witness naturally interprets its initial vertical rise into the sky as a movement towards him. Subsequently, once an air current with a little more wind is encountered, the lantern follows its direction, towards the South, progressively, by following a curve.
Classification as "A": Confusion with a Thai lantern.
