Description: The GEIPAN continues to publish its entire archives on its public website www.geipan.fr. In its publications, it includes older cases classified at the time (A, B, C, or D) that are now being re-examined, with the sole purpose of being more relevant in the conclusions. Thanks to new technical means (software) and the investigative experience gained over all these past years, this re-examination sometimes leads to new observations or even a change in classification. This previously classified D case and named NOGENT-LE-ROTROU (28) 1980 is part of a set of cases recently re-examined (see the investigation report). On March 10, 1980, around 9:30 PM, gendarmes of a brigade heard on their radio, on the frequency of another brigade, that a driver traveling on D13 between Les Etilleux (28) and Nogent-le-Rotrou (28) had spotted a PAN. Several gendarmes scan the sky in the indicated direction but make no observations. At 10:10 PM, a gendarme (T1) closing the shutters of his room at the Authon-du-Perche (28) barracks, along with his wife, spots a glowing ball in the clear sky heading towards Les Etilleux (28). The gendarme calls upon his superior (T2) and the other gendarmes: all observe the movements of the PAN in the form of a ball or disk (change in color from bright orange to red and movement by successive jumps and stops) until its disappearance (the exact time is not indicated in the report). At 22:40, four gendarmes (T3, T1, T4, T5) leave in a car towards Les Etilleux (28) on the D9, then towards Coudray-au-Perche (28) before returning to Authon-du-Perche (28). During their patrol, the gendarmes notice a dark red glowing mass (PV). Then, during the stop at the location known as "Moulin Neuf" (in the commune of Coudray-au-Perche), the gendarmes again observe a phenomenon described as a stationary, silent, oval-shaped luminous object at low altitude. The report states that the object was composed of two parts: a lower first third with dazzling lights and a upper second third with a predominately red-orange glow and red dots scattered along a horizontal line. Around the object, a glow formed a "aura". No other testimony was collected in the localities crossed. We have an investigation report from the Gendarmerie's preliminary inquiry (without individual statements from the 5 witnesses) and a private investigation report from the Cosmographic Research Group (GRC) in Le Mans, dated December 10, 1980. This report (attached document) provides, among other things, the transcription of the recording of each testimony, as well as drawings of the PAN observed. The GEIPAN not being able to master the conditions of the private investigation for the collection of testimonies, this private report is cited for information and to illustrate the differences in the collection of testimonies according to the conditions. It turns out that the conclusions of the GEIPAN can be established solely on the basis of the statement (PV) without the private report providing decisive confirming or denying elements. Without further information, we will not consider the initial observation of an automobilist driving on CD13 between Les Etilleux (28) and Nogent-le-Rotrou (28), which occurred around 21:30 and was reported to the gendarmes via radio, with no additional details. This observation case is complex and difficult to exploit: - Complex because it involves the observation by five gendarmes of several phenomena very likely distinct, which they were unable to recognize or identify. - Difficult to exploit because the consistency is poor: 1/ Regarding the procès-verbal that should be the key and decisive piece of evidence in the absence of other testimony collections conducted directly by the GEIPAN. The weakness of the information is noted, with an aggregation without distinction of origins appropriate to each of the witnesses not allowing the degree of convergence and divergence to be assessed. 2/ Furthermore, the confused or contradictory nature of certain information in the private investigation report of GRC, particularly regarding exchanges with the investigator, would not be of a nature to modify the assessment of consistency. Three distinct phases of observation are identified, each well-defined, concerning different phenomena:

- A first phase, observed from the Gendarmerie, which was determined to involve the observation of the planet Venus, which was perfectly visible that evening as the witnesses reported seeing stars in the direction of observation. Venus is setting, low on the horizon, and the particularly unfavorable conditions allow for the misidentification to be explained. In fact, in addition to the effects of atmospheric turbulence, the confusion with Venus was accompanied by a group autocinetic effect, for the naked-eye observation, and various optical defects related to witness T2 (astigmatism), to the equipment used (optical aberrations), and to its use (focusing problems) for the binocular observation. For this phase, compared to the previous information, the only available details in the report, "a glowing ball ten times larger than the stars appearing in a sky clear of cloud masses," attest to the general appearance of the PAN and the visibility of stars in the direction of observation, without the witnesses mentioning the presence of Venus, which is distinctly visible, are sufficient to establish the hypothesis of a mistake with Venus. The private investigation conducted under unknown conditions of GEIPAN yields nothing to either support or refute this hypothesis. As a result, the GEIPAN concludes for this first phase: A, very probable observation of VENUS. - A second phase, having taken place on a portion of the path traveled by the witnesses in their car, concerning the observation of three orange-red glowing points. Very little data regarding this intermediate phase is available, either in the PV (police report) or in the private investigation report. In particular, we are missing basic data such as observation azimuths and heights, angular dimensions of the PAN and the space separating them, etc... Consequently, the GEIPAN concludes for this second phase: C, lack of reliable data. - A third phase, having taken place with the vehicle stopped, on the return trip to the Gendarmerie. It concerns the observation of a PAN by four witnesses, who give different descriptions of it. This phase could not be studied for the following reasons: -- absence of exploitable data in the report, in particular, the existing data presented in the report are structured to suggest that all witnesses observed and described the phenomenon exactly the same way, when in fact it is a compilation made by one of the gendarmes for all witnesses, which absolutely does not allow us to see the degree of divergence or convergence among all the witnesses, while the GEIPAN aims to gather individual testimonies from each witness. In this case, this aggregation is not favorable to the consistency of the testimony. Moreover, the fact that the private investigation (GRC) reports contradictory elements between witnesses and confused testimonies supports this. As a result, the GEIPAN concludes for this third phase: C, lack of reliable data.
