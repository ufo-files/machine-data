Description: On August 25, 2019, in the morning, a witness was surprised to see in the clear sky the silent movement of an object shaped like a "wavy tube" that was black with twinkling lights. The witness went to retrieve his nearby mobile phone and, hindered by the sun, lost sight of the PAN. But he found it again in the sky and took photographs. The PAN moved upward until it became just a dark point. Only one testimony was collected. The witness very likely observed a partially deflated toy solar balloon, moving along a regular upward trajectory while being carried by the local light breeze toward the west (as per the investigation report). This conclusion is based on the following elements:

- The elongated shape is consistent with that of cylindrical solar balloons (in the form of a "tube," as described by the witness) of the "sausage" type. This type of balloon can be partially inflated while still flying. This visually manifests as a distortion of the structure, which no longer retains its rigid "sausage" form (the witness describes it as having "undulations"), but bends on itself.

- The color, black, is commonly used for solar balloons, as it absorbs solar radiation the best. However, the material used (polyethylene) does have some reflectivity, which can thus reflect solar light at times (the witness mentions twinkling lights here and there).

- The dimensions and distances measured and calculated from the testimony and photographs are entirely consistent with those of such a balloon carried by the local light breeze of 7-8 km/h. It was initially at a distance of about 880 meters from the witness and at a distance of about 2260 meters at the end of the observation. The calculated possible dimensions range between 3.9 meters and 5.8 meters.

- The conditions for using such a solar balloon are optimal: no or little wind, ideal temperature, not too high, clear sky.

- The balloon moves in the direction of the dominant wind, generally toward the west, while gradually gaining altitude. It is late summer, school vacation time, and it is possible that such a balloon was released by a child from the airfield installations, located not far from the initial observation azimuth.

The GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": confusion with a partially deflated toy solar balloon.
