Summary: Observation of a static black ring in the sky: certain observation of a smoke ring phenomenon created by pyrotechnic tests conducted on the day of the observation at the Valbonne military camp.
--- seg-bb60ce7b3d8260a8524b004d [needs-review] ---
Description: On April 14, 2016, a witness, a farmer by profession, left his home with his father to perform agricultural work on his land near the hamlet of Proulieu (01). The observation began at 09:50 as the witnesses moved in their vehicle, at approximately 30 km/h, along a dirt road bordering the former Blyes road (01). The weather was clear with a bit of wind and a temperature of 16°. The witness's father was the first to spot the PAN, which he showed to his son. The PAN appeared as a static black ring, located, according to the witnesses, above a forest at a distance estimated to be around 500 meters. The witness believed it was located in the southwest, towards the commune of Saint-Vulbas (01). He quickly grabbed his mobile phone to film and photograph the PAN from the lowered passenger-side window, while continuing to drive on the dirt road, for about 2 to 2 minutes 30 seconds. The PAN eventually disappeared, dissipating "like a small cloud." From the Gendarmerie investigation, it emerged that "this event was the subject of an article in the edition of the newspaper Le Progrès, edition la Côtière, on Sunday, April 18, 2016. It emerges [from this publication] that other witnesses also saw the event." They also noted that on the day of the observation, "a civil society had conducted explosive tests during the day at the Valbonne military camp." Only one testimony was collected. There is little doubt that the witnesses observed a smoke ring produced by the use of pyrotechnic devices during explosive tests at the Valbonne military camp (see the investigation report). Since the location of these tests was quite distant from the witnesses' position, they likely did not hear the sound of the detonations that created this black ring, or heard it later without making the connection. Finally, it is worth noting that the weather conditions were favorable for maintaining the structure of the ring for several minutes, as the wind was very light at the time of its formation. The strangeness of this case is low and is mainly due to the unusual appearance of the PAN, which the witnesses were unable to associate with a known phenomenon to them, probably having never observed it before. The testimony is consistent, with sufficient data provided by the witness and exploitable, and the Gendarmerie protocol is complete and well-informed. There is also a video and six photographs that were used for the case analysis. This case is to be classified as "A," as a certain observation of a smoke ring phenomenon created by pyrotechnic tests conducted on the day of the observation at the Valbonne military camp.
