Description: On August 21, 2014, at 12:43, a witness aboard an airplane en route to Lisbon was intrigued by a PAN at the same altitude as the aircraft. The witness observed, through the window, what they thought was a bird or a drone. As the object drew closer to the witness's field of vision, they described it as "a small black box next to the head of the plane and trails shaped like the fins of a Japanese fish..." "The trails of what I thought was its propulsion were not horizontal, but falling..." The witness then considered taking photos with their cell phone. Only one photo contains the PAN. Only one testimony was collected, along with photos and sketches of the PAN and the scene. The witness very likely observed the trail of a commercial airliner that appears on the traffic report and which had just crossed (from left to right and 900 meters below) the trajectory of the witness's plane (as detailed in the investigation report). The conditions favored a black trail (the sun was behind the witness, the background of the trail was relatively bright) and the black color of one of the clouds present in the photo results from the same conditions and validates them. The relative motion of the two planes allowed for a precise reconstruction of the evolution of the position and orientation of the trajectory (and thus the trail) as seen by the witness. However, this reconstruction is only consistent 52 seconds after the photo was taken. This reconstruction error could come from a combination of a poorly set phone time (radio control disabled or disturbed) and imprecision in the Planefinder site report used for the reconstruction (for example, incremental location by too wide an interval of time and not synchronized between planes, introducing imprecision in the respective caps and positions). If the PAN was not that airplane, it would be highly unlikely that, within a few dozen seconds, a spatial configuration would produce the same perspective between the two planes as between the witness's plane and a PAN. Similarly, it would be difficult to explain why the airplane and the PAN are not simultaneously visible on the photo, or why the already alerted witness would not have reacted 52 seconds later, when the airplane "replayed" the same scenario as the PAN. The testimony is very consistent, with enough data provided by the witness and exploitable. There is also a photograph that has been analyzed. Consequently, the GEIPAN classifies the case as "A": observation of the trail of a commercial airliner.
