Description: On March 14, 2020, at 10:34 PM, the witness was watching television sitting on his couch. From there, he noticed a bright, glowing point in the sky. The PAN was round and orange-colored. The witness observed it for a long time, decided to record a video using his smartphone, then left his observation post to wake up his daughter and returned with her to observe the light in the sky. Unfortunately, the PAN had disappeared. The 27-second video was of poor quality due to the device, but upon frame-by-frame viewing, light intensity variations due to air transparency and strong zoom can be observed. Frame-by-frame, there was no visible movement that could have led to a non-astronomical misperception. The initial observation lasted a total of seven minutes. The case is consistent due to the video, which is useful for study. The witness saw the same PAN several times, with a timing shift, coming to rest to the right of its position. The misperception with Sirius is certain. The telephone interview clarifies any ambiguity. The characteristics are now well-known, and the witness's phone report provides valuable evidence to support the misperception. The witness had the excellent reflex to film the scene. The investigation corrected the azimuth error, and the witness confirms this point. He saw the same thing several times in the same place, demonstrating a misperception with the star Sirius. There are no strong indications of strangeness after analysis. The Geipan classifies the case as A: misperception with Sirius.
