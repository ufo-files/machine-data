Summary: Observation in the night sky of the presence of 3 PAN bright objects in a slow movement: observation of celestial lanterns carried by the wind, also known as Thai lanterns.
--- seg-cfef63649162fd50c4c532ef [machine-unreviewed] ---
Description: On December 19, 2025, at 6:05 PM, while walking his dog in the commune of Samoreau (77), a single witness observed three perfectly aligned "yellow" glowing orbs in the sky, which he perceived as "static" or "moving slightly." The witness observed the phenomenon for 5 minutes during which he took two videos and a photo, then resumed his walk, ending his observation. Only one testimony was collected. The case appears of good consistency* with a complete testimony, supplemented by a cognitive interview providing two videos that were exploitable for the investigation. The witness's sense of strangeness is explained by the absence of immediate identification of the phenomenon, the absolute silence of the object, and its very low rate of motion. Checks conducted during the investigation indicate a weak northwest wind (6 to 7 km/h). This corresponds to the north component of the north-south motion of the PAN deduced from video analysis, the south component being related to a slow ascent of the object. It is therefore a wind-driven object. (see annex). The "spherical" shapes and "yellow" color correspond to characteristics of sky lanterns, which were likely released from a nearby location. It is noted that "La Maison des Cultures de Thomery," the neighboring village, had released several lanterns in September 2024 for the inauguration of the priory. This organization, contacted by the investigator, however, denied having conducted a release on the day of the observation. The lantern hypothesis thus reduces the strangeness perceived by the witness. The GEIPAN classifies this case as "A," observation of wind-driven sky lanterns.

*CONSISTANCE: According to the criteria of the GEIPAN, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony.
