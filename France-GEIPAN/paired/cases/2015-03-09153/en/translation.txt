Summary: Observation of two thin streaks around a glowing disk: observation of a lunar halo (paraselene).
--- seg-8a28b6ca78e1563d18e4e458 [needs-review] ---
Description: On March 4, 2015, around 8:15 PM, a witness observes a circular, pale white object in the sky that is as big and bright as the moon. The object is above the trees, surrounded by two thin, glowing lines. The object moves slowly, then takes on an elongated shape followed by two red lights and a white light before disappearing from the witness's view, leaving them feeling concerned. Only one testimony was collected. The GEIPAN contacted the witness to clarify certain points of the testimony: the witness was observing in the northeast direction and had indeed noticed the Moon to their right. They accurately described two thin lines around the glowing disk. This description closely matches the atmospheric optical phenomenon known as a paraselene, located at 22° and/or 46° from the Moon, at an elevation near 46°. This phenomenon is an image of the Moon created by reflection off ice crystals in high-altitude cold clouds (cirrus). See the Wikipedia article on paraselenes. The weather report for the day is available on Météociel. The GEIPAN classifies this case as A: lunar halo.
