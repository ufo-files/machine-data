Description: The witness photo shows a characteristic airplane trail in the shape of a horseshoe. This particular shape is caused by ejections that diverge from the aircraft's fuselage before converging in its wake. The aircraft's direction away from the witness (rather than across its path) allows for a prolonged separate view of the two trails. The uniqueness of the trace in this witness case is due to the lighting conditions. These trails are often bright because they are directly illuminated by the sun. In this instance, the trail is not directly illuminated by the sun (due to the earth's shadow) but the background in the witness's line of sight consists of a high part of the sky still illuminated by the sun, allowing the trail to stand out against a brighter background. Indeed, we can clearly see increasing brightness towards the top in the witness's photo. Flightradar shows several possible aircraft in the direction indicated by the witnesses (southeast from Ancinnes near Alençon) and at the time indicated (after 18:10 local time, 17:10 UTC). One track very likely corresponds to a PAN because the aircraft is seen by the witness from "below and behind," which gives the upward orientation of the horseshoe-shaped trail. Furthermore, this track is within the area no longer illuminated by the sun. The attached "reconstruction" document provides further details on these explanations. The witnesses indicate a duration of approximately 10 minutes. This is inconsistent with an airplane trail that moves significantly in the sky over such a period. The observation was shorter, and overestimating the duration is a common error. Moreover, the two witness photos, taken one minute apart, do not show the same trail. Consequently, GEIPAN classifies the case as A: Airplane trail observation.
