**Description:** On February 6, 2024, at 12:12, a witness observed from her living room in Paris (75) "a small black dot" on the horizon, darker than the airplanes or helicopters she is accustomed to seeing pass. This point moved "from the south" along a "straight" trajectory and appeared to be "quite high" in the sky. Intrigued by the object, she rushed to open her apartment window and took photos as well as a short video. The phenomenon then flew over the building, and she quickly lost track of it. The observation lasted approximately 1 minute and 30 seconds. The case consistency is deemed good. Despite a single witness, she was able to take photos and a video of the PAN, which were provided but not original. They were examined for the investigation (see the investigation report).

*According to the GEIPAN criteria, consistency is the amount of reliable and objective information gathered for a testimony. It was shown that the wind trajectory corresponded to the direction of movement of the PAN, allowing us to prioritize the hypothesis of a wind-carried object, such as a reflective Mylar party balloon. Furthermore, a park frequented by children is located along the trajectory of the original position described by the witness (Montsouris Park). The strangeness perceived by the witness remains the relatively fast and straight movement, as well as the absence of noise. The explanation of the party balloon addresses the perceived absence of noise; as for the fast speed, it can be explained by:

- the proximity of the phenomenon, while it was perceived as distant by the witness,
- the wind speed, particularly strong that day with gusts reaching 67 km/h.

On the other hand, the GEIPAN was unable to formally identify the origin of the release, and consequently, this case is classified as "B": probable observation of a wind-carried party balloon.
