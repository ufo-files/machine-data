Description: A witness reports various observations of luminous phenomena of several colors in the night sky during the months of January and February 2017. There is a series of observations of PAN. A PAN was observed by the witness, his companion, and two gendarmes who arrived at the scene. Information gathered during the telephone contact between GEIPAN and one of the gendarmes, and later with the main witness, reveals a misidentification with Arcturus regarding the observation made jointly by the witnesses and the gendarmes (0:50 to 1:40 on January 24). The elevation and azimuth information provided over the phone (witness) does indeed match, with Arcturus ranging between 14 and 25 degrees of elevation and between 75 and 85 degrees East. The same is true for the perceived intensity levels of light compared to other stars. Misidentification sources are fairly common when observing this star at low elevation. Atmospheric thickness and turbulence disrupt light rays (scintillation), creating color variations and the impression of movement. A stronger impression of movement can also result from autocinetic phenomenon (see the autocinetic effect article) especially when fixating very attentively on a bright point. The fact that the witness's other observations also correspond to stars known to create this type of misidentification strengthens this explanation. In the evening around 8:00 PM, the PAN observed could have created a similar effect in approximately the same location in the sky, but it was then Procyon. Similarly, the one observed on February 27 at 2:30 AM and opposite in azimuth (250 degrees from the other side of the house) could be explained by Sirius (15 degrees of elevation). Consequently, GEIPAN classifies all observations as star observations (not always the same one).
