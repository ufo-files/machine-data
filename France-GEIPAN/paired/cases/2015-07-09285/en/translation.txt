Description: A witness reports several successive observations of luminous phenomena seen in the sky. He is making a video of the phenomena. Only one testimony is collected. This observation is quite complex, as it involves two types of phenomena: -a stationary phenomenon, which is very clearly visible in the full west on the video (see astro map): it is the planet Venus, very visible at 30 seconds in the sky (Jupiter can also be slightly seen to the right, at the same height in the sky. Their observation took place at 23:45 that day. - Several other mobile phenomena moving slowly and erratically from the West towards the South (see map). They appear as yellow or yellow-orange lights. "It looks like a big airplane beacon facing you coming from the horizon." The observed doubling could correspond to two planes whose trajectories overlap and then diverge. The light variation is due to the aircraft's heading change: the beacon appears all the more powerful the closer the observer is to the aircraft's axis. This hypothesis seems entirely plausible: aircraft approaching an airport do indeed appear in this manner: from a great distance (>15 km), the strobe lights are much less visible than the landing lights. But the GEIPAN did not receive this testimony quickly enough to be able to confront this observation with air traffic. This hypothesis cannot be strictly confirmed. This GEIPAN case includes the observation of Venus and Jupiter as fixed lights, but it is classified C for mobile lights, which appear to be airplanes seen face-on.
